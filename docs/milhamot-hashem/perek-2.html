2:13

As for your questions that you wrote, that one should ask about what the Rambam of blessed memory wrote: "Let him direct his heart toward the Shekhinah and pray," and in Moreh Nevukhim he wrote that the Shekhinah is a created light, etc. Behold, in your question you have revealed that you are wise in your own eyes and have not learned discipline. Is it, then, the way of one who is asked to question his questioner? In any case I will not withhold my quill from replying, even though you have not acted properly! And I say that it is plain and known to every learned man well versed in the books of Israel that the name Shekhinah is said of 3 things: either of the Name, may He be blessed, as our Master wrote in Laws of Repentance, chapter 7: "Great is repentance, which brings a person near to the Shekhinah, as it is said: 'Return, O Israel, to the LORD your God,' etc., and it is said: 'Yet you did not return to Me, says the LORD,' and it is said: 'If you return, O Israel, to Me you shall return.' That is to say, if you turn in repentance, you will cleave to Me, etc." Again there, on the excellence of repentance: "Yesterday this man was separated from the LORD, the God of Israel, etc., and today he is attached to the Shekhinah, as it is said: 'And you who cleave to the LORD your God,' he cries out and is answered at once, as it is said: 'And it shall be that before they call, I will answer.'" And so is the sense of his words in Laws of Prayer, that if he was in a place where he cannot determine the directions, etc. And so our forebears rendered it: "And if he is in a place and cannot tell the direction, let him direct his heart to his Creator and pray."

Grammar
  • Opening: the questioner has raised a contradiction between two statements of the Rambam; the reply first rebukes him (the rhetorical question and the exclamation) and only then answers.
  • The answer is built on a list: the name Shekhinah is said of 3 things, introduced by the particle for the first one (either ... ), and continued in 2:14 (the second) and 2:15 (the third).
  • Quotations inside quotations: the two passages from the Rambam's Laws of Repentance carry five verses between them (Hos 14:2, Amos 4:6, Jer 4:1; Deut 4:4, Isa 65:24), each introduced by the niphal formula 'as it is said' or 'and it is said'; 'that is to say' (kelomar) glosses Jer 4:1 and stands inside the quotation as the author gives it.
  • The participles in the Rambam's sentences ('separated', 'attached', 'cries out and is answered') describe a state, not a past event; 'separated' and 'attached' are passive participles.
  • The last sentence is Judeo-Arabic (Arabic words in Hebrew letters): a one-line proverb, introduced by 'so our forebears rendered it'. It opens with wa-in, 'and if', followed by the verb kana, 'is'.

The reply claims that the Rambam uses the word Shekhinah in three senses, which resolves the apparent contradiction between the Laws of Prayer ('toward the Shekhinah') and the Guide ('a created light'). The Arabic proverb is translated from the Arabic as it stands. The word the text writes with the letters bet, vav, tzadi, ayin is read letter by letter as bawdi' (cf. mawdi', 'place'; buqca, 'spot', is the alternative, with the same sense), and the word written with an abbreviation mark as yuhayyi'; both readings are the translator's, since the pack offers no lexicon for Judeo-Arabic. The proverb is presented as the Arabic rendering of the Rambam's rule in the Laws of Prayer, and the sense of rendered (he'etiku) is given in the token note. The Rambam passages are translated from the Hebrew the author quotes.

  • Verse: Jer 4:1; the author's text omits the words 'says the LORD' that stand after 'O Israel' in the verse, and the English follows his wording.
  • Verse: Deut 4:4 ('And you who cleave to the LORD your God', cut off at the comma).
  • Verse: Isa 65:24 ('And it shall be that before they call, I will answer').

Two spellings are uncertain: bet-vav-tzadi-ayin, read bawdi' (cf. mawdi', 'place'; alternative buqca, 'spot', the same sense); and the word with the abbreviation mark, read yuhayyi' ('prepares, makes ready'; the English 'direct' follows the context and the Rambam's own 'direct his heart'). Both are translated as the evident word.
2:14

And the second is in a place where the name "the revelation of the Shekhinah" is mentioned, for example, regarding Abraham our father when he went to bind Isaac, of whom it is said: "And he saw the place from afar." Our Rabbis of blessed memory said that he saw a light from afar on Mount Moriah, and as the liturgical poet said: "And he saw a likeness of glory, splendor and honor, and further asked his young men, 'A light: did you see it shining on the top of Mount ha-Mor?' etc." This is the created light that our Master wrote of. And so at the bush: "and he saw, and behold, the bush was burning with fire," and explicitly it is said there: "And the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the bush," and this is a created light. And so in every place where the name "the revelation of the Shekhinah" is mentioned, it is a created light.

Grammar
  • The second sense is identified by a phrase that the text itself sets in quotation marks: the revelation of the Shekhinah. The word shem after the verb 'is mentioned' may be 'name' or 'there'; the English takes 'name' (as in 2:13, 'the name Shekhinah') and keeps the quotation marks of the text.
  • Three witnesses for one light: the Rabbis on Mount Moriah, a liturgical poet, and the burning bush. Each is brought by a different formula: 'said', 'as the poet said', and a bare quotation of the verse at the bush.
  • The same verb is used in Hebrew for 'saw' and 'appeared' (vayar / vayera): Abraham 'saw' the place; the angel 'appeared' to Moses in the fire.
  • The poet's question begins with a ha- that makes it interrogative ('did you see'), and his phrase 'Mount ha-Mor' is a form of Moriah.
  • The conclusion is a rule: wherever the revelation of the Shekhinah is mentioned, it is a created light.

The word shem in 'where the name ... is mentioned' may be 'name' or 'there'; the English takes 'name', as in 'the name Shekhinah' in 2:13, and the other reading is in the apparatus and the readings. The text has the participle 'burning' (bo'er, with a vav) in the bush verse, as Exod 3:2 has the participle. The poet is not named and the quoted lines are not identified.

Other readings (2)
  • שם: there shem as the adverb 'in a place where, there, "the revelation of the Shekhinah" is mentioned'; the English takes 'name' (decisions, pilot ruling).
  • שם: there shem as the adverb The same alternative at the close of the rule.
Apparatus
  • Verse: Gen 22:4 ('And he saw the place from afar').
  • Verse: Exod 3:2. 'And he saw, and behold, the bush was burning with fire' is the second half of the verse (the participle bo'er, which the author spells with a vav); 'And the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the bush' is the first half, which the author quotes second.
  • The poetic lines are quoted from an unnamed liturgical poem; no source is given and none is added.
  • Alternative reading: shem as 'there' ('in a place where "the revelation of the Shekhinah" is mentioned there'), in 2:14.4 and 2:14.77; the English takes 'name'.
2:15

And the third: the providence of the Name, may He be blessed, over us is also called Shekhinah, as our Rabbis of blessed memory said: "They went into exile to Babylon; the Shekhinah was with them." That is to say, His providence, may He be blessed, clung to us, as He promised us in the Torah: "And yet even so, when they are in the land of their enemies, I will not reject them and I will not abhor them so as to destroy them, to break My covenant with them, for I am the LORD their God." And so it is with everything of the kind. Every place is to be understood according to its subject in one of these three mentioned aspects.

Grammar
  • The third sense is God's providence over His people. The proof is a rabbinic saying with no verb for the second clause (the Shekhinah with them) and a verse from the Torah.
  • The verb of the promise in the verse is a perfect, 'I have not rejected them', which in this context states a promise about the future, as the English renders it.
  • Dabekah in 'clung to us' is feminine to agree with 'providence'; the verb 'clung' reuses the stem of the word the Rambam used for repentance (2:13).
  • The paragraph closes with a rule: each occurrence of the word is to be understood according to its subject as one of the three senses.

The rabbinic saying is quoted without a source; none is added. The verse is Lev 26:44 as the author gives it. His spelling of two words differs from the verse; the meaning is not affected.

Other readings (1)
  • לא מאסתים ולא געלתים: I have not rejected them and I have not abhorred them perfect as written The verbs are perfects (Lev 26:44); the English takes them as a promise about the future, which the author's 'as He promised us' supports.
Apparatus
  • Verse: Lev 26:44 ('And yet even so, when they are in the land of their enemies, I will not reject them ... for I am the LORD their God'); the author's text spells 'their enemies' and 'to destroy them' with an added vav.
  • Rabbinic saying: 'They went into exile to Babylon; the Shekhinah was with them'; no source is given.
