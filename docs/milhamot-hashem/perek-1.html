1:2

Let the enlightened man reflect, and let him who fears and trembles at the word of the LORD lament over the state of the people of Israel, upon seeing that every people and each people, of all the divisions of the nations, cherish their tongue and magnify their language, in which they were educated. And in it their faith and their religion were engraved, each man in his own tongue. And we, the people of Israel, since the time that we were exiled from our land, have abandoned our holy tongue, in which the first Adam spoke and in which our holy Torah was given, when His glory, may He be blessed, was revealed on Mount Sinai, and He said to His people, “I am the LORD your God,” etc., and we have not done as we were commanded, and you shall teach them to your children, to speak of them. And as Rashi of blessed memory explained in Parashat Ekev, “from here they said: ‘When the child begins to speak, his father speaks with him in the holy tongue and teaches him Torah.’” Thus far his words, but all our conversation with our children is in the language of the people among whom we dwell, and most of the children’s teachers too, are not able to speak all their needs in the holy tongue, even though they know it, for they have not tried these, and there are no systems for teaching the children of the elementary school. It is known and famous, the compassion of the father for his sons, and how many toils and exertions the parents exert in raising their children. With abundant attention to all their children’s needs: his food and his drink, suited to him, his bedding, and his clothing! But when it comes to striving to bring him into school, he casts him off with a teacher of children, in narrow and dark houses, on remnants of mats on the ground, without cleanliness, for the teacher does not clean the children’s abode, only from one festival to the next. And all day they sit crowded, without order, and breathe foul and musty air. And they drink salty water, and because of this, when some months pass since he was brought to school, growths and a bad boil come up on his head. And their fathers do not look after them to bring them clear, sweet water to drink, nor clean bedding to sit on, and on most days they sit on the ground without a mat! And in the period of the new era, from the year 5650 of the full count of creation, many of the enlightened were stirred and sought an organisation of the schools and an organisation of their children for the study of Torah and the study of the languages Hebrew, Arabic and Turkish, which are in use! And they strove to seat enlightened teachers versed in the Torah of the LORD, to manage the children of the school in moral upbringing and cleanliness of the place of learning, in bright houses and spacious windows in order to breathe fresh and clean air! And all the labour of those enlightened men came to nothing, and their plan did not come to fruition, and the children of the elementary school remained as they were, with a teacher, in dark and musty rooms, sitting in a crowd: this one learns the alef-bet, and this one learns Scripture, and this one learns with the Targum of Onkelos and the translation of Rabbi Saadia Gaon in Arabic, without understanding. Also the very smallest, who are not at all fit to learn, like a child of two and three years, are brought there. And many times the child relieves himself there. And the teacher sends to his mother that she should come and wipe her son’s excrement! And at the call of the one who calls in the newspapers in those days, from Mr. Eliezer Ben-Yehuda, for the revival of the holy Hebrew language, our soul yearned only in words and mere conversations, and it did not come to realization until the year 5670, when our exalted government, the government of Turkey, then deigned to open schools for the children of Israel, and there was made there a fine organisation for the children of Israel, in spacious upper rooms with open windows, and bedding of wool, and curtains of goat-hair, and also tables for the older children to learn and to write on them! Then we said: behold, the time has come to bring out Ashtonot Ish Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, which, before she travailed, bore the great man in the revival of the Hebrew language, namely Eliezer Ben-Yehuda, and we acknowledge our debt to him for what he did and made, and with all his strength he toiled for the good of the people of Israel and the settlement of the land of Israel and the revival of the Hebrew language; this holy work he performed with wondrous alacrity and diligence; he did, and succeeded in raising it from the dust, and after its wearing out it had delight, and because of his love for the holy tongue its wrinkles were smoothed out, and the appearance of its face budded and blossomed, and it found grace and honour in the eyes of its seekers, its knowers, and its acquaintances, and they awoke from their sleep to gird up their senses, to widen it and to enrich it as in the days of its youth, before the exile of the people of Israel! And because of this mitzvah, may his soul be bound up in the bundle of life with the LORD our God, and from the fruit of his labour may his soul be satisfied in Eden, the garden of God!

Grammar
  • יתבונן and ויתאונן are jussives (hitpa'el): 'let him reflect' and 'and let him lament', a wish addressed to the enlightened and the fearful, not a plain report.
  • בראותו is a construct infinitive with a pronominal suffix, 'upon his seeing'; the clause that follows, אשר כל עם ועם ..., states what he sees.
  • Rashi's quotation runs from כשהתינוק to תורה, introduced by שמכאן אמרו ('from here they said') and closed by עכ״ל. The words before it are the author's own.
  • ולמדתם אותם את בניכם לדבר בם is Deut 11:19 in the second person plural, embedded in the first person plural of ולא עשינו: the author speaks for the community, and the verse's 'you' is the people it addresses.
  • חתור in הגיע חתור להכניסו is the infinitive of חתר, 'to strive'; the clause is 'when it comes to striving to bring him in'.
  • מרגל לרגל: רגל here is a festival (the three pilgrim festivals), so 'from festival to festival', i.e. rarely.

This paragraph is in the author's own voice (the publisher's voice begins at 1:10). The Hebrew full stops are kept as the English sentence breaks, and the long periods are divided into sentences without dropping a clause.

Ancient Near East
  • Eliezer Ben-Yehuda (1858-1922) led the revival of Hebrew as a spoken language, in Jerusalem.
  • Beit rabban, 'house of the master', was the traditional elementary school; the author says children there learned the alphabet and Scripture with a translation, without understanding it.

1:3

From the time the government opened the school, the children were studying Scripture and its translations, Onkelos and Rabbi Saadia Gaon, Mishnah and the Rambam, and grammar and the explanation of the words, all was done properly the study of the Torah, Prophets and Writings with clear explanation, a grammar book of the Holy Tongue! and the study of the Turkish language through the Turkish teacher, upright of heart, Chafetz Effendi. And after him Zia Bey with wonderful diligence! and we accustomed them to speak one with another in Hebrew and in Turkish, so that they would be well accustomed in their mouths, and at the turn of the year the examiners came from the government, may his majesty be exalted, and the children arranged their studies in Turkish and they showed them their writing, and the examiners saw their talents on display in their studies, and they praised them, that they are educated! Also the Hebrew examiners came on the day after and the children arranged their studies before them in Hebrew and in Arabic, and they found favour in their eyes for the ability of their studies, and praised them! And the following year the children did so, studying with diligence and alacrity, and at the end of the year the Turkish examiners came and examined them with uprightness of heart, and praised them, that they were making progress in their studies, and urged them with words and blessed them! But as for the Hebrew examiners who came after them, there had already begun to flare up among them the hatred of enlightenment and of the study of languages. And they began to provoke them and to ask them about what they had not yet studied, and whoever could not answer anything, in any place where they asked him as is due to a complete sage, was counted by them as nothing at all. In order to diminish their honour, to sadden them and to slacken their hands from the studies, and they sought faults and blemishes in each and every one, and out of malice they did not turn to the studies and the knowledge that the children had acquired. Only to trip them up over what they had not yet studied, and to question them on the order of Nezikin, while they were still at the order of Moed, and when they showed them the beauty of their writing, the splendid and orderly Ashuri script, they sought a fault in the joining, as fine as a hair's thread, in some of the letters, as if they were writing Torah scrolls, tefillin and mezuzot. And they did not look at the beauty of the script and its order. And in the interpretation of the verses they asked them to solve for them difficult verses, such as "Binding his foal to the vine", and the like, and they went out from before them in low spirits, good, grieved, sad and with limp hands.

Grammar
  • The paragraph is a chain of clauses joined by ו and by the time phrases that open each school year: ולתקופת השנה (3:63), ולשנה האחרת (3:111), ובצאת השנה (3:119). The sentence that begins with ומעת has its main verb in היו הילדים לומדים (3:8).
  • והרגלנום לדבר זה עם זה (3:50) is a hif'il perfect with an object suffix; the purpose clause כדי שיהיו מורגלים בפיהם היטב (3:58) follows it.
  • The sentence ואחריו זיא ביי בחריצות נפלאה! (3:45-3:49) has no verb, and the English adds none beyond the words.
  • The turn to hostility comes at אך (3:139): the Hebrew examiners who came after them are set against the Turkish examiners, and the verb התחילה (3:145) starts the change. The purpose clause כדי להפחית כבודם (3:176) states the aim.
  • The phrase אסרי לגפן עירה (3:248-3:250) is quoted as an example of a hard verse, so the English keeps quotation marks; the source is Gen 49:11.

1:4

And at the turn of the third year the war broke out against the good studies and the pleasant arrangements, as we are commanded by our Rabbis of blessed memory. The opponents spread an evil slander, saying that through this the children have gone out, God forbid, into bad ways, and many of the fools believed their lies and took their children out of the school. Also the foolish boys who had gone out of the school are hostile toward their teachers, because in their humble opinion they are heretics and stand outside the community. But those of understanding among them know and recognise that a lie is in the right hand of those who spread this evil slander, and they were greatly distressed that their fathers had taken them out of school in their folly! And when they had led many astray, the hand of the benighted grew strong and by false informing they informed against the teachers before the government, that they wished to incite the inhabitants of the land to rebel. And they informed that they were writers of libel against the government, to the government of England, to shelter in its shadow, and also to H.N.Ts. And because of this informing the teachers were led to prison, and also expelled from the school. And they brought in their place another teacher, who knew neither Hebrew thoroughly nor Arabic as it should be. And he had no duty to teach them except the weekly portion, with the Targum of Onkelos and the Targum of Rabbi Saadia Gaon in Arabic. And he was to teach them the Haftarah with the Targum of Jonathan in Aramaic, with no understanding at all, like the other teachers! And after some days the school closed entirely! All this came upon us because we did not object, for the honour of our Creator, when we were hearing from those who study the Zohar and other Kabbalists, things that are forbidden to hear, and we were silent before them as our Rabbis of blessed memory said: "R. A. was seized for heresy, etc.; perhaps a word of heresy, etc." and there was fulfilled in us the saying of our Rabbis of blessed memory: "Whoever concedes to them falls into their hands; whoever relies on them, what is his is theirs." Thus far! Therefore we abandoned our way of studying the languages, Hebrew and Arabic, in their grammar, against our will, because of the wrath of the oppressor, through false informing, and we turned to the path of holiness, to the honour of our Father who is in heaven, in the nullification of the opinions of the philosopher, the false prophet, the author of the Zohar, who gathered into his hands the opinions of ancient philosophy, the worshippers of idols, images and hidden powers, which they had invented from their own heart. And the author of the Zohar seized their opinions, and thought to bring them into the faith of our holy Torah, and falsely called his name by the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna, and said that the Holy One, blessed be He, who is called by him Atika Kadisha, was revealed to him and revealed to him hidden mysteries. And he hung his prophecy and his ideas on Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna, peace be upon him. And he made his composition, the Zohar, and said that the higher ones agreed to his composition, because this philosopher saw that the Mishnah, the two Talmuds and the true midrashim of our Rabbis of blessed memory stood against him in the belief in His oneness, may He be blessed, therefore he thought to disqualify them, and he despised and mocked them and all who occupy themselves with them, and he called them by names of contempt and abasement, as the reader will see within! And he promised that Israel would not merit redemption until they turned from their way of occupying themselves with the Mishnah and the Talmud. Let them occupy themselves only with his new Torah, full of idols of images and faces of monstrous form, and various couplings. And by cunning and deceit they buried it in a chest in the ground, so that it would grow old and begin to rot; after some time they published it and brought it out through a king from among the kings of the idolaters (as Rabbi Chida wrote in his book Shem ha-Gedolim, in the section on books), as if it were an ancient composition of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna. And many of our later rabbis were drawn after the aforementioned philosopher with eyes shut, for he took them with the smoothness of his lips and his trimmings, and they did not give heed to his lies! Heaven forbid that any Jew should believe that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna or another of our Rabbis of blessed memory believed in such beliefs to exchange and replace the LORD our God, who made known His ways to Moses and His deeds to the children of Israel, for "He is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in kindness," etc. for a strange god, quick to anger (Ze'ir Anpin), and to conjoin and associate with Him five partzufim whose existence has not been established even by proof (Rabbi Ovadiah Sforno, Deuteronomy 13:3; and there 29:25; and see there 28:14) and to call them by the name of the LORD our God, and the LORD, the God of truth, whose existence has been established by many proofs, strong as a cast mirror, as Rabbi Saadia Gaon wrote in Emunot ve-De'ot, and the Rabbi, the author of Chovot ha-Levavot, and the Rambam in Moreh Nevukhim and in the Yad, let us abandon Him and leave Him, and say that He has no name and no appellation, and let us worship the partzufim and the forms which, according to his words, were created and descended from Him! And we have already expounded at length within, with clear proofs from our Rabbis of blessed memory, on the nullification of these strange opinions of the philosopher, the false prophet, the aforementioned! For it is the aim of our holy Torah to distance us from belief in idols, whether corporeal or spiritual. And its aim is to know that the LORD is God; there is no other besides Him. And He has neither father nor brother nor son (Rabbah, parashat Yitro), and did not receive His kingship from another and is not destined to hand it on to another, as our Rabbis of blessed memory explained (Rabbah, Bamidbar; Shir ha-Shirim, parashah 1; Yerushalmi, Sanhedrin, chapter Dinei Mamonot) on the verse "I am the first and I am the last, and besides Me there is no God," and on what Nebuchadnezzar said: "and the appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods!" And I have seen Rabbi Menachem di Lonzano, in the book Derekh ha-Chayyim, who opened his mouth wide to speak error about the holy pietist, the author of Chovot ha-Levavot, concerning his saying, in the name of the philosopher, that one could not worship the Cause of causes, except the prophet of the generation by his nature, or the outstanding philosopher in what he acquired from wisdom; but others worship other than Him, because they do not understand: the result is only composite! And at these words of the pietist he was angered and thought that here was ground for complaint against him, and found a pretext to mislead the listeners and alarm them by saying: "Is it so that such-and-such and such-and-such of our later famous sages worship other than Him?" And he counted them, in his language, one by one by name. And he did not take to heart that many of the sages whom he counted went along with eyes shut and believed the words of the author of the Zohar who called himself by the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna, in order to attach his false prophecies to the name of the Tanna, and spoke rebellion against the LORD our God, who was revealed to him in his study house as Atika Kadisha, and revealed to him hidden secrets and hidden powers (Zohar, Bereshit 22), for the Creator is the idol called Abba, who used to command his consort, the she-idol Imma, and she does all that he says to her, except for the creation of the first man: it was she, Imma, who said and made the first man, not with the will of Abba! And in Parashat Vayera, folio 112, and likewise in Parashat Balak, folio 191, he ascribes our worship and our prayers to a strange god, quick to anger (Ze'ir Anpin), who did not make the heavens and the earth. And he calls these idols by the name of the LORD our God. And about Ze'ir Anpin, Jeremiah the prophet, peace be upon him, proclaimed "Thus shall you say to them: the gods that did not make the heavens and the earth shall perish from the earth and from under these heavens." For it is clear in many places in the Zohar that he is created, not creator (see Zohar, Bereshit 22; and Yosher Levav, folio 3b). And yet he says to worship him, and to bless him, and to accept him as God, and to fear his consort! And many of our later rabbis, whom he mentioned in the book Derekh ha-Chayyim, in his amazement at the pietist, the author of Chovot ha-Levavot, went along with eyes shut and were drawn after him and believed his belief and accepted upon themselves the idol, quick to anger (Ze'ir Anpin), as God! And all this came upon them because they did not contemplate the wisdom of the Creator of all, which is to investigate and examine the works of Creation as explained in the words of the Rabbi, the author of Moreh Nevukhim, and in the words of the pietist, the author of Chovot ha-Levavot, and our master Saadia Gaon and Rabbi Yehudah Halevi in the Kuzari, as the prophet, peace be upon him, said: "They do not regard the deed of the LORD." For they thought their words were Greek wisdom, and they kept far from it, as far as a bow-shot, and they also did not take to heart the true words of our forefathers of blessed memory in the midrashim that Abraham our father did not recognise his Creator except by contemplating the work of the LORD, and investigated and expounded them and brought proofs from them until he attained the truth; then the Holy One, blessed be He, was revealed to him and said to him: "You love righteousness and hate wickedness," etc. A parable: to one walking on the road who saw a castle, etc. (Midrash ha-Gadol, at the beginning of Parashat Lekh Lekha). And perforce we acknowledge the truth that the words of the pietist, the author of Chovot ha-Levavot, clearly say that one cannot worship the God of truth except, etc. but others worship other than Him, as the prophet said: "For many days Israel was without a true God," and as our eyes see and our ears hear, to the point that a man can say to his fellow! "Your people is my people, and your God is not my God!" And to the God of truth let us pour out our prayer, to guide us on paths of uprightness, and to deliver us from every strange belief. Amen, may it be His will.

Grammar
  • Voice: 1:4 is in the author's own voice; 'we' (עלינו, עזבנו, נודה) is the author and the circle of the Yemenite teachers. The long periods are divided into English sentences, and no clause is dropped.
  • Aramaic quotations: 1:4.198-206 (דמודי להון) is Bavli Aramaic; 1:4.274-275 (עתיקא קדישא) and 1:4.278 (רזין טמירין) are Zohar Aramaic; 1:4.591-600 is Biblical Aramaic (Dan 3:25) and 1:4.800-815 is Biblical Aramaic (Jer 10:11).
  • Allusions with no quotation formula are translated from the verse and take no marks: 'שקר בימין' (1:4.61-62, Isa 44:20), 'בחלק שפתיו' (1:4.396-397, Prov 7:21), and 'ולדעת כי ה' הוא האלהים אין עוד מלבדו' (1:4.540-547, Deut 4:35). The other verses behind the author's phrases are listed in the apparatus.
  • 1:4.667 'וכי' opens a rhetorical question, kept as a question. 1:4.688 'שַמָנָה' as pointed would be 'grew fat' (Deut 32:15), but the context requires 'שֶׁמָּנָה', 'whom he counted': he counted the sages 'one by one by name' (1:4.676-680), and 1:4.844-847 says the same again ('whom he mentioned'). The English follows the context.
  • 1:4.76 התעו is Hif'il, 'they led astray' (transitive, with רבים as object). 1:4.90 להמריד is Hif'il of מרד, 'to make rebel'. 1:4.198-206 is Aramaic: דמתרחץ is Itpa'al of רחץ 'to trust, to rely' (cf. Dan 3:28 רחצו עלוהי), not 'to bathe'.
  • Sentences that open after a Hebrew full stop with no main clause of their own: 1:4.94 ושהם, 1:4.146 והפטרה, 1:4.540 ולדעת. The English keeps the full stop and supplies a main clause from the sentence before (token notes); 1:4.896 ורבינו is a continuation of a list and is joined to the sentence before it.

The paragraph is the author's own voice, a continuous prose period. The pack has no morphology, lexicon or commentary, so the reasons for the choices are in the token notes and the apparatus.

Other readings (4)

  • ב״ע: in Arabic context ב״ע read as בערבי, the abbreviation of the 'in Arabic' of 1:4.145.
  • וגיזומיו: and his exaggerations גוזמא גיזומיו may come from the Aramaic גוזמא, 'exaggeration'; the English keeps 'trimmings' (cuttings) pending the owner's ruling.

Ancient Near East
  • Chida is Rabbi Chayyim Yosef David Azulai; his Shem ha-Gedolim is a bibliographical dictionary of Jewish authors and books, which the author cites here.
  • Ze'ir Anpin, Abba, Imma and Atika Kadisha are the partzufim of the Zohar's divine structure. The author calls them idols in his own charge, and the English gives his words as he writes them.
  • Saadia Gaon's Arabic translation of the Torah is taught in Yemenite communities beside the Aramaic Targum Onkelos; Targum Jonathan is the Aramaic Targum to the Prophets, from which the Haftarah is read.

1:5

And I named the book The Wars of the LORD for His unity, because the philosopher, the author of the book the Zohar, spoiled for us the truth of the unity of the Name, may He be blessed, and several rabbis and great men of the Torah followed him with eyes shut. And of them Solomon said in his wisdom: "For she has cast down many slain, and mighty are all her slain." As our Rabbis of blessed memory expounded (Sanhedrin, the Rif): "For she has cast down many slain" this is a student who has not reached instruction and teaches, (as we see with our own eyes how many authors who bring an elephant through the eye of a needle, from the scantness of their expertise) "and mighty are all her slain" this is a student who has reached instruction and does not teach (who does not set his mind to teach others correctly; "mighty" is the word of "and he shuts his eyes", Rashi of blessed memory). Grammar
  • The paragraph is one long period. The quotation from Lev 19:4 (in the Yalkut, Midrash ha-Gadol and Rambam) runs from 'Do not turn' to 'Thus far'; its parenthesis is Sforno's gloss, kept in parentheses.
  • The Midrash ha-Gadol passage (Rabbinic Hebrew) chains verses with 'and it is written' (וכתיב): Ps 19:9, Prov 23:31 (in the Midrash's wording) and Ps 104:15. Its analogy 'just as ... so' (מה ... כך) carries the argument, and the second half turns on 'too much' (מדאי).
  • The infinitives after עשה (לתור, לחשב, להעור, ולסתום, ולהגדיל) give the purpose of the philosopher's deed; לתור אחרי לבבו alludes to Num 15:39, and להעור עיני חכמים to Deut 16:19.
  • בחשבם שהוא לרשב״י התנא: the ל of לרשב״י is possessive. The early students thought the book was Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna's.
  • The verbs in the sentence about the early students (חשבו, והחשיבום, וכיבדו, עשו, וקיבלום) narrate what followed from the first error, in the perfect with waw.
  • דאגדתא, מינין and לא אמרינן כלא חד are Talmudic Aramaic inside the author's Hebrew; the last two clauses are the halakhic conclusion of the paragraph and the English keeps them as parallel sentences.

One period in the Hebrew, divided at the quotation boundaries and at the Midrash's clauses. Each quotation formula is translated and each closing ע״כ is 'Thus far'.

Grammar
  • כל שכן and וקל וחומר are the two Talmudic forms of the a fortiori argument, and the English names the argument once, at the start.
  • The שלא clause depends on הקפידה: the Torah insisted that one not attach to God or associate with Him anything created.
  • Exod 22:19 is quoted with שנאמר, an explicit quotation, so it takes double quotation marks; the verse itself is in the apparatus.
  • ולא אמרינן כלא חד כיון ש… is the reasoning: since the Torah has made clear that He is one, we may not speak of Him as one of the ones.
  • הנה כן המזכירים את ה׳ אל דמי לכם takes Isa 62:6 ("you who mention the LORD, do not be silent") and applies it to teaching God's oneness. אל דמי is "do not be silent" (דמי, silence or rest), לכם is an ethical dative, and מלהודיע "from making known" completes it: "do not stay silent about making known His oneness".

Rendered by sense: כלא חד is the one clause whose sense is taken from its context; the literal is 'as one'.
