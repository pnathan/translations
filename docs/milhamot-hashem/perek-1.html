1:7

All the more so, a fortiori, regarding our God, blessed be He, and blessed be His name, that the Torah insisted that one should not attach to Him or associate with Him anything of all created things, whether corporeal or spiritual, as it is said: "He who sacrifices to the gods shall be destroyed, save to the LORD alone." And Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai the Tanna, peace be upon him, said: "Whoever associates the name of Heaven with something else is uprooted from the world." And we should not say that He is one as the others are one, since the Torah has made clear plainly in many places that He is one, unique, apart from all the other ones. Thus, you who mention the LORD, do not stay silent about making known His oneness, may He be blessed, to your children and your disciples, as you are commanded.

Grammar
  • כל שכן and וקל וחומר are the two Talmudic forms of the a fortiori argument, and the English names the argument once, at the start.
  • The שלא clause depends on הקפידה: the Torah insisted that one not attach to God or associate with Him anything created.
  • Exod 22:19 is quoted with שנאמר, an explicit quotation, so it takes double quotation marks; the verse itself is in the apparatus.
  • ולא אמרינן כלא חד כיון ש… is the reasoning: since the Torah has made clear that He is one, we may not speak of Him as one of the ones.
  • הנה כן המזכירים את ה׳ אל דמי לכם takes Isa 62:6 ("you who mention the LORD, do not be silent") and applies it to teaching God's oneness. אל דמי is "do not be silent" (דמי, silence or rest), לכם is an ethical dative, and מלהודיע "from making known" completes it: "do not stay silent about making known His oneness".

Rendered by sense: כלא חד is the one clause whose sense is taken from its context; the literal is 'as one'.

Other readings (1)
  • ולא אמרינן כלא חד: and we do not say, "All is one," Kabbalistic formula כולא חד כלא חד may be the Aramaic formula "all is one", which the author disowns; the English reads it as "as one", which supplies "that He is one as the others are one".
Apparatus
  • Exod 22:19, quoted with שנאמר: זובח לאלהים יחרם בלתי לה׳ לבדו. The author writes לה׳ where the verse has ליהוה; the English gives 'the LORD'.
  • Isa 62:6, embedded without quotation marks: המזכירים את יהוה אל דמי לכם ("you who mention the LORD, do not be silent"). The author writes ה׳ for יהוה and continues the clause with מלהודיע אחדותו.
1:8

Because the beginning of this composition was made at first by the question of one student: whom shall we worship? Therefore I placed at its opening the question of the student, and its reply from one of the learners who persisted the first, second and third time.

and-since that-the-beginning-of this-composition was-made at-first by-way-of the-hand-of the-question-of a-student one (object) whom we-shall-worship on-so I-placed its-beginning the-question-of the-student and-its-reply from-one of-the-learners who-persisted the-time first and-second and-third

Grammar
  • ולפי שתחלת החבור הזה נעשה… על ידי שאלת תלמיד אחד: the clause gives the reason for the book's form, a dialogue opened by a student's question.
  • The student's question את מי נעבוד is a direct question inside the author's sentence; it is kept as a question.
  • על כן הנחתי התחלתו: 'therefore I placed its beginning', in the author's first person.
  • פעם ראשונה ושנייה ושלישית is an adverbial string: 'the first, second and third time'.

The student's question is kept as a question; the English places the question mark, which the Hebrew does not write.

Grammar
  • הצעיר is an adjective in apposition to יחיה: the author's self-description, 'the humble'.
  • בן סלי׳ אלקאפח is the patronymic and the surname; the abbreviated form is kept as written.
  • יצ״ו is an abbreviated blessing in the optative: 'may his Rock keep him and give him life'.

1:11

The book The Wars of the LORD makes known the way of the truths of His unity, may He be blessed, according to the Torah and its interpreters, our Rabbis of blessed memory, in the Mishnah, in the Talmud, Bavli and Yerushalmi, and the Midrashim of our Rabbis of blessed memory, and in accordance with the view of the Geonim, Rabbi Saadia Gaon, and Rabbeinu Bachya in Chovot ha-Levavot, and Rabbi Yehuda Halevi in his poems, and in the Kuzari, the Rambam, the Semag (Sefer Mitzvot Gadol) and the author of the Ikkarim, of blessed memory, and as is accepted by every Jew. In it is a severe warning not to worship any created thing, in accordance with the tradition of all our prophets and all our sages, the Tannaim, the Amoraim, the Geonim and the early decisors, from the mouth of Moses, Aaron and the elders, not to deviate from them and not to add to them.

Grammar
  • The sentence has the subject ספר with the participle המודיע and the object דרך אמתות יחודו; the rest is a list of sources.
  • The list is joined by ו and by ב (in): 'in the Mishnah', 'in the Bavli', 'in the Kuzari'.
  • ובו אזהרה חמורה שלא לעבוד: 'and in it a severe warning not to worship'; the שלא clause gives the content of the warning.
  • כפי קבלת … מפי משה ואהרן וזקנים לא לזוז מהם ולא להוסיף עליהם: the chain of tradition ends in two infinitives, 'not to deviate' and 'not to add'.

Title per the decisions (Q1). The Geonim and the Kuzari are given as context.

Ancient Near East
  • The Geonim (singular Gaon) were the heads of the Babylonian academies in the centuries after the Talmud.
  • The Kuzari is set at the court of the king of the Khazars.
Apparatus
  • Num 21:14, the title: ספר מלחמת ה׳ (The Wars of the LORD), as the title page gives it.
1:12

Know, esteemed reader, that you will not grasp the intention of this book, unless you read and study all of it, for its author made nights as days, and labored and scrutinized many books, and drew from seas and rivers proofs strong as a cast mirror, and the matters did not come from his own opinion. Therefore I ask you, esteemed reader, that you not come with the fist of Esau, in mockery, but with the voice of Jacob, and to consider the matters, and whoever is lacking in strength, let him be silent from speech and be still, and may El Shaddai give you mercy, and the merit of the author will protect us, and upon all His people Israel.

Grammar
  • ודע לך: an imperative with 'to you', 'know'; the author addresses the reader in the second person throughout.
  • לא תשיג כונת הספר הזה אלא אחרי שתקרא: 'you will not grasp… unless after you read…'.
  • כי מחברו עשה לילות כימים: the second כי is causal, 'for its author made nights as days'.
  • ולכן אבקשך שלא תבוא באגרונו של עשו בהתולים אלא בקולו של יעקב: the request contrasts the fist of Esau with the voice of Jacob.

The allusion to Gen 27:22 is kept without quotation marks; the author's 'fist' is kept where the verse has 'hands'.

Apparatus
  • Gen 27:22, an allusion without quotation marks: 'the voice is the voice of Jacob, but the hands are the hands of Esau'. The author writes אגרון (fist) where the verse has ידים (hands).
  • Job 37:18, embedded without quotation marks: חזקים כראי מוצק ("strong as a cast mirror"). The author puts כראי מוצק before חזקים.
  • Gen 43:14, embedded without quotation marks: ואל שדי יתן לכם רחמים ("and may El Shaddai grant you mercy"). The author stops the verse there, without לפני האיש.
