3:1

§2. And all the more so we should not believe him when he says that Elijah was revealed to him, or the Elder of Elders, who is Atika Kadisha, which is called Keter Elyon (the Supreme Crown), for surely he is a false prophet, and his death is by strangulation. As is explained in the words of the Rambam in his commentary on the Mishnah, in his introduction to the Order of Zera'im. And in the Gemara, Pesachim 34, we read: "It was stated: the distraction of the mind. Rabbi Yochanan said: a disqualification by impurity, for if Elijah should come and declare it clean and say that impurity had not touched them, we listen to him; and Resh Lakish said: it is a disqualification of the body, for if Elijah should come and declare it clean, we do not listen to him." Thus far his words. And Rashi, of blessed memory, explains: "A disqualification of the body is a special feature in sacred offerings, in that they are disqualified by it; and there the disqualification is in itself; and even if Elijah should come and say that they were not defiled during all those days that he distracted his mind from them, we do not listen to him." Thus far. Hence, anyone who comes to change and to permit what is forbidden, even a rabbinic prohibition, we do not listen to him.

Grammar
  • שלא נאמין לו: 'that we should not believe him', a clause with שלא and the imperfect after כ"ש, 'all the more so'.
  • סבא דסבין is Aramaic: 'the Elder of Elders', with the Aramaic relative ד and the title given as a name-like epithet.
  • The Talmud passage is quoted with 'גרס' (גרסינן, 'we read'); the closing 'עכ"ל' ends it, and 'ע"כ' ends Rashi's words after it.
  • שאם in the two rulings is explanatory, 'for if': Elijah's testimony is offered as the test of whether the disqualification is one of impurity or of the body. הסח הדעת is a noun phrase, 'the distraction of the mind'.
  • ויטהרנה: the verb with the object suffix -נה; the English 'declare it clean' takes the suffix as 'it'.

The paragraph has three parts: the refusal to believe the claimed revelation, a Talmud passage with Resh Lakish's view, and Rashi's explanation with the conclusion. The Hebrew is the Wikisource base text.

§3. And in truth, you began the words of your mouth with folly and wicked madness, in that you said: "How my heart emboldened me to inquire and to investigate, etc." And why not? On the contrary, the reverse is more reasonable (since they say to worship Ze'ir Anpin, who is created, Yosher Levav, folio 3b, and strength to God). It is a complete obligation upon us and upon each and every one of Israel to know the LORD, that He is Creator and not created, and to worship Him, as it is written: "And you shall know this day and take it to your heart." "And you shall know that the LORD your God is God, etc." And it says: "Know the God of your father and worship Him." And the Sages permitted restraining from the forbidden, even in the privy and in the bathhouse, for it is forbidden to meditate in them on words of Torah. And to restrain from the forbidden is permitted. And as to your astonishment, how could they err? There is no wonder in this. Nor is the sect of the later Kabbalists any better, those who went in their simplicity after the command of the philosopher, the author of the Zohar who attributed it to Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, and Rabbi Eleazar his son, and his companions of the Great Sanhedrin who sat in the chamber of the Hewn Stone about whom Scripture said: "If a matter is too hard for you in judgment, etc." "And you shall rise and go up to the place that the LORD your God will choose, etc." "And you shall act according to the word that they tell you, etc." And yet, for all this, the Torah said that if the Great Court erred in a ruling and the people acted on their word, they bring an offering for their error. Grammar
  • שהחלת דברי פיך סכלות והוללות רעה: echoes Eccl 10:13; 'folly and wicked madness' is what they began with, and 'שאמרת' ('in that you said') introduces the reader's own words.
  • איך מלאני לבי לדרוש ולחקור: the infinitives לדרוש and לחקור follow 'emboldened me'; the reader's question is closed by 'וכו'' and the author's reply begins with 'ולמה לא'.
  • ועוז לאלוהים sits in the author's parenthesis and no formula introduces it, so it takes no quotation marks; the parenthesis is kept as written.
  • לאפרושי מאיסורא: Aramaic causative infinitive, 'to restrain (someone) from a prohibition'; the author's point is that the Sages allowed even speech of Torah in the forbidden places to restrain from a transgression.
  • ואומר: 'and it says' introduces a verse; 'ועבדהו' is the verb 'worship' with the object suffix -הו, 'worship him'.
  • The conditional clauses (אם שגגו ... מביאים; ואם שגגו ... מביא) are built on perfects of 'to err' with present-tense apodosis, as the verses have them.

4) The authority of our rabbis, the masters of the new Kabbalah, shall not be greater than the authority of the Great Beit Din who sat in the Chamber of Hewn Stone, and their grasp is no greater than the grasp of the Great Sanhedrin! And yet the Torah said that if they erred and ruled to permit the forbidden, or sinned inadvertently in idolatry, etc. We have learned from the Torah and from our rabbis, in the tractate Horayot, that the Great Beit Din can err and rule a permission in some particular case of idolatry. For man is formed of clay, whether great or small, and is prone to err; therefore the Torah commanded: "If a matter is too hard for you in judgment, etc. and you shall arise and go up to the place that the LORD your God will choose," in order to lessen the error. How is it not impossible that even the Great Beit Din should not err. For it is said in the Torah, "and a matter is hidden from the eyes of the assembly. And it shall be, if from the eyes of the congregation it was done in error." Grammar
  • ולא יהא כח ... גדול מכח: a negated future (יהא) with a comparison; the English keeps the repeated 'the authority of' so that the comparison is plain.
  • איך אינו נמנע שלא יטעו: נמנע is 'impossible', so the clause says that it is impossible for even the Great Beit Din not to err, that is, error is unavoidable; the full stop of the Hebrew is kept and the English keeps 'How is it not ...'.
  • Three Torah passages are cited: Deut 17:8 (quoted, with וכו' as the author's truncation sign), Lev 4:13 and Num 15:24 (introduced by שהרי נאמר בתורה and continued by והיה).
  • The long period ולפי קוצר דעתינו ... is divided at 'when we see' and 'how was their faith'; no clause is dropped and the order of the list of authors is kept. It has no main clause of its own: the main clause is ואמונה זו החדשה רחוקה מאד in 3:4.

Plain sentences follow the Hebrew full stops. The Torah quotations take double quotation marks; the author's וכו' is kept as 'etc.'. The list of authors is elliptical in the Hebrew after the full stop at הנבוכים, and the English keeps that ellipsis. The period that begins ולפי קוצר דעתינו has no main clause here; 3:4 supplies it.

Grammar
  • The paragraph is a chain of first-person plural past acts (פירשנו, ודבקנו, שאלנו); the reply of the other side is in the perfect (והיתה).
  • ושאלנו אתכם ... איך יסכימו: the question is indirect (איך with the imperfect); the English keeps it as a report, 'how would ... agree'.
  • וכראותכם ... חרה אפכם: כראותכם is the infinitive with כ, 'upon your seeing'; the tense comes from the main verb חרה, hence 'when you saw'.
  • וברמות רוחא טפלתם עלינו שקר זדים: the Aramaic idiom ברמות רוחא is translated by its sense; the rest adapts Ps 119:69 (טפלו עלי שקר זדים), in which זדים, 'the arrogant', is the subject; here it names those addressed.

The list of sources is kept in the order the author gives it. Midrash ha-Gadol is a title; Nur al-Tzalam is transliterated as printed (see apparatus).

Grammar
  • The Haggadah's wicked son asks 'What is this worship to you?' (מה העבודה הזאת לכם, Exod 12:26) and the Haggadah adds 'לכם ולא לו'; the author quotes both, and the English sets them in quotation marks as a quoted question.
  • In 'וידעת היום והשבות אל לבבך' the verbs are second-person forms of Deut 4:39; 'כי ה' הוא האלהים' has no copula in Hebrew, so 'is God' supplies it.
  • 'ומן התורה אחד דיני ממונות ואחד דיני נפשות בדרישה וחקירה' has no finite verb; אחד ... ואחד means 'both ... and', so civil and capital cases alike are subject to inquiry and examination; 'require' is supplied from 'צריכים' in the next sentences.
  • 'אמרו חכמים' is an explicit quotation formula, so the words it introduces take double quotation marks; the author gives no source, and none is added. The quotation is closed after 'וכו'', because what follows ('במה דברים אמורים') carries no formula.
  • 'במה דברים אמורים' is a fixed Mishnaic question: 'in what case is this said?' 'ואין צריך לומר' is 'needless to say', and its object is understood from the preceding clause.

And in our case all of them are in it, and how did your heart dare to accept evil talk from foolish men who walk barefoot, like men banned by heaven, and to consider it complete testimony, to hold your fellow in the Torah and in the commandments of Scriptural and rabbinic law as apikorsim and heretics, who are among those whom one lowers and does not raise? Grammar
  • The quotation opens with כ' (he wrote) after the verse לא תעמוד על דם רעך, and closes at עכ"ל. Within it, direct address is in single marks, and the verse לא תעמוד על דם רעך is a single-marked quotation inside the outer quotation.
  • ולכך נסמכה זה לזה: 'for this reason this was placed next to that'. The verse לא תלך רכיל is the second member of the pair; ותאמר ('and you might say') begins the objection that the author answers.
  • עמדו ולא ענו עוד: both verbs are third-person plural, and the words are Job 32:16. 'They stood and answered no more' follows the verse; the Hebrew names no subject and none is added.
  • כששומע רשע מדבר סרה על צדיק, או לוחש מלחש בשקרים על חברו: לוחש is a participle parallel to מדבר, both governed by כששומע ('when he hears a wicked one speak ... or whisper ...'), so the wicked one is the whisperer.
  • וכל שכן ('all the more so') introduces the consequence. The three participles כשומע, כעונה, כמדבר ('as listener, as answerer, as speaker') are the ways of taking part, and כולם יספו ('all of them shall perish') refers back to them; for the other reading of the כ ('like'), see readings.

The opening verse is in double marks because it stands outside the quotation; the verse quoted inside the quotation (3:8.90-3:8.96) is in single marks.

Grammar
  • לא דייך שלא עשית (3:9.5-7): an idiom, "it was not enough for you that you did not do". The charge is not only the omission but the belief and acceptance that follow ("but", אלא).
  • בעד אחד ודיין אחד, ושלא בפני בעל דין, ובלא חקירה ובלא דרישה, ובלא בדיקה (3:9.16-29): the grounds of acceptance are listed in order, each negative with its own ובלא; the English keeps the repetition.
  • The Pesachim passage, after גרסינן, is Talmudic Hebrew with its own formulas (אמר רב ששת, כתיב בהדיא, וקרי ביה). It cites Exod 22:30 and Exod 23:1; the Talmud's reading (לא תשיא) is set against the written form (לא תשא).
  • The Machzor Vitry citation: the author introduces it (ובמחזור ויטרי ... על הא דתנן), quotes the Mishnah (אל תדון, printed על), and "כ' ע"ש" opens the quotation, which runs to עכ"ל. The opening formula stands outside the opening mark and the closing formula outside the closing mark. Inside it, single marks hold Solomon's wife's words, the voice, the bat kol and Hos 13:1. In ואתה מחייבו, "him" is Solomon.
  • הבאתם (3:9.371) is second person plural, while the address is singular elsewhere (ואתה, הזעקת, והוספת): "you (and those with you)".
  • The last part turns from the story to the addressee: ואתה הגדלת (and you have magnified) and כי אחרי אשר (for after) make the charge that his informing brought the author to prison.

Grammar
  • The paragraph is the author's own argument in its first half. From וז"ל (3:13.102) to עכ"ל (3:13.393) the words are Rabbeinu Tam's, as cited in Rivid ha-Zahav, and the quotation is marked in double quotation marks.
  • ולא סמכינן עליה (3:13.37-39): סמכינן is the Talmudic Aramaic first-person plural of 'to rely', and the English gives it as the plain 'we do not rely on it'.
  • ועמודיה יתפלצון (3:13.280-281) is Job 9:6, where the pillars shudder. The pillars of the Torah are therefore the subject of יתפלצון, and the destroyers have only 'the corners of the Torah' as their object. The pillars shudder at the sound of the מחצצים (Judg 5:11, a word of disputed sense).
  • The speech of the unlearned (3:13.307-320) has no introductory formula beyond באמרם. It is marked as direct speech inside the quotation, in single quotation marks.
  • אין ספק בידם להגיע (3:13.342-345): the printed ספק is 'doubt', but after קצרה יד שכלם (the hand of their intellect is too short) and before the potsherd (3:13.349-352) the clause must say that they lack the means to arrive. The word is read as סיפק, 'capacity, means' (the rabbinic idiom אין סיפק בידו, 'it is not in his power'), and the English follows the sense of the context. The plain reading of ספק as 'doubt' is in readings.

Paragraph 13 of the book's third chapter. The opening 'we learned' frame is the author's; the central quotation is Rabbeinu Tam's, and it closes with the author's 'Thus far his words'.

Grammar
  • ראה והבן ... איך ... צוח: 'see and understand how ... cried out'; the two imperatives open one long period, which runs to the end of the paragraph.
  • ותחת אשר חשבו להתקרב ... תעו: 'instead of thinking to draw near, they strayed'; the author sets the intention against the act.
  • וכדברי ר' תם ... כ' גם כן מהר"י אלבו: כ' is כתב; the verb comes first in the Hebrew and the English places it after the name.
  • וכתב דמאחר ... אין לנו להחמיר ... והוי כמו מינות: an indirect report with no quotation marks; אין לנו + infinitive is 'it is not for us to ...' (we may not), so the stringency against the Gemara is itself 'like heresy'.
