# Genesis: decisions for every unit

Editorial decisions set before translation. A reviewer may question one in a
`question` patch; the project owner decides.

## Divine names

- יְהוָה (MAM writes יְהֹוָה): "the LORD". Never "Jehovah", never "Yahweh".
- אֱלֹהִים, of the God of Israel: "God". Of other beings (6:2 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים): give
  the sense the normative commentators take, and record the rest.
- יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים: "the LORD God".
- אֵל עֶלְיוֹן (14:18–22): "God Most High"; אֵל שַׁדַּי (17:1): "El Shaddai", with a
  note on the commentators' readings of the name.

## Persons and places

- The familiar English form of a name, where one exists: Adam, Eve, Cain, Abel, Seth,
  Noah, Shem, Ham, Japheth, Terah, Abram, Abraham, Sarai, Sarah, Lot, Hagar, Ishmael,
  Melchizedek, Egypt, Canaan, Babel. Otherwise a plain transliteration
 , without diacritics, the same in every unit.
- The meaning of a name the text explains (חַוָּה 3:20, קַיִן 4:1, נֹחַ 5:29, בָּבֶל 11:9,
  אַבְרָהָם 17:5) goes in the gloss and the grammar notes, not in the English line.
- הָאָדָם with the article: "the human". אָדָם without the article as a proper name
  (4:25, 5:1–5): "Adam". Where the reading is in doubt, record it.

## Style

- Plain, exact, modern English. No archaism ("thee", "unto", "behold" as a filler).
- The prefix ו is rendered by its sense: and, then, so, but, now, or nothing. The
  literal line keeps "and".
- Numbers in words.
- The English line does not explain: explanation goes in the notes.

## Genesis 18–50

Added with chapters 18–50. They hold for every unit of those chapters; units of
1–17 were translated before them, and where they differ the editor decides.

### Divine names and beings

- אֲדֹנָי with qamats, where it may address God (18:3, 19:18): give the reading the
  normative commentators take ("my Lord" for God, "my lord" or "my lords" for a
  man or an angel) and record the other. With pataḥ (אֲדֹנַי 19:2): "my lords".
- הָאֱלֹהִים, of the God of Israel (20:6, 22:1): "God".
- A divine epithet that the text uses as a name is given in transliteration, its
  meaning in the notes: El Shaddai (28:3, 35:11, 43:14, 48:3), Shaddai (49:25),
  El Olam (21:33). An epithet that describes is translated: פַּחַד יִצְחָק (31:42, 53)
  "the Fear of Isaac"; אֲבִיר יַעֲקֹב (49:24) "the Mighty One of Jacob"; הָאֵל בֵּית־אֵל
  (31:13) "the God of Bethel".
- A name that contains the divine name is given as it is read, never with the
  letters of the name: יְהוָה יִרְאֶה (22:14) "Adonai-yireh". The names of altars and
  places, אֵל אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל (33:20), אֵל בֵּית־אֵל (35:7): transliterated, meaning in
  the notes.
- מַלְאָךְ: "angel" for a being sent by God, "messenger" for one sent by a man.
  Where the normative commentators differ on which is meant (32:4: Rashi, מַלְאָכִים
  מַמָּשׁ), follow one and record the other.

### Persons, peoples and places

- The familiar English form: Isaac, Rebekah, Bethuel, Laban, Esau, Jacob, Israel,
  Edom, Leah, Rachel, Bilhah, Zilpah, Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali,
  Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Dinah, Joseph, Benjamin, Tamar, Er, Onan, Shelah,
  Perez, Zerah, Manasseh, Ephraim, Abimelech, Keturah, Potiphar, Sodom, Gomorrah,
  Zoar, Moab, Ammon, Beersheba, Bethel, Hebron, Gerar, Goshen, Haran,
  Paddan-aram, Machpelah, Mamre, Seir, Gilead, Midian, Shechem. Otherwise the plain
  transliteration of the rule above.
- Jacob after 32:29 is "Israel" where the text says יִשְׂרָאֵל and "Jacob" where it
  says יַעֲקֹב. The English never exchanges them.
- בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל: "the sons of Israel" where Jacob's sons are meant (42:5, 45:21,
  46:5), "the children of Israel" where the people are (32:33).
- בְּנֵי־חֵת (ch. 23, 25:10, 49:32): "the Hittites"; the literal line keeps
  "sons-of Heth". בְּנוֹת חֵת (27:46): "Hittite women".
- The names the text explains add to the list above: מוֹאָב and בֶּן־עַמִּי (19:37–38),
  יִצְחָק (21:6), בְּאֵר שָׁבַע (21:31, 26:33), עֵשָׂו, יַעֲקֹב and אֱדוֹם (25:25–30), the
  wells (26:20–22), בֵּית־אֵל (28:19), Jacob's sons (29:32–30:24, 35:18), גַּלְעֵד and
  יְגַר שָׂהֲדוּתָא (31:47–48, Aramaic), מַחֲנָיִם (32:3), יִשְׂרָאֵל (32:29), פְּנִיאֵל (32:31),
  סֻכּוֹת (33:17), פֶּרֶץ and זָרַח (38:29–30), מְנַשֶּׁה and אֶפְרַיִם (41:51–52), אָבֵל מִצְרַיִם
  (50:11). Words of Egyptian or doubtful origin (אַבְרֵךְ 41:43, צָפְנַת פַּעְנֵחַ 41:45) are
  transliterated in the English; the commentators' readings go in the notes.
- שִׁילֹה (49:10): "Shiloh" in the English. Rashi's and Onkelos' reading (the
  Messiah, the king to whom the kingdom belongs) and the others go in the notes and
  the commentary.

### Text and style

- Ketiv and qere: translate the qere (the token's text); the ketiv and any comment
  on it go in the apparatus.
- Direct speech in double quotation marks; speech inside speech in single ones.
- הִנֵּה: "here", "look", "now", "see", or nothing, as the sense requires; never
  "behold".
- The poetry of ch. 49 is set as prose in the English line; its parallel members
  are named in the grammar notes.
