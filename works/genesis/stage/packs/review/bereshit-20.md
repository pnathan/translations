- בֵּן is indefinite: the unnamed son is named in the next verse.

Translator's note: Rashi's midrashic remark on 'a son' is a homily, not a reading of the words.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:5:28:1` not-applicable: Rashi, from Bereshit Rabbah: שֶׁמִּמֶּנּוּ נִבְנָה הָעוֹלָם, 'from him the world was built', a play on בֵּן and בנה. It is a homily and does not alter the plain sense.

- `rashi:5:29:1` follows: Rashi: יָנַח מִמֶּנּוּ אֶת עִצְּבוֹן יָדֵינוּ, 'he will give rest from the toil of our hands'; before Noah they had no plough, and the earth gave thorns when they sowed wheat. Otherwise the wordplay on the name would fail and one would have to read his name מְנַחֵם.
- `ibn-ezra:5:29:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the sense is 'this one will give us rest (וננוח) from the toil of our hands'; Hebrew keeps the sense and not the exact words, as in יְרֻבַּעַל and יְרֻבֶּשֶׁת, and נֶחָמָה is rest from toil of the heart.
- `radak:5:29:3` follows: Radak: מִמַּעֲשֵׂנוּ means work of the ground, as in 'from his work from the field' (Judges 19:16).
- `sforno:5:29:1` follows: Sforno: it is a prayer that this one comfort us by bringing rest from their work, since נֹחַ teaches rest, as in 'and rest from their enemies' (Esther 9:16).
- `radak:5:29:6` records: Radak: Lamech did not speak by prophecy but gave the name as a sign, in blessing and prayer, as Rachel named Joseph; the English leaves it open as a statement of hope.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders the verb with 'comfort' (יְנַחֲמִנָּנָא) and 'from our toil' (מֵעוֹבָדָנָא); 'מִלֵּאוּת יְדָנָא' for 'toil of our hands'.
