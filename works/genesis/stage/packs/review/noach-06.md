Grammar notes:

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:23:1` follows: Rashi: וַיִּמַח is an active (Qal) form, not Niphal, like וַיִּפֶן and וַיִּבֶן from roots ending in he, pointed with ḥireq under the yod. The English has an active verb with God understood.
- `rashi:7:23:2` follows: Rashi: אַךְ נֹחַ means 'Noah alone', and that is the plain sense; he adds the homily that Noah groaned and spat blood from the work of tending the animals, or that a lion bit him.
- `ibn-ezra:7:23:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: וַיִּמָּחוּ מִן הָאָרֶץ means that their names were wiped out from the earth, since they left no offspring; וַיִּשָּׁאֶר means only Noah and those with him remained, a full answer to those who say the flood was not over all the earth.
- `ramban:7:23:2` records: Ramban: the doubling may stress that no eggs or nests of birds and creeping things were left; the rabbis read 'wiped out' for this world and 'from the earth' for the world to come.
- apparatus: 7:23.20 vowels: WLC reads וַיִשָּׁאֶר without the dagesh in yod; the base text וַיִּשָּׁאֶר is followed.

- The merkha on וַיִּגְבְּרוּ and the tipḥa on הַמַּיִם lead to the etnaḥta on הָאָרֶץ; the duration is a separate clause-end.

- וַיָּשֹׁכּוּ is from שׁכך; its stem and root meaning differ from שׂכך of Esther 7:10 only in spelling, and it has the sense 'settle, abate'.

Translator's note: The English keeps 'God' for אֱלֹהִים by decision, even though Rashi reads it as the attribute of justice turned to mercy. 'Noah' stands for Noah with his sons and wives, as Ibn Ezra says. 'Wind' (not 'spirit') because the verb is Hiphil of 'pass over' and the object is something that blows over the earth; Rashi's 'wind of comfort' is recorded.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:1:1` not-applicable: Rashi: this name, אֱלֹהִים, is the attribute of justice, turned to mercy by the prayer of the righteous; the wickedness of the wicked turns mercy to justice. A homily on the divine name.

- `rashi:8:1:5` follows: Rashi: וַיָּשֹׁכּוּ is like כְּשֹׁךְ חֲמַת הַמֶּלֶךְ, a word for the settling of anger. The English: subsided.
- `ibn-ezra:8:1:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: his sons and the women are included in Noah, who is the main one. The English names Noah.
- `ibn-ezra:8:1:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: it includes beasts of the field, birds, and every creeping thing; the sense of 'remembered' is the oath God swore to Noah. The English has 'every living creature'.
- `ibn-ezra:8:1:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: he made it pass continually. The English has the causative verb without an end point.
- `ibn-ezra:8:1:4` follows: Ibn Ezra: they rested and no longer prevailed; so too the king's anger subsided (Esther 7:10); the dagesh in the kaf stands for a missing ayin, and both verbs are Qal. The English: the waters subsided.
- `ramban:8:1:1` records: Ramban: the mention of Noah is for his merit; the mention of the animals is not for merit but because God remembered his word at the creation and wished the species to continue.
- `radak:8:1:2` records: Radak: the wind stood against the water so that it could no longer rise, and the water settled and went down by its nature.

### Translation under review (8:3)

English: The waters withdrew from the earth steadily, and the waters decreased at the end of a hundred and fifty days.

Literal: and-returned the-waters from-upon the-earth going and-returning and-decreased the-waters from-end-of fifty and-hundred-of day

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8:3.1 | and they withdrew |  | Qal 2 | wayyiqtol of שׁוב: 'return', here 'go back' from the earth. |
| 8:3.2 | the waters |  |  |  |

| 8:3.4 | the earth |  | 1.a |  |

Phrases:
- 8:3.1 8:3.2 8:3.3 8:3.4 | The waters withdrew from the earth | 'and-returned the-waters from-upon the-earth'
- 8:3.5 8:3.6 | steadily | 'going and-returning': a pair of infinitive absolutes showing continuing action.
- 8:3.7 8:3.8 | and the waters decreased | 'and-decreased the-waters'
- 8:3.9 8:3.10 8:3.11 8:3.12 | at the end of a hundred and fifty days | 'from-end-of fifty and-hundred-of day'

Grammar notes:
- הָלוֹךְ וָשׁוֹב: two infinitive absolutes after a finite verb express continuing action (compare 8:5, הָלוֹךְ וְחָסוֹר).
- The tevir on הַמַּיִם and the etnaḥta on וָשׁוֹב divide the verse after the first clause; the zaqef gadol on מִקְצֵה sets the number apart.
- וַיַּחְסְרוּ is a stative Qal wayyiqtol of a verb with a stative vowel (חָסֵר).
- The word order of the second clause puts the verb before its subject: וַיַּחְסְרוּ הַמַּיִם.

Translator's note: 'Steadily' translates the idiom; the words 'going and returning' are in the literal line. The English follows the timing of Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Radak: the waters began to decrease at the end of the hundred and fifty days. Ramban joins מִקְצֵה with the following 'the ark came to rest'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:3:1` follows: Rashi: they began to decrease on the first of Sivan. How? The rains stopped on 27 Kislev; there are three more of Kislev, twenty-nine of Tevet, and so on to one hundred and fifty.
- `ibn-ezra:8:3:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: וַיָּשֻׁבוּ means below the earth as usual, at the end of the hundred and fifty days; he rejects the attempts to compute the months by the sun or by intercalation. The English: steadily, at the end of the days.
- `radak:8:3:1` follows: Radak: little by little, until they decreased at the end of a hundred and fifty days; they began to decrease on 1 Sivan, when the rising stopped.
- `ramban:8:4:6` records: Ramban: the waters were decreasing little by little until the ground was dry; the words מִקְצֵה חֲמִשִּׁים וּמְאַת יוֹם join to וַתָּנַח הַתֵּבָה: on that day the decrease was great.

Grammar notes:
- וַתָּנַח: the verb is feminine, as תֵּבָה is feminine; a play on נֹחַ, whose name comes from נוח.
- The date has three parts: the month by ordinal (הַשְּׁבִיעִי), the day by cardinal (שִׁבְעָה עָשָׂר יוֹם), and then 'of the month'.
- The zaqef qaton on הַשְּׁבִיעִי and the etnaḥta on לַחֹדֶשׁ separate the date from the place.

Translator's note: The English does not say which reckoning gives 'the seventh month'. Rashi and Radak count from the stopping of the rain (Sivan); Ramban attributes another reckoning to Ibn Ezra and others. Rashi's inference that the ark sank eleven cubits is a calculation from the days and cubits.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:4:1` records: Rashi: the seventh month is Sivan, the seventh from the stopping of the rains in Kislev. The English leaves the starting point of the count open.
- `rashi:8:4:2` not-applicable: Rashi: from here you learn that the ark was sunk eleven cubits in the water, by a calculation from the exposure of the mountain tops at a rate of one cubit in four days. A computation that does not bear on the words.
- `ramban:8:4:1` records: Ramban: rejects Rashi's counts as not suiting the wording and says the sinking is not proved.
- `radak:8:4:1` records: Radak: the seventh month is Sivan, the seventh from the rain's stopping; the ark rested on the seventeenth of it.
- `radak:8:4:2` not-applicable: Radak: the mountains of Ararat may be the high ones, or the ark was in that region; some are lower, since not all were seen until the tenth month.

- The tenth month is named twice, in the ordinal with and without the preposition (עַד הַחֹדֶשׁ הָעֲשִׂירִי; בָּעֲשִׂירִי).

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:5:1` records: Rashi: the tenth is Av, the tenth from Marheshvan, when the rain began; the seventh month he counts from the stopping, but the tenth cannot be counted from the stopping. The English leaves the count open.
- `ibn-ezra:8:5:1` records: Ibn Ezra: Noah fixed the first of the month without seeing the moon, since the window was not yet open; the tenth month is counted from the day of the flood, and he sent the raven on the tenth of Shevat. The English leaves the reckoning open.
- `radak:8:5:1` follows: Radak: they kept diminishing, as in 'the creatures ran and returned' (Ezekiel 1:14).
- `radak:8:5:2` follows: Radak: it does not say all the water was gone in the tenth month; only the tops of the mountains were seen then, and the decrease went on after.
- `ramban:8:5:1` records: Ramban: the order is inverted: the waters kept diminishing until the mountain tops were seen in the tenth month, which is Tammuz.
