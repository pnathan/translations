Phrases:

- 10:30.3 | from Mesha | 'from-Mesha'
- 10:30.4 10:30.5 | as you go toward Sephar | 'as-you-come Sephar-ward'; the idiom marks the direction of the boundary
- 10:30.6 10:30.7 | to the eastern mountain | 'mountain-of the-east'; the English adds 'to' to complete the range

Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי is a wayyiqtol of הָיָה, here 'was situated' with the singular verb before the subject מוֹשָׁבָם.
- בֹּאֲכָה is an infinitive construct with a 2nd person suffix: 'as you come'; with סְפָרָה (directional ה) the phrase means 'in the direction of Sephar'.
- הַר הַקֶּדֶם is a construct chain: 'the mountain of the east'.
- The etnaḥta on מִמֵּשָׁא divides the starting point from the boundary.

Translator's note: Hebrew has no 'to' before הַר הַקֶּדֶם. It may stand in apposition to סְפָרָה ('Sephar, the eastern mountain'), or mark the second boundary. The English follows Radak and reads it as the end of the range.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:10:30:1` follows: Radak: after the division they chose the land from Mesha to הַר הַקֶּדֶם, the eastern mountain, and settled there. Saadia identifies מֵשָׁא with Mecca, where the Ishmaelites go on pilgrimage.
- `onkelos:10:30:1` follows: Onkelos: 'and their dwelling was from Mesha, reaching to Sephar, the mountain of the east', and so he reads the range as continuing to the mountain.
- apparatus: The location of מֵשָׁא and סְפָר is uncertain; Saadia as quoted by Radak identifies מֵשָׁא with Mecca.

Literal: these clans-of sons-of Noah to-their-generations in-their-nations and-from-these were-divided the-nations in-the-earth after the-flood

Phrases:
- 10:32.1 10:32.2 10:32.3 10:32.4 | These are the clans of the sons of Noah | 'these clans-of sons-of Noah'; verbless clause, 'are' supplied
- 10:32.5 | by their lineages | 'to-their-generations'; 'lineages' suits the genealogical use of תּוֹלְדֹת
- 10:32.6 | in their nations | 'in-their-nations'
- 10:32.7 | and from these | 'and-from-these'
- 10:32.8 10:32.9 | the nations were divided | 'were-divided the-nations'; English puts the subject first
- 10:32.10 | on the earth | 'in-the-earth'
- 10:32.11 10:32.12 | after the flood | 'after the-flood'

Grammar notes:
- Hebrew puts the verb before its subject: נִפְרְדוּ הַגּוֹיִם, 'were divided the nations'; the nifal gives a passive or reflexive sense.
- The geresh on וּמֵאֵלֶּה and the tevir on הַגּוֹיִם mark the second half, which looks beyond the genealogy to the spread of nations.
- הַמַּבּוּל, 'the flood', is the word used for the Flood of chapters 6 to 9 alone.
- פ at the end is a paragraph break (petuḥa), which closes the whole table of nations.

Translator's note: The English keeps 'after the flood' as stated. Radak notes that the division came only after the dispersal of Peleg's time (10:25), so 'after the flood' is read loosely.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:10:32:1` records: Radak: they were not divided until after the Peleg division (הַפַּלָּגָה), and he goes on to give the verse's sense as such. The English keeps the plain 'after the flood' and records his timing here.
- `radak:10:32:2` records: Radak: those born after the flood spread out over the earth when they spread out, which was after the Peleg division.
- `onkelos:10:32:1` follows: Onkelos translates 'and from these the peoples were separated in the earth after the flood', in line with the English.
- apparatus: Radak reads the dispersal as later than the flood by the interval of the Peleg division (10:25).
