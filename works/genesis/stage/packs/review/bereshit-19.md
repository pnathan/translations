Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:5:22:1` follows: Ibn Ezra says 'he walked with God' has the sense of 'after the LORD your God you shall walk' (Deut 13:5), and compares Noah, 'with God Noah walked' (6:9), noting that some take 'Noah walked' as 'was accustomed'. The English 'walked with God' takes it as a way of life.
- `onkelos:5:22:1` records: Onkelos: 'Enoch walked in the fear of the LORD' (וְהַלִּיךְ חֲנוֹךְ בְּדַחַלְתָּא דַּיְיָ); a paraphrase that the English does not adopt but that agrees in sense.
- `sforno:5:22:1` records: Sforno: he walked in God's ways, doing good to others by righteousness and by reproof; a further specification not in the text.

### Translation under review (5:24)

English: Enoch walked with God, and he was no more, for God took him.

Literal: and-walked Enoch with the-God and-not-he for took [object-marker]-him God

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5:24.1 | and walked |  | Hithp | wayyiqtol: the sequential past of narrative |
| 5:24.2 | Enoch |  |  |  |
| 5:24.3 | with |  | 1 | preposition 'with' |
| 5:24.4 | God |  | 3 | with the article |
| 5:24.5 | and he was no more |  | 2 | וְאֵינֶנּוּ: אַיִן with a 3rd person masculine suffix, 'and he is not'; here 'he was no more' |
| 5:24.6 | for |  | 3 |  |
| 5:24.7 | took |  | Qal 3.b | Qal perfect |
| 5:24.8 | him |  |  | object marker אֹת with suffix |
| 5:24.9 | God |  | 4 | without the article |

Phrases:
- 5:24.1 5:24.2 5:24.3 5:24.4 | Enoch walked with God | 'and-walked Enoch with the-God'
- 5:24.5 | and he was no more | 'and-not-he'; Rashi and Ibn Ezra take it as death
- 5:24.6 | for | 'for', giving the ground
- 5:24.7 5:24.8 5:24.9 | God took him | 'took him God'; verb first, then the pronominal object, then the subject

Grammar notes:
- וְאֵינֶנּוּ is the particle אֵין with a suffix: 'and he was not'. It is a verbless clause.
- לָקַח אֹתוֹ אֱלֹהִים has verb-object-subject order, putting stress on the agent: God took him.
- The etnaḥta on הָאֱלֹהִים closes the first half; the zaqef gadol on וְאֵינֶנּוּ separates the disappearance from its reason.
- The first אֱלֹהִים has the article (the true God); the second does not, and is the subject.

Translator's note: The English keeps the ambiguity of 'was no more' and 'took': the commentators read it as death, an early one (Rashi) or a taking of the soul (Ibn Ezra); Radak also records the view that Enoch entered Eden alive.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:5:24:1` records: Rashi (from Genesis Rabbah): Enoch was righteous but easily turned to wickedness, so God hastened to remove him and made him die before his time; hence the text changes the language of his death to 'and he was not'. The English keeps 'was no more' but does not add the reason.
- `rashi:5:24:2` follows: Rashi: 'he took him' means before his time, as in 'I am taking from you the desire of your eyes' (Ezek 24:16). The English 'took him' keeps the sense of removal.
- `ibn-ezra:5:24:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: וְאֵינֶנּוּ means he died, like 'take my life' (Jonah 4:3) and 'I am taking from you the desire of your eyes' (Ezek 24:16); but here there is no mention of a plague or of 'and he died', and the sense is that of 'afterward you will take me with honor' (Ps 73:24) and 'God will redeem my soul ... for he will take me' (Ps 49:16). The English 'he was no more, for God took him' leaves the manner open.
- `radak:5:24:5` records: Radak: God took his soul and raised it to the upper beings, as in Ps 73:24 and 49:16; but the Targum and some sages say that Enoch and Elijah entered the garden of Eden alive in body and soul. The English does not choose.
- apparatus: Enoch's departure is described by the verb לָקַח, 'take'; the verse does not say 'and he died'.
