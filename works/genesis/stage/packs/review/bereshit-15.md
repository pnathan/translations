Grammar notes:
- וְצִלָּה גַם־הִוא: the independent pronoun after the subject stresses it: Zillah, she too, bore, matching Adah's sons in vv. 20-21.
- The zaqef gadol on לֹטֵשׁ and the etnaḥta on וּבַרְזֶל divide the verse: Tubal-cain and his craft, then Naamah.
- לֹטֵשׁ is a participle in construct with the phrase כָּל־חֹרֵשׁ, which in turn governs נְחֹשֶׁת; the phrase can be read 'sharpener of every worker in bronze and iron' or, with Rashi, 'sharpener of every craft-tool of bronze and iron'.
- וַאֲחוֹת: a verbless clause with no verb 'was' in Hebrew; the sister is introduced as a name only.

Translator's note: חֹרֵשׁ is the hard word. Rashi takes it as a noun (worker, tool of the craft) and has Tubal-cain sharpening and polishing all such implements; Ibn Ezra glosses לֹטֵשׁ as 'sharpen'. The English follows both on לֹטֵשׁ and takes Rashi's sense of the object. Tubal-cain is not said to be the 'father' of the craft here, unlike 4:20-21 (Ramban notes Onkelos adds 'father of'). BDB lists only Qal 1-3 for the participle; Qal 1 (cut in) is the closest to 'forge, cut'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:22:1` records: Rashi: Tubal is from tavlin 'spice': Tubal-cain refined and prepared Cain's craft, making weapons for murderers. The English gives the name without this etymology.
- `rashi:4:22:2` follows: Rashi: לֹטֵשׁ means sharpening, as in Job 16:9; חוֹרֵשׁ is a noun (worker, with qamets qatan), so he sharpened and polished every tool of the bronze and iron craft.
- `rashi:4:22:3` records: Rashi: Naamah is Noah's wife. The text itself names her only as Tubal-cain's sister.
- `ibn-ezra:4:22:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: לוטש is 'sharpening', and compares 1 Sam 13:20, 'to sharpen each his ploughshare'.
- `ramban:4:22:1` records: Ramban: the sense is that he sharpened and worked all bronze and iron; Onkelos reads 'father of every sharpener of bronze and iron'.
- `ramban:4:22:2` records: Ramban on the sister: the verse says 'a sister was born to him, named Naamah'; midrashim identify her with Noah's wife or the seductress of 6:2.
- apparatus: The Hebrew has no verb in the last clause: 'sister of Tubal-cain, Naamah'.
- apparatus: Onkelos: 'father of' is added to the participle (Ramban 4:22:1).

Grammar notes:
- The two imperatives in the feminine plural, שְׁמַעַן and הַאְזֵנָּה, are in parallel lines: this short poem has the parallelism of Hebrew verse (voice/speech, man/boy, wound/bruise).
- There is no ה of question, but Rashi reads the lines as questions 'in astonishment' (בִּתְמִיהָ): did I kill a man for my wound? The reading is possible but not forced; the plain statement 'I have killed a man for my wound' is also grammatical.
- הָרַגְתִּי is a qatal; Ibn Ezra takes it as a prophetic or hypothetical perfect standing for אהרוג, 'I would kill', like נָתַתִּי in Gen 23:13.
- The ל in לְפִצְעִי and לְחַבֻּרָתִי is 'for', either in exchange for or on account of; Rashi: 'for my wound' means the wound counted in my name, or by my hand.

Translator's note: The poem is one of the hardest in Genesis. The English follows Rashi's rhetorical-question reading (4:23:2), because it fits his plain-sense gloss on 'for my wound' as a wound I caused, and it makes the sequel in v. 24 an argument from the lesser to the greater. Ibn Ezra reads a threat instead: if any adult or child wounds me, I will kill him; that reading is recorded. The Hebrew allows a flat confession too ('I have killed a man for wounding me'); the three readings differ in tense and mood only, not in vocabulary.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:23:1` records: Rashi (midrash): the wives had withdrawn from him because he killed Cain and his son Tubal-cain; the blind Lamech, led by Tubal-cain, shot at Cain, thinking him an animal, then clapped his hands and killed his son. This story is not in the Hebrew words.
- `rashi:4:23:2` follows: Rashi: the verses are questions in astonishment: is a man killed because of my wound? did I wound him deliberately? Was a boy killed by my bruise? I acted in error, not wilfully.
- `rashi:4:23:3` follows: Rashi: פֶּצַע is a blow by sword or arrow (old French mecaldure); the English has 'wound'.
- `ibn-ezra:4:23:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the story is out of order; Adah and Zillah feared to bear sons who would be killed as seventh from Cain; Lamech answers that if anyone, man or boy, wounds him he will kill; הרגתי stands for אהרוג, as נתתי in Gen 23:13.
- `ibn-ezra:4:23:2` records: Ibn Ezra: Lamech tells his wives the decree against Cain is annulled; he killed deliberately but I did not; Lamech's sons died in the Flood and Cain kept no name from his brother's murder.

Grammar notes:
- וַיֵּדַע ... אֶת־אִשְׁתּוֹ: 'know' used of intimacy, with עוֹד 'again', recalling 4:1.
- The subject of וַתִּקְרָא is the woman, who names her child, as in 4:1.
- שֵׁת / שָׁת: the name and the verb שִׁית 'put, set' sound alike, the sense of the name being 'he has set (another for me)'.
- The זֶרַע אַחֵר, 'another offspring', replaces Abel; תַּחַת הֶבֶל is 'in place of Abel' (Ibn Ezra: במקומו).
- The two כִּי clauses: the first explains the name, the second explains why a replacement was needed.

Translator's note: אָדָם without the article is the proper name Adam, per the decisions. The English uses 'was intimate' for וַיֵּדַע, which is plain modern English for the Hebrew idiom; the literal line keeps 'knew'. Rashi's remark that Lamech came to Adam is a midrash and is not in the text.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:25:1` not-applicable: Rashi (Genesis Rabbah): Lamech complained to Adam about his wives; they told Adam to look after himself, having separated for 130 years; at once Adam knew his wife; 'again' means his desire was added to his desire. A homily.
- `ibn-ezra:4:25:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: תחת הבל means 'in his place'.
- apparatus: The reading שָׁת 'set, appointed' with the name Seth is a wordplay, not an etymology stated by the text.

Grammar notes:
- וּלְשֵׁת גַּם־הוּא: the ל of possession is put first with a resumptive pronoun, matching 4:22 (וְצִלָּה גַם־הִוא).
- יֻלַּד is Pual; the father named the child, since the verb וַיִּקְרָא is masculine singular.
- הוּחַל is Hofal of חלל; two roots are possible: 'begin' (חלל I, as Ibn Ezra and the English) or 'profane' (חלל II, as Rashi).
- לִקְרֹא בְּשֵׁם יְהוָה: 'call on the name of the LORD', worship or prayer by name.
- The disjunctive zaqef on הוּחַל divides the sentence: 'then it was begun | to call on the name of the LORD'.

Translator's note: The English follows Ibn Ezra: הוּחַל is from חלל 'begin', and the meaning is that people began to pray. Rashi reads it as 'profane' (חֻלִּין): people began to give men's names and idols the Name of God, making them gods. Ibn Ezra's grammar argument is that if it were from 'profane' the form would have been construct with 'the name'. The subject is not named in Hebrew (impersonal Hofal); 'people' is supplied. The name is rendered 'the LORD' per the decisions.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:26:1` records: Rashi: הוּחַל is from חֻלִּין 'profane': then people began to call men's names and idols by the name of the Holy One, making them idols and calling them gods.
- `ibn-ezra:4:26:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: הוחל is from the doubled roots, and but for the guttural ח would have a dagesh; it is from 'begin', meaning that they began to pray; if it were from 'profane' the word 'name' would be in the construct after it.
- apparatus: הוּחַל: 'began' (Ibn Ezra) or 'was profaned' (Rashi).
- apparatus: The subject of the Hofal is not stated; 'people' is an addition.
