### Translation under review (18:17)

English: The LORD said, "Shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing?

Literal: and-the-LORD said: question-covering I from-Abraham what I doing?

Phrases:
- 18:17.1 18:17.2 | The LORD said | 'and-the-LORD said'; the Hebrew names no addressee
- 18:17.3 18:17.4 18:17.5 18:17.6 18:17.7 18:17.8 | Shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing | 'question-covering I from-Abraham what I doing'; the question opens the divine speech, which runs to the end of 18:19

Grammar notes:
- וַיהוָה אָמָר is a narrative qatal with the subject after the verb; it opens the speech, and the Hebrew gives no addressee.
- הַמְכַסֶּה is a piel participle with the interrogative ה: a rhetorical question, which Rashi reads as astonishment.
- אֲשֶׁר אֲנִי עֹשֶׂה is a relative clause with an active participle; the object of the doing is not stated in the verse.
- The tipḥa on אָמָר and the mahpakh on הַמְכַסֶּה divide the clause; the silluq closes it at עֹשֶׂה.

Translator's note: The English keeps the question form. Rashi supplies 'in Sodom' as the object of 'what I am doing'; the English does not add it, and the commentary records it. The quotation is not closed here: the speech runs to the end of 18:19.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:17:1` follows: Rashi: the הֲ of הַמְכַסֶּה marks astonishment (בִּתְמִיהָ), so God asks in wonder whether he can hide the matter from Abraham. The English keeps the question.
- `rashi:18:17:2` records: Rashi supplies 'in Sodom' as the object: it is not fitting to do this without Abraham's knowledge, since God gave him the land and its five cities (Gen. 10). The English 'what I am doing' does not name Sodom; Rashi's reading is kept here.

### Translation under review (18:18)

English: Since Abraham shall surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed through him.

Literal: and-Abraham surely-become shall-become to-a-nation great and-mighty and-shall-be-blessed through-him all nations-of the-earth

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18:18.1 | since Abraham |  |  | The vav gives a reason here (Rashi: 'seeing he is dear to me'); the literal line keeps 'and'. |
| 18:18.2 | surely |  | Qal II.2 | infinitive absolute before the yiqtol of the same verb: emphasis, 'surely become'. |
| 18:18.3 | shall become |  | Qal II.2 |  |
| 18:18.4 | a nation |  |  | לְ after a verb of becoming marks the predicate: 'become a nation'. |
| 18:18.5 | great |  | 10 | The lexicon prints no sense for the attributive 'great'. Sense 10 (greatness) is the nearest of the printed paths; the data is kept. |
| 18:18.6 | and mighty |  |  | The lexicon entry lists 'mighty, numerous' with no senses; 'mighty' is the first gloss. |
| 18:18.7 | shall be blessed |  | Niph | nifal weqatal, third plural, continuing the yiqtol of 18:18.3. The lexicon's 'bless oneself' is the reflexive reading that Ibn Ezra rejects here: the verb is passive. |
| 18:18.8 | through him |  |  | בְּ with the suffix; Ibn Ezra's בִּשְׁבִילוֹ, 'on his account'. |
| 18:18.9 | all |  | 1 | construct: 'all the nations of'. |
| 18:18.10 | the nations of |  |  |  |
| 18:18.11 | the earth |  | 1.a | the earth as the whole world, not the land of Canaan. The word is ambiguous with 'land' (sense 2). |

Phrases:
- 18:18.1 18:18.2 18:18.3 | Since Abraham shall surely become | 'and-Abraham surely-become shall-become'; the reason clause follows Rashi's plain sense
- 18:18.4 18:18.5 18:18.6 | a great and mighty nation | 'to-nation great and-mighty'; the lamed is predicative
- 18:18.7 18:18.8 18:18.9 18:18.10 18:18.11 | and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed through him | 'and-shall-be-blessed through-him all nations-of the-earth'; the passive follows Ibn Ezra

Grammar notes:
- הָיוֹ יִהְיֶה: the infinitive absolute before the yiqtol of the same verb intensifies it: 'surely shall become'.
- לְגוֹי גָּדוֹל וְעָצוּם: the predicate after הָיָה is marked by the lamed, and the two adjectives are joined by the vav.
- וְנִבְרְכוּ בוֹ: the weqatal of the nifal continues the yiqtol of 18:18.3; its subject is 'all the nations', and the verb is passive in Ibn Ezra's reading.
- The zaqef qaton on the first word and the tipḥa on גָּדוֹל divide the clause; the silluq closes the verse at הָאָרֶץ.

Translator's note: Rashi's midrash is that the name is repeated for a blessing (זֵכֶר צַדִּיק לִבְרָכָה); the plain sense is that God would not hide from one dear to him. The English gives the plain sense. The verse continues the quoted speech begun in 18:17.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:18:1` follows: Rashi: the name is given again as a blessing (זֵכֶר צַדִּיק לִבְרָכָה), but the plain sense is 'shall I hide from him, when he is dear to me, to become a great nation and be the blessing of all the nations of the earth'. The English reads the vav as 'since', the reason for the question in 18:17.
- `ibn-ezra:18:18:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the verb is nifal (נפעל), meaning that they will be blessed on his account. The form in 22:18 (והתברכו) is not the same; there they bless themselves. The English is passive.

Grammar notes:
- כִּי יְדַעְתִּיו: a causal clause; the qatal is first person singular with the third masculine singular suffix on the verb (the pointing shows the suffix on the verb).
- לְמַעַן אֲשֶׁר יְצַוֶּה: Rashi reads 'because he commands'; the yiqtol is habitual and the clause gives the reason for the knowing.
- וְשָׁמְרוּ: the weqatal continues the yiqtol of יְצַוֶּה, so the sons and household keep the way as a consequence of the charge.
- The second לְמַעַן with the infinitive construct הָבִיא gives the purpose: 'so that the LORD may bring'.
- The revia on יְדַעְתִּיו and the munaḥ on כִּי divide the first clause; the silluq closes the verse at עָלָיו.

Translator's note: Rashi reads ידעתיו as affection and the first למען as 'because'; the English follows him. Ramban's reading, that 'I knew him' means 'I raised him up, so that he would command', is recorded and not adopted. The quotation of the LORD's speech closes here.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:19:1` follows: Rashi: ידעתיו is a word of affection (לְשׁוֹן חִבָּה), like מוֹדָע לְאִישָׁהּ (Ruth 2); the root sense of all such words is knowledge, since one who loves draws near and knows the beloved. The English keeps 'known'.
- `rashi:18:19:2` follows: Rashi: למען אשר means 'because (he commands his sons concerning me to keep my ways)'. Read as the Targum does ('I know that he will command'), למען would not fit. The English reads 'because'.
- `rashi:18:19:3` follows: Rashi: יצוה is in the present tense (לשון הווה), like 'so Job does' (Job 1). The English gives 'he commands'.
- `rashi:18:19:4` follows: Rashi: the second למען is purpose: Abraham charges his sons to keep the way of the LORD, so that the LORD brings upon Abraham what he spoke. The verse says 'upon Abraham', not 'upon his house', and the English keeps that.
- `ramban:18:19:1` records: Ramban: ידעתיו may mean 'I raised him up and exalted him', so that he would command his sons (a purpose sense), or 'I will know that he commands'. He holds that the true sense is knowledge itself, a sign of providence over the righteous. The English keeps knowledge and does not adopt the purpose sense.

Grammar notes:
- וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה: the wayyiqtol introduces the speech, and the Hebrew does not name the one addressed.
- זַעֲקַת סְדֹם וַעֲמֹרָה is a construct chain; the outcry is the subject of the first clause.
- כִּי רָבָּה: a qatal, third feminine singular, agreeing with זַעֲקָה; the accent above the resh is Rashi's evidence for 'has grown'.
- וְחַטָּאתָם כִּי כָבְדָה מְאֹד: a second clause with the same כִּי, so the two clauses stand side by side; the adverb מְאֹד intensifies the qatal.

Translator's note: Rashi has the speech addressed to Abraham, and the English says so; Ramban says all the commentators read it that way. The English keeps 'outcry' for זַעֲקַת, which covers both Ibn Ezra's readings; the note records them. The lexicon entry for רבב given in the pack is an adjective; see the note on 18:20.7.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:20:1` follows: Rashi: 'to Abraham', who had done as God said (not to hide the matter from him). Ramban reports that all the commentators take the speech to Abraham. The English adds 'to Abraham'.
- `rashi:18:20:2` follows: Rashi: every רַבָּה in Scripture has its accent below the bet, because it translates 'great'; this one has it above the resh, because it translates גָּדְלָה, 'has grown'. The English 'has grown great' follows him.
- `ibn-ezra:18:20:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the outcry is either words spoken against God, or the outcry of violence (זעקת החמס). The English keeps 'outcry', and the note records both readings.
- `ibn-ezra:18:20:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'and their sin, that it is very heavy', so heavy that 'the land cannot bear it' (ולא תכילנה הארץ). The English 'very heavy' gives the sense of כָּבְדָה; his image is recorded in the note.

Grammar notes:
- אֵרְדָה נָּא וְאֶרְאֶה: two cohortatives, first person singular, 'I will go down' and 'I will see'; נָא softens the request.
- הַכְּצַעֲקָתָהּ הַבָּאָה אֵלַי: the question-particle with כְּ opens the clause; the article on the qatal הַבָּאָה makes it a relative: 'the outcry that has come to me'.
- עָשׂוּ כָּלָה: a qatal, third common plural, with the noun כָּלָה; Rashi's pesiq divides them, and the English reads the consequence after the verb.
- וְאִם־לֹא אֵדָעָה: the second half of the conditional, a yiqtol of 'know' in the first person singular, 'and if not, I will find out'; the sense is 'see and discern' (Qal 1.c).

Translator's note: The English reads the verse as a conditional: if they have done according to the outcry, the LORD brings destruction; if not, the LORD finds out. Rashi and Ibn Ezra agree on the destruction. On אֵדָעָה, Ibn Ezra (the normative commentator) reads 'I will see whether they all did this evil'; the English follows him, and Rashi's 'I will know what to do' is recorded. The vowel variant at 18:21.1 does not change the reading.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:21:1` records: Rashi gives two readings of 'I will go down and see': first a homily (the LORD's descent teaches judges to decide capital cases only on evidence); second, 'I will go down to the end of their deeds'. The English keeps 'go down and see'; the second reading is recorded here.
- `rashi:18:21:2` records: Rashi: הַכְּצַעֲקָתָהּ means 'of the city' (שֶׁל מְדִינָה), the outcry of the city. The English 'the outcry' does not name the city; Rashi's reading is recorded here.
- `rashi:18:21:3` records: Rashi: if they remain in their rebellion, 'I will do destruction to them' (כָּלָה אֲנִי עוֹשֶׂה בָהֶם); if they do not, 'I will know what to do' to punish them with afflictions and will not destroy them. A pesiq separates עָשׂוּ from כָּלָה. The destruction agrees with the English; his 'I will know what to do' does not, and is recorded here.
- `ibn-ezra:18:21:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'if so, as they did, I will do to them destruction' (אם כן עשו אעשה בהם כלה). The English follows; this is the same consequence as Rashi.
- `ibn-ezra:18:21:2` follows: Ibn Ezra gives two readings of 'and if not, I will know': I will have mercy on them (as in 'God knew', Exod. 2:25); or, in his view, I will see whether they all did this evil, since God's knowledge is of the whole and not of the part. The English adopts his view: 'I will find out', that is, see and discern. The mercy reading is recorded here.
- apparatus: 18:21.1 vowels: WLC reads אֵֽרֲדָה; the base text reads אֵֽרְדָה. The verb is the same; only the vowels differ.

### Translation under review (18:23)

English: Abraham drew near and said, "Will you in anger sweep away the righteous with the wicked?

Literal: and-drew-near Abraham and-said: will-you-in-anger sweep-away the-righteous with the-wicked?

Phrases:

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּגַּשׁ אַבְרָהָם וַיֹּאמַר: two wayyiqtol verbs; the approach comes first, and the speech follows it.
- הַאַף: the interrogative ה with the noun אַף ('anger'); the question is 'will anger make you sweep away ...?'.
- תִּסְפֶּה: the yiqtol is second person singular, Abraham speaking to God.
- צַדִּיק עִם־רָשָׁע: two adjectives in the absolute state, set against each other; the sweeping is of both groups together.

Translator's note: Rashi's first gloss is הֲגַם, 'even, also', but he then gives the Targum's 'anger' as the meaning; the English follows his conclusion. Ibn Ezra takes the verb as transitive, 'consume'. The quotation runs on into 18:24.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:23:1` records: Rashi: the verb וַיִּגַּשׁ is used for approach to battle, to appease, and to pray; Abraham drew near for harsh words, for appeasement and for prayer. The English keeps 'drew near' and does not name one purpose.
- `rashi:18:23:2` follows: Rashi: הָאַף means anger, so the sense is that anger will lead God to sweep away the righteous with the wicked. He first gives הֲגַם ('even, also'), then the Targum's 'anger' as the meaning. The English 'in anger' follows his conclusion.
- `ibn-ezra:18:23:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: תִּסְפֶּה is a transitive verb (פועל יוצא) with the sense 'consume', and so 'sweep away' keeps that sense.

Grammar notes:
- אוּלַי introduces a tentative question: 'perhaps there are fifty'.
- יֵשׁ חֲמִשִּׁים צַדִּיקִם בְּתוֹךְ הָעִיר: an existential clause with יֵשׁ; the city is where the righteous would be found.
- הַאַף תִּסְפֶּה וְלֹא־תִשָּׂא: a second question, with the same verb as 18:23 and the negative on the verb of sparing.
- לְמַעַן חֲמִשִּׁים הַצַּדִּיקִם אֲשֶׁר בְּקִרְבָּהּ: a purpose phrase; the relative clause 'who are within it' refers back to the righteous.

Translator's note: Abraham's argument opens here and the quotation continues into 18:25, so the English has no closing quotation mark. Rashi's count is ten for each of the five cities; the English keeps the Hebrew's singular 'the city'. The English names Sodom as Ibn Ezra does, and 'spare' follows his 'forgive'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:24:1` records: Rashi: the fifty righteous are ten for each of the five cities, since there are five places; he also asks why the righteous are destroyed if they cannot save the wicked. The English keeps 'within the city' and records Rashi's distribution.
- `ibn-ezra:18:24:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: לֹא תִשָּׂא here is like תִּסְלַח, forgive, as in 'bearing iniquity' (נושא עון, Exod. 34:7). The English 'spare' keeps that sense: not forgive the place.
- `ibn-ezra:18:24:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: לַמָּקוֹם is Sodom, because it was for its sake that Abraham drew near to save Lot. The English names Sodom; Radak's wider reading of the place is not adopted.
