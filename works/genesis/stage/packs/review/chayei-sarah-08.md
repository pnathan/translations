Phrases:
- 24:33.1 24:33.2 24:33.3 | Then there was set before him to eat | and-was-set to-faces-of-him to-eat: the hofal has no named doer, so the English passive has none; 'there was' is the English form of the unnamed passive.
- 24:33.4 24:33.5 24:33.6 | He said, "I will not eat | and-said not I-eat: the negative with the yiqtol makes the refusal.

Grammar notes:
- הוּשַׂם is the hofal of שׂוּם, a passive in the wayyiqtol; the Hebrew names no doer, and the English passive keeps that.
- The infinitive לֶאֱכֹל gives the purpose of the setting; the negative לֹא אֹכַל is the refusal, and the qatal דִּבַּרְתִּי in the clause after עַד אִם is completed before the eating.
- The second וַיֹּאמֶר introduces the imperative דַּבֵּר; the speaker changes without a name in the Hebrew.

Translator's note: Ibn Ezra (normative) reads the hofal as the binyan in which the doer is not named; the English follows him. Radak's servants and his name for the second speaker are recorded, not adopted.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:24:33:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: this is the binyan in which the name of the doer is not mentioned, as in ויושב את משה; the English therefore gives an unnamed passive.
- `rashi:24:33:1` follows: Rashi: אִם here is used in the sense of כִּי, as in עַד כִּי יָבֹא שִׁילֹה; the sages count four senses of that particle, and one of them is אִם. The English reads 'until'.
- `radak:24:33:1` records: Radak: the ketiv ויישם is taken from the root ישם; he sets the ketiv beside the form read here.
- `radak:24:33:2` records: Radak: the form is hofal, and the food was set before him by the servants of the house; the Hebrew names no servants, so the English does not add them.
- `radak:24:33:3` records: Radak: the one who says 'speak' is Laban or Bethuel; the Hebrew names neither, so the English keeps 'he'.
- apparatus: Ketiv ויישם; qere וַיּוּשַׂם, the form translated. Radak takes the ketiv from the root ישם.

Grammar notes:
- The speech of Abraham is in single quotes inside the servant's speech; the outer double quotation stays open.
- הִתְהַלַּכְתִּי is hitpael: 'walk about'; with לְפָנָיו it reads 'walk before him'.
- The two future clauses: the LORD will send his angel and will make the way prosper; the second is a perfect with waw continuing the future.
- The suffix on אִתָּךְ is parsed as 2nd feminine singular while the addressee is a man; the English 'you' keeps the number and leaves the gender open.

Translator's note: The subject of the final instruction is 'you' (the servant), as in 24:38.
