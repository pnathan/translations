### Translation under review (17:15)

English: God said to Abraham, "Sarai your wife: do not call her name Sarai, for Sarah is her name."

Grammar notes:
- שָׂרַי אִשְׁתְּךָ is a nominative absolute (casus pendens): the topic is named first and then taken up by the pronoun in אֶת־שְׁמָהּ.
- לֹא־תִקְרָא with the imperfect is a prohibition ('you are not to call'), not a prediction.
- The etnaḥta on שָׂרָי divides the verse: the prohibition, then the reason.
- כִּי שָׂרָה שְׁמָהּ has no verb; the English supplies 'is'. Radak reads it as 'shall be her name', as in 17:5 וְהָיָה שִׁמְךָ אַבְרָהָם.
- The change שָׂרַי to שָׂרָה echoes 17:5, where אַבְרָם becomes אַבְרָהָם.

Translator's note: The meaning of the new name is not given in the English line (decisions.md). Both names are rendered by transliteration, in the usual forms Sarai and Sarah. Radak notes that the husband, not the wife, is the one who 'calls' her name, which is why the order is addressed to Abraham alone.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:17:15:1` records: Rashi: שָׂרַי means 'my princess' (שָׂרַי, mine), 'mine and not others'', which is why the name was too narrow. The English does not carry this etymology.
- `rashi:17:15:2` records: Rashi: שָׂרָה is 'a plain name', that she be 'sarah' over all (b. Berakhot 13). The reading is kept as the reason for the change; the English line only states it.
- `radak:17:15:1` not-applicable: Radak: only the husband calls the wife by name; the wife does not call the husband by name out of respect. This explains why Abraham alone is addressed; it does not change the English.
- `radak:17:15:3` follows: Radak: כִּי שָׂרָה שְׁמָהּ means 'shall be her name', as in 17:5, and others will call her so once Abraham does.
- `onkelos:17:15:1` follows: Onkelos renders the verse closely, 'do not call her name Sarai, for Sarah is her name'.

### Translation under review (17:16)

English: I will bless her, and I will also give you a son from her. I will bless her, and she shall become nations; kings of peoples shall come from her.

Literal: and-I-will-bless [object-marker]-her and-also I-gave from-her to-you son and-I-will-bless-her and-she-will-be to-nations kings-of peoples from-her they-will-be

Phrases:

- 17:16.3 17:16.4 | I will also give | 'and-also I-gave'; perfect with future sense

- 17:16.9 17:16.10 | she shall become nations | 'and-she-will-be to-nations'

- 17:16.13 17:16.14 | shall come from her | 'from-her they-will-be': 'will arise from her'

Grammar notes:
- וּבֵרַכְתִּי and וְהָיְתָה are weqatal forms: they continue the promise as future.
- נָתַתִּי is a perfect that stands for the future (Ibn Ezra), a prophetic perfect, like נָתַתִּי כֶּסֶף הַשָּׂדֶה in 23:13.
- The etnaḥta after בֵּן separates the gift of the son from the second blessing; the double pashta on וּבֵרַכְתִּיהָ marks the repeated verb.
- מַלְכֵי עַמִּים: 'kings of peoples' is a construct chain; מִמֶּנָּה is stressed by being put last.

Translator's note: וּבֵרַכְתִּי אֹתָהּ and וּבֵרַכְתִּיהָ are the same verb twice. Rashi gives each blessing a content (restored youth; milk for the nursing at Isaac's feast); Sforno reads the second as childbearing and rearing without pain. These readings are kept in the commentary entries and not added to the English. מִמֶּנָּה, 'from her', is kept as in the Hebrew.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:17:16:1` records: Rashi: the first blessing is that she was restored to her youth (Gen 18:12 'I have had pleasure'). Kept as an interpretation; the English says only 'bless'.
- `rashi:17:16:2` records: Rashi: the second blessing is that she could nurse at Isaac's weaning feast, when she nursed the children brought by the other women. Kept as an interpretation.
- `ibn-ezra:17:16:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: וְגַם נָתַתִּי is like נתתי כסף השדה (23:13); both perfects stand for the future. The English uses the future.
- `radak:17:16:1` records: Radak: the blessing is that she regains menstruation and the way of women, and then bears.
- `onkelos:17:16:1` follows: Onkelos has the same sequence of blessing, a son, nations and kings.

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּצְחָק, 'and he laughed', uses the root of the name יִצְחָק (17:19) before the name is given.
- הַלְּבֶן and הֲבַת open the questions with the interrogative ה; the second question continues with וְאִם ('and if / or').
- The etnaḥta on וַיִּצְחָק separates the act from what he said in his heart.
- יִוָּלֵד is Niphal ('be born') and תֵּלֵד is Qal ('bear'): the verbs are chosen to fit the subject.

Translator's note: Whether וַיִּצְחָק is laughter of joy or of mockery is the ambiguity of the verse. Rashi (and Onkelos וַחֲדִי, Radak, Ramban) take Abraham's laughter as joy, in contrast to Sarah's laughter in 18:12 as scorn; the English keeps the plain word 'laughed' and the reading goes in the commentary. The questions are the genuine wonder of Rashi and Ibn Ezra, not doubt. 'A child' translates בֵּן (here 'son'); the English line keeps 'child' for the idiom 'the son of a hundred years'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:17:17:1` follows: Rashi: וַיִּצְחָק is the laughter of joy (Aramaic חֲדִי), unlike Sarah's laughter of mockery; Abraham believed and rejoiced, Sarah did not believe.
- `rashi:17:17:2` follows: Rashi: some questions of wonder stand as real questions (compare 1 Sam 2:27, Ezek 8:6): Abraham wonders whether such kindness will be done for him.
- `rashi:17:17:3` not-applicable: Rashi: Sarah was fit to bear; the earlier generations bore children at five hundred, but by Abraham's time the years had shrunk. A comment on the numbers, not on the words.
- `ibn-ezra:17:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: Abraham marvels; the seed of an old man is cold, and the greater wonder is that a woman whose blood had stopped should bear.
- `ramban:17:17:1` follows: Ramban: the laughter is joy (Onkelos); the question is a wonder, not disbelief.
- `radak:17:17:2` follows: Radak: he rejoiced in his heart, not in mockery.
- apparatus: Radak: the wonder is not unbelief (Gen 15:6 'he believed in the LORD').

### Translation under review (17:18)

English: Abraham said to God, "If only Ishmael might live before you!"

Literal: and-said Abraham to the-God if-only Ishmael he-will-live before-you

Grammar notes:
- לוּ introduces a wish ('if only'), here with the imperfect יִחְיֶה.
- The etnaḥta on הָאֱלֹהִים separates the speech from the introduction.
- לְפָנֶיךָ is 'before you', the idiom of being in God's presence.

Translator's note: Rashi reads לוּ as 'would that' and takes it to mean that Abraham is unworthy of so great a reward. Ramban: לוּ is like אִם and Abraham fears that Ishmael may die. The English takes the wish plainly. Rashi's 'live in fear of you' is kept as a reading of לְפָנֶיךָ.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:17:18:1` follows: Rashi: הַלְוַאי, 'would that Ishmael live'; Abraham says he is not worthy of such a great reward.
- `rashi:17:18:2` records: Rashi: 'live before you' means live in fear of you, like הִתְהַלֵּךְ לְפָנַי 'serve before me' (17:1). Ramban rejects this because God replies 'I have heard you' for Ishmael.
- `ramban:17:18:1` records: Ramban: לוּ is 'if'; Abraham fears for Ishmael and asks that he live.
- `radak:17:18:1` records: Radak: 'enough for me what you have given; let Ishmael live a good life.'
