English: These are the generations of Noah. Noah was a righteous man, blameless in his generations; Noah walked with God.

Grammar notes:
- תּוֹלְדֹת is in the construct state; the account that follows begins not with Noah's sons but with his character (so Rashi).
- The repeated נֹחַ after the revia breaks the construct chain and begins a new clause: 'Noah was a righteous man'.
- אִישׁ צַדִּיק תָּמִים: the two adjectives stand side by side, each describing 'man'.
- הִתְהַלֶּךְ is a hitpael perfect with no vav; Rashi notes that the pattern serves for past or future, here the past.
- The etnaḥta on בְּדֹרֹתָיו divides the verse: the first half describes Noah, the second his relation to God.

Translator's note: 'Generations' for תּוֹלְדֹת keeps the word's range (descendants, history, account); Ibn Ezra reads it as 'events', Ramban as the plain sense 'descendants'. The phrase בְּדֹרֹתָיו is left as 'in his generations', which keeps both readings (in his own age, and in the ages after the flood) and the disputed praise/blame reading of Rashi's rabbis. 'With God' for אֶת־הָאֱלֹהִים follows the preposition אֵת; the old 'walked before' of Abraham is a different construction.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:9:1` follows: Rashi: since the text mentions Noah, it goes on to praise him, as 'the memory of the righteous is for a blessing'; or it teaches that the true generations (offspring) of the righteous are their good deeds. The English keeps 'generations', which allows this.
- `rashi:6:9:2` records: Rashi: some of our rabbis read בְּדֹרֹתָיו as praise (he would have been more righteous in a righteous generation) and some as disparagement (righteous only relative to his generation; beside Abraham he would count for nothing). The English leaves the phrase as 'in his generations' and does not decide between them.
- `rashi:6:9:3` follows: Rashi: of Abraham it says 'before whom I walked', but Noah needed support to hold him up, while Abraham strengthened himself and walked in his righteousness on his own. The English 'walked with God' keeps the preposition אֵת; the contrast with Abraham is in the commentary.
- `rashi:6:9:4` follows: Rashi: הִתְהַלֵּךְ is past in meaning; in the pattern of this stem the same form serves for the future and the past, as with קוּם הִתְהַלֵּךְ (future) against הִתְהַלֶּךְ נֹחַ (past). The English uses the past tense.
- `ibn-ezra:6:9:1` records: Ibn Ezra: תּוֹלְדֹת means 'events', as in מַה יֵּלֶד יוֹם and אֵלֶּה תּוֹלְדֹת יַעֲקֹב. The English 'generations' does not exclude it, and the note records it. Ramban rejects this and keeps 'descendants'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:9:2` follows: Ibn Ezra on אִישׁ צַדִּיק: 'in his deeds'. Righteous is read as righteous in conduct, which is the plain English.
- `ibn-ezra:6:9:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: תָּמִים is 'in his heart'; the word describes God's works as well. The English 'blameless' is read as complete and sound, so the pair means righteous in act, whole in heart.
- `ibn-ezra:6:9:4` follows: Ibn Ezra: בְּדֹרֹתָיו means in his generation at the time of the flood and in the generations after, for Noah lived until Abraham was fifty-eight. The plural 'in his generations' in the English admits this reading.
- apparatus: Rashi's two rabbinic readings of בְּדֹרֹתָיו (praise or blame, b. Sanhedrin 108a) are both left open.
- apparatus: אֶת־הָאֱלֹהִים: אֵת is read as the preposition 'with' (cf. Rashi's comparison with Abraham's 'before whom'); the pack gives the lemma as the preposition for this token.

### Translation under review (6:12)

English: God saw the earth, and indeed it was corrupt, for all flesh had corrupted its way on the earth.

Grammar notes:
- וְהִנֵּה marks what God sees, from the point of view of the one who sees: 'and there, it was corrupt'.
- נִשְׁחָתָה (nifal, 'was corrupted') and הִשְׁחִית (hifil, 'corrupted') are the same root in two stems: the earth is ruined because all flesh ruined its way.
- כׇּל־בָּשָׂר is a fixed phrase for all living things; in the first half of the Flood story it takes in humans and animals.
- The suffix in דַּרְכּוֹ is singular, referring back to 'flesh' taken as a whole.
- The etnaḥta after נִשְׁחָתָה divides the seeing from the reason.

Translator's note: 'All flesh' and 'its way' are left as they are: Rashi takes 'all flesh' to include beasts and birds mating across kinds, Ibn Ezra's ancients say each creature did not keep its natural way of generation, and Radak takes 'flesh' as human beings. 'Way' (דֶּרֶךְ) could be conduct or nature; the English does not choose. The hifil הִשְׁחִית is taken as a pluperfect ('had corrupted') because it gives the earlier ground of the earlier state.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:12:1` follows: Rashi on כִּי הִשְׁחִית כׇּל בָּשָׂר: even beasts, wild animals and birds joined with kinds not their own. The English 'all flesh' does not exclude animals, so this is kept open.
- `ibn-ezra:6:12:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the ancients said that every living thing did not keep the way of its begetting and perverted the planted path, which he calls correct; and a further homily says they corrupted themselves with water and God judged them with water. The English 'its way' keeps both the conduct and the nature of each kind open.
- `ibn-ezra:6:11:2` follows: Ibn Ezra on 'before God' and the following כִּי: some say 'openly', others 'in secret', but the nearer reading is that the Torah speaks in human language, like a servant who sins before his master without being seen. The English 'before God' keeps the plain 'in his sight' and does not decide between open and hidden.
- `ramban:6:12:1` not-applicable: Ramban: a discussion of what 'all flesh' means and of what 'its way' is; it bears on interpretation rather than on the wording of the English.
- `radak:6:12:1` not-applicable: Radak: a gloss on כִּי הִשְׁחִית, which explains the sense of the verb; it does not change the English.
- `radak:6:12:2` records: Radak: 'all flesh' means human beings, as in other verses. The English does not narrow 'all flesh' to people.
- `radak:6:12:3` records: Radak: 'its way' is the way of its begetting, like 'and he shall cleave to his wife'. The English leaves 'way' open.
- `onkelos:6:12:1` records: Onkelos: 'And the LORD saw the earth, and it was corrupted, for all flesh, man, had corrupted its way': אֱנַשׁ glosses כָּל בָּשָׂר as humans, which is a narrower reading than the English.
- `sforno:6:12:1` not-applicable: Sforno on וְהִנֵּה נִשְׁחָתָה: it was corrupt of itself, with no punishment having come; it describes the cause and does not change the wording.
- apparatus: Subject of 6:12.1: אֱלֹהִים without the article, 'God'; the LORD in Onkelos and the Targum rendering.
- apparatus: 'All flesh' (so Rashi, Ramban) or 'all humans' (Radak, Onkelos): the English keeps the wider phrase.
