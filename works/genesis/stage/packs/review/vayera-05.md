### Translation under review (18:33)

English: And the LORD went away when he had finished speaking to Abraham, and Abraham returned to his place.

Literal: and-went the-LORD as-which finished to-speak to-Abraham and-Abraham returned to-his-place

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18:33.1 | and went away |  | Qal II.2 | wayyiqtol of qal halakh. The lexicon gives no 'go' under Qal I; 'walk' under Qal II.2 is the nearest sense. |
| 18:33.2 | the LORD |  |  | YHWH, rendered LORD in capitals (decisions: divine names). |
| 18:33.3 | when |  | 3 | kaf of comparison with the relative asher: a temporal conjunction. |
| 18:33.4 | had finished |  | Pi 1.c | piel qatal of kalah, subject the LORD. |
| 18:33.5 | speaking |  | Pi | piel infinitive construct of dabar with the prefix lamed: 'to speak'. |
| 18:33.6 | to |  | 1 |  |
| 18:33.7 | Abraham |  |  |  |
| 18:33.8 | and Abraham |  |  | the vav opens a disjunctive clause; the subject comes first, then the verb. |
| 18:33.9 | returned |  | Qal 2 | qal qatal of shuv, 'return, go back'. |
| 18:33.10 | to his place |  | 2.a | lamed with the construct of makom and the 3ms suffix. |

Phrases:
- 18:33.1 18:33.2 | And the LORD went away | 'and-went the-LORD': the verb comes first in the Hebrew; the English puts the subject first.
- 18:33.3 18:33.4 18:33.5 18:33.6 18:33.7 | when he had finished speaking to Abraham | 'as-which finished to-speak to-Abraham': the temporal clause, with the subject 'he' understood from the verb.
- 18:33.8 18:33.9 18:33.10 | and Abraham returned to his place | 'and-Abraham returned to-his-place': the main clause of the second half of the verse.

Grammar notes:
- The first verb is wayyiqtol (the sequential narrative form, 'and went'); the second half of the verse changes to a disjunctive clause, 'and Abraham returned', with the subject first.
- The clause 'when he had finished speaking' is circumstantial: kaf and asher make a temporal conjunction, and the piel qatal kilah gives the completed action before the departure.
- The etnahta on Abraham (18:33.7) divides the verse: the first part is the LORD's departure, the second Abraham's return to his place.
- The place to which Abraham returns is not named; the commentators identify it (Ibn Ezra: Hebron). See the commentary.

Translator's note: The lexicon lists no sense of halakh meaning simple 'go' under Qal I. The English 'went away' takes the sense 'walk' and the context of departure.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:33:1` records: Rashi's midrash: when the advocate (Abraham) fell silent, the judge (the LORD) went away. The English gives the plain sense of the departure after the speech; the allegory is recorded here.
- `rashi:18:33:2` records: Rashi, again allegorically: once the judge and then the advocate had gone, the accuser remained and the two angels went to Sodom to destroy it. The English keeps the plain 'returned to his place'; the allegory is recorded here.
- `ibn-ezra:18:33:1` records: Ibn Ezra identifies 'his place' as Hebron: the vision took place where Abraham had gone to send the angels, from where he looked over Sodom; the sun had risen when he looked, since Sodom was not overturned at night. The English says 'his place' and does not name Hebron; the identification is recorded here.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders the subject as 'the Glory of the LORD' (yeqara d'Adonai); the English keeps 'the LORD' per the decisions.
- apparatus: Ramban and Radak (not normative) read the departure as the end of the prophetic vision; not adopted here.

Phrases:
- 19:1.1 19:1.2 19:1.3 19:1.4 19:1.5 | The two angels came to Sodom in the evening | 'and-came two the-angels to-Sodom in-the-evening': the verb comes first, the subject follows.
- 19:1.6 19:1.7 19:1.8 19:1.9 | and Lot was sitting in the gate of Sodom | 'and-Lot sitting in-gate-of Sodom': a participle gives the background to the next action.
- 19:1.10 19:1.11 | When Lot saw them | 'and-saw Lot': the object of the seeing is not written; 'them' is supplied from the context.
- 19:1.12 19:1.13 | he rose to meet them | 'and-rose to-meet-them': the sequence of the two verbs.
- 19:1.14 19:1.15 19:1.16 | and bowed down, face to the ground | 'and-bowed-down nostrils to-the-ground': the dual 'faces' with the bowing, the English 'face'.

Grammar notes:
- The chain of wayyiqtols (came, saw, rose, bowed down) gives the narrative sequence: the arrival, the sighting, the rising and the bowing.
- The dual shenei (two) with the definite noun 'the angels' is a construct phrase: 'two of the angels' is read as 'the two angels'.
- yoshev (19:1.7) is a participle: the English 'was sitting' gives the state that Lot is in when the angels arrive.
- The dual apayim (19:1.15) is a noun used adverbially with the hitpael to express the bowing: 'face to the ground'.

Translator's note: The English 'he rose to meet them' keeps the subject Lot; the verb 'rose' with the lamed infinitive is a sequence of two actions.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:19:1:1` follows: Rashi: the three visitors of 18:2 divided: two went to Sodom, one to destroy it and one to save Lot; the third, sent to heal Abraham and to tell Sarah, left once his task was done. The English 'two' follows.
- `rashi:19:1:2` follows: Rashi: they are called 'men' with Abraham, while the Shekhinah was with them, and 'angels' with Lot. The English 'angels' follows the plain sense; the other explanation is recorded here.
- `rashi:19:1:3` not-applicable: Rashi asks whether the angels really took so long from Hebron to Sodom, and answers that they were angels of mercy who waited in case Abraham could find a defence for the city. A homily on the delay; 'in the evening' keeps the plain time.
- `rashi:19:1:4` records: Rashi: 'and Lot was sitting' is written with yashav; that day they made him judge over them (Bereshit Rabbah). The English 'was sitting' keeps the plain sense; the judgeship reading is recorded here.
- `rashi:19:1:5` not-applicable: Rashi: Lot learned from Abraham's house to go out and meet guests. A comment on conduct; the English reports the act.
- `ibn-ezra:19:1:1` records: Ibn Ezra refers 'in the evening' back to his comment on the Passover of Egypt (Exod. 12:6, between the evenings): a note on the time of the event. The English keeps 'in the evening' and records the cross-reference.
- `ibn-ezra:19:1:2` records: Ibn Ezra takes 'apayim' as the nostrils, which are high on the face: Lot bowed until his nostrils touched the ground. The English takes the dual as 'face' and records this reading.
- apparatus: Ibn Ezra's reading 'nostrils' (19:1.15) is recorded in the commentary; the English follows the usual sense 'face'.

Phrases:
- 19:2.1 | And he said | 'and-said': the vav opens the speech.
- 19:2.2 19:2.3 19:2.4 | Here now, my lords | 'Here now my-lords': the exclamation with the address.

- 19:2.7 19:2.8 19:2.9 | to your servant's house | 'to house-of your-servant': the construct chain, with Lot's address to the men.
- 19:2.10 19:2.11 19:2.12 | and spend the night, and wash your feet | 'and-spend-the-night and-wash your-feet': the Hebrew order (lodge first, then wash) is kept.
- 19:2.13 19:2.14 19:2.15 | then you can rise early and go on your way | 'and-rise-early and-go to-your-way': the qatal forms continue the imperatives as a consequence.
- 19:2.16 | But they said | 'and-said': the men's reply, adversative.
- 19:2.17 19:2.18 19:2.19 19:2.20 | No, but we will spend the night in the square | 'no for in-the-square we-spend-the-night': the refusal, then the alternative.

Grammar notes:
- The imperatives suru, vilinu and verahatsu (plural) address the men; the vav links the imperatives in a request.
- Hinneh na is an exclamation with the particle na: 'here now'. The second na (19:2.6) is an exhortation, 'please'.
- The qatal forms vehishkamtem and vahalakhtem (19:2.13-14) continue the imperatives: 'then you can rise early and go on your way'.
- lo ... ki (19:2.17-18) is 'No, but': ki is adversative here, as Rashi reads it.

Translator's note: The imperative 'spend the night' (lun) and the yiqtol 'we will spend the night' (19:2.20) use the same verb: the men refuse Lot's offer of a night in his house.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:19:2:1` follows: Rashi: 'here now, my lords' means you are my lords now that you have passed through me; his second reading, that the men must take care not to be recognised by the wicked men of the city, is recorded here. The English 'my lords' follows the address.
- `rashi:19:2:2` records: Rashi: 'turn aside' means make the road to my house crooked, so that Sodom does not see them going in (Bereshit Rabbah). The English keeps the plain 'turn aside'; the crooked-road reading is recorded.
- `rashi:19:2:3` records: Rashi explains the order 'spend the night, then wash': Lot wanted their feet to show the dust of the road, so that Sodom would not say they had been in his house for days. The English keeps the Hebrew order; the motive is recorded here.
- `rashi:19:2:4` not-applicable: Rashi: they refused Lot, a lesser man, but did not refuse Abraham (18:5). A comment on the refusal; the English 'No' stands.
- `rashi:19:2:5` follows: Rashi: ki here means 'but' (ela): they would not turn aside to his house but would spend the night in the city square. The English 'No, but' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:19:2:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'gentlemen', a common form of address; sur with a mem is the plain 'turn aside'. The English 'my lords' and 'turn aside' follow the plain sense of the words.
- `ibn-ezra:19:2:2` records: Ibn Ezra takes lo here as a formal refusal, part of the urging (as 'if you do not press' in 1 Sam. 15:23). The English 'No' is the plain negative; the reading as urging is recorded here.
- apparatus: WLC note v on 19:2.14 (vahalakhtem): one or more vowels in the Leningrad codex are read differently from BHS. The pointing in the pack is followed.

- 19:3.12 19:3.13 | and baked unleavened bread | 'and-unleavened-cakes baked': the English puts the verb before the object.
- 19:3.14 | and they ate | 'and-ate': the closing wayyiqtol.

Grammar notes:
- The wayyiqtols (urged, turned aside, came, made, ate) carry the narrative. The vav of 'urged' is adversative: the men had refused, and Lot presses them.
- The adverb me'od (19:3.3) intensifies the verb: 'greatly'. The preposition beth on the 3mp suffix gives the object of the urging.
- The meal is named by two nouns, mishteh and matsot, and one verb, afah: the Hebrew names the feast before the baking. The English reverses the order.

Translator's note: The verb 'turned aside' (vayyasuru) is the same as 19:2.5 'turn aside'. The men who refused the road now turn to Lot's house.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:19:3:1` follows: Rashi: 'they turned aside' means they bent the road toward his house. The English 'turned aside to him' follows: they left the road to go to Lot's house.
- `rashi:19:3:2` records: Rashi: the unleavened bread shows that it was Passover. The English keeps 'unleavened bread' and does not name the feast; the Passover reading is recorded here.
- `ramban:19:3:1` not-applicable: Ramban (not normative): Lot had merit in pressing the men, and they were reluctant at first. A comment on Lot's character; not the plain sense of the words.
- `radak:19:3:3` records: Radak (not normative): if the men were in a vision, they only seemed to eat. The English keeps the text's 'they ate'; the vision reading is recorded here.
- apparatus: Targum Onkelos (19:3) renders the urging as 'and he pressed them'; the English keeps 'urged' with the adverb 'strongly'.
