### Translation under review (18:9)

English: They said to him, "Where is Sarah your wife?" He said, "Look, in the tent."

Literal: and-they-said to-him where Sarah your-wife and-he-said look in-the-tent

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18:9.1 | and they said |  | Qal 1 | wayyiqtol (sequential imperfect), 3rd person masculine plural, of אמר |
| 18:9.2 | to him |  |  |  |
| 18:9.3 | where |  |  |  |
| 18:9.4 | Sarah |  |  |  |
| 18:9.5 | your wife |  | 2 | the noun is in the construct form before the suffix 2nd person masculine singular |
| 18:9.6 | and he said |  | Qal 1 | wayyiqtol, 3rd person masculine singular |
| 18:9.7 | look |  |  |  |
| 18:9.8 | in the tent |  | 1 |  |

Phrases:
- 18:9.1 18:9.2 | They said to him | and-they-said to-him; the vav is rendered by the sense of the sequence
- 18:9.3 18:9.4 18:9.5 | Where is Sarah your wife | where Sarah your-wife; the Hebrew has no verb, the English supplies 'is'
- 18:9.6 | He said | and-he-said; the speaker changes to the one who answers
- 18:9.7 18:9.8 | Look, in the tent | look in-the-tent; the answer gives the place

Grammar notes:
- The two wayyiqtol verbs (וַיֹּאמְרוּ, וַיֹּאמֶר) carry the narrative sequence: first the visitors speak, then the one addressed answers.
- The question has no verb: אַיֵּה שָׂרָה אִשְׁתֶּךָ is 'where Sarah your wife', and the English supplies 'is'.
- The etnaḥta on אִשְׁתֶּךָ closes the question; the silluq on בָאֹהֶל closes the verse.
- The vav of אֵלָיו carries a puncta extraordinaria (dot above the letter); the dot is read by Rashi (see the commentary), and the English does not adopt that reading.

Translator's note: The answer 'in the tent' is taken as the modest place of a woman who stays inside (Rashi). The English states the place only.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:9:1` records: Rashi: the dot over the vav of אֵלָיו teaches that the visitors also asked Sarah where Abraham was (שֶׁאַף לְשָׂרָה שָׁאֲלוּ אַיּוֹ אַבְרָהָם), and that a man should ask the wife about the man in his host's house and the reverse. The English keeps 'to him' and records the dot reading.
- `rashi:18:9:2` follows: Rashi: the answer 'Look, in the tent' tells that she is modest (צְנוּעָה הִיא): she stays inside the tent. The English gives the place; the modesty is in this note.
- apparatus: Puncta extraordinaria on the vav of אֵלָיו (WLC note 4); Rashi, Radak and Bereshit Rabbah read it as a teaching about the question to Sarah.

Grammar notes:
- The infinitive absolute שׁוֹב before the yiqtol אָשׁוּב is the emphatic form: 'I will surely return'.
- The clause כָּעֵת חַיָּה is verbless: 'at this time, alive'. The appointed season is the one named in 17:21.
- The verse has no verb for the son: לְשָׂרָה בֵן is 'a son to Sarah', and the English supplies 'will have'.
- The revia on וַיֹּאמֶר and the silluq on אַחֲרָיו divide the verse; the speaker of the first clause is the visitor who speaks in God's name.

Translator's note: כָּעֵת חַיָּה is read by Rashi and Ibn Ezra as 'this time next year' and 'alive'. The English follows that. Rashi's second reading of חַיָּה ('alive for you') is recorded in the commentary.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:10:1` follows: Rashi: the angel does not promise his own return; he speaks on the errand of the Place (בִּשְׁלִיחוּתוֹ שֶׁל מָקוֹם), as in 16:10 where the angel says 'I will multiply', so the words are God's. The English keeps the first person of the speech.
- `rashi:18:10:2` follows: Rashi: כָּעֵת is this season of the next year, which was Passover, and Isaac was born at the next Passover; the word is read כָּעֵת, not כְּעֵת. The English gives 'at this time next year'.
- `rashi:18:10:3` records: Rashi's second reading: 'this time' is a time that will be alive for you (שֶׁתְּהֵא חַיָּה לָכֶם), that you will all be whole and enduring. The English does not adopt it.
- `rashi:18:10:4` follows: Rashi: הַפֶּתַח הָיָה אַחַר הַמַּלְאָךְ, the entrance was behind the angel. The English says 'it was behind him'.
- `ibn-ezra:18:10:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'this time in the other year' (כעת הזאת בשנה האחרת), and Sarah is alive, like כה לחי in 1 Samuel 25:6. The English agrees on the time and reads 'alive' of the one who lives.
- `ibn-ezra:18:10:2` records: Ibn Ezra: the clause refers to the angel who speaks with Abraham, and Abraham, sitting at the tent entrance, did not see Sarah; others say Sarah's entrance was behind Abraham's tent (וי"א פתח האהל שלה היה אחרי אהל אברהם). The English keeps Rashi's reading and records this.
- apparatus: The lexicon for שָׁמַע lists Niph and Pi senses only; the qal participle שֹׁמַעַת is given no sense.

### Translation under review (18:11)

English: Now Abraham and Sarah were old, advanced in days; Sarah had ceased to have the way of women.

Literal: and-Abraham and-Sarah old coming in-days; ceased to-be for-Sarah way as-women

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18:11.1 | and Abraham |  |  |  |
| 18:11.2 | and Sarah |  |  |  |
| 18:11.3 | old |  | 1 |  |
| 18:11.4 | advanced |  | Qal 2 | qal participle of בּוֹא, 'come': 'come into days' |
| 18:11.5 | in days |  | 4 | the plural of יוֹם in the sense 'days', with ב |
| 18:11.6 | had ceased |  | Qal 2 | qatal, 3rd person masculine singular; the subject is the custom of women |
| 18:11.7 | to be |  | Qal III | infinitive construct of היה, with ל: 'to be for her' |
| 18:11.8 | for Sarah |  |  |  |
| 18:11.9 | way |  | 3 | the way (custom) of women; the phrase is a euphemism for the monthly course |
| 18:11.10 | as women |  | 1 | כ with the plural of אִשָּׁה |

Phrases:
- 18:11.1 18:11.2 | Now Abraham and Sarah | and-Abraham and-Sarah; the vav is rendered 'now' as the start of the new clause
- 18:11.3 18:11.4 18:11.5 | were old, advanced in days | old coming in-days; 'advanced in days' is the reading of Ibn Ezra (reached many days)
- 18:11.6 18:11.7 18:11.8 | Sarah had ceased | ceased to-be for-Sarah; the verb takes the infinitive, and the subject is the matter of the verse
- 18:11.9 18:11.10 | to have the way of women | way as-women; 'the way of women' is the euphemism of Rashi's reading (the monthly course)

Grammar notes:
- חָדַל לִהְיוֹת is 'ceased to be': a qatal of cessation with an infinitive construct; Rashi glosses it as 'ceased from her'.
- בָּאִים בַּיָּמִים is the idiom for an old man: 'coming into days', with ב of time.
- אֹרַח כַּנָּשִׁים is a noun phrase with כ of comparison: 'the way like women's'. The silluq on כַּנָּשִׁים closes the verse.
- The pashta on חָדַל marks the verb before its infinitive; the verse divides into the description of the couple and the cessation.

Translator's note: The English 'the way of women' is the Hebrew's own euphemism. Rashi names it as the custom of the menstrual course; the English does not name it.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:11:1` follows: Rashi: חָדַל לִהְיוֹת means פָּסַק מִמֶּנָה, it ceased from her. The English reads 'had ceased'.
- `rashi:18:11:2` follows: Rashi: אֹרַח here is אֹרַח נִדּוֹת, the custom of the menstrual course. The English uses 'the way of women', the euphemism of the text, and does not name it.
- `ibn-ezra:18:11:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: בָּאִים בַּיָּמִים means הגיעו לימים רבים, they reached many days. The English gives 'advanced in days'.

Grammar notes:

- The verb הָיְתָה is a qatal used as a question of disbelief; the English 'shall I have' is the future sense that Ibn Ezra gives.
- The verse turns on two clauses: 'after I am worn out' (the infinitive of בלה) and 'my lord is old' (a qatal of זקן, with the subject אֲדֹנִי).
- The wayyiqtol וַתִּצְחַק and the infinitive לֵאמֹר introduce the speech, which is in her own words and not the LORD's.

- `ibn-ezra:18:12:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: הָיְתָה לִּי means תִּהְיֶה לִּי, shall it be to me. The English reads the qatal as 'shall I have'.

### Translation under review (18:14)

English: Is anything hidden from the LORD? At the appointed time I will return to you, at this time next year, alive, and Sarah will have a son.

Grammar notes:
- הֲיִפָּלֵא is a question with the interrogative ה and a nifal yiqtol: 'is anything hidden from the LORD?'.
- The verb לַמּוֹעֵד אָשׁוּב names the appointed time, and כָּעֵת חַיָּה repeats the words of 18:10; the verse restates the promise as a question and answer.
- The verse has no verb for the son: וּלְשָׂרָה בֵן is 'and to Sarah a son'.
- The silluq on בֵן closes the verse on the promise.

Translator's note: Rashi and Ibn Ezra take הֲיִפָּלֵא as 'hidden' (היתכסי, היעלם), and the English follows them. The lexicon's Niph senses ('beyond one's power, difficult') are the other reading; Radak takes it so, and it is recorded.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:14:1` follows: Rashi: הֲיִפָּלֵא is הֲיִתְכַּסִּי, 'is it concealed?' (as its Targum). Is any wondrous, separated or hidden thing hidden from me in doing my will? The English reads 'hidden from the LORD'.
- `rashi:18:14:2` follows: Rashi: לַמּוֹעֵד is that appointed time that was set for you yesterday, at this time next year. The English gives 'at the appointed time' and 'at this time next year'.
- `ibn-ezra:18:14:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: הֲיִפָּלֵא means היעלם, is hidden. Others say it means 'not a wonder for God', but then the mem would not be attached to the Name; he rejects that. The English follows 'hidden'.
- `radak:18:14:1` records: Radak (not normative): things that seem strange to people are hidden from them, but nothing is hard for the LORD (לֹא יִפָּלֵא מִמֶּנּוּ דָּבָר). The English does not adopt this reading; it is recorded here.
- apparatus: 18:14.2 vowels: WLC reads מֵיְהוָה; the base text reads מֵיְהֹוָה. The English is the same.

### Translation under review (18:15)

English: Sarah denied, saying, "I did not laugh," for she was afraid. He said, "No, but you did laugh."

Literal: and-she-denied saying not I-laughed for she-was-afraid and-he-said no but you-laughed

- 18:15.9 18:15.10 18:15.11 | No, but you did laugh | no but you-laughed

Grammar notes:
- The first כִּי gives the reason for the denial ('for she was afraid'); the second corrects it ('no, but you did laugh'), as Rashi reads the two.
- The wayyiqtol וַתְּכַחֵשׁ is piel, the denial; the two qatal forms (צָחַקְתִּי, יָרֵאָה) are the past of the speech.
- The etnaḥta on יָרֵאָה closes the clause of the reason, and the silluq on צָחָקְתְּ closes the verse on the correction.
- The English 'did laugh' supplies the auxiliary for the second person feminine qatal.

Translator's note: Sarah's denial is a lie told out of fear (Radak); the LORD answers that she laughed. The English keeps both quotations in direct speech.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:15:1` follows: Rashi: the first כִּי means 'for' (הָרִאשׁוֹן ... דְּהָא), giving the reason for the denial: she was afraid. The second means 'but' (אֶלָּא): 'No, but you did laugh'. The English follows both.

### Translation under review (18:16)

English: The men rose from there and looked down toward Sodom, and Abraham walked with them to escort them.

Grammar notes:
- The three wayyiqtol and participle clauses carry the sequence: the men rise, look, and Abraham walks with them.
- The hiphil וַיַּשְׁקִפוּ takes the preposition עַל with פְּנֵי, 'toward the face of Sodom', and the Hebrew uses it of looking down.
- The participle הֹלֵךְ is the progressive: 'was walking'; the infinitive לְשַׁלְּחָם gives the purpose: 'to send them on their way'.
- The silluq on לְשַׁלְּחָם closes the verse on the escort.

Translator's note: Rashi reads the verb of looking down as a look for evil; the English keeps the neutral 'looked down' and records this. Rashi reads לְשַׁלְּחָם as escort, as one who supposes they are guests.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:16:1` records: Rashi: every look (hashqafah) in Scripture is for evil, except the look from the holy dwelling of Deut 26:15, because gifts to the poor turn anger into mercy. Here the men looked down on Sodom with evil intent. The English keeps the neutral 'looked down' and records this.
- `rashi:18:16:2` follows: Rashi: לְשַׁלְּחָם means לְלַוּוֹתָם, to escort them, as one who supposes they are guests (כַּסָּבוּר אוֹרְחִים הֵם). The English reads 'to escort them'.
