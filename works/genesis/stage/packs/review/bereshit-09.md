### Translation under review (2:25)

English: The two of them were naked, the human and his wife, and they were not ashamed.

Literal: and-were two-of-them naked the-human and-his-wife and-not they-were-ashamed

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2:25.1 | and were |  | Qal III | wayyiqtol of הָיָה with a plural subject: 'and they were' |
| 2:25.2 | the two of them |  |  | dual construct of שְׁנַיִם with a 3rd masculine plural suffix: 'the two of them', both together |
| 2:25.3 | naked |  |  | plural of עָרוֹם 'naked', with a doubled מ; a different word from עָרוּם 'shrewd' in 3:1 |
| 2:25.4 | the human |  | 2 | with the article: 'the human' (not the proper name Adam) |
| 2:25.5 | and his wife |  | 2 | אִשְׁתּוֹ is the construct form of אִשָּׁה with the suffix 'his' |
| 2:25.6 | and not |  |  |  |
| 2:25.7 | they were ashamed |  | Hithp | hitpolel of בוש; the imperfect after wayyiqtol gives a continuing state; pausal qamets at the silluq |

Phrases:
- 2:25.1 2:25.2 2:25.3 | The two of them were naked | 'and-were two-of-them naked'; both of them, together, in one state
- 2:25.4 2:25.5 | the human and his wife | 'the-human and-his-wife'; names the two in apposition
- 2:25.6 2:25.7 | and they were not ashamed | 'and-not they-were-ashamed'; Rashi: they did not yet know the way of modesty

Grammar notes:
- שְׁנֵיהֶם is the dual construct of שְׁנַיִם with a suffix, 'the two of them'; הָאָדָם וְאִשְׁתּוֹ then names the two in apposition.
- עֲרוּמִּים 'naked' (the מ is doubled) is a different word from עָרוּם 'shrewd' in 3:1. The sound-play joins the two scenes; Ibn Ezra notes the pair at 3:1.
- וְלֹא יִתְבֹּשָׁשׁוּ is a yiqtol after a wayyiqtol: it describes a continuing state, 'and they were not ashamed', not a single event. The verb is a hitpolel (reflexive or reciprocal) of בוש; Ibn Ezra: from a root with a hidden quiet letter, the last letter doubled, as in יִתְבּוֹנָן (Job 11:11).
- The etnaḥta on וְאִשְׁתּוֹ divides the verse after the naming of the two; the qamets of יִתְבֹּשָׁשׁוּ is pausal, because the word ends the verse (Ibn Ezra).

Translator's note: 'Ashamed' renders the hitpolel; the Hebrew does not say before whom (BDB: ashamed before one another). Rashi and Radak differ on the reason for the absence of shame (no knowledge of modesty and the evil inclination; no sexual desire yet); the English gives the fact, not the reason.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:2:25:1` follows: Rashi: they did not know the way of modesty, to tell good from evil; knowledge had been given to the human to name the animals, but the evil inclination was not given until he ate from the tree. The English states the plain fact of no shame.
- `ibn-ezra:2:23:5` not-applicable: This comment is filed at 2:23 and was matched to וְלֹא only by the dibbur ha-matḥil: it concerns the dagesh in the qof of לֻקֳחָה (2:23), which Ibn Ezra says eases the tongue. The text breaks off and it does not bear on 2:25.
- `ibn-ezra:2:25:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: עֲרוּמִּים is an adjective, as in 'and you strip the clothes of the naked' (Job 22:6). He adds that some take 'ערום ראה רעה' (Prov. 22:3) the same way; the end of the comment is obscure.
- `ibn-ezra:2:25:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: יִתְבֹּשָׁשׁוּ comes from בושת (shame), from the roots with a hidden quiet letter between the two; the last letter is doubled as in יִתְבּוֹנָן. The shin takes qamets because it is the end of the verse.
- `radak:2:25:2` not-applicable: Radak: the verse tells that they had not eaten from the tree of knowledge and had no shame at their uncovered nakedness, since they had not yet had sexual desire. This gives the reason, not a reading of the words.
- apparatus: Onkelos: עַרְטִילָאִין 'naked' and לָא מִתְכַּלְמִין 'not ashamed'; the same sense as the Hebrew.
- apparatus: Sforno: their organs served God's will alone, so the act of union was like eating and drinking; this explains the absence of shame and does not change the words.

Grammar notes:
- וְהַנָּחָשׁ הָיָה עָרוּם is waw + subject + qatal: a background clause that introduces a new character, not a step in the narrative sequence.
- עָרוּם 'shrewd' and עֲרוּמִּים 'naked' (2:25) sound alike; Ibn Ezra takes this as deliberate play on the two senses, as in Judges 15:16 and 10:4. Radak distinguishes them by vowels: shureq for wisdom, holem for 'naked'.
- מִכֹּל after the adjective is the comparative min: 'shrewder than all'. Rashi sets it beside 'cursed above all' (3:14).
- אַף כִּי has no interrogative particle: the question is carried by the elliptical אַף כִּי and by tone. The serpent says אֱלֹהִים and not יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים, the name used by the narrator (Ibn Ezra: they did not know the name).
- The etnaḥta on אֱלֹהִים (3:1.10) ends the narrator's description; a pashta pair on וַיֹּאמֶר opens the speech.

Translator's note: אַף כִּי is difficult. Ibn Ezra reads it as showing that other words came first and ending in an a fortiori ('how much more, that He said you may not eat from any tree'); Rashi reads 'perhaps He said you may not eat from any of them', an exaggeration meant to draw her answer. The English 'Is it really so that' keeps the question and the overstatement and drops 'even/also'. מִכֹּל עֵץ can mean 'from any tree at all' or 'from every tree'; the English keeps 'any'. The serpent is read as a real serpent that speaks (Ibn Ezra, Radak), not as a figure; Sforno's reading of it as the evil inclination is recorded in the apparatus.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:1:1` not-applicable: Rashi: why is this here? It should follow with 'And He made garments of skin' (3:21); it teaches by what counsel the serpent leapt at them: he saw them naked and engaged in intercourse in public, and desired her. A homily on the link to 2:25; the English keeps the link with 'Now'.
- `rashi:3:1:2` follows: Rashi: 'by his shrewdness and greatness was his downfall; shrewd above all, cursed above all' (Gen. Rabbah). ערום is 'shrewd', and מכל is paired with the 'above all' of the curse; the English 'than every'.
- `rashi:3:1:3` follows: Rashi: 'perhaps He told you not to eat from any'; although the serpent had seen them eating other fruit, he overstated so that she would answer him and speak of that tree. The English keeps the question and the overstatement ('any tree').
- `ibn-ezra:3:1:1` follows: Ibn Ezra rejects the readings of the serpent as a sign, an angel or Satan, and holds that the words are as they sound: the serpent spoke and walked upright. 'Shrewd' means wise in contrivance, 'but not like the human'; the play ערום after ערומים is the same kind of wordplay as in Judges 15:16.
- `ibn-ezra:3:1:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: אף כי shows that he said other words first and ended with an a fortiori: He said not to eat of any tree at all. The serpent did not use the divine name because they did not know it; the woman later added 'and you shall not touch it'.
- `radak:3:1:8` follows: Radak: 'אף' means 'in addition': other words passed between the woman and the serpent, and Scripture gives only the end of the talk, as with 'כי' in Joshua 2:24.
- apparatus: Onkelos: חַכִּים 'wise' for עָרוּם, and בְּקוּשְׁטָא 'in truth' for אַף כִּי.
- apparatus: Sforno: the serpent is the evil inclination, which Scripture names after the creature it resembles; Ibn Ezra rejects the view that it was Satan. Radak records the midrash of the serpent ridden by Samael.
- apparatus: Radak (3:1:3) and Ibn Ezra differ on the nature of the serpent's wisdom: Radak says it was the imaginative faculty found in animals, as in the fox, not the intellect; Ibn Ezra says it was a real, if limited, wisdom.

- `radak:3:5:2` follows: Radak: 'like angels', as Ibn Ezra.
- `radak:3:5:3` not-applicable: Radak: the serpent took this phrase 'good and evil' from the woman's earlier words, and it is explained at 2:17.
- apparatus: Onkelos: אֲרֵי גַּלֵּי קֳדָם יְיָ ... וּתְהוֹן כְּרַבְרְבִין חַכִּימִין 'for it is revealed before the LORD ... and you will be like great, wise ones': אֱלֹהִים as 'great ones' (BDB 1.a), 'knowing' as 'wise'.
- apparatus: Sforno: the serpent says God forbade the fruit because He knows you would gain added knowledge, not from fear of death.
- apparatus: Whether יֹדְעֵי goes with 'you' or with 'divine beings' is open; the accents join it with 'you'.

Grammar notes:
- Three כִּי clauses follow וַתֵּרֶא: good for eating, a delight to the eyes, desirable for gaining insight. The third has no כִּי of its own and uses a Niphal participle.
- נֶחְמָד is a Niphal participle, 'to be desired'; לְהַשְׂכִּיל is the Hiphil infinitive of שׂכל, 'to have insight' or 'to make wise': Ibn Ezra and Rashi take it as gaining insight, 'knowing good and evil' (3:5).
- תַאֲוָה־הוּא has the pronoun הוּא as copula: 'it is a delight'. The maqqef joins the two; the accent on הוּא is munaḥ.
- The series of wayyiqtols is quick: she took, ate, gave, and he ate. The husband is the subject of the last verb only.
- The woman is אִשָּׁה and her husband אִישׁ: the pair of 2:23 is used again, now as 'her husband'.
- The accents: the pazer on הָאִשָּׁה, the geresh on לְמַאֲכָל, the darga on וְכִי, and the etnaḥta on וַתֹּאכַל; the tevir on לְאִישָׁהּ.

Translator's note: 'Saw' is kept: the commentators take the seeing as inner, 'in her heart' (Ibn Ezra, Radak), and Rashi as seeing his words; they do not call for another verb in the English. 'Delight' renders תַאֲוָה, a thing desired; the Hebrew has no preposition for 'the eyes' beyond ל. 'To the eyes' is kept, with Ramban's 'he desires with his eyes' in mind. עִמָּהּ can go with the giving ('together with her') or with the husband ('who was with her', Sforno); the English 'with her' keeps both.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:6:1` records: Rashi: she saw the serpent's words, and they pleased her, and she believed him. The 'seeing' is taken as inner perception; the English 'saw' leaves this open.
- `rashi:3:6:2` records: Rashi: 'good' for being like God. The English keeps 'good for eating', the plain sense of לְמַאֲכָל.
- `rashi:3:6:3` records: Rashi: 'a desire to the eyes' as he said to her, 'your eyes will be opened'. The English keeps 'a delight to the eyes'.
- `rashi:3:6:4` follows: Rashi: 'desirable for gaining insight' as he said to her, 'knowing good and evil'. The English 'for gaining insight' keeps the link with 3:5.
- `rashi:3:6:5` not-applicable: Rashi: she gave also to her husband so that she not die and he live and marry another. A homily on her motive.
- `rashi:3:6:6` not-applicable: Rashi: גַּם comes to include the animals and wild creatures. A homily; the English gives 'also' in its plain sense.
- `ibn-ezra:3:6:1` records: Ibn Ezra: 'in her heart'. A real alternative: the seeing is inner. The English 'saw' does not decide.
- `ibn-ezra:3:6:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: desirable 'so that she would gain insight and her eyes would be opened'. The English 'for gaining insight'.
- `ibn-ezra:3:6:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: עִמָּהּ means they ate it together, and she told him the serpent's secret; he was not deceived, and so was punished. He also holds that the tree was not a fig and that 'knowing' is a euphemism for union; the English 'with her' keeps the first.
- `ibn-ezra:3:6:4` not-applicable: Filed at the end of the verse, but the comment is on 'tree of life' (3:22) and the question of death: 'לעולם' does not mean forever. It does not bear on 3:6.
- `ramban:3:6:2` follows: Ramban: 'desire to the eyes': he desires with, and goes after, his eyes. The English 'a delight to the eyes'.
- `sforno:3:6:3` records: Sforno: he was drawn by her words because he was her husband and because he was with her. The English 'with her' keeps this as a possible reading of עִמָּהּ.
- apparatus: Onkelos: אַסֵּי הוּא לְעַיְנִין 'it is healing to the eyes' for תַאֲוָה־הוּא לָעֵינַיִם, and מְרַגֵּג אִילָנָא לְאִסְתַּכָּלָא בֵהּ 'the tree pleases to look at'; a different sense for לְהַשְׂכִּיל.
- apparatus: Radak (3:6:4): she ate twice, he once.
- apparatus: The identity of the tree is not given in the text: Rashi and Radak, a fig; others, wheat or citron; Ibn Ezra, one of two trees in the garden.

### Translation under review (3:7)

English: Then the eyes of the two of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together and made themselves loincloths.

Phrases:

- 3:7.4 3:7.5 3:7.6 3:7.7 | and they knew that they were naked | 'and-knew that naked they'; Rashi: even a blind man knows he is naked

Grammar notes:
- וַתִּפָּקַחְנָה is a wayyiqtol Niphal with the feminine plural ending -נָה, agreeing with the feminine plural עֵינֵי; it is the passive of the serpent's וְנִפְקְחוּ (3:5), and the two pashta accents are a rare pair.
- וַיֵּדְעוּ כִּי introduces the content of what they knew, as כִּי in 3:5 introduced what God knows.
- עֵירֻמִּם הֵם puts the predicate first and adds the pronoun for emphasis: 'naked they were'. The word-play עֲרוּמִּים (2:25), עָרוּם (3:1), עֵירֻמִּם is a chain of near-homonyms.
- The wayyiqtols וַיִּתְפְּרוּ and וַיַּעֲשׂוּ narrate their act quickly; the etnaḥta falls after הֵם.
- חֲגֹרֹת is a plural noun from the root חגר 'to gird': what is bound around the waist.

Translator's note: The text writes עֵירֻמִּם without the second yod, the plural עֵירֻמִּים; Rashi and Radak quote it in the full spelling and the sense is the same. 'Eyes were opened' is kept as the idiom of 3:5; Rashi and Ramban say it speaks of insight, not of actual sight, and the end of the verse ('they knew') shows it. 'Fig leaves' is a collective, 'leaf of fig'. חֲגֹרֹת is rendered 'loincloths' (BDB: girdle, loin-covering, belt).

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:7:1` follows: Rashi: the verse speaks of insight and not of actual sight, and the end of the verse proves it. The English keeps 'eyes were opened' with 'they knew'.
- `rashi:3:7:2` records: Rashi: even a blind man knows he is naked; so what is 'they knew that they were naked'? They had one commandment in their hand and were stripped of it (Gen. Rabbah). The English gives the plain 'knew that they were naked' and leaves this as a reading.
- `rashi:3:7:3` records: Rashi: the fig is the tree they ate from, so they were put right with what ruined them; the other trees kept them from taking leaves. The identity of the tree is not in the English, which says 'fig leaves'.
- `ramban:3:7:1` follows: Ramban, after Rashi: the Scripture speaks of knowledge and not of actual sight; its end proves it, 'and they knew that they were naked', as in 'open my eyes that I may behold wonders' (Ps. 119:18).
- `radak:3:7:2` records: Radak: it says 'they knew' and not 'they saw', since what they now saw was what they had seen before; after eating, desire arose in them and the organ was stirred, so they were ashamed. A reading of what the knowing was.
- `radak:3:7:3` not-applicable: Radak: the word is written with a yod, the long form. A note on spelling; the text here has no second yod.
- `radak:3:7:4` follows: Radak: fig leaves are broad, so they sewed them to one another and made belts to cover their nakedness. This agrees with 'sewed together' and 'loincloths'.
- `sforno:3:7:2` records: Sforno: they knew that the place of nakedness should be covered, since their acts were now aimed at pleasure that is loathsome and harmful. A reading of what they knew.
- apparatus: MAM writes עֵירֻמִּם (defective) where Rashi, Radak and the Targum use the plural עֵירֻמִּים; no change of sense.
- apparatus: Onkelos: טַרְפֵי תְאֵנִין 'fig leaves' and זְרָזִין 'belts' for חֲגֹרֹת; עַרְטִילָאִין 'naked'.
- apparatus: Radak: the Rabbis name the tree a fig, wheat or citron; Ibn Ezra (3:6) thinks the fig is not the tree of knowledge, because the verse would then say 'leaves of the tree of knowledge'.
