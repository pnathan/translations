### Translation under review (10:15)

English: Canaan fathered Sidon, his firstborn, and Heth.

Literal: and-Canaan begot [object-marker] Sidon his-firstborn and-[object-marker] Heth

Phrases:
- 10:15.1 10:15.2 | Canaan fathered | 'and-Canaan begot'; the waw only continues the list of Ham's sons
- 10:15.3 | - | object marker, no English counterpart
- 10:15.4 | Sidon | 'Sidon'
- 10:15.5 | his firstborn | 'his-firstborn'
- 10:15.6 10:15.7 | and Heth | 'and-[object-marker] Heth'

Grammar notes:
- The verb יָלַד, with a man as subject, means 'beget'; the objects are marked by אֶת.
- בְּכֹרוֹ stands in apposition to צִידֹן and is set off by the tipḥa.
- The waw on וּכְנַעַן is a simple connective with the preceding sons of Ham.

Translator's note: The ancestor-name Canaan is also the ethnic name; the English keeps the name. Ramban's discussion of how the ten peoples relate to the land promised to Abraham is a historical comment, not about the words.

Commentary rulings:
- `ramban:10:15:1` not-applicable: Ramban: these are the ten peoples of Canaan given to Abraham; their names changed over time. This concerns the identification of the peoples, not the plain sense of the clause.
- `radak:10:15:3` follows: Radak finds no clear reason for בְּכֹרוֹ here; he suggests Sidon is named for both rank and birth order. The English simply says 'his firstborn'.
- apparatus: Onkelos: וּכְנַעַן אוֹלִיד יָת צִידוֹן בָּכְרֵיהּ וְיָת חֵת, in agreement with the Hebrew.
