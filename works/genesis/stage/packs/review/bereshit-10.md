"אֵלֵךְ אָשׁוּבָה אֶל מְקוֹמִי" (הושע ה טו), וְהוּא עִנְיַן גִּלּוּי שְׁכִינָה בַּמָּקוֹם הַהוּא אוֹ הִסְתַּלְּקוּתוֹ מִן הַמָּקוֹם שֶׁנִּגְלָה בּוֹ. וְטַעַם "לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם", כִּי בְּהִגָּלוֹת הַשְּׁכִינָה תָּבֹא רוּחַ גְּדוֹלָה וְחָזָק, כְּעִנְיָן שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (מלכים א יט יא) "וְהִנֵּה ה' עֹבֵר וְרוּחַ גְּדוֹלָה וְחָזָק מְפָרֵק הָרִים וּמְשַׁבֵּר סְלָעִים לִפְנֵי ה'", וְכֵן "וַיֵּדֶא עַל כַּנְפֵי רוּחַ" (תהלים יח יא), וְכָתוּב בְּאִיּוֹב (איוב לח א) "וַיַּעַן ה' אֶת אִיּוֹב מִן הַסְּעָרָה". וּלְפִיכָךְ אָמַר בְּכָאן כִּי שָׁמְעוּ קוֹל ה' שֶׁנִּתְגַּלָּה הַשְּׁכִינָה בַּגַּן כְּמִתְקָרֵב אֲלֵיהֶם לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם, כִּי רוּחַ ה' נָשְׁבָה בּוֹ בַּגַּן כְּרוּחַ הַיָּמִים, לֹא רוּחַ גְּדוֹלָה וְחָזָק בַּמַּחֲזֶה בִּשְׁאָר הַנְּבוּאוֹת, שֶׁלֹּא יִפְחֲדוּ וְיִבָּהֲלוּ. וְאָמַר Grammar notes:
- וַיִּשְׁמְעוּ: wayyiqtol 3mp of שׁמע. The gershayim (disjunctive) falls on the first word.
- מִתְהַלֵּךְ is a hitpael participle, masculine singular. It agrees with קוֹל ('the sound ... walking') or with יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים; the Hebrew does not say which.
- The etnaḥta on הַיּוֹם closes the first half: what they heard, and when. The second half, וַיִּתְחַבֵּא ..., is the reaction.
- וַיִּתְחַבֵּא is singular, before a compound subject, the man first and his wife second.
- בְּתוֹךְ עֵץ הַגָּן: singular עֵץ in a collective sense, 'among the trees'.

Translator's note: לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם is kept literal. Rashi takes רוּחַ as the direction (west, where the sun is toward evening); Ibn Ezra, Radak and Ramban take it as the wind or breeze of the day, that is, toward evening. 'Breeze of the day' leaves that open; the choice among them is recorded under Rashi's comment. מִתְהַלֵּךְ is also left open between the LORD God and his sound (Ibn Ezra and Ramban: the sound goes; Radak: or the man walks).

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:8:1` not-applicable: Rashi says that there are many aggadic midrashim, already arranged elsewhere, and that he comes only for the plain sense of the verse and for an aggadah that settles the words of the verse.
- `rashi:3:8:2` follows: Rashi: 'What did they hear? They heard the sound of the Holy One, blessed be He, who was walking in the garden.' The English keeps the sound as the thing heard.
- `rashi:3:8:3` records: Rashi: לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם is 'toward that direction where the sun comes', the west, 'for toward evening the sun is in the west'. The English keeps the words 'breeze of the day' and does not choose the direction reading.
- `ibn-ezra:3:8:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the sound is the LORD's, and this was near evening 'when the wind of the day blows' (בעת התנופף רוח היום); the sound can 'walk' as in 'her sound goes like a serpent' (Jer 46:22). The English keeps 'the sound ... walking about'.
- `radak:3:8:5` records: Radak: at evening when the day's wind cools; they knew the sound was God coming to speak to them about their sin, and hid for shame among the trees.
- apparatus: Whether רוּחַ הַיּוֹם is 'wind/breeze of the day' (Ibn Ezra, Radak, Ramban) or 'the western direction' (Rashi).
- apparatus: Whether מִתְהַלֵּךְ qualifies קוֹל or יהוה אלהים (Ibn Ezra, Ramban); Radak records the view that it refers to the man.
- apparatus: The verb שׁמע (Qal) has no sense in the lexicon entry shown in the pack, which lists Niph and Pi only.

Grammar notes:
- The etnaḥta on אָתָּה divides the verse: the first question (who told you), then the second (did you eat).
- הֲמִן־הָעֵץ ... אָכָלְתָּ: the interrogative ה is attached to the fronted phrase, and the verb אָכָלְתָּ is last; the sentence is a yes-or-no question, and Rashi takes it as a question of astonishment (בתמיהה).

Grammar notes:
- The two telisha qetana accents on וַיֹּאמֶר and the zinor on הַנָּחָשׁ mark the introduction to the sentence; the serpent is not asked to answer.
- אָרוּר אַתָּה: a passive participle with a pronoun, as a curse; no copula.
- מִכָּל הַבְּהֵמָה: מִן can be comparative ('more than') or partitive ('from among'); the English takes the first.
- עַל־גְּחֹנְךָ תֵלֵךְ: the phrase comes before the verb, which puts the stress on 'belly'; תֵלֵךְ is a yiqtol of הלך.
- וְעָפָר תֹּאכַל: the object is fronted again, so that the two curses balance, 'on belly ... and dust ...'.
- כָּל־יְמֵי חַיֶּיךָ: an idiom for 'as long as you live'; חַיִּים is a plural noun.

Translator's note: 'Cursed are you more than all the livestock' reads מִן as comparative. Rashi and Radak read it so; the other reading is 'cursed from among all the livestock', separated from them. The serpent's curse is expressed in the rabbis as the loss of legs and shortened life; the Hebrew says only that it goes on its belly.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:14:1` not-applicable: Rashi (Sanhedrin 29): from 'Because you did this' we learn that one does not argue for the seducer; if he had been asked 'why did you do this?', he could have answered 'the words of the master and the words of the pupil, whose should one obey?'.
- `rashi:3:14:2` follows: Rashi: if he was cursed from among the livestock, then from the wild animals all the more so; the rabbis based on this verse in Bekhorot 8 that the serpent's gestation is seven years. The English takes מִן as 'more than'.
- `rashi:3:14:3` follows: Rashi on עַל גְּחֹנְךָ תֵלֵךְ: 'It had legs, and they were cut off.'
- `ibn-ezra:3:14:1` records: Ibn Ezra: some say the curse is that the serpent's life would be short, 'but the right view is that it would be deficient in its going'. The English leaves the curse as the Hebrew has it.
- `ibn-ezra:3:14:2` records: Ibn Ezra: גחון is 'the chest'; it is not known whether the נ is a root letter, as in אדון, or an addition, as in זדון; he rejects the derivation from גיחה 'emerge'. The English has 'belly'.
- apparatus: מִן in מִכָּל: comparative or partitive (Rashi and Radak read it as comparative).
- apparatus: גָּחוֹן: belly or chest (Ibn Ezra).
- apparatus: Radak records the midrash that the serpent once walked upright.

### Translation under review (3:15)

English: I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring. He will strike you on the head, and you will strike him on the heel.

Literal: and-enmity I-will-put between-you and-between the-woman and-between offspring-your and-between offspring-her he he-will-strike-you head and-you you-will-strike-him heel

Grammar notes:
- וְאֵיבָה אָשִׁית: the object is fronted, so that 'enmity' is what God sets; בֵּין is repeated before each term.
- בֵּין ... וּבֵין is repeated for each of the two pairs: you and the woman, your offspring and her offspring.
- זֶרַע is a collective singular, 'offspring', and הוּא is masculine singular: the reading is 'he', a single descendant or each of them; the Hebrew does not say.
- יְשׁוּפְךָ and תְּשׁוּפֶנּוּ use the same verb שׁוּף; the likeness of the words is deliberate, and the English uses 'strike' twice.
- רֹאשׁ and עָקֵב are accusatives of the part affected, with no preposition.

Translator's note: שׁוּף appears twice, with the same form, and 'strike' is used for both so that the echo is not lost; the lexicon gives 'bruise', Rashi 'crush' for the head and (by wordplay with נשף) 'bite' for the heel, Radak 'a blow and a crushing'. 'He' is kept for הוּא, for the collective offspring (cf. Sforno: it is not between Adam and Eve only). The paragraph break (ס) follows the verse.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:15:1` follows: Rashi: 'You intended only that Adam should die when he ate, and that you would take Eve ... therefore I will put enmity.' The English 'enmity' states what is set.
- `rashi:3:15:2` follows: Rashi: יְשׁוּפְךָ means 'will crush you', like 'and I crushed it' (Dt 9:21); Onkelos translates וְשָׁפִית יָתֵי. The English 'strike' covers a blow, and Rashi's 'crush' is recorded in the note.
- `rashi:3:15:3` follows: Rashi: you will have no stature and will bite him on his heel, and from there kill him; the word תְּשׁוּפֶנּוּ is from נָשַׁף ('he blew on them', Is 40:24): the serpent when it bites hisses, and as the language falls upon the language, the same form is written for both.
- `ibn-ezra:3:15:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'He will strike you on the head' means that her offspring will strike the head, as in 'who bruises me with a hair' (Job 9:17); the preposition is omitted, as in 'the house of the LORD' (2 Kings 12:17). The English supplies 'on'.
- `ibn-ezra:3:15:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: עָקֵב is the foot; if the head is first, this is last, as in 'and their heel in the water' (Josh 3:13). The English has 'heel'.
- `radak:3:15:1` follows: Radak: you showed that you loved her; I will turn love into enmity.
- `sforno:3:15:2` records: Sforno: the enmity will not be between Adam and Eve only.
- apparatus: הוּא: 'he' of a single descendant or of the offspring as a group (Sforno: not between Adam and Eve only).
- apparatus: שׁוּף: strike (Ibn Ezra, Radak), crush (Rashi on the head), bite (Rashi on the heel); Rashi derives the second from נשׁף 'blow, hiss'.
- apparatus: Radak ends the enmity in the days of the messiah (Is 11:8).
