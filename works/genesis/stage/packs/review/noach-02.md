Grammar notes:
- קֵץ כׇּל־בָּשָׂר is a construct chain: 'the end of all flesh'; the pashta on בָּשָׂר closes the subject.
- The perfect בָּא ('has come') and מָלְאָה ('is full') state the present result of what has happened.
- וְהִנְנִי with the participle מַשְׁחִיתָם is the idiom of imminent action: 'and behold I am about to destroy'.
- אֶת־הָאָרֶץ: אֶת is the usual object marker, but the commentators read it as 'from' or 'with' because the object of מַשְׁחִית is already -ם.
- The etnaḥta on מִפְּנֵיהֶם divides the verse: reason for the decree, then the decree itself.

Translator's note: אֶת־הָאָרֶץ: Rashi first reads אֶת as 'from the earth' (like כְּצֵאתִי אֶת הָעִיר), and then, as a second explanation, as 'with the earth', so that the earth too is ruined. Ibn Ezra lists 'from' and 'with' but prefers that מַשְׁחִיתָם does double duty: 'I am destroying them and destroying the earth'. The English takes 'with', the reading the two share; the other readings are recorded. The pack's parse gives אֶת as a preposition, which agrees. חָמָס is the word on which Rashi (Sanhedrin 108) says the decree was sealed.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:13:1` not-applicable: Rashi: wherever one finds sexual immorality and idolatry, a destroying force (androlomusia) comes into the world and kills good and wicked alike. A homily on 'the end of all flesh'; it does not change the plain sense.
- `rashi:6:13:2` not-applicable: Rashi, citing Sanhedrin 108: their decree was sealed only because of robbery (גזל). A homily on the weight of חָמָס; it does not bear on the English.
- `rashi:6:13:3` follows: Rashi: אֶת means 'from the earth', like כְּצֵאתִי אֶת הָעִיר and חָלָה אֶת רַגְלָיו; another explanation: 'with the earth', since even three handbreadths of plowed depth were dissolved. The English adopts the second explanation, 'with'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:13:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: בָּשָׂר means 'the body' (הוּא הַגּוּף). The English 'flesh' is used for the living creature as a bodily being.
- `ibn-ezra:6:13:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: בָּא means 'arrived': the end of their calamity (הִגִּיעַ קֵץ אֵידָם) has arrived. The English 'has come' is the same.
- `ibn-ezra:6:13:3` records: Ibn Ezra: אֶת is 'from the earth' or 'with', like אֶת יַעֲקֹב אִישׁ וּבֵיתוֹ, but he prefers that מַשְׁחִיתָם serves two objects: 'I am destroying them, and destroying the earth'. The English follows the 'with' option; his preferred construction is recorded.
- apparatus: Ibn Ezra's preferred reading: 'I am about to destroy them and to destroy the earth' (מַשְׁחִית has a second, implied object).
- apparatus: Rashi's first reading of אֶת: 'from the earth', that is, I will destroy them out of the earth.
- apparatus: The Targum Onkelos renders 'with the earth' (עִם אַרְעָא), agreeing with 'with'.

Grammar notes:
- The two verbs עֲשֵׂה (imperative) and תַּעֲשֶׂה (imperfect) repeat 'make', the second adding what the ark is to have.

- מִבַּיִת וּמִחוּץ: 'from the inside and from the outside', an idiom for 'inside and out'.

Grammar notes:
- צֹהַר תַּעֲשֶׂה לַתֵּבָה puts the object first: 'a skylight you shall make for the ark'; the ׀ (paseq) after צֹהַר marks a pause.
- וְאֶל־אַמָּה תְּכַלֶּנָּה מִלְמַעְלָה: 'and to a cubit you shall finish it from above'; the sense is that the roof ends in a single cubit.
- The adjectives תַּחְתִּיִּם שְׁנִיִּם וּשְׁלִשִׁים are used as nouns for the three storeys: 'lower, second, third'.
- The tevir on תַּחְתִּיִּם and the tipḥa on וּשְׁלִשִׁים divide the three storeys from the verb.
- The final verb תַּעֲשֶׂהָ has a feminine object suffix: 'you shall make it (the ark)'.

Translator's note: צֹהַר: the pack gives BDB 'roof'; Rashi says a window or a precious stone that shone for them (Bereshit Rabbah); Ibn Ezra: a place by which light enters, derived from צָהֳרַיִם, made at the top. The English 'skylight' keeps the two commentators' 'light opening at the top' and does not decide between window and gem; the roof sense is recorded. וְאֶל־אַמָּה תְּכַלֶּנָּה מִלְמַעְלָה is hard: Rashi takes the roof as sloping up until it is only a cubit wide at the top, so that water runs off; Ibn Ezra takes the top as a cubit long and a sixth of a cubit wide. The English keeps the sense 'finish off the top to a cubit'. The pack notes that the WLC reads תְּכַלֶנָּה, without the dagesh.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:16:1` follows: Rashi: some say a window and some a precious stone that gave them light. The English 'skylight' follows the first and records the second.
- `rashi:6:16:2` follows: Rashi: its roof was sloping and rose until it was narrow at the top, standing on one cubit, so that the water runs off on both sides. The English 'finish it off to a cubit at the top'.
- `rashi:6:16:3` follows: Rashi: set the door in the side so that the rains would not fall into it. The English 'in its side'.
- `rashi:6:16:4` follows: Rashi: three decks, one above another: the upper for people, the middle for dwelling, the lower for waste. The English 'lower, second, and third stories'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:16:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: צֹהַר is a place by which light enters, from צָהֳרַיִם, made at the top; the ark is like a triangle with a sharp top, so that it does not turn over; the door is in one side and reached by a ladder. The English 'skylight'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:16:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: תַּחְתִּיִּם is an adjective of the noun, and so שְׁנִיִּם and שְׁלִשִׁים; he answers those who ask why the text does not say שְׁלִישִׁיִּים. The English reads the three words as 'lower, second and third'.
- apparatus: צֹהַר: roof (BDB), window (Rashi, Radak), a gem (Rashi, Radak), an opening for light (Ibn Ezra).
- apparatus: Rashi: וְאֶל־אַמָּה is the roof narrowing to a cubit. Ibn Ezra: the ark is a triangle in section, with its top a cubit long.
- apparatus: Radak on תַּחְתִּיִּים: the yod is the yod of relation, and the yod of the plural has been dropped, like עֲבָדִים נָכְרִים.
- apparatus: Sforno: תַּחְתִּיִּם שְׁנִיִּם וּשְׁלִשִׁים means those that are used to ships (הַמּוּרְגָּלִים בָּאֳנִיּוֹת), that is, ships' decks.

Grammar notes:
- וַאֲנִי הִנְנִי: the pronoun וַאֲנִי is emphatic, with הִנְנִי and a participle for what is about to happen.
- הַמַּבּוּל מַיִם: the second word explains the first; Ibn Ezra reads 'the flood, (a flood of) waters'.
- The infinitive לְשַׁחֵת (piel) gives the purpose of the flood.
- The relative clause אֲשֶׁר־בּוֹ רוּחַ חַיִּים describes the creatures meant by 'all flesh'.
- כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־בָּאָרֶץ יִגְוָע: the verse closes on the single verb 'shall expire'.

Translator's note: וַאֲנִי: Rashi hears God agreeing with those who urged him (Psalm 8:5); Ramban reads 'I also', as in וְיִסַּרְתִּי אֶתְכֶם אַף אָנִי. The English keeps the emphasis ('As for me') without choosing. מַבּוּל is the word for the flood; Rashi and Ibn Ezra give derivations (from בלל, בלה, נבל) but the sense is 'flood'. Radak (6:17:4) says כֹּל אֲשֶׁר בָּאָרֶץ means on dry land, not fish.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:17:1` not-applicable: Rashi: 'I am ready to agree with those who urged me and said, What is man that You remember him (Ps 8)'. A homily on the emphatic וַאֲנִי; it does not alter the plain sense.
- `rashi:6:17:2` follows: Rashi: מַבּוּל is from the sense of destroying (בִּלָּה), confusing (בִּלְבֵּל), or carrying (הוֹבִיל) all from the high to the low; Onkelos renders טוֹפָנָא, flooding everything. The English 'the flood'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the dagesh in the bet of מַבּוּל stands for an assimilated nun, as in מַבּוּעַ, from אָבְלָה נָבְלָה הָאָרֶץ; others derive it from בְּלוּלָה בַּשֶּׁמֶן. The English 'flood'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:17:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: הַמַּבּוּל מַיִם is a short way of saying 'the flood, a flood of waters', as in לְמִן הַיּוֹם הִוָּסְדָה. The English 'the flood of waters'.
- apparatus: Ramban on וַאֲנִי: 'I too', as in וְיִסַּרְתִּי אֶתְכֶם אַף אָנִי; the flood is God's own act against those who ruined the earth.
- apparatus: Radak on הַמַּבּוּל מַיִם: the construct is understood (מַבּוּל מַיִם), and מַבּוּל is a descriptive name for whatever falls from heaven.
- apparatus: Radak on כֹּל אֲשֶׁר בָּאָרֶץ: on dry land; fish are not included (the creatures of the sea did not share the wicked way, on the view of the Sages).

Grammar notes:
- וַהֲקִמֹתִי is a weqatal verb continuing the speech; the vav is not 'and' in the English, and the verb is future.
- The suffix of אִתָּךְ is written with a final ךְ and a qamets: this is the pausal form of 'with you' (masculine) before the etnaḥta and silluq.
- הֲקִמֹתִי אֶת־בְּרִיתִי: the hifil of קוּם takes בְּרִית with the sense of 'confirm, carry out' a covenant.
- The list אַתָּה וּבָנֶיךָ וְאִשְׁתְּךָ וּנְשֵׁי־בָנֶיךָ names the eight persons: men before women, as the commentators note.
- The zaqef gadol on אַתָּה begins the list of those who enter.

Translator's note: הֲקִמֹתִי אֶת־בְּרִיתִי: the first mention of בְּרִית in the Torah. Ibn Ezra reads it as an oath that Noah and his sons would not die in the flood and gives a second reading in which the covenant alludes to the rainbow; Ramban takes the plain sense as a covenant of survival (the commitment to bring them safely through). The English keeps 'covenant' without deciding. Rashi, citing Bereshit Rabbah, takes it as a pledge for the food not to spoil and for Noah's safety against the wicked.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:18:1` records: Rashi (Bereshit Rabbah): a covenant was needed so that the food would not rot or spoil and that the wicked of the generation would not kill him. A reading of what the covenant covers; the English leaves the content open and records this.
- `rashi:6:18:2` not-applicable: Rashi: 'the men by themselves and the women by themselves'; from this they were forbidden marital relations in the ark. A homily on the order of the list.
- `ibn-ezra:6:18:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the sign that God swore to Noah that he and his sons would not die in the flood, though not stated before; or the covenant refers to the rainbow; בְּרִית is an agreement, from בְּרוּ לָכֶם אִישׁ. The English keeps 'covenant' and records his specific readings.
- apparatus: Ramban: the plain sense is a covenant of survival: that Noah and his household and two of every kind would live and come out alive.
- apparatus: Sforno: the covenant is made after the flood (אַחַר הַמַּבּוּל).
- apparatus: Radak: the covenant that I make with you, that you and all who are with you will be saved from the waters, I will confirm for you.

### Translation under review (6:19)

English: And of every living thing, of all flesh, you shall bring two of each into the ark to keep them alive with you; they shall be male and female.

Grammar notes:
- The verse begins with two phrases in a row, וּמִכׇּל־הָחַי מִכׇּל־בָּשָׂר: the general term, and its explanation.
- שְׁנַיִם מִכֹּל 'two from each' is the distributive: two of every kind.
- The verb תָּבִיא is a hifil imperfect: Noah must bring them in; the infinitive לְהַחֲיֹת gives the purpose.
- זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה יִהְיוּ: a plural verb for a pair; the subject is the 'two'.
- The telisha gedola on הָחַי and the gershayim on בָּשָׂר divide the opening phrase.

Translator's note: The verb in יִהְיוּ is plural, agreeing with שְׁנַיִם. לְהַחֲיֹת אִתָּךְ: the object is implied: the animals are kept alive with Noah, and the English supplies 'them'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:19:1` not-applicable: Rashi (Bereshit Rabbah): 'even demons'. A homily on the scope of 'every living thing'.
- `rashi:6:19:2` follows: Rashi: from the smallest of them, not fewer than two, one male and one female. The English 'two of each'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:19:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: after saying 'two' it explains that they are male and female; after the general 'all the living, all flesh' it lists birds, animals and creeping things; fish too are 'flesh'. The English 'every living thing, all flesh ... male and female'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:19:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: תָּבִיא is a command to Noah not to leave them but to bring them with him, to keep alive the seed of every kind. The English 'you shall bring'.
- apparatus: Ramban: it is a miracle that the ark held them; it was made large so that people would see it and repent.
- apparatus: Radak on תָּבִיא: once they come of themselves in front of the ark, Noah brings them in; on לְהַחֲיֹת: Noah provides their food day by day.
- apparatus: Onkelos: 'two of all you shall bring into the ark, to be kept alive with you; male and female they shall be'.

### Translation under review (6:20)

English: Of the birds according to their kind, and of the animals according to their kind, of every creeping thing of the ground according to its kind, two of each will come to you to be kept alive.

Literal: from-the-bird to-its-kind and-from-the-animal to-its-kind from-all creeping-thing-of the-ground to-its-kind two from-all they-shall-come to-you to-keep-alive

Phrases:
- 6:20.1 6:20.2 | Of the birds according to their kind | 'from-the-bird to-its-kind': collective singular rendered in the plural
- 6:20.3 6:20.4 6:20.5 | and of the animals according to their kind | 'and-from-the-animal to-its-kind'
- 6:20.6 6:20.7 6:20.8 6:20.9 | of every creeping thing of the ground according to its kind | 'from-all creeping-thing-of the-ground to-its-kind'
- 6:20.10 6:20.11 6:20.12 6:20.13 6:20.14 | two of each will come to you to be kept alive | 'two from-all they-shall-come to-you to-keep-alive'

Grammar notes:
- The three classes are introduced with מִן: birds, animals, creeping things; each takes לְמִינוֹ.
- עוֹף, בְּהֵמָה and רֶמֶשׂ are collectives in the singular; the suffixes of לְמִינֵהוּ and לְמִינָהּ agree with them.
- שְׁנַיִם מִכֹּל יָבֹאוּ אֵלֶיךָ: the verb is now 'come' (qal) where verse 19 had 'bring' (hifil): they come to him.
- The infinitive לְהַחֲיוֹת is hifil: 'to keep alive'.
- The verse picks up the list that verse 19 summarised with the 'all flesh' phrase.

Translator's note: The English reads לְהַחֲיוֹת as 'to be kept alive' (the animals are the ones who survive). The hifil is active, 'to preserve life', and the agent could be Noah (as in verse 19, 'with you'); the Hebrew is not explicit. The English gives the plural 'their kind' for the first two phrases and 'its kind' for the third; the Hebrew has the singular suffix each time.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:20:1` follows: Rashi (Bereshit Rabbah): those that clung to their own kinds and did not corrupt their way came of themselves; and whatever the ark took in he brought in. The English 'will come to you'.
- `ibn-ezra:6:20:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: יָבֹאוּ אֵלֶיךָ means that they will come of themselves, and he will not have to search for them on the islands or hunt every kind of bird; he is to prepare food for all. The English 'will come to you'.
- apparatus: Ramban: the animals come of themselves two by two, but those taken for sacrifice (seven of each clean kind) Noah took himself.
- apparatus: Radak: הַבְּהֵמָה includes the wild animals, since the two classes overlap.
- apparatus: Onkelos: 'from the fowl according to its kind and from the cattle according to its kind and from all the creeping things of the earth according to its kind, two of each will come to you to be kept alive'.
