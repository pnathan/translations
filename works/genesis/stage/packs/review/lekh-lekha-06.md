### Translation under review (14:1)

English: It happened in the days of Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Ellasar, Chedorlaomer king of Elam, and Tidal king of Goiim,

Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי with a time phrase begins a long sentence whose subject-verb comes in verse 2 (עָשׂוּ מִלְחָמָה).
- Each king is joined to his land by a construct מֶלֶךְ־; the etnaḥta on אֶלָּסָר and zaqef on שִׁנְעָר/עֵילָם group the four kings in pairs.
- The list is asyndetic until the last member, which takes וְ.

Translator's note: גּוֹיִם is kept as a place name, 'Goiim', following Rashi and the first reading of Ibn Ezra; the reading 'king of nations' is recorded. The names are transliterated without comment on their meaning.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:14:1:1` not-applicable: Rashi, after Genesis Rabbah, identifies Amraphel with Nimrod, who said to Abraham 'fall (פֹּל) into the fiery furnace'; a homiletic identification that does not bear on the plain translation.
- `rashi:14:1:2` follows: Rashi: there is a place named Goiim because people gathered there from many nations and made a man named Tidal king over them. The English takes Goiim as a place name.
- `ibn-ezra:14:1:1` records: Ibn Ezra: Goiim is the name of a province like Elam, or else 'king of nations' whose names the text does not list, being others than the Shinarites and their allies. The first reading is followed; the second is kept as an alternative.
- apparatus: The word גּוֹיִם can also be read as the common noun 'nations' (Ibn Ezra's second reading).

Grammar notes:
- בָּא is singular because Chedorlaomer is the chief actor; וְהַמְּלָכִים is added after him, then the plural wayyiqtol וַיַּכּוּ takes over.
- Three peoples are struck at three places, each with אֵת + people and בְּ + place.
- The zaqef on אִתּוֹ ends the account of who came, and the etnaḥta on בָּהָם ends the second pair.
- הָרְפָאִים, הַזּוּזִים and הָאֵימִים are gentilic names of ancient peoples; הָאֵימִים also means 'terrors'.

Translator's note: The ancient peoples are named by transliteration: Rephaim, Zuzim, Emim. The English 'In the fourteenth year' follows the Hebrew order; 'came' is singular because the sentence starts with Chedorlaomer as the leader.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:14:5:1` not-applicable: Rashi on the fourteenth year: 'for their rebelling'; comment on the cause, not on the words.
- `rashi:14:5:2` not-applicable: Rashi: Chedorlaomer came first because he was the master of the affair, 'he entered into the thick of the beam'; a homiletic remark.
- `rashi:14:5:3` follows: Rashi on וְהַמְּלָכִים: 'these three kings' who were with him; the English has 'the kings who were with him'.
- `rashi:14:5:4` records: Rashi: הַזּוּזִים are the Zamzummim (זַמְזֻמִּים); an identification recorded, the English transliterates Zuzim.
- `ibn-ezra:14:5:1` records: Ibn Ezra: everyone who saw the Rephaim would have his heart die and think of the Rephaim as dead; he derives the name from weakness/death. An etymology, recorded.
- `ibn-ezra:14:5:2` records: Ibn Ezra: the Emim are so called because there is terror (אֵימָה) from them; an etymology, recorded.

Grammar notes:
- הַחֹרִי is a singular gentilic with the article used as a collective, 'the Horite(s)'.
- בְּהַרְרָם has a doubled resh; Rashi 'their mountain', Ibn Ezra a different reading (see commentary).
- The yetiv on עַד and the zaqef on פָּארָן bound the geographical phrase.
- עַל־הַמִּדְבָּר: 'on' the wilderness means 'beside' it.

Translator's note: אֵיל פָּארָן is kept as the proper name El-paran; the lexicon gives 'terebinth of Paran', and Rashi argues that אֵיל is part of a name, not 'plain'. עַל rendered 'by' following Rashi.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:14:6:1` follows: Rashi on בְּהַרְרָם: 'in their mountain'; the English reads 'in their mountain'.
- `rashi:14:6:2` follows: Rashi: the Targum renders אֵיל as 'plain', but Rashi thinks אֵיל is not 'plain'; it is the name of the plain of Paran, as Elonei is that of Mamre and Kikkar of the Jordan. The English keeps El-paran as a name.
- `rashi:14:6:3` follows: Rashi: עַל הַמִּדְבָּר means 'beside the wilderness', like 'and beside him the tribe of Manasseh' (Num 2); the English has 'by the wilderness'.
- `ibn-ezra:14:6:1` records: Ibn Ezra: בְּהַרְרָם is from the intensive stem, like בְּדַבְּרָם (Exod 7:7), meaning 'when they went up to Mount Seir'; recorded as an alternative to 'their mountain'.
