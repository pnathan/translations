Grammar notes:
- הֵן is a particle of attention ('look!') opening Cain's complaint.
- The word פָּנִים 'face' occurs twice: 'the face of the ground' (surface) and 'your face' (presence): a repetition that makes Cain's loss of ground also a loss of God's presence.
- נָע וָנָד is a pair of participles of similar sound and sense: 'wavering and wandering'.
- The three weqatal verbs (וְהָיִיתִי, וְהָיָה) continue a future or consequential sense after the yiqtol אֶסָּתֵר.
- The etnaḥta falls on אֶסָּתֵר and divides the verse into two halves: the loss of the ground and presence, and the danger that follows.

Translator's note: 'And from your face I will be hidden' is kept close to the Hebrew; Ibn Ezra reads it as meaning that there is a place in which God's power is seen, though all is full of his glory, which means that Cain's hiding is not from God's presence everywhere but from the place of his manifest favour. נָע is rendered 'restless' (lit. 'wavering, tottering') and נָד 'wandering'.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:4:14:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'ומפניך אסתר' means that there is a place that receives power and in which the might of God is seen, even though everything is full of his glory; the English keeps 'from your presence' and does not say that God is absent everywhere.
- `radak:4:14:2` records: Radak: God was angry and hid his face and providence from Cain, leaving him to everyone; he cites Bereshit Rabbah, 'Yesterday you drove out my father and today me; shall I be hidden from you?'
- apparatus: Onkelos renders 'from before you it is not possible to hide', taking the verb as a question or an impossibility; the English follows the plain Hebrew assertion.

- לָכֵן כָּל־הֹרֵג קַיִן is an incomplete threat: the speech states the condition and leaves the penalty to be understood (Rashi: an expression of rebuke).
- שִׁבְעָתַיִם is a dual of 'seven': 'seven times over'; Ibn Ezra insists that it means seven generations and not fourteen or 343.
- יֻקָּם is Hophal, passive: 'it will be avenged'; the subject is not stated, so who is avenged is not fixed by the grammar.
- לְבִלְתִּי with the infinitive הַכּוֹת expresses purpose: 'so that [one] not strike'; כׇּל־מֹצְאוֹ is 'anyone who finds him' (lit. 'all of the finder of him').
- The mark (אוֹת) is not described: Ibn Ezra says the text does not reveal it.

Translator's note: The English follows Rashi and Ibn Ezra, who agree that שִׁבְעָתַיִם means seven generations (Rashi: God postpones his vengeance on Cain until the seventh generation, when Lamech kills him); the English leaves the vengeance impersonal, as the Hebrew does. The reading that the killer of Cain will suffer sevenfold vengeance is the common one and is kept here as the alternative, because the Hebrew grammar does not exclude it.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:15:1` follows: Rashi: this is one of the verses that is abbreviated and hints without explaining; 'לכן כל הורג קין' is an expression of rebuke, meaning 'thus shall be done to him', 'so and so is his punishment', and the punishment is not stated.
- `rashi:4:15:2` follows: Rashi: 'I do not wish to take vengeance on Cain now; at the end of seven generations I will take my vengeance from him', since Lamech, one of his descendants, will kill him; the first part of the verse is a rebuke so that no creature should harm him.
- `rashi:4:15:3` follows: Rashi: God engraved for him a letter of his name on his forehead; another reading: Cain feared beasts and animals, since there were no other people beside his parents.
- `ibn-ezra:4:15:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: שִׁבְעָתַיִם means 'until seven generations', since the word does not mean fourteen or 343; he cites 'and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold' (Isaiah 30:26) which the prophet explains as 'like the light of seven days'.
- `ibn-ezra:4:15:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: יֻקָּם means that his vengeance will be taken; God was patient with Cain until seven generations; he gives alternatives about the sign (a horn, strength in his heart) and prefers that God made a sign until Cain believed, which the text does not reveal.
- apparatus: Rashi mentions a variant understanding (ס"א) that 'whoever finds me' refers to animals.
- apparatus: The common alternative: whoever kills Cain will be avenged sevenfold (the killer suffers sevenfold vengeance).

Grammar notes:
- וַיֵּדַע 'knew' is a standard idiom for marital relations; it is used in 4:1 of Adam and Eve.
- The two feminine wayyiqtols וַתַּהַר וַתֵּלֶד narrate conception and birth in sequence.
- וַיְהִי בֹּנֶה עִיר: 'he was building a city', a wayyiqtol of 'be' with a participle, giving a continuing action.
- The subject of 'was building' and 'called' is Cain according to Rashi; Ibn Ezra takes the city's name as 'called Enoch'.
- כְּשֵׁם בְּנוֹ is 'like the name of his son': the city was named for his son.

Translator's note: The subject of בֹּנֶה is Cain (Rashi: the city was named in memory of his son Enoch); the Hebrew leaves open whether Cain or Enoch is the builder, and the English keeps 'He'. Ibn Ezra compares Samaria, named for Shemer.

Grammar notes:

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:20:1` follows: Rashi: he was the first of the herdsmen of animals in the deserts, a dweller of tents who moved his tent when the pasture ran out; a midrash reads 'builder of temples for idol worship', as 'the image of jealousy that provokes jealousy' (Ezekiel 8).
- `ibn-ezra:4:20:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: אבי means 'first', like אב and אביב; the word is bound only with yod, and 'his brother' is אחיו.
