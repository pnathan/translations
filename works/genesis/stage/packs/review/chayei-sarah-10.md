### Translation under review (24:49)

English: And now, if you are dealing kindly and faithfully with my lord, tell me; and if not, tell me, and I will turn to the right or to the left."

Grammar notes:
- The etnaḥta on לִי (24:49.10) divides the verse: the first conditional, with its request, ends there, and the second begins with וְאִם.
- The first conditional has no verb of its own apart from the imperative הַגִּידוּ; the second clause repeats that imperative, so the English repeats 'tell me'.
- יֶשְׁכֶם is יֵשׁ with the suffix ־כֶם: 'there are to you', which introduces the participle עֹשִׂים. The participle gives a continuing practice.
- This segment closes the servant's speech, opened in an earlier unit: the closing quotation mark is at the end of the verse.

Translator's note: The servant asks the family to decide now between a yes and a no, and he will go to the right or the left either way. The English keeps the two alternatives parallel as the Hebrew does.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:24:49:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: חסד here is 'a thing that is not obligatory' (דבר שאינו חיוב): the servant asks for a kindness that is freely done, not one the family owes. The English 'kindly' keeps the sense of the word; the point about obligation is recorded here.
- `ibn-ezra:24:49:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: ואמת is there to carry out the matter of kindness (לקיים דבר החסד). The word comes from the root אמן, and its ת is feminine. The English takes it as faithfulness.
- `rashi:24:49:1` declines: Rashi: 'on the right' stands for the daughters of Ishmael. The words give directions, not a people, so the English does not adopt this.
- `rashi:24:49:2` declines: Rashi: 'on the left' stands for the daughters of Lot, who sat at Abraham's left. The words do not say this; the English does not adopt it.
