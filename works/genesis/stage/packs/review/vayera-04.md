### Translation under review (18:25)

English: Far be it from you to do such a thing, to kill the righteous with the wicked, so that the righteous would be like the wicked. Far be it from you! Will not the Judge of all the earth do justice?

Phrases:
- 18:25.1 18:25.2 18:25.3 18:25.4 18:25.5 | Far be it from you to do such a thing | 'far-be-it from-you from-doing like-the-matter this': the infinitive construct with מ and the noun with כ, rendered 'such a thing'.
- 18:25.6 18:25.7 | to kill the righteous | 'to-kill righteous': the Hifil infinitive and its object, the righteous as a class.
- 18:25.8 18:25.9 | with the wicked | 'with wicked': the preposition 'with', here 'together with'.
- 18:25.10 18:25.11 18:25.12 | so that the righteous would be like the wicked | 'and-would-be like-righteous like-wicked': the weqatal gives the consequence of the two infinitives.
- 18:25.13 18:25.14 | Far be it from you | 'far-be-it to-you': the second exclamation.
- 18:25.15 18:25.16 18:25.17 18:25.18 18:25.19 18:25.20 | Will not the Judge of all the earth do justice | 'will-the-judge all-of the-earth not will-do justice': the rhetorical question, with the interrogative he on the participle.

Grammar notes:
- The two infinitive constructs, מֵעֲשֹׂת and לְהָמִית, depend on חָלִילָה לְךָ: 'far be it from you to do ... to kill'. Each carries its own preposition (מ, ל).
- וְהָיָה (18:25.10) is a weqatal after the infinitives. It states the result of the act: the righteous would become like the wicked.
- The question הֲשֹׁפֵט כָּל־הָאָרֶץ (18:25.15) takes the interrogative he. The English puts 'Will not' at the front to make the question clear.
- The etnaḥta on כָּרָשָׁע (18:25.12) divides the verse: the first part is the proposal, the second the appeal to God's justice. The silluq on מִשְׁפָּט closes it.

Translator's note: Both occurrences of חָלִילָה are rendered 'Far be it'. The second opens the appeal, and the English keeps it as a separate sentence.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:18:25:1` follows: Rashi glosses חָלִילָה לְךָ as חֻלִּין הוּא לְךָ, 'it is profane (not holy) for you'. He adds that people would say God's craft is to sweep away all, righteous and wicked alike, as in the flood and the dispersal. The English adopts the sense 'far be it'; the charge itself is not in the English.
- `rashi:18:25:2` follows: Rashi: כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה means 'not this, and not anything like it' (לֹא הוּא וְלֹא כַּיּוֹצֵא בוֹ). The English 'such a thing' follows this.
- `rashi:18:25:3` records: Rashi refers the second חָלִילָה לָּךְ to the world to come (לָעוֹלָם הַבָּא). The English does not adopt this temporal reference; it is recorded here.
- `rashi:18:25:4` follows: Rashi: the he of הֲשֹׁפֵט takes a hataf patah and gives the clause an interrogative tone: 'וְכִי מִי שֶׁהוּא שׁוֹפֵט לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה מִשְׁפַּט אֱמֶת', is it possible that a judge would not do true justice? The English is a question.
- `ibn-ezra:18:25:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: דָּבָר שֶׁלֹּא יִתָּכֵן, a thing that cannot be; some take the word as profane, with nothing in it. The English 'far be it' carries the impossibility.
- `ibn-ezra:18:25:2` records: Ibn Ezra reads כַצַּדִּיק כָּרָשָׁע as a short form of 'the righteous like the wicked and the wicked like the righteous', citing Gen 44:18 and Ps 139:12. Radak takes the same two-way sense. The English gives the one-way reading, which the Hebrew also allows; the reciprocal is recorded here.
- apparatus: Ibn Ezra's reciprocal reading of כַצַּדִּיק כָּרָשָׁע (18:25.11-12) is the chief variant of this verse.

- `rashi:18:27:2` records: Rashi: 'I deserved already to be dust at the hands of kings and ashes at the hands of Nimrod, but for your mercy which stood for me.' The English gives the plain abasement; Rashi's history is recorded here.

- `ibn-ezra:18:27:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'dust and ashes' means I was dust and I shall return to ashes; the chief point is the foundation of the body, the bones. The English 'dust and ashes' follows this plain sense.
