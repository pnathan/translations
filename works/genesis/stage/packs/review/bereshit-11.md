### Translation under review (3:16)

English: To the woman he said, “I will greatly multiply your pain and your pregnancy; in pain you will bear children. Your desire is for your husband, and he will rule over you.”

Phrases:
- 3:16.1 3:16.2 3:16.3 | To the woman he said | 'to the-woman he-said'; the object is placed first, so the woman is addressed directly.
- 3:16.4 3:16.5 | I will greatly multiply | 'multiplying I-will-multiply'; infinitive absolute with finite verb, rendered by 'greatly'.
- 3:16.6 3:16.7 | your pain and your pregnancy | 'your-pain and-your-pregnancy'; the two nouns may be a pair or one idea (Rashi and Ibn Ezra take them as two).
- 3:16.8 3:16.9 3:16.10 | in pain you will bear children | 'in-pain you-will-bear sons'; בָּנִים is 'children' (Rashi, Radak: also the pain of raising them).
- 3:16.11 3:16.12 3:16.13 | Your desire is for your husband | 'and-toward your-husband your-desire'; a verbless clause, the English supplies 'is'.
- 3:16.14 3:16.15 3:16.16 | and he will rule over you | 'and-he he-will-rule over-you'.

Grammar notes:
- הַרְבָּה אַרְבֶּה: the infinitive absolute before a finite verb of the same root strengthens it (Ibn Ezra: שם הפועל, 'the name of the action'; Radak: it enlarges the matter).
- עִצְּבוֹנֵךְ וְהֵרֹנֵךְ: two nouns with the same 2nd feminine singular suffix; the second has the conjunction and (Ibn Ezra) an added final nun.
- The etnaḥta falls on בָנִים and divides the verse: the first half concerns childbearing, the second the relation to the husband.
- The second half has two verbless or nearly verbless clauses (your desire toward your husband; he will rule over you), joined by the pronoun וְהוּא for contrast.
- Wordplay: עִצָּבוֹן, עֶצֶב and later בְּעִצָּבוֹן (3:17) share one root, binding the woman's sentence to the man's.

Translator's note: תְּשׁוּקָה is kept as 'desire' because the commentators differ on its object. Rashi reads it as sexual desire which she cannot ask for openly; Ibn Ezra as obedience (משמעתך). The English keeps the plain 'desire for your husband'. עִצְּבוֹנֵךְ is 'pain', the broad sense, with Rashi's child-raising and Ibn Ezra's menstrual senses recorded rather than adopted.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:16:1` records: Rashi: עִצְּבוֹנֵךְ is 'זה צער גידול בנים', the pain of raising children. The English keeps the broader 'pain' and does not limit it.
- `rashi:3:16:2` follows: Rashi: וְהֵרֹנֵךְ is 'זה צער העיבור', the distress of pregnancy; the English 'your pregnancy' with the word 'pain' carried over from the first noun.
- `rashi:3:16:3` follows: Rashi: בְּעֶצֶב תֵּלְדִי בָנִים is 'זה צער הלידה', the pain of giving birth.
- `rashi:3:16:4` follows: Rashi: the woman's desire is for intercourse (לתשמיש), yet she has not the boldness to demand it with her mouth; he rules, 'everything from him and not from you'. The English keeps 'desire' and 'rule'.
- `rashi:3:16:5` follows: Rashi: תְּשׁוּקָתֵךְ means תַּאֲוָתֵךְ, as in נֶפֶשׁ שׁוֹקֵקָה (Isaiah 29:8): longing, craving.
- `ibn-ezra:3:16:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: הַרְבָּה is the infinitive ('שם הפועל'), placed before the finite verb to intensify it.
- `ibn-ezra:3:16:2` records: Ibn Ezra: עִצְּבוֹנֵךְ is the time of distress, at the flow of virginal blood. This is a real alternative reading that the English does not adopt.
- `ibn-ezra:3:16:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: the nun of הֵרֹנֵךְ is added, since pregnancy and birth are heavy on a woman. The English reads 'pregnancy'.
- `ibn-ezra:3:16:4` records: Ibn Ezra: תְּשׁוּקָתֵךְ means 'your obedience' (משמעתך): she will obey whatever he commands. Rashi's and Radak's 'desire' is preferred in the English; the reading is kept here.
- `ramban:3:16:1` records: Ramban rejects Rashi's reading as too favorable to the woman and takes the verse as longing for her husband with no heed to the pain of childbirth, he holding her like a servant. A real alternative.
- `radak:3:16:9` follows: Radak: even though she bears in pain she will long still to lie with him.
- apparatus: עִצָּבוֹן vs. עֶצֶב: the pack gives two lemmas of one root with no numbered senses; both are rendered 'pain'.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders צַעֲרַיְכִי וְעִדּוּיַיְכִי 'your pains and your pregnancies' and תִּאוּבְתִּיךְ 'your desire'; it agrees with the English.

Grammar notes:
- The speech opens with a long כִּי clause giving the reason (you listened and ate); the verdict comes only at אֲרוּרָה, the first word after the etnaḥta, in a verbless clause with a passive participle.
- The first clause is the mirror of 3:14: the same ארור is said of the ground, not the man himself.
- שָׁמַעְתָּ לְקוֹל is 'obey': the man's fault is set in the same words as the woman's.
- בְּעִצָּבוֹן תֹּאכְלֶנָּה: the suffix on the verb refers to the ground; by metonymy it means its produce (Ibn Ezra: תאכל תבואתה).
- הָאֲדָמָה echoes אָדָם: the ground from which the man was taken is cursed for his sake.

Translator's note: אָדָם without the article is rendered 'Adam' because the pack parses it as a proper name; the possible reading 'the man' (a common noun with ל) is not otherwise supported here, since the speaker has addressed 'the woman' in the previous verse with the article. בַּעֲבוּרֶךָ is 'because of you': the ground suffers on the man's account, so Rashi's view that it brings up cursed things, and Ibn Ezra's that it yields little, both fit. The English does not choose between them.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:17:1` records: Rashi: the ground will bring up cursed things (flies, fleas, ants), like a son who goes out into bad culture so that people curse the breasts he suckled. The English keeps only the bare 'cursed is the ground'.
- `ibn-ezra:3:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'cursed is the ground' means that it will not bring forth abundant produce (שלא תוציא תבואה רבה).
- `ibn-ezra:3:17:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: תֹּאכְלֶנָּה means 'you will eat its produce', a short way of saying it, as in 1 Kings 2:7 'those who eat at your table'. The English 'eat of it' keeps the metonymy.
- `ibn-ezra:3:18:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: בְּעִצָּבוֹן means in the toil of ploughing, harrowing and sowing, and a further evil is that thorns will grow in the grain. The English 'in toil' is general.
- `radak:3:17:4` follows: Radak: you will have to work the ground until you eat its produce; until now you ate the fruit of the garden's trees without toil.
- `radak:3:17:1` follows: Radak: מִן הָעֵץ means 'from the fruit of the tree', for the fruit comes from the tree. The English 'ate from the tree' keeps the same compression.
- `sforno:3:17:3` records: Sforno: שָׁמַעְתָּ means 'you accepted her opinion that He is mistaken'; the English keeps the plain 'listened to the voice'.
- apparatus: 3:17.20 vowels: WLC reads תֹּאכֲלֶנָּה; the base text (MAM) has תֹּאכְלֶנָּה; the English is the same.
- apparatus: 3:17.4: WLC note c records an accent reading differing from BHS; the English is not affected.
- apparatus: Onkelos: אֲרֵי קַבֵּילְתָּ לְמֵימַר אִתְּתָךְ 'because you accepted the word of your wife' (obey).

### Translation under review (3:18)

English: It will sprout thorns and thistles for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

Literal: and-thorn and-thistle it-will-cause-to-sprout for-you and-you-will-eat [object-marker] plant-of the-field

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3:18.2 | and thistle |  |  | דַּרְדַּר: Ibn Ezra takes it as the smaller of the two. |
| 3:18.3 | it will sprout |  | Hiph 2 | Hiphil imperfect, 3rd feminine singular; the subject is the ground (feminine). 'It causes to sprout', so thorns and thistles are the object, placed first. |

Phrases:
- 3:18.1 3:18.2 3:18.3 3:18.4 | It will sprout thorns and thistles for you | 'thorn and-thistle it-will-sprout for-you'; the object stands first for emphasis, which English cannot show.
- 3:18.5 3:18.6 3:18.7 3:18.8 | and you will eat the plants of the field | 'and-you-will-eat [object-marker] plant-of the-field'; the object marker אֶת has no English counterpart.

Grammar notes:
- The object (קוֹץ וְדַרְדַּר) is fronted before the verb, and the subject (the ground) is not named; it is the feminine of תַּצְמִיחַ.
- תַּצְמִיחַ has a tiny accent (the gaʿya, a bar) on the final syllable: the stress is on the penultimate; the verb is Hiphil of צמח.
- The etnaḥta on לָךְ divides the verse: the curse (thorns) and then the resulting food (plants of the field).
- וְאָכַלְתָּ is a weqatal: a future continuing the preceding imperfect.
- עֵשֶׂב in the construct state is followed by הַשָּׂדֶה: 'plants of the field', contrasted with the fruit of the garden trees.

Translator's note: Rashi and Ibn Ezra differ on what עֵשֶׂב הַשָּׂדֶה is. Rashi: the plants that grow among the sown crops, which the man must eat of necessity; Ibn Ezra: it is the bread, since in the garden he lived on the fruit of the trees. The English 'plants of the field' keeps both open.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:18:1` follows: Rashi: when you sow seeds of various kinds, the earth will sprout thorn and thistle (such as artichoke and wild varieties), which are edible once prepared. The English names the plants generally.
- `rashi:3:18:2` follows: Rashi: this is a curse because after the toil of sowing legumes or garden vegetables the ground sprouts thorns and thistles and other plants of the field, which you must eat of necessity (על כרחך).
- `ibn-ezra:3:18:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: קוֹץ is the larger; דַּרְדַּר is smaller and grows alone.
- `ibn-ezra:3:18:3` records: Ibn Ezra: עֵשֶׂב הַשָּׂדֶה is the bread, since in the garden his life was from the fruit of the tree. A real alternative which the English keeps open.
- `radak:3:18:1` follows: Radak: in place of the seed there will come up thorns and thistles which damage the grain growing beside them.
- apparatus: Onkelos: וְכוּבִין וְאַטְדִּין תַּצְמַח לָךְ 'thorns and briars it will sprout for you'; יָת עִסְבָּא דְחַקְלָא 'the grass of the field'.

Grammar notes:

- Ibn Ezra: ו and י interchange (as in הֹוָה for הָיָה, Exodus 9:3 and Nehemiah 6:6), so the name is a form of חַיָּה, but is written with ו to distinguish her from the animals.

- חָי as a collective noun without the article.

### Translation under review (3:21)

English: The LORD God made garments of skin for Adam and for his wife, and clothed them.

Literal: and-made the-LORD God for-Adam and-for-his-wife tunics-of skin and-clothed-them

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3:21.1 | and made |  | Qal II.1 | wayyiqtol; a narrative past. |
| 3:21.2 | the LORD |  |  | the divine name, rendered 'the LORD' (decisions.md). |
| 3:21.3 | God |  | 4 | יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים together are 'the LORD God' (decisions.md). |
| 3:21.4 | for Adam |  | 3 | אָדָם with no article after ל: 'for Adam'; possibly 'for the man'. |
| 3:21.5 | and for his wife |  | 2 | construct state with a 3rd masculine suffix. |
| 3:21.6 | garments of |  |  | construct plural of כֻּתֹּנֶת, 'tunic'. |
| 3:21.7 | skin |  | 2 | עוֹר is 'skin' or 'hide'. |
| 3:21.8 | and clothed them |  | Hiph | Hiphil wayyiqtol: the object suffix is 3rd masculine plural (them); 'and he clothed them'. |

Phrases:
- 3:21.1 3:21.2 3:21.3 | The LORD God made | 'and-made the-LORD God'.
- 3:21.4 3:21.5 | for Adam and for his wife | 'for-Adam and-for-his-wife'.
- 3:21.6 3:21.7 | garments of skin | 'tunics-of skin'; Rashi and Ibn Ezra differ on what these were.
- 3:21.8 | and clothed them | 'and-clothed-them'.

Grammar notes:
- וַיַּעַשׂ: a wayyiqtol of עשׂה with two telisha accents; the shortened form of the Qal.
- The word order is verb, subject, indirect objects, direct object, and then a second verb with a suffix.
- כָּתְנוֹת עוֹר: a construct chain, 'tunics of skin'; whether the tunics were of skin or for the skin (כתנות לעורם) is the question the commentators take up.
- The silluq closes the verse with a Hiphil wayyiqtol: 'he made them wear', the causative of לָבַשׁ.

Translator's note: כָּתְנוֹת עוֹר is rendered 'garments of skin'; Rashi's alternative 'garments from skin' (wool of the hares) is a variant and the English does not choose between them. Ibn Ezra's own conclusion is that we only need to believe that God made them.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:21:1` records: Rashi: some aggadic sources say the tunics were smooth like a fingernail, stuck to the skin; others say something from the skin, like soft, warm hare's wool, from which He made tunics. The English keeps the plain 'garments of skin'.
- `ibn-ezra:3:21:1` follows: Ibn Ezra lists several views (skin over bone and flesh; tunics for their skin; an animal like a man whose skin was flayed), and concludes that we need only believe that God made garments of skin for the man and his wife. The English keeps the plain sense.
- `radak:3:21:1` follows: Radak: one need not ask how God made these garments, for it is no greater wonder than the rest of creation; God commanded and they were made.
- `sforno:3:21:2` not-applicable: Sforno: He clothed them so that He would not drive them out naked, so that they would not later think they had added a merit by clothing themselves.
- apparatus: Onkelos: לְבוּשִׁין דִּיקָר עַל מְשַׁךְ בִּשְׂרֵיהוֹן 'garments of glory upon the skin of their flesh'; this paraphrase reads עוֹר as the skin they already had.
