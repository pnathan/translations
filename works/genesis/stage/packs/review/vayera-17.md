### Translation under review (22:1)

English: It happened after these things that God tested Abraham. He said to him, "Abraham." He said, "Here I am."

Literal: and-it-happened after the-things these and-the-God tested [object] Abraham and-he-said to-him Abraham and-he-said here-I

Phrases:

Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי is a wayyiqtol of הָיָה that opens a new episode; the English keeps the formula 'it happened'.
- וְהָאֱלֹהִים נִסָּה: the subject comes before the qatal, which gives the situation before the speech. It is not a sequential verb.
- The etnaḥta on אַבְרָהָם divides the verse. The silluq on הִנֵּנִי closes it.
- The name Abraham is repeated as the address of the call, and הִנֵּנִי is the reply that accepts it.

Translator's note: The English keeps 'God' for הָאֱלֹהִים and reads נִסָּה as 'tested', the sense Ibn Ezra fixes. The two wayyiqtols of speech carry the call and the answer.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:22:1:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: נִסָּה is to be read in its plain sense, 'tested'; a reading that changes the letters is refuted by the whole passage. He sets aside the Gaon's reading, that the test was to show Abraham's righteousness to people, since no one was present at the binding. The test was for reward, and the verse's first words remove the objection that a command could be countermanded.
- `ibn-ezra:22:1:2` not-applicable: On 'now I know' (22:12), outside this segment: the phrase is like 'if I do not know' (18:21), and he will explain the secret at Exodus 33:12. It does not bear on 22:1–8.
- `rashi:22:1:1` declines: Rashi's midrash fills 'these things' with the Adversary's accusation that Abraham gave only a small offering (Sanhedrin 89b) and with Ishmael's boast over Isaac's circumcision. The English keeps 'these things' open, since the midrash supplies a cause the verse does not state.
- `rashi:22:1:2` follows: Rashi: הִנֵּנִי is the answer of the righteous, a word of humility and of readiness (זִמּוּן). The English 'Here I am' carries readiness; the humility is in the commentary only.
- `radak:22:1:1` records: Radak takes 'these things' as the affair with Abimelech and Abraham's long stay in that land, followed by his coming to Beersheba to live. He adds that the test was to show later generations Abraham's love, which is not the plain sense. The English does not name the referent.
- `radak:22:1:2` records: Radak takes the call as made in a night vision, since Abraham is said to rise early in the morning (22:3). The English says nothing of a vision.
- `onkelos:22:1:1` records: Onkelos renders the divine name here as 'the LORD' (ייָ) and Abraham's answer as 'Here I am' (הָא אֲנָא). The English keeps 'God' for הָאֱלֹהִים, as the decisions set for the God of Israel.
- `ramban:22:1:1` follows: Ramban takes the test (נִסָּיוֹן) as a trial of the one tested: God commands so that the act passes from potential to deed, and Abraham receives the reward of a good deed and not only of a good heart. This is the sense 'tested' that the English gives.
- `sforno:22:1:1` not-applicable: Sforno: the test was so that Abraham's love and fear would pass from potential into act and he would resemble his Maker, who is good to the world in act (cf. 1:26). It gives a reason for the test, not the sense of the word.
- apparatus: 22:1.6: the reading 'tested' is the one the Hebrew gives; Ibn Ezra notes another reading of the letters and the Gaon's reading of the sense (see commentary).

Grammar notes:
- קַח נָא is an imperative with the particle of entreaty: 'Please take'. The request tone is Rashi's reading (see commentary).
- The son is described in stages: your son, your only one, whom you love, and then Isaac. The name comes after the relative clause, as an apposition.
- אָהַבְתָּ is a qatal in the relative clause, a love that holds; the English uses the present, 'whom you love'.
- וְלֶךְ־לְךָ is an imperative with the dative; the idiom is the one of 12:1, and the English keeps 'go'.
- וְהַעֲלֵהוּ is a hifil imperative with the suffix 'him'; the aim, 'as a burnt offering', follows with the preposition ל.

Translator's note: The English keeps 'your only one' and 'Moriah' as the Hebrew has them. 'Offer him up' is the sense of the hifil with a burnt offering as its aim; it does not add a slaughter, which the words do not state.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:22:2:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the house was built there, as 2 Chronicles 3:1 says; it is not a high mountain, and the threshing floor of Araunah was there. The English keeps 'mountains', since har is mountain or hill and the height of the site is not in the verse. His claim of a low height is recorded, not adopted.
- `rashi:22:2:1` follows: Rashi: נָא is a term of request: God asks Abraham to stand by him in this test, so that the earlier righteous will not say there was no real trial. The English 'Please take' takes the request sense.
- `rashi:22:2:2` records: Rashi: 'your only one' means only to his mother, since Abraham had another son; 'whom you love' names both, and 'Isaac' names the one meant (Sanhedrin 89b). He explains the stages as a gradual disclosure, so that Abraham would not be thrown into confusion. The English keeps the word as it stands.
- `rashi:22:2:3` records: Rashi: the land of Moriah is Jerusalem, as in 2 Chronicles 3:1; the Sages say instruction went out from there, and Onkelos renders it 'the land of worship' for the incense of the place. The English keeps the name.
- `rashi:22:2:4` follows: Rashi: הַעֲלֵהוּ means bring him up the mountain to make him a burnt offering; God did not say 'slaughter him', and after the ascent God said to take him down (22:12). The English 'offer him up' adds no slaughter.
- `rashi:22:2:5` not-applicable: Rashi: God conceals the matter from the righteous and reveals it afterwards to increase their reward, as with 'the land which I will show you' (12:1). A homily on why no mountain is named; not the sense of the words.
- `ramban:22:2:1` records: Ramban: Isaac is called 'your only one' because he is the son of the free wife and alone is called seed, and the word magnifies the command: take your son, the only one, the beloved. The English keeps the word as it stands.
- `ramban:22:2:2` records: Ramban takes Moriah as Jerusalem, as Rashi does, and reads Onkelos's 'land of worship' as the place where God is served. He derives the name from 'fear' (מוֹרָא) and cites the Sages' saying that instruction went out from there.
- `radak:22:2:1` not-applicable: Radak: 'your only one' is said although you love him greatly, since he was born to you from your wife in your old age. A remark on the father's feeling.
- `radak:22:2:2` not-applicable: Radak: the matter is hard for you, that he is your only son, and all the more that he is beloved, the son of your old age.
- `radak:22:2:3` follows: Radak: 'go for yourself' is an idiom of the language, like 12:1, Jeremiah 5:5 and Exodus 18:27; the dative adds nothing to 'go'. The English 'go' takes it so.
- `radak:22:2:4` records: Radak: Jerusalem and its territory is called the land of Moriah, and the mountain of Araunah's threshing floor is called Mount Moriah (2 Chronicles 3:1). The text does not say why; the Sages say from there instruction went out. Onkelos renders 'land of worship'.
- `radak:22:2:5` not-applicable: Radak: God did not yet say which mountain he meant, only 'one of the mountains'.
- `radak:22:2:6` not-applicable: Radak: 'which I will tell you' magnifies Abraham's love. God did not say why, and sent him a three-day journey rather than at once, to give him time to think; and the place was to be the one where his sons would build God's house and altar.
- `onkelos:22:2:1` records: Onkelos renders the land of Moriah as 'the land of worship' and 'offer him up' as 'offer him up before me', with 'before me' not in the Hebrew. The English keeps the name and adds nothing.
- apparatus: 22:2.5: WLC note c: the edition reads one or more accents differently from BHS. No effect on the English.
- apparatus: 22:2.22: the height of the mountain is not stated. Ibn Ezra says the place is not high; the English keeps 'mountains' (see commentary).

- `rashi:22:3:2` not-applicable: Rashi: he saddled the donkey himself and did not order a servant, for love spoils the usual order. A remark on his care, not the sense of the verb.

- `onkelos:22:3:1` records: Onkelos renders 'saddled' by a word for 'hastened' (זָרֵז) and names the place as the LORD had told him. The English keeps 'saddled' and 'God'.
- `sforno:22:3:1` records: Sforno: the place is the land of Moriah. The English keeps 'the place'.

- `radak:22:4:1` records: Radak: the waw of 'and he lifted' is added, as in other places (36:24), so the day may be read with what precedes: on the third day he came near the place, and saw it from afar on entering its border; the mountain he did not yet know until God told him.

- `radak:22:4:3` not-applicable: Radak gives a midrash: he saw a pillar of fire on the mountain and asked Isaac and the servants what they saw; they saw nothing, and he told them to stay with the donkey. Not the plain sense.
- `onkelos:22:4:1` follows: Onkelos: he raised his eyes and saw the place from afar. The same sense as the English.
- `sforno:22:4:1` records: Sforno: 'the place' is the place of the sacrifice on Mount Moriah. The English keeps 'the place'.
- `sforno:22:4:2` not-applicable: Sforno: his eye was given power to see from afar by the will of God, as in 'and the LORD showed him all the land' (Deuteronomy 34:1); he understood the place of the sacrifice would be there. A reason for the seeing, not its sense.

### Translation under review (22:5)

English: Abraham said to his servants, "Stay here with the donkey. I and the boy will go as far as there, and we will bow down and we will return to you."

Grammar notes:
- שְׁבוּ לָכֶם פֹּה: the imperative 'stay' with the dative; the English 'stay here'.
- The verbs נֵלְכָה, וְנִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה, וְנָשׁוּבָה are first-person plural: 'we' are Abraham and the boy. The pack parses נֵלְכָה and נָשׁוּבָה as cohortatives and נִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה as a yiqtol.
- The subject 'I and the boy' comes before its verb, and the English keeps that order.
- The etnaḥta on כֹּה divides the verse: the staying and the going first, then the three verbs of Abraham's plan.

Translator's note: Abraham's words are the speech; the English keeps 'we will return' as a statement of plan. The commentators differ on what he meant by it (see commentary).

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:22:5:1` records: Ibn Ezra asks how Abraham could say 'and we will return'. Others answer that he meant to bring back Isaac's remains, or that he put them off with words so that they would not turn back and Isaac would not perceive and flee. He rejects the Sages' age of thirty-seven for Isaac, takes him to be near thirteen and bound against his will, and gives as evidence that his father hid the secret; the English keeps the words and takes no side on the intention.
- `rashi:22:5:1` records: Rashi: 'as far as there' means a short way to the place before us; perhaps Abraham will see where it is, as the place told him: 'so shall your seed be' (15:5). The English keeps the distance open.
- `rashi:22:5:2` records: Rashi: 'we will return' is a prophecy by Abraham that both would come back. The English keeps the words as a statement of plan.
- `radak:22:5:1` not-applicable: Radak: the servants understood that a sacrifice would be made with the bowing, since they carried wood and fire; whoever goes to a place to bow down there brings an offering (1 Samuel 1:3). A remark on what the servants understood.
- `onkelos:22:5:1` records: Onkelos renders 'bow down' by 'worship' (וְנִסְגּוּד). The English keeps 'bow down', the sense the verb gives.
- `sforno:22:5:1` not-applicable: Sforno: so that they would not hinder him or trouble him in making the sacrifice.
- apparatus: 22:5.16: the words 'and we will return' are read differently: Ibn Ezra gives others' readings of Abraham's intention (see commentary).

### Translation under review (22:8)

English: Abraham said, "God will see for himself the sheep for a burnt offering, my son." And the two of them went together.

Literal: and-he-said Abraham God will-see for-him the-sheep to-burnt-offering my-son and-they-went the-two-of-them together

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22:8.1 | and he said |  | Qal 1 | Wayyiqtol of אָמַר: Abraham's answer. |
| 22:8.2 | Abraham |  |  | The name in its familiar English form. |
| 22:8.3 | God |  | 4 | אֱלֹהִים without the article, the name of God. |
| 22:8.4 | will see |  | Qal 1.a | Yiqtol of רָאָה, third masculine singular. The verb of seeing takes 'the sheep' as its object; Radak reads it as providing (see commentary). |

Phrases:
- 22:8.1 22:8.2 | Abraham said, | 'and-he-said Abraham'
- 22:8.3 22:8.4 22:8.5 | "God will see for himself | 'God will-see for-him'
- 22:8.6 22:8.7 | the sheep for a burnt offering, | 'the-sheep to-burnt-offering'

- 22:8.9 22:8.10 22:8.11 | and the two of them went together. | 'and-they-went the-two-of-them together'

Grammar notes:
- The subject אֱלֹהִים comes before the yiqtol יִרְאֶה, and לּוֹ is 'for himself'.
- יִרְאֶה לּוֹ הַשֶּׂה: the verb of seeing takes 'the sheep' as its object. Its sense 'see for himself' is open between the readings in the commentary and the apparatus.
- The vocative 'my son' comes last, after the words for the sheep; the English keeps the Hebrew order.
- The last clause repeats 22:6 word for word.

Translator's note: The English 'God will see for himself' keeps the verb's sense and does not choose between providing the sheep and the sheep being Isaac. Both readings are recorded.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:22:8:1` records: Radak: the answer can be taken in two ways. God will provide the sheep, since He knows who it will be and designates it; or the sheep is Isaac himself, and Isaac understood that he would be the sheep. The English keeps the words open between the two.
- `rashi:22:8:1` records: Rashi: 'God will see and choose for himself the sheep'; the choosing is his gloss, and the rest of his comment is cut off in the pack. The English keeps 'see' and adds no 'choose'.
- `rashi:22:8:2` not-applicable: Rashi: although Isaac understood that he was to be slaughtered (the rest is cut off in the pack). A remark on Isaac's understanding, not on the words.
- `rashi:22:8:3` records: Rashi: 'together' means with one heart (בְּלֵב שָׁוֶה). The English keeps 'together'.
- `radak:22:8:2` not-applicable: Radak: 'my son' here is the answer to a call, as in 'Here I am, my son' (22:7). A remark on the formula.
- `radak:22:8:6` not-applicable: Radak: 'together' in one heart: the son accepted out of love to give his soul to God and offer himself before him.
- `onkelos:22:8:1` records: Onkelos reads 'God will see' as 'will reveal' (גְּלֵי) and adds 'before the LORD' (קֳדָם יְיָ), which the Hebrew does not have. The English keeps 'see for himself' and adds nothing.
- apparatus: 22:8.3–5: the answer has two readings (Radak): God will provide the sheep; or the sheep is Isaac himself (see commentary). The English keeps the words.
