### Translation under review (16:1)

English: Now Sarai, Abram's wife, had not borne him a child. She had an Egyptian maidservant, and her name was Hagar.

Literal: and-Sarai wife-of Abram not bore to-him and-to-her maidservant Egyptian and-name-her Hagar

Phrases:
- 16:1.1 16:1.2 16:1.3 | Now Sarai, Abram's wife, | 'and-Sarai wife-of Abram'; the opening waw is circumstantial, setting the scene
- 16:1.4 16:1.5 16:1.6 | had not borne him a child | 'not bore to-him'; the object (a child) is understood
- 16:1.7 16:1.8 16:1.9 | She had an Egyptian maidservant | 'and-to-her maidservant Egyptian': possession expressed by lamed + suffix
- 16:1.10 16:1.11 | and her name was Hagar | 'and-name-her Hagar'

Grammar notes:
- The sentence opens with waw + subject, not a wayyiqtol: it gives background, not a new event.
- Possession is expressed with the preposition ל: וְלָהּ שִׁפְחָה 'and to her (there was) a maidservant'.
- The accents pair מִצְרִית with שִׁפְחָה under a tipha and divide the verse at the etnahta after לוֹ.

Translator's note: The negated perfect יָלְדָה is rendered 'had not borne' since it reports the state at the time of the story. 'A child' supplies the unexpressed object.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:16:1:1` records: Rashi, from Bereshit Rabbah: Hagar was Pharaoh's daughter, who preferred that her daughter be a servant in Sarai's house rather than a mistress elsewhere. The English keeps the plain 'Egyptian maidservant' and records this identification.

Grammar notes:
- אִבָּנֶה is a Niphal imperfect of בנה with the first radical-like doubling: the sound-play with בֵּן 'son' is the point.
- בֹּא־נָא is a polite imperative: 'come now, please'.
- שָׁמַע לְקוֹל, 'to listen to the voice of', means to obey.
- The zaqef on מִלֶּדֶת and the etnahta on מִמֶּנָּה mark the two halves of her speech.

Translator's note: The English keeps 'built up' so that the connection with 'son' is preserved in the notes. 'Listened to' keeps the idiom; 'obeyed' is the sense.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:16:2:1` follows: Rashi: the verse teaches that one who has no children is not built up but ruined. This supports 'built up' as the metaphor.
- `rashi:16:2:2` records: Rashi: 'I shall be built up' through the merit of bringing my rival into my house. This reading of the cause is recorded, not adopted.
- `rashi:16:2:3` records: Rashi, from Bereshit Rabbah: 'to the voice of Sarai' means to the holy spirit in her. Recorded as homiletic.
- `ibn-ezra:16:2:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: אִבָּנֶה derives from בֵּן; the son is like a building and the father like its foundation. The English follows this derivation.
- `ibn-ezra:16:2:2` records: Ibn Ezra glosses וַיִּשְׁמַע אַבְרָם as 'he said so to her', that is, he assented to her. The English keeps 'listened to', the ordinary meaning of the idiom.
- apparatus: Radak and Ramban (not normative) read Abram's listening as consent given for Sarai's sake.

Commentary rulings:

- `rashi:16:5:2` records: Rashi: the full spelling of וּבֵינֶיךָ allows reading 'and your sons', since Sarai cast an evil eye on Hagar and caused her to miscarry. A homiletic reading, recorded.

Grammar notes:
- אֵי־מִזֶּה בָאת: 'where from this (place) did you come'.
- The participle בֹּרַחַת gives a present state: 'I am fleeing'.
- The pausal vowel on בֹּרַחַת is unusual; Ibn Ezra explains it by the guttural.
- Hagar is addressed by name and by her status.

Translator's note: The question is rhetorical: the angel knows the answer and uses it to open a conversation. The apparatus keeps the Ibn Ezra and Radak points on form.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:16:8:1` follows: Rashi: the angel knew; he asks in order to open a conversation with her. 'Where did you come from' means, what place can you say you come from.
- `ibn-ezra:16:8:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: אֵי is like אַיֵּה, as in אֵי הֶבֶל; the sense is 'from what place did you come'.
- `ibn-ezra:16:8:2` not-applicable: Ibn Ezra: the resh has patah because of the following guttural, as in other forms. This concerns the vocalization only.
- apparatus: Radak: the opening וַיֹּאמַר has patah with revia as if at a pause (not normative).
