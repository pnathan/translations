Phrases:
- 9:8.1 9:8.2 | Then God said | 'and-said God'
- 9:8.3 9:8.4 | to Noah | 'to Noah'
- 9:8.5 9:8.6 9:8.7 | and to his sons with him | 'and-to sons-of-him with-him'; the sons are addressed together with Noah
- 9:8.8 | - | 'to-say': a formula introducing the speech; the colon of the English line carries it

Grammar notes:
- וַיֹּאמֶר is a wayyiqtol, the narrative tense; the subject אֱלֹהִים follows the verb.
- The pashta on אֱלֹהִים and zaqef on נֹחַ divide the address: God, then Noah, then the sons.
- לֵאמֹר (lamed + infinitive construct) is the standard introduction to quoted speech.
- אִתּוֹ 'with him' is the form of אֵת with a suffix; it is distinct from the object marker אֵת.

Translator's note: The English omits לֵאמֹר, rendered by the colon. אֱלֹהִים is plural in form with a singular verb, as always for the God of Israel; 'God' per decisions.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:9:8:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: God spoke to Noah and his sons 'through their father' (על ידי אביהם); some say all four were prophets. The English says only that God spoke to them.
- `ramban:9:8:2` records: Ramban: God spoke by means of their father, since the sons were not prophets and Ham never reached the rank of prophecy. The English does not decide how the sons heard.
- apparatus: Radak allows that the sons were also prophets, or that Noah was told to tell them.

Phrases:

- 9:9.4 | - | object marker
- 9:9.5 9:9.6 | my covenant with you | 'covenant-of-me with-you'
- 9:9.7 9:9.8 9:9.9 | and with your offspring after you | 'and-[with] seed-of-you after-you'

Grammar notes:
- The independent pronoun וַאֲנִי is emphatic and contrasts God's act with the human task of the preceding verse.
- הִנְנִי מֵקִים: הִנְנִי plus a participle announces an act already under way.
- מֵקִים is a hifil participle of קום; הֵקִים אֶת בְּרִית is the idiom 'to establish a covenant'.
- זֶרַע is a collective singular: 'seed' means all descendants.

Translator's note: The 'covenant' is God's one-sided promise, spelled out in 9:11.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:9:1` records: Rashi: הִנְנִי means 'I agree with you': Noah was anxious about procreation until God promised not to destroy the world again, and God now agrees to confirm the promise with a covenant and give a sign. The English keeps the plain 'here I am establishing'.
- `ibn-ezra:9:9:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the second אֵת (in וְאֵת זַרְעֲכֶם) means 'with'; the repetition of 'with you' is for added clarity; and the covenant to be established is that no flesh be cut off.
- `radak:9:9:1` follows: Radak: the ו adds to 'be fruitful and multiply': you attend to populating the world, and I too will ensure it is never cut off.

Grammar notes:
- The object marker is absent: וְאֵת is the preposition 'with', continuing the covenant 'with' of 9:9.
- The beth of בָּעוֹף and בַּבְּהֵמָה means 'among' or 'of': birds and livestock specify the living creatures.
- מִכֹּל יֹצְאֵי הַתֵּבָה and לְכֹל חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ extend the list beyond those that are 'with you'; the comments below take them as additions.
- The long verse is divided by etnaḥta after the second אִתְּכֶם, with the final clause set apart as an extension.

Translator's note: Rashi reads the two final clauses as including what the first part leaves out; the English follows, keeping the Hebrew's repetition. נֶפֶשׁ הַחַיָּה: 'living creature'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:10:1` follows: Rashi: חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ אִתְּכֶם are those that walk about with people, i.e. tame creatures.
- `rashi:9:10:2` follows: Rashi: מִכֹּל יֹצְאֵי הַתֵּבָה comes to include creeping and swarming things (שקצים ורמשים).
- `rashi:9:10:3` follows: Rashi: the last לְכֹל חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ includes the harmful beasts (המזיקין), which are not among the animals 'with you' because they do not live alongside people.
- `radak:9:10:4` records: Radak: the last clause also brings in the offspring of the animals, as 'your offspring after you' did for humans.

Grammar notes:
- The promise has two negated clauses: flesh will not be cut off, and no flood will ever again destroy the earth.
- וַהֲקִמֹתִי is a weqatal with future sense, tied to 9:9.
- מִמֵּי הַמַּבּוּל: the min marks the cause or means by which flesh would be cut off.
- לְשַׁחֵת is a piel infinitive construct in a purpose clause: 'a flood meant to destroy'.

Translator's note: בָּשָׂר is rendered 'flesh', as the commentators take it as body. The weqatal is rendered as a plain future.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:11:1` follows: Rashi: 'I will make a fulfilment of my covenant' (קיום); and what is it? The sign of the bow, as Scripture goes on to say.
- `ibn-ezra:9:11:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: בָּשָׂר means the body, which has sensation; the covenant is that no body be cut off by flood waters, and since some might escape if a flood came, the covenant adds that there will be no flood at all (cf. Isaiah 54:9).
- `radak:9:11:3` records: Radak: 'all flesh' means in the way of the flood generation; some flesh may still be cut off by a sweeping rain according to punishment.

- אֲנִי נֹתֵן with a participle states the act in progress.
- לְדֹרֹת is written defectively (without a vav), a point Rashi draws a lesson from.

Translator's note: First use of אוֹת in the story. 'Sign' because the commentators' sense is a mark of the covenant.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:12:1` not-applicable: Rashi: לדרת is written defectively, since some generations needed no sign, being wholly righteous, such as the generation of Hezekiah and of Rabbi Shimon bar Yohai. A homiletic point that does not alter the English.
- `ibn-ezra:9:12:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: אוֹת is both masculine and feminine, so זֹאת (feminine) agrees with it.
- `radak:9:12:1` follows: Radak: God explains to Noah and his sons that this is the sign so that they will not fear a flood when he sends rain.

Grammar notes:
- The object אֶת קַשְׁתִּי is placed first for emphasis; נָתַתִּי is a perfect.
- קַשְׁתִּי 'my bow' has the suffix of God, a word picture that the commentators discuss.
- וְהָיְתָה לְ is the idiom 'it shall become'.
- בֵּינִי וּבֵין הָאָרֶץ: the earth, not only the people, is a party; verse 12 had 'every living creature'.

Translator's note: The perfect נָתַתִּי is a point on which the commentators divide: Ibn Ezra says the bow is newly set now; Radak records Saadya, for whom it was set in nature at creation and is now made a sign. The English follows Ibn Ezra.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:9:13:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'I have now set a bow in the cloud'; the verse does not mean, as the Gaon said, that it existed at first.
- `radak:9:13:5` records: Radak, reporting Saadya: נָתַתִּי has its plain meaning of the past: the bow set in nature in the cloud will be a sign for you, though it was not seen during the flood.
- `radak:9:13:3` records: Radak: the bow arises from the sun's rays against the cloud after rain; God strengthened the sun's light after the flood, as Ibn Ezra writes in the name of a Gaon.
- apparatus: Ramban (on 9:12) argues the bow was in nature from creation.

- `radak:9:14:1` follows: Radak: 'I will cloud' by the intermediate causes that I have ordained.
