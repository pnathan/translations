### Translation under review (19:32)

English: Come, let us make our father drink wine, and let us lie with him, and let us keep alive seed from our father.

Literal: come, let-us-make-drink (object marker) our-father wine, and-let-us-lie with-him, and-let-us-keep-alive from-our-father seed

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19:32.1 | come |  | Qal I.1 | Imperative of הָלַךְ with the paragogic ה. Radak takes לְכָה as an interjection of agreement, like לְכוּ and הָבָה: it opens a joint proposal rather than sending anyone anywhere. |
| 19:32.2 | let us make drink |  | Hiph 2 | Hifil of שָׁקָה, the causative of drinking. The form is a yiqtol in the first common plural, read here as a proposal ('let us'). |
| 19:32.3 | (object marker) | bdb:a.gm.aa |  | The object marker אֵת, not the preposition: it marks אָבִינוּ as the one who drinks. |
| 19:32.4 | our father |  | 1 |  |
| 19:32.5 | wine |  |  |  |
| 19:32.6 | and let us lie |  | Qal 3 | Cohortative of שָׁכַב. With עִם the verb means to lie with, that is, to have intercourse. |
| 19:32.7 | with him |  | 1.e |  |
| 19:32.8 | and let us keep alive |  | Pi 1 | Piel of חָיָה: to preserve alive, keep alive. The seed is the object. |
| 19:32.9 | from our father |  | 1 | מִן marks the source: the seed would come through the father. |
| 19:32.10 | seed |  | 4 | זֶרַע here is offspring, the line that carries on. |

Phrases:
- 19:32.1 | Come | 'come': the interjection that opens the proposal (Radak)
- 19:32.2 19:32.3 19:32.4 19:32.5 | let us make our father drink wine | 'let-us-make-drink (object marker) our-father wine': the causative hifil; the object marker is not translated
- 19:32.6 19:32.7 | and let us lie with him | 'and-let-us-lie with-him': the second act of the proposal
- 19:32.8 19:32.9 19:32.10 | and let us keep alive seed from our father | 'and-let-us-keep-alive from-our-father seed': the piel 'keep alive' takes seed as its object; 'from our father' is the source

Grammar notes:
- לְכָה is the one word of address in the verse. It is singular and masculine, but Radak notes that such interjections do not vary for number or gender, since they express agreement.
- The three verbs that follow are all first common plural: נַשְׁקֶה, וְנִשְׁכְּבָה, וּנְחַיֶּה. Each one is a step in the proposal, and the English 'let us' carries them all.
- The verb נַשְׁקֶה is a yiqtol in form, a causative (hifil) of שָׁקָה. The English 'make ... drink' keeps the causative and the object.
- The second and third steps are joined to the first by ו (and). The English keeps 'and' only where the Hebrew has the conjunction on the verb.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:19:32:1` follows: Radak: לָכָה, like לְכוּ and הָבָה, expresses agreement and mutual help ('let us'), and does not vary for gender or number. The English 'Come' with 'let us' keeps the sense of a proposal made to companions.
- `radak:19:32:2` not-applicable: Radak gives the purpose of the drinking: until the father is drunk and does not know what he does. The English line states the act and leaves its purpose to the reader.
- `ramban:19:32:2` not-applicable: Ramban reads the women's plan as a deed they thought fitting: God would show mercy, they would bear sons and daughters, and through them the world would endure, since God had not saved them in vain. The English gives no motive.

Grammar notes:
- The narrative is in the wayyiqtol throughout (וַתַּשְׁקֶין, וַתָּבֹא, וַתִּשְׁכַּב): the sequence of past events.
- The verb of perception is the qatal יָדַע, negated, and the two infinitives that follow are what he did not notice. The sentence says the father did not notice either act.
- The verb שָׁכַב takes אֵת here and עִם in 19:35. The English uses 'lay with' for both, so the difference of preposition is kept in the literal line.
- The infinitives בְּשִׁכְבָהּ and וּבְקוּמָהּ are a fixed pair: the first is the lying down, the second the rising, and the verse denies notice of both.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:19:33:1` follows: Ibn Ezra calls בְּשִׁכְבָהּ a verbal noun (שֵׁם הַפּוֹעַל). He adds that the ב may carry a hireq, as in בְּשִׁבְרִי לָכֶם (Lev 26:26), which is the same infinitive. The English reads it as the event: 'when she lay down'.
- `radak:19:33:1` follows: Radak: the demonstrative הוּא stands after the noun without the article, so הַלַּיְלָה הוּא means 'that night'. The English keeps 'that night'.
- `radak:19:33:2` follows: Radak: 'he did not perceive' covers even her rising, because of his drunkenness; the verse says it so that the reader knows that a waking father would have quarrelled. The English keeps the negation over both acts.
- `rashi:19:33:2` not-applicable: Rashi reads the difference between the two verses (the firstborn 'lay with' her father and the younger 'lay with him') as a moral choice of the text, which names the elder and spares the younger. The English renders both acts alike; the wording is a point of moral reading, not of the words.
- `rashi:19:33:3` declines: Rashi: the puncta on the ו of בּוּקוּמָהּ show that the father knew at her rising, though he did not guard himself the second night. The English declines this: the verb's negation governs both infinitives, as Radak's plain reading also takes it, and the pointing is a midrashic reading, not the plain sense.
- `radak:19:33:5` declines: Radak's note on the dotted ו gives the midrashic reading that the father knew at her rising. The English does not adopt it, for the reason given at rashi:19:33:3.
- apparatus: The ו of וּבְקוּמָהּ carries a puncta extraordinaria in the Masoretic text (the WLC note in the pack). Rashi and Radak cite the midrash that the point means he knew at her rising; the English declines it.

### Translation under review (19:38)

English: And the younger also bore a son, and named him Ben-Ammi; he is the father of the Ammonites to this day.

Phrases:
- 19:38.1 19:38.2 | And the younger also | 'and-the-younger also': the subject, with the conjunction and the adverb
- 19:38.3 | - | 'she': the emphatic pronoun, resumed by the subject 'the younger'; the English does not repeat it
- 19:38.4 19:38.5 | bore a son | 'bore son': the qatal, then the object
- 19:38.6 19:38.7 19:38.8 19:38.9 | and named him Ben-Ammi | 'and-named his-name Ben-Ammi': the idiom 'named him' for 'called his name'
- 19:38.10 19:38.11 | he is the father of | 'he father-of': a nominal clause with 'is' implicit
- 19:38.12 19:38.13 | the Ammonites | 'sons-of Ammon': the people, as in 19:37
- 19:38.14 19:38.15 | to this day | 'to the-day': the fixed phrase

Grammar notes:
- The verse repeats the frame of 19:37 with the younger daughter as subject. The emphatic pronoun גַם־הִוא is the one difference in the subject.
- The name בֶּן־עַמִּי is the younger's naming of her son; its meaning ('son of my people') is in the note, not in the English line.
- The Hebrew marks the end of a section after this verse (the ס in the Masoretic text), which closes the account of the two daughters.
- בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן is a collective: 'the sons of Ammon' is the people, so the English gives 'the Ammonites'.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:19:38:1` not-applicable: Radak reads the name as 'from my kin': the son was of her family. He takes the younger daughter to have been the more modest, since she did not disclose the matter to her father. The English keeps the transliteration; the reading is recorded here.
- `radak:19:38:2` follows: Radak: the people is called the sons of Ammon, which is why the English gives 'the Ammonites' for the Hebrew phrase.
