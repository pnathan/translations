- 20:17.9 20:17.10 | and his wife | 'and-(object marker)-his-wife': the marker has no English counterpart; the conjunction joins the second object
- 20:17.11 | and his maidservants | 'and-his-maidservants': the same object marker governs this noun
- 20:17.12 | gave birth | 'and-they-gave-birth': the English names the wife and the maidservants as the subject, which is Ibn Ezra's reading

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּתְפַּלֵּל and וַיִּרְפָּא are both wayyiqtol, the sequential form: each verb follows the one before in the narrative, so the prayer comes first and the healing follows it.
- The object of the healing is a list of three: Abimelech, then his wife and his maidservants, the second and third joined by וְ. The English repeats the conjunction and the nouns where the Hebrew has them only once.
- וַיֵּלֵדוּ is masculine plural, and its subject is not named in the clause. The nearest names are the wife and the maidservants. Ibn Ezra's reading, which the English follows, takes them as the subject; the verse's next clause names only the wombs of the house, not Abimelech.
- The etnaḥta on הָאֱלֹהִים (20:17.4) divides the verse after the prayer. The geresh on אֱלֹהִים (20:17.6) and the silluq on וַיֵּלֵדוּ (20:17.12) close the clauses of the healing and of the birth.

Translator's note: The English repeats 'his wife and his maidservants' after 'gave birth' so that the subject is explicit. This is the reading of Ibn Ezra (commentary); the Hebrew has only the masculine plural verb.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:20:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the verb returns to 'the wife of Abimelech and his maidservants, not to Abimelech' (שב אל אשת אבימלך ואמהותיו לא אל אבימלך). He supports this with other verses where a verb has a subject other than the nearest name, and with 20:18, which says 'the wombs of the house of Abimelech' and does not name Abimelech; he is long on this because some commentators held that Abimelech himself was held back. The English makes the wife and the maidservants the subject of 'gave birth'.
- `rashi:20:17:1` records: Rashi, after the Targum: 'they were relieved' (ואתרוחו): their passages were opened and they expelled, and this is their birth (והיא לדה שלהם). The English does not take the opening of passages; the verb is rendered 'gave birth' (ילד).
- `ramban:20:17:1` follows: Ramban: the verb means birth (לֵדָה), and not the opening of a passage, since 'womb' (רחם) cannot refer to another opening. He asks when the wombs were closed, and suggests the women were already in labour and could not deliver (אולי היו על פרקן ואחזום חבלי יולדה), with Abraham's prayer perhaps delayed for many days. He says Onkelos's 'relieved' takes the womb in its plain sense and includes Abimelech in the verb. The English takes the verb as birth.
- `radak:20:17:4` records: Radak: the subject may be the wife and the maidservants, or also Abimelech, and the matter is 'the emission of seed' (הוֹצָאַת זֶרַע). He allows that Onkelos's 'relieved' may mean birth, and cites the rabbinic reading of blocked passages as another possible background. The English follows Ibn Ezra's narrower subject and does not include Abimelech.
- `radak:20:17:2` not-applicable: Radak: the women had reached labour and could not deliver through that day, that night and the next day, though the palace physicians tried; the answer came after Abraham's prayer, and they knew it was 'the finger of God' (אֶצְבַּע אֱלֹהִים). - apparatus: Subject of וַיֵּלֵדוּ: the wife and the maidservants (Ibn Ezra, followed here); the household, Abimelech included (Onkelos, Ramban's reading of Onkelos, Radak's alternative). See the commentary.

Phrases:
- 20:18.1 20:18.2 20:18.3 20:18.4 | For the LORD had closed fast | 'for closing-fast closed the-LORD': the infinitive absolute intensifies the finite verb; 'for' gives the reason
- 20:18.5 20:18.6 20:18.7 | all the wombs | 'against all-the-womb': the preposition has no separate English; the verb's closing is over the wombs
- 20:18.8 20:18.9 | of the house of Abimelech | 'to-house-of Abimelech': the construct chain names the household

Grammar notes:
- כִּי here is causal ('for'). The verse gives the cause of the healing in 20:17: the wombs had been closed, so no one could bear.
- עָצֹר עָצַר: the infinitive absolute before the perfect of the same root makes the verb emphatic, 'had surely closed'. The English has no separate word for the emphasis, and the phrase 'closed fast' carries it.
- לְבֵית אֲבִימֶלֶךְ is a construct chain. 'House' here means household, the family in the king's power, and the English keeps the word 'house' for it.
- עַל דְּבַר שָׂרָה is an idiom built on the noun דָּבָר, 'on account of Sarah'. Rashi reads the noun as 'word'; that reading is in the commentary.
- The disjunctive accents divide the verse: the pashta on עָצַר (20:18.3), the tipḥa on רֶחֶם (20:18.7) and on שָׂרָה (20:18.12), and the silluq on אַבְרָהָם (20:18.14). The closed paragraph mark (ס) after the verse ends a section.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:20:18:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'all the wombs' (כל רחם) is an argument against those who held that Abimelech himself was restrained, and the verb וַיֵלֵדו refers to the women, as in 'ויחמו הצאן' (Genesis 30:39), where the verb is plural and its subject a collective. The English takes the wombs as the women's and does not make Abimelech the subject of the birth.
- `rashi:20:18:1` records: Rashi glosses בְּעַד as כְּנֶגֶד כָּל פֶּתַח, 'against every opening': the closing is a barring of each opening. The English keeps 'closed fast all the wombs' and does not adopt the gloss, so the preposition's sense is left open.
- `rashi:20:18:2` records: Rashi: עַל דִּבּוּרָהּ שֶׁל שָׂרָה, 'by the speech of Sarah'. He reads דָּבָר as 'word' (I), so that Sarah's speaking was the cause of the closing. The English follows the idiom 'on account of' (IV) and does not say what Sarah said.
- `radak:20:18:1` records: Radak: the closing was on the women, or also on the animals, as the sages said that a hen did not lay her egg (בְּנָשִׁים אוֹ גַּם בִּבְהֵמוֹת). The English 'all the wombs of the house' does not limit the wombs to women, and Radak's wider reading is recorded here; Ibn Ezra's reading is the one followed in 20:17.
- `sforno:20:18:1` not-applicable: Sforno: the closing was to bring death on the offspring if Abimelech did not repent, as God had warned him: 'know that you shall surely die, you and all that is yours' (20:7). This is the purpose of the closing, not a reading of the words.

### Translation under review (21:1)

English: The LORD attended to Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did for Sarah as he had spoken.

Literal: and-the-LORD attended-to (object marker) Sarah as-he-said, and-did the-LORD for-Sarah as-he-spoke

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 21:1.1 | and the LORD |  | I | The divine name with the conjunction, the subject of the clause. |
| 21:1.2 | attended to |  | Qal A.1.b | Qal of פָּקַד with the object marker: to attend to a person, to see to. Ramban's 'remembrance and oversight' is the same sense. |
| 21:1.3 | (object marker) |  |  |  |

Phrases:
- 21:1.1 21:1.2 21:1.3 21:1.4 | The LORD attended to Sarah | 'and-the-LORD attended-to (object marker) Sarah': the subject is the LORD; 'and' is not translated at the start of the sentence
- 21:1.5 21:1.6 | as he had said | 'as-he-said': the promise of 17:16 and 18:10–14
- 21:1.7 21:1.8 21:1.9 | and the LORD did for Sarah | 'and-did the-LORD for-Sarah': the second clause names its subject and object again
- 21:1.10 21:1.11 | as he had spoken | 'as-he-spoke': the same promise under the verb 'speak'

Grammar notes:
- The verse has two parallel clauses, each with כַּאֲשֶׁר and a qatal: 'as he had said' (אָמָר) and 'as he had spoken' (דִּבֵּר). The English keeps both verbs.
- פָּקַד here is attention, not visitation: its object is Sarah, and the sense is 'to attend to' (A.1.b).
- The second clause names the LORD again, so the verse ends with the LORD's act. The subject of the first clause is the LORD, so the sentence reads as one act with two halves.
- The accents divide the verse: the tevir on the divine name (21:1.1) opens the first clause, the etnaḥta on אָמָר (21:1.6) closes it, and the silluq on דִּבֵּר (21:1.11) closes the verse.

Commentary rulings:
- `ramban:21:1:1` follows: Ramban: פְּקִידָה is 'remembrance and oversight' (לְשׁוֹן זְכִירָה וְהַשְׁגָּחָה) over the one attended to, as in 'the LORD remembered Rachel'. He reads 'as he had said' as the conception and 'as he had spoken' as the birth, following Rashi. The English 'attended to' follows his sense of the verb; the split between the two clauses is not carried in the English.
- `rashi:21:1:1` not-applicable: Rashi: the verse is placed after the prayer to teach that one who prays for another is answered first. This is a homily, not the sense of the words.
- `rashi:21:1:2` records: Rashi: 'as he had said' (כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמָר) means as to the conception (בְּהֵרָיוֹן). The English leaves both clauses as 'as he had said' and 'as he had spoken' and does not assign them to conception and birth.
- `rashi:21:1:3` records: Rashi: 'as he had spoken' (כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר) means as to the birth (בְּלֵדָה), since the promise of the child is the speech of 17:19. The English does not assign the clause to the birth.
- `rashi:21:1:4` records: Rashi glosses לְשָׂרָה as לְאַבְרָהָם, 'for Abraham'. The English keeps 'for Sarah', as the Hebrew names her.
- `sforno:21:1:1` not-applicable: Sforno: Abraham prayed for Abimelech, and the LORD attended to Sarah, as 'the LORD returned the captivity of Job when he prayed for his friend' (Job 42:10). He links the two events; the English states only the words.
- `sforno:21:1:2` not-applicable: Sforno: 'as he had said' points to 'I will bless her' (17:16), by which the LORD removed from her the curse of Eve in conception, bearing and growth. A cross-reference.
- apparatus: 21:1.1: WLC points the name וַֽיהוָ֛ה (without the holem); the base text points וַֽיהֹוָ֛ה. The name is the same and the English is the same.

Grammar notes:
- צְחֹק (laughter) is the noun from the root צחק, the root of the name Isaac. Sarah's words give the reason for the name, which is explained in the verse itself and is not put into the English of 21:3.
- עָשָׂה has God as its subject and laughter as its object: 'God has made laughter for me'.
- כָּל־הַשֹּׁמֵעַ is 'everyone who hears', with the participle with the article. The yiqtol יִצְחַק is 'will laugh'.

- `rashi:21:6:1` records: Rashi: יִצְחַק means 'will rejoice over me' (יִשְׂמַח עָלַי). The rest of his comment is a homily on the barren women who were given children with her. The English keeps 'laugh', the sense of the qal in the lexicon.
- `radak:21:6:1` follows: Radak: she says the name is apt, since the birth is a wonder to her, and everyone who hears of it will laugh 'on my account' (בַּעֲבוּרִי), as one laughs at a thing that astonishes. The English 'laugh for me' keeps the dative open to this sense.
- `onkelos:21:6:1` records: Targum Onkelos renders צְחֹק as 'joy' (חֶדְוָא) and יִצְחַק as 'rejoice with me' (יֶחֱדֵּי לִי). The English keeps 'laughter' and 'laugh'.
- `sforno:21:6:1` not-applicable: Sforno: though circumcision brings pain to the child, God gave joy to her heart. This is a comment on her feeling, not on the words.
- `sforno:21:6:2` records: Sforno: the word means 'will rejoice on my behalf' (וְיִשְׂמַח בַּעֲדִי), and not be anxious about the pain of circumcision. The English keeps 'laugh'; the reading is recorded.
