### Translation under review (6:5)

English: The LORD saw that the evil of the human was great on the earth, and every inclination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil all day long.

Literal: and-saw the-LORD that great evil-of the-human in-the-earth and-every formation-of thoughts-of his-heart only evil all-the-day

Phrases:
- 6:5.1 6:5.2 | The LORD saw | 'and-saw the-LORD'; wayyiqtol opens the narrative
- 6:5.3 | that | 'that', introducing the content of the seeing
- 6:5.4 6:5.5 6:5.6 6:5.7 | the evil of the human was great on the earth | 'great evil-of the-human in-the-earth'; the English puts the verb 'was' where Hebrew has none
- 6:5.8 6:5.9 6:5.10 6:5.11 | and every inclination of the thoughts of his heart | 'and-every formation-of thoughts-of his-heart'; a chain of three constructs
- 6:5.12 6:5.13 | was only evil | 'only evil'; the copula is supplied
- 6:5.14 6:5.15 | all day long | 'all-the-day'

Grammar notes:
- וַיַּרְא is a wayyiqtol; כִּי introduces the object clause: what the LORD saw.
- רַבָּה רָעַת הָאָדָם: the predicate adjective comes first, and the verb 'to be' is not written. רָעַת is the construct of רָעָה.
- וְכׇל־יֵצֶר מַחְשְׁבֹת לִבּוֹ is a chain of constructs (every / inclination / of thoughts / of his heart) with no verb; רַק רַע is its predicate.
- The etnaḥta on בָּאָרֶץ divides the verse: the evil on the earth, and then the inward cause.

Translator's note: יֵצֶר is from יצר 'to form, shape'; Radak takes it as the desire of the heart's thinking, which forms and works out matters within the heart. The English 'inclination' follows that. הָאָדָם is 'the human', mankind generally (a collective), per decisions.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:6:5:3` follows: Radak: of the two inclinations in a person's heart, one good and one bad, here all was bad; the desire of the heart's thought is called יֵצֶר because the heart forms and works things out within itself.
- `sforno:6:5:1` not-applicable: Sforno: כִּי רַבָּה רָעַת הָאָדָם refers to the past (לְשֶׁעָבַר), and the next clause to the future; this bears on tense reading, not on the English words.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders the verse closely: 'and every inclination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil all day' (יִצְרָא מַחְשְׁבַת לִבֵּהּ לְחוֹד בִּישׁ כָּל יוֹמָא).

### Translation under review (6:6)

English: The LORD regretted that he had made the human on the earth, and was grieved in his heart.

Literal: and-regretted the-LORD that-made [object-mark] the-human in-the-earth and-grieved-himself to his-heart

- 6:6.8 | and was grieved | 'and-grieved-himself'; hitpael
- 6:6.9 6:6.10 | in his heart | 'to his-heart'; Rashi and Ramban take the heart as the seat of thought, so 'in his heart'

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּנָּחֶם is a Niphal wayyiqtol; the stem is reflexive-passive, 'he let himself be sorry'.
- וַיִּתְעַצֵּב is a Hithpael wayyiqtol, 'he pained himself'; it is the counterpart of the Niphal verb before it.
- אֶל־לִבּוֹ: the phrase is literally 'to his heart', the inward place of thought and feeling.
- The etnaḥta on בָּאָרֶץ divides the verse: the regret over the making, and then the grief.

Translator's note: The two verbs speak of God in human terms. Ibn Ezra, Radak and Ramban all say the Torah speaks in human language, so the English keeps the verbs plain ('regretted', 'was grieved'). Rashi also records the other sense of נִחוּם: a change of intention. The English keeps 'regretted'; the second Rashi reading is recorded. בָּאָרֶץ (on the earth) modifies the making: Radak reads it as made of earth.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:6:1` follows: Rashi: it was a נֶחָמָה (regret) before him that he made man among the lower beings, for had he been made from the upper beings he would have rebelled; the English adopts 'regretted' with the made-on-earth link.
- `rashi:6:6:2` records: Rashi, after Onkelos: 'it arose in the thought of the Place to grieve him' (the object is the human), and another reading: his thought turned from mercy to judgment, as every נִחוּם in Scripture means 'he took counsel what to do'. The English keeps 'regretted' and 'was grieved'; this alternative is kept here.
- `rashi:6:6:3` follows: Rashi: he mourned the loss of the work of his hands, as in 'the king was grieved (נֶעֱצַב) over his son'; the narrative of R. Joshua ben Korha shows that God's foreknowledge does not remove the grief.
- `ibn-ezra:6:6:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: it is known that God is not a human to regret; the Torah speaks in the language of humans, since a destroyer sees both what he made.
- `ibn-ezra:6:6:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: וַיִּתְעַצֵּב אֶל לִבּוֹ is the opposite of 'the LORD rejoices in his works' (Ps 104:31); he also rejects the view that 'his heart' means the prophet, for then the text would not say אֶל after the verb.
- `radak:6:6:9` records: Radak: בָּאָרֶץ means man mixed with the products of the earth, whose body is all earthly, unlike the heavenly image of God; this is why God did not regret making Noah.
- apparatus: Onkelos: 'the LORD turned in his Memra because he made man on the earth, and said in his Memra to break their strength as his will' (a paraphrase that avoids saying God regretted).

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:7:1` follows: Rashi: man is dust and God will bring water on him and wipe him out; this is why the verb is of 'wiping' (מִחוּי), which suits that image.
- `rashi:6:7:2` records: Rashi: the animals also corrupted their ways (Genesis Rabbah); another reading: everything was made for man, and with him gone what need is there for them. The English gives the plain list and records both reasons.
- `rashi:6:7:3` records: Rashi: כִּי נִחַמְתִּי means 'I have thought what to do about what I made', i.e. נִחוּם as a change of thought. The English keeps the plainer 'I regret'.

### Translation under review (6:8)

English: But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD.

Literal: and-Noah found favor in-eyes-of the-LORD

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6:8.1 | and Noah |  |  | the vav contrasts Noah with the human of the previous verse |
| 6:8.2 | found |  | Qal 1 | perfect, 3rd masculine singular, in a clause that begins with the subject |
| 6:8.3 | favor |  | 2 | 'favor, acceptance' |
| 6:8.4 | in the eyes of |  | 5 | idiom: 'in the eyes of' means 'in the opinion of' |
| 6:8.5 | the LORD |  |  |  |

Phrases:
- 6:8.1 | But Noah | 'and-Noah'; the contrastive vav
- 6:8.2 | found | 'found'
- 6:8.3 | favor | 'favor'
- 6:8.4 6:8.5 | in the eyes of the LORD | 'in-eyes-of the-LORD'

Grammar notes:
- וְנֹחַ מָצָא: the subject comes before the verb, so the clause is a contrast with the preceding judgment; hence 'But'.
- מָצָא חֵן בְּעֵינֵי is an idiom for 'was acceptable to', literally 'found favor in the eyes of'.
- The zaqef gadol on נֹחַ marks Noah as the subject standing apart, and the silluq closes the paragraph (פ).

Translator's note: The vav is rendered 'But', following the contrast. The lexicon's עַיִן senses list 5 for 'in the presence of', which is the idiom's sense; the sense 2.b etc. are for other uses.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:6:8:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: חֵן is from the root of 'mercy', and so תַּחֲנָה; the sense of 'finding favor' is like 'no eye had pity on you' (Ezekiel 16:5).
- `ramban:6:8:2` not-applicable: Ramban: all his deeds were pleasing before God, and this is set against the generation whose deeds caused grief; the next verse then says why.
- `sforno:6:8:1` not-applicable: Sforno: Noah's children were saved by favor, not desert, since he did not teach his generation to know God.
