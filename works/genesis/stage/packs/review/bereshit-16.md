### Translation under review (5:1)

English: This is the record of the descendants of Adam. When God created Adam, he made him in the likeness of God.

Literal: this book-of generations-of Adam in-day-of create-to God Adam in-likeness-of God made ACC-him

Phrases:
- 5:1.1 5:1.2 | This is the record of | 'this book-of'; a written account
- 5:1.3 5:1.4 | the descendants of Adam | 'generations-of Adam'
- 5:1.5 5:1.6 5:1.7 5:1.8 | When God created Adam | 'in-day-of create-to God Adam'; a temporal clause with an infinitive construct
- 5:1.9 5:1.10 5:1.11 5:1.12 | he made him in the likeness of God | 'in-likeness-of God made ACC-him'; English order is rearranged

Grammar notes:
- סֵפֶר תּוֹלְדֹת אָדָם is a construct chain: 'book of the generations of Adam'. The zaqef on סֵפֶר and the etnaḥta on אָדָם close the first clause.
- בְּיוֹם בְּרֹא is a temporal construct: the noun יוֹם stands bound to the infinitive בְּרֹא, with אֱלֹהִים as its subject and אָדָם as its object.
- The revia on בְּיוֹם opens the second clause; the pashta on אֱלֹהִים and the zaqef on אָדָם divide the creating from the making.
- בִּדְמוּת אֱלֹהִים is placed before the verb עָשָׂה for emphasis. The silluq falls on אֹתוֹ.
- בָּרָא (create) in the first clause and עָשָׂה (make) in the second are two verbs for one act.

Translator's note: אָדָם at 5:1.8 has no article; the sense 'the human' of Genesis 1 and 2 is possible, but the verse then names the person whose descendants follow, so 'Adam' is given and the doubt recorded.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:5:1:1` not-applicable: Rashi: 'this is the account of the generations of Adam', and there are many midrashic expositions. The first clause is read as a plain 'account' (סְפִירָה, counting); the midrash does not bear on the English.
- `rashi:5:1:2` not-applicable: Rashi: the verse teaches that on the very day he was created he fathered. This is a midrashic reading of בְּיוֹם; the English keeps the plain temporal clause 'when God created Adam'.
- `ibn-ezra:5:1:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the sons and grandsons he fathered; but he himself was not fathered by father and mother, rather made in the likeness of God (בדמות אלהים נעשה). The English reads the first clause as the record of Adam's descendants and keeps 'he made him'.
- `ramban:5:1:1` records: Ramban: the 'book' may hint at the whole Torah as the book of the generations of Adam; the clause 'when God created Adam' goes back to Adam himself, created from nothing. Recorded as an alternative reading of סֵפֶר.
- `radak:5:1:2` not-applicable: Radak: he was created in the likeness of an angel, by the upper soul placed in him. A theological gloss on דְּמוּת, not on the grammar.
- `sforno:5:1:2` not-applicable: Sforno: made with free choice, so that his generations were punished when they angered God.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders ברא by the divine name 'the LORD' (יְיָ) in this verse, and בִּדְמוּת אֱלֹהִים by 'in the likeness of God'; the Hebrew reads אֱלֹהִים in both places.

### Translation under review (5:2)

English: Male and female he created them, and he blessed them and called their name Adam, on the day they were created.

Literal: male and-female created-them and-blessed ACC-them and-called ACC-name-their Adam in-day-of be-created-they

- 5:2.10 5:2.11 | on the day they were created | 'in-day-of be-created-they'

Grammar notes:
- זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה stands first, before the verb, as the object of בְּרָאָם; the etnaḥta on בְּרָאָם closes the first half.
- וַיְבָרֶךְ and וַיִּקְרָא are wayyiqtols, a sequence of narrated acts, both with God as the unstated subject.
- The masculine plural suffixes on בְּרָאָם, אֹתָם, שְׁמָם and הִבָּרְאָם refer to the pair, male and female; the pair together is called אָדָם.
- בְּיוֹם הִבָּרְאָם is a temporal construct with a Niphal infinitive and a suffix (compare בְּיוֹם בְּרֹא in 5:1).

Translator's note: אָדָם here names both the man and the woman; Radak and Ramban take it as a name for the whole human kind, which the English keeps by leaving the name as 'Adam'.

Commentary rulings:
- `ramban:5:2:1` not-applicable: Ramban: God gave them the power of begetting, so that they would be blessed with many sons and daughters; they were not born but created from nothing.
- `ramban:5:2:3` follows: Ramban: since the name Adam covers the whole human kind, the verse says that God gave the first pair this name, for all of mankind was in them in potential.
- `radak:5:2:2` follows: Radak: on the day they were created he called them Adam; the woman is called Adam because 'let us make Adam' was said of both.
- `radak:5:2:1` not-applicable: Radak: the blessing is the one of Genesis 1:28, 'be fruitful and multiply'.
- apparatus: Onkelos: דְּכַר וְנוּקְבָא בְּרָאנוּן; the Aramaic agrees with the Hebrew in the order male-female.

### Translation under review (5:3)

English: Adam lived one hundred thirty years, and he fathered a son in his likeness, according to his image, and named him Seth.

Phrases:
- 5:3.1 5:3.2 | Adam lived | 'and-lived Adam'
- 5:3.3 5:3.4 5:3.5 | one hundred thirty years | 'thirty and-hundred year'; the smaller number stands first
- 5:3.6 | and he fathered a son | 'and-begot'; the Hebrew has no object, 'a son' is supplied from the context and from the name that follows
- 5:3.7 | in his likeness | 'in-likeness-his'
- 5:3.8 | according to his image | 'like-image-his'
- 5:3.9 5:3.10 5:3.11 | and named him | 'and-called ACC-name-his'; the object marker has no English counterpart
- 5:3.12 | Seth | the name

Grammar notes:
- וַיְחִי and וַיּוֹלֶד are wayyiqtols; the first has a shortened form because the root is final-ה, the second is Hiphil of ילד, 'begot'.
- שְׁלֹשִׁים וּמְאַת שָׁנָה: the smaller number comes first, then the hundred, then the noun in the singular, the usual form for ages.
- בִּדְמוּתוֹ כְּצַלְמוֹ reverses the words of 1:26 (בְּצַלְמֵנוּ כִּדְמוּתֵנוּ) and echoes 5:1: Adam was made in the likeness of God and Seth is begotten in the likeness of Adam.
- The etnaḥta on כְּצַלְמוֹ divides the begetting from the naming.

Translator's note: The verb וַיּוֹלֶד has no direct object; the English supplies 'a son', which the next clause confirms through the name Seth. The meaning of the name, as with the other names the text does not explain here, is not given.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:5:3:1` not-applicable: Rashi: up to here Scripture has spoken of the woman (Bereshit Rabbah); it is a midrashic remark on the number of years and does not bear on the plain sense.
- `ibn-ezra:5:3:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: why does it say 'he fathered in his likeness, according to his image'? Because God gave him the power of begetting, so that his work would resemble the one above; this is not said of Cain and Abel, whose names were blotted out in the flood.
- `ramban:5:3:1` follows: Ramban: all children resemble their parents, but because Adam was raised up in his likeness and image, the verse says that his descendants too were in that same elevated likeness.
- `radak:5:3:2` follows: Radak on בִּדְמוּתוֹ: the child resembled Adam in shape (בצורתו).
- `radak:5:3:3` records: Radak on כְּצַלְמוֹ: in his knowledge and intellect; he also quotes the midrash that Adam, while excommunicated, fathered spirits and demons. Recorded, not adopted.
- `sforno:5:3:1` not-applicable: Sforno: Seth was righteous among the earlier ones, for even Abel did not bring an offering until Cain had done so.
- apparatus: Onkelos: וְאוֹלִיד בִּדְמוּתֵהּ דְּדָמֵי לֵהּ, 'he fathered in his likeness, which resembled him', a gloss on the preposition and no separate word for 'image'.
