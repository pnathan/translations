### Translation under review (25:16)

English: These are the sons of Ishmael, and these are their names, in their villages and in their palaces: twelve princes to their tribes.

Grammar notes:
- אֵלֶּה הֵם is a verbless clause; the pronoun הֵם repeats the subject, and 'are' is the English copula.
- The second clause, 'and these are their names', is verbless too. The names are those of 25:13 to 15.
- The two locatives בְּחַצְרֵיהֶם וּבְטִירֹתָם are parallel, each with ב and a 3rd masculine plural suffix. The etnaḥta on וּבְטִירֹתָם closes them; the numeral and the title come after it.
- The numeral is compound, שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר ('two and ten'), with the plural noun נְשִׂיאִם; it stands in apposition to the names above.

Translator's note: Palaces follows Ibn Ezra's normative reading of טירות; the lexicon's 'encampment, battlement' and Radak's fortified cities are recorded in commentary. The lamed of לְאֻמֹּתָם is rendered 'to' per Ibn Ezra.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:25:16:1` not-applicable: Radak: the general statement 'these are the sons' comes after the particulars, a usual form in Scripture; the repetition adds clarity.
- `rashi:25:16:1` records: Rashi: חצרים are large cities without walls, and he takes the Targum's word for them as 'opened places'.
- `ibn-ezra:25:16:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: טירות are palaces (ארמונים), like the טירת כסף of the Song of Songs.
- `ibn-ezra:25:16:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: the lamed of לְאֻמֹּתָם is an added preposition, like the lamed of לְגוֹיֵיהֶם, not part of the root; the word is the root of 'nation' (as in Gen. 25:23), and the meaning is the same without the lamed.
- `radak:25:16:2` not-applicable: Radak: the repetition tells the reader that wherever a people is named with one of these names, it is one of Ishmael's sons.
- `radak:25:16:3` records: Radak: where their portion fell they lived in greatness, in their ḥatserim (open towns) and their tirot (great cities and fortresses).
- `radak:25:16:4` follows: Radak: each of the twelve was a prince and the head of a great family named after him, as Jetur Naphish in the war of the Hagrites; Kedar, Dumah, Massa and Tema are each named in the prophets as a separate nation.
- `onkelos:25:16:1` records: Onkelos renders the two locatives as 'open places' (פַּצְחֵיהוֹן) and 'great cities' (כַּרְכֵּיהוֹן).

Grammar notes:
- The age is given as three sums in sequence, a hundred years, and thirty years, and seven years; the English adds them as one figure, and the literal keeps the Hebrew's three.
- Three wayyiqtol verbs, וַיִּגְוַע וַיָּמָת וַיֵּאָסֶף, carry the sequence: 'expired', 'died', and the idiom of being gathered to one's people.
- The etnaḥta on שָׁנִים closes the clause of the age; the silluq on עַמָּיו closes the verse.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:25:17:1` not-applicable: Rashi (midrash): asks why Ishmael's years are counted, so that Jacob's years can be set against them; Jacob spent fourteen years in Eber's house before he left his father, since Ishmael died when Jacob left.
- `rashi:25:17:2` not-applicable: Rashi: the verb גְּוִיעָה is said only of the righteous.
- `ramban:25:17:1` not-applicable: Ramban: the plain sense is that the text gives the years of the righteous' offspring to show that the seed of the righteous is blessed; of the midrash's reasons for Ishmael's years, he finds most correct that Ishmael repented.
- `ramban:25:17:2` records: Ramban: גְּוִיעָה is death without painful illness or suffering, which only the righteous obtain; where the verb stands beside 'died' or 'gathered', it points to a righteous death.
- `radak:25:17:1` not-applicable: Radak: the years are recorded in Abraham's honour; the rabbis record them only to compare Jacob's years.
- `onkelos:25:17:1` records: Onkelos renders וַיִּגְוַע as וְאִתְנְגִיד, which Ramban glosses as a swoon (עִלּוּף).

Grammar notes:
- The verse is a wayyiqtol (וַיִּשְׁכְּנוּ) with the boundary formula 'from Havilah as far as Shur', then a relative clause placing Shur 'before Egypt', then a second boundary phrase, 'toward Asshur'.
- בֹּאֲכָה has a 2nd masculine singular suffix by the parse, while the narrative is in the third person; the English keeps the suffix, 'as you go', and the literal keeps 'your'.
- נָפָל is a qatal after a wayyiqtol series, so it reads as a separate statement rather than the next step of the dwelling. The third masculine singular is unnamed; the English 'he' is the person of the form.
- The etnaḥta on אַשּׁוּרָה closes the first half at Asshur, and the silluq on נָפָֽל closes the verse. The paragraph mark (פ) after it ends the section.

Translator's note: English 'fell' keeps the verb. Rashi, Radak and Onkelos read 'dwelt', and Ibn Ezra offers three readings (lot, movement, death in a brother's lifetime); all are recorded in commentary, since the words do not settle the sense. 'Before' is the idiom על פני, which leaves Ibn Ezra's death reading open without choosing it.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:25:18:1` records: Rashi: נָפָל here means 'dwelt' (שָׁכַן), as in the Midianites and Amalekites who lay in the valley (Judg. 7:12). He notes Gen. 16:12, 'before all his brothers he shall dwell': before Abraham died Ishmael dwelt, and after Abraham died he fell.
- `ibn-ezra:25:18:1` records: Ibn Ezra: 'all his brothers' (על פני כל אחיו) are Zimran and Jokshan.
- `ibn-ezra:25:18:2` records: Ibn Ezra gives three readings of נָפָל: his portion or lot fell to him; most of Ishmael's sons moved from place to place, as in 'to the Chaldeans you fall' (Jer. 37:13); or the verse speaks of Ishmael's death in one brother's lifetime, since the cause of his death is not known. He finds the second a little far, since his brothers are in the east and Egypt and Asshur are west of the Land.
- `radak:25:18:1` records: Radak: the sons and their generations dwelt from Havilah to Shur, for there their portion fell to them.
- `radak:25:18:2` records: Radak: the phrase 'before all his brothers' is as the angel told Hagar, 'before all his brothers he shall dwell' (Gen. 16:12); the comment is cut off in the pack.
- `radak:25:18:3` records: Radak: נָפָל means 'dwelt', as in 'lying in the valley' (Judg. 7:12).
- `onkelos:25:18:1` records: Onkelos renders נָפָל as שְׁרָא, 'dwelt'.
- apparatus: בֹּאֲכָה: the parse gives a 2nd masculine singular suffix, against the third-person narrative; the form is a crux, and the English keeps 'you' as the suffix gives it.
