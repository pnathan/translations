Grammar notes:
- The blessing is three imperatives: פְּרוּ, וּרְבוּ, וּמִלְאוּ.
- The etnaḥta on בָּנָיו divides the narration from the speech.
- וַיְבָרֶךְ is a Piel wayyiqtol; the verb of blessing takes the object marker twice, for Noah and for his sons.

Translator's note: The blessing repeats the language of 1:28 to the new beginning after the flood.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:9:1:1` not-applicable: Radak: though they were blessed at the creation, the world was now renewed after being tohu va-vohu under water; the blessing is 'be fruitful and multiply', and their fear and dread (9:2).
- `radak:9:1:2` not-applicable: Radak: He spoke to the sons through Noah, or the sons were prophets too.
- `onkelos:9:1:1` not-applicable: Onkelos renders 'and the LORD blessed Noah and his sons and said to them: be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth', following the Hebrew closely.

Grammar notes:
- וּמוֹרַאֲכֶם וְחִתְּכֶם is a hendiadys of two near synonyms: fear and dread.
- The mahpakh and pashta join the two nouns; the zaqef on יִהְיֶה closes the subject and verb.
- בְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר תִּרְמֹשׂ הָאֲדָמָה: the verb is feminine to agree with הָאֲדָמָה, so 'the ground' can be its subject or the place.

- `ibn-ezra:9:2:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: a doubled-root form from the root of תִּרְאוּ חֲתַת, the same as חִתָּתְכֶם.
- `ibn-ezra:9:2:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: the sense is 'on the ground', or else the ground is itself the acting subject; the English takes the first.
- `ibn-ezra:9:2:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: the sense of בְּיֶדְכֶם is 'because in your hand'; the English keeps the plain 'into your hand they are given'.

- apparatus: Onkelos: 'and the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon all beasts of the earth ...' reads the verse closely.

### Translation under review (9:4)

English: Only flesh with its life, its blood, you shall not eat.

Literal: only flesh in-its-life its-blood not you-shall-eat

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9:4.1 | only |  |  | restrictive particle |
| 9:4.2 | flesh |  | 2 |  |
| 9:4.3 | with its life |  | 1 | בְּ of accompaniment |
| 9:4.4 | its blood |  | 1 | construct form with suffix |
| 9:4.5 | not |  |  |  |
| 9:4.6 | you shall eat |  | Qal 1 | yiqtol, plural, prohibition |

Phrases:
- 9:4.1 9:4.2 | Only flesh | 'only flesh'
- 9:4.3 9:4.4 | with its life, its blood | 'in-its-life its-blood'; the second noun stands in apposition to the first, or the two are read as one clause
- 9:4.5 9:4.6 | you shall not eat | 'not you-shall-eat'

Grammar notes:
- אַךְ restricts the grant of v. 3 with a single exception.
- בְּנַפְשׁוֹ דָמוֹ: the two nouns are bare in apposition, with no conjunction between them.
- The negative with the yiqtol, לֹא תֹאכֵלוּ, is a prohibition.

Translator's note: The English keeps the Hebrew order and the juxtaposition נַפְשׁוֹ דָמוֹ, which supports both of the commentators' readings.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:4:1` follows: Rashi: this forbids a limb from a living animal, that is, you shall not eat the flesh while its life is in it.
- `rashi:9:4:2` follows: Rashi: 'while its life is still in it'.
- `rashi:9:4:3` follows: Rashi: a limb of a living animal is forbidden, and blood from a living animal as well.
- `ibn-ezra:9:4:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the sense is 'only flesh together with its life, which is its blood, you shall not eat', like Deut 12:23, since the life of all flesh is its blood; the English keeps both readings open with the apposition.

Grammar notes:
- The object, אֶת דִּמְכֶם, is fronted for emphasis and set against the earlier permission.
- אֶדְרֹשׁ is repeated three times, with אֶדְרְשֶׁנּוּ in the middle, a cumulative structure.
- The etnaḥta and zaqef divide the verse by the three sources of the demand: lives, animals, humans.
- מִיַּד means 'from the hand of', an idiom for 'from, at the hands of'.

Translator's note: The English keeps the sense Ibn Ezra gives (any blood of yours is not permitted; I will require it) rather than Rashi's limit to suicide. מִיַּד אִישׁ אָחִיו is rendered 'from each man for his brother', leaving 'brother' as a fellow human.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:5:1` records: Rashi: though I permitted the taking of an animal's life, I will require your blood from the one who sheds his own.
- `rashi:9:5:2` records: Rashi: this includes the one who strangles himself, even though no blood came out.
- `rashi:9:5:3` not-applicable: Rashi: because the generation of the flood sinned and were left to wild beasts, it was needful to warn the animals about them (Psalm 49).
- `rashi:9:5:4` follows: Rashi: from the one who kills deliberately where there are no witnesses, I will demand it.
- `rashi:9:5:5` records: Rashi: this speaks of a man who loved him as a brother and killed him by accident; I will demand an accounting if he does not confess and seek forgiveness, since even unintended killing needs atonement.
- `ibn-ezra:9:5:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: I have permitted you to shed the blood of every living creature except your own, as persons, which I have not permitted, but I will demand it; and he explains that this covers many killing one, or one killing one, and even animals commanded to kill. He finds Rashi's suicide reading forced.

Grammar notes:
- The verse is poetic: a chiasm of shedder and shed, blood and human, with a sound-play on שֹׁפֵךְ and יִשָּׁפֵךְ.
- The participle שֹׁפֵךְ opens a casus pendens, resumed by דָּמוֹ.
- בָּאָדָם can mean 'by a human (agent)' or 'for the sake of the human'.
- The noun clause כִּי בְּצֶלֶם אֱלֹהִים gives the ground for the penalty.

Translator's note: The English follows decisions (the human, with the article). 'By the human' is the plain agent reading; the other readings are kept in the commentary entries.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:6:1` follows: Rashi: if there are witnesses, you put him to death; why? because in the image of God ...
- `rashi:9:6:2` records: Rashi: this is an elliptical verse; it should read 'the Maker of the human' (הָעוֹשֶׂה); many verses are like this. The English reads the plain 'He made'.
- `ibn-ezra:9:6:1` records: Ibn Ezra: בָּאָדָם means by witnesses, or before everyone's eyes, or for the sake of the human whose blood was shed; many such uses exist. The English takes 'by the human' with the first.
