### Translation under review (13:13)

English: Now the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners against the LORD, exceedingly.

Literal: and-men-of Sodom wicked and-sinners to-the-LORD exceedingly

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13:13.1 | and the men of |  |  | construct plural of אִישׁ; the vav is circumstantial or contrastive: 'now' |
| 13:13.2 | Sodom |  |  |  |
| 13:13.3 | wicked |  | 10 | adjective; predicate with no verb 'to be' written |
| 13:13.4 | and sinners |  |  |  |
| 13:13.5 | to/against the LORD |  |  | ל with יהוה: 'in relation to the LORD', 'against' |
| 13:13.6 | exceedingly |  | 2 | the noun מְאֹד used as an adverb |

Phrases:
- 13:13.1 13:13.2 | Now the men of Sodom | 'and-men-of Sodom'; the vav marks a new circumstance after the separation of Abram and Lot
- 13:13.3 | were wicked | 'wicked'; the copula is supplied
- 13:13.4 13:13.5 | and sinners against the LORD | 'and-sinners to-the-LORD'; ל = 'toward, against'
- 13:13.6 | exceedingly | 'exceedingly'

Grammar notes:
- The verse is a verbless clause: subject אַנְשֵׁי סְדֹם, predicates רָעִים and חַטָּאִים.
- The etnaḥta falls on וְחַטָּאִים, so לַיהוָה מְאֹד is read as a closing phrase that intensifies both predicates.
- מְאֹד is a noun used adverbially, 'exceedingly'.

Translator's note: The adjectives are kept apart, 'wicked' and 'sinners', because Rashi and Ibn Ezra distinguish them: wicked toward people, sinning against the LORD. 'Against' renders ל as the context requires.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:13:13:1` not-applicable: Rashi: even so, Lot did not refrain from living among them; the rabbis learn from here 'the name of the wicked rots' (Prov 10:7). This is a comment on Lot, not on the words.
- `rashi:13:13:2` follows: Rashi: רעים בגופם, wicked in their bodies, that is in their persons. The English keeps 'wicked' and the note records the sense.
- `rashi:13:13:3` follows: Rashi: וחטאים במלונם, sinners in their wealth (Sanhedrin 109). This distinguishes the two terms; the English keeps them separate.
- `rashi:13:13:4` follows: Rashi: they knew their Master and intended to rebel against Him. This supports 'sinners against the LORD, exceedingly' as deliberate sin.
- `ibn-ezra:13:13:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: רעים is said of people, and compares Ezekiel 16:49, 'they did not strengthen the hand of the poor and needy'. So 'wicked' is toward other people, and 'sinners' toward the LORD.
- `radak:13:13:1` records: Radak: wicked toward heaven and toward people, all of it sin against the LORD, since they broke the commandments given to Adam and Noah.
- `ramban:13:13:2` not-applicable: Ramban: the verse blames Lot for living among them, and also shows the merit of the righteous, since the cities of the plain were destroyed.
- apparatus: 13:13.5 vowels: WLC reads לַיהוָה; the base text has לַיהֹוָה. The English is the same.
- apparatus: Onkelos: wicked 'in their wealth' and guilty 'in their bodies'; the reverse of Rashi's distribution.

- כַּעֲפַר הָאָרֶץ is a simile of number, not of substance.

- The apodosis גַּם־זַרְעֲךָ יִמָּנֶה has no particle of its own; גַּם marks the parallel.
- The wordplay on the root מנה: יוּכַל ... לִמְנוֹת, then יִמָּנֶה.

Translator's note: 'Dust' is the plain sense of עָפָר here; the verb שַׂמְתִּי is rendered 'make' in the sense 'turn into', as in 'make a nation'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:13:16:1` follows: Rashi: just as it is impossible for the dust to be counted, so your seed will not be counted. This is the English's reading of the conditional.
- `radak:13:16:1` records: Radak: a hyperbole, as in 'like the sand of the sea and the stars of heaven' (Gen 22:17); the midrash applies the dust to Israel when it does not do His will and the stars when it does.
- `radak:13:16:2` records: Radak: if this can be, so can that; that is, since the first is impossible so is the second. He compares Jer 13:23.
- apparatus: The sentence has a pause at ׀ after אֲשֶׁר (legarmeh in the base text); the reading follows the zaqef qaton after הָאָרֶץ.

Grammar notes:
- Four wayyiqtols in a row (וַיֶּאֱהַל, וַיָּבֹא, וַיֵּשֶׁב, וַיִּבֶן) give the narrative sequence of Abram's move.
- בְּאֵלֹנֵי מַמְרֵא: a construct chain; Mamre is a personal name, not a place.
- אֲשֶׁר בְּחֶבְרוֹן is a verbless relative clause on the oaks.
- The paragraph mark פ closes the unit.

Translator's note: I follow Rashi and Ibn Ezra on Mamre, taking it as a man's name and the oaks as the oaks in Hebron. The Hebrew verb is intransitive: 'he pitched tent', so 'moved his tent' is an interpretive rendering.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:13:18:1` follows: Rashi: מַמְרֵא is a man's name. So the English says 'the oaks of Mamre' as 'Mamre's oaks'.
- `ibn-ezra:13:18:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: here he explains that the oaks of Mamre are in Hebron. The English reads אֲשֶׁר בְּחֶבְרוֹן as modifying the oaks.
- `radak:13:18:2` records: Radak: these are not the oak of Moreh (Gen 12:6); there were two or more plains belonging to a man called Mamre, brother of Eshkol and Aner.
- `radak:13:18:1` follows: Radak: he took up his tent from there and pitched it from place to place until he arrived.
- `sforno:13:18:1` records: Sforno: he set his livestock's tents in order here and there.
- apparatus: 13:18.12 vowels: WLC reads לַיהוָה; the base text has לַיהֹוָה.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders אֵלֹנֵי as 'plains' (מֵישְׁרֵי) rather than 'oaks'.
