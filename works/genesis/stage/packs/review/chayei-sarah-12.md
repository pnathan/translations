Phrases:
- 24:65.1 24:65.2 24:65.3 | And she said to the servant | 'and-she-said to the-servant': the wayyiqtol gives the narrative sequence; the feminine verb names her as subject.
- 24:65.4 24:65.5 24:65.6 | Who is this man | 'who the-man this': an interrogative question about a person; 'is' is the English copula.

- 24:65.10 24:65.11 | And the servant said | 'and-said the-servant': the verb is followed by its subject.
- 24:65.12 24:65.13 | He is my lord | 'he my-lord': a verbless clause; 'is' is the English copula.
- 24:65.14 24:65.15 24:65.16 | And she took the veil and covered herself | 'and-she-took the-veil and-she-covered-herself': two wayyiqtol verbs, the second reflexive.

Grammar notes:
- וַתֹּאמֶר is a wayyiqtol (sequential imperfect); the English 'and she said' keeps its feminine subject.
- The interrogative מִי is followed by הַלָּזֶה and the attributive participle הַהֹלֵךְ, so the question reads 'who is this man walking in the field'.
- The etnaḥta on אֲדֹנִי closes the servant's answer; the merkha on וַתִּקַּח and the tipḥa on הַצָּעִיף carry the last clause to the silluq on וַתִּתְכָּס.
- וַתִּתְכָּס is hitpaʿel (reflexive), so the verb has no separate object: 'she covered herself'.

Translator's note: The two speeches are each introduced with a comma and close within this verse. 'Veil' renders צָעִיף, which the lexicon gives as wrapper, shawl or veil. Ibn Ezra's ruling on the binyan of וַתִּתְכָּס is followed; Radak's account of what happened before the question is recorded in the commentary, not the English.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:24:65:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: the form is of the hitpaʿel; its first tav is the feminine marker, which does not occur in the root, and its second tav is the hitpaʿel's.
- `rashi:24:65:1` follows: Rashi: the form is of the wattitpaʿel pattern, like the forms wattiqqaber and wattishshaber.
- `radak:24:65:1` not-applicable: Radak: before she fell from the camel she asked the servant who the man was, since she feared he was Isaac or one of his household.

Grammar notes:
- וַיְבִאֶהָ is a hifil wayyiqtol with the feminine suffix as object: 'brought her'.
- הָאֹהֱלָה שָׂרָה אִמּוֹ has no verb or preposition of its own; the directional he and the construct give 'into the tent of Sarah his mother' on Ibn Ezra's elided reading.
- וַיִּנָּחֵם is a niphal wayyiqtol, 'was comforted', from נָחַם; the sentence has a chain of wayyiqtol verbs.
- The etnaḥta on וַיֶּאֱהָבֶהָ divides the verse; the verse ends a section of the text (the paragraph mark after it).

Translator's note: 'Of Sarah' follows Ibn Ezra's reading of the words as an elided construct (the tent [of] Sarah his mother); Rashi's 'became like the tent of Sarah' is recorded. Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:24:67:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: a short way of speaking, 'the tent (of) Sarah his mother'; he cites the same ellipsis in 'the prophecy, Oded the prophet' (2 Chronicles 15:8) and 'your throne, God' (Psalm 45:7).
- `rashi:24:67:1` records: Rashi: the tent became like the tent of Sarah his mother, as if she were Sarah; the lamp lit from Sabbath to Sabbath, the blessing in the dough and the cloud over the tent, which had ended at Sarah's death, returned with Rebekah (Bereshit Rabbah).
- `rashi:24:67:2` records: Rashi: it is the custom of the land that while a man's mother lives he clings to her, and after her death he is comforted by his wife.
- `ramban:24:67:1` follows: Ramban: the construct is elided, 'the tent (of) Sarah his mother'; since Sarah died they did not pitch her tent, and Isaac brought Rebekah into it as a mark of honour to his mother.
- `onkelos:24:67:1` records: Onkelos: Isaac was comforted after the death of his mother (batar demitat immeh).
- `radak:24:67:5` not-applicable: Radak: 'and he loved her' means a greater love, because he saw in her good qualities and modesty like his mother's; most men love their wives.

Grammar notes:

Translator's note: Keturah is named as a wife, and the English does not identify her with Hagar: Ibn Ezra's ruling (normative) is followed. Rashi and Radak identify her with Hagar; both are recorded.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:25:1:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: Keturah is not Hagar. Verse 6 speaks of 'the sons of the concubines'; he takes pilegesh as a handmaid, a word that does not apply to a man, and so reads 'concubines' as women.
- `rashi:25:1:1` records: Rashi: Keturah is Hagar, so called because her deeds were fragrant like incense, and because she tied up her opening and did not couple with a man from the day she parted from Abraham.
- `radak:25:1:1` records: Radak: the sages say Keturah is Hagar and that Abraham had only one concubine, deriving this from the missing yod in 'the concubines' of verse 6; Radak found the yod in all accurate copies.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:25:3:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: some say Asshurim were men who knew the roads and Letushim were watchers from afar; he holds that they are names with no adjectival sense, and that is the correct view.

Phrases:
- 25:5.1 25:5.2 | And Abraham gave | 'and-gave Abraham': the wayyiqtol with its subject.
- 25:5.3 25:5.4 25:5.5 25:5.6 | all that is his | '[acc] all that-to-him': the object of the verb.
- 25:5.7 | Isaac | 'to-Isaac': the recipient.

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּתֵּן is the qal wayyiqtol of נָתַן, 'gave', with Abraham as subject.
- The object is כׇּל־אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ, 'all that is his', with the object marker before כׇּל; the recipient לְיִצְחָק comes last.
- The darga on וַיִּתֵּן and the silluq on לְיִצְחָק make a single clause.

Translator's note: 'All that is his' keeps the Hebrew without an explanation. Rashi's and Radak's accounts of the giving (a blessing handed on, a division of property during his life) are recorded in the commentary.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:25:5:1` not-applicable: Rashi, citing R. Nehemiah: the blessing is a diatiki (a testamentary deed). God told Abraham to be a blessing, the blessings were handed to him to give to whom he chose, and he gave them to Isaac.
- `radak:25:5:1` not-applicable: Radak: he divided his property during his life so there would be no dispute over the inheritance, and made Isaac heir, as God had told him that his seed would be called through Isaac.
