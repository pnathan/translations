Grammar notes:
- הֵן הָאָדָם הָיָה: הֵן 'look' introduces a statement of fact; הָיָה is a perfect, 'has become'.
- כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ: כְּאַחַד is the construct form (pataḥ), so 'like one of ...'; מִמֶּנּוּ is plural, 'from us'.
- וְעַתָּה פֶּן־יִשְׁלַח ... וְלָקַח ... וְאָכַל וָחַי: the clause after פֶּן is never completed; the weqatal verbs continue the feared sequence 'lest he stretch out ... and take ... and eat ... and live'.
- The accents divide the speech: zaqef on מִמֶּנּוּ, etnaḥta on וָרָע ends the first sentence; revia on יָדוֹ.
- וָחַי is from חיי, a by-form of חיה;.

Translator's note: The verse breaks off after 'lest': the English leaves the sentence unfinished as the Hebrew does. 'Look' renders הֵן. 'Us' follows Ibn Ezra: God speaks with the angels.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:22:1` records: Rashi: 'He is unique among the lower beings as I am unique among the upper; and what is his uniqueness? To know good and evil, which is not so of beast or animal.' The English 'like one of us' does not state this, which Ibn Ezra rules out on grammatical grounds.
- `rashi:3:22:2` follows: Rashi: once he lives forever he is near to leading creatures astray after him, to say that he too is divine; midrashic explanations exist but do not settle on the plain sense. The English keeps the feared outcome as the reason for the removal.
- `ibn-ezra:3:22:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: כְּאַחַד with pataḥ is the construct form (as in כְּאַחַד שבטי ישראל, 49:16), so it cannot mean 'like the unique one'; it means 'like one of ...'. He also remarks on the accents, which separate מִמֶּנּוּ from לָדַעַת (the pack's text is cut here).
- `ibn-ezra:3:22:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: מִמֶּנּוּ is plural, like אִישׁ מִמֶּנּוּ (23:6); the sense is like 'you shall be as gods, knowing good and evil', or it refers to God's own thought; compare 'Let us make man' and 'Come, let us go down'. This is God speaking with the angels.
- `radak:3:22:1` records: Radak: God includes himself with the angels, as in 'in our image' (1:26).
- `onkelos:3:22:1` records: Onkelos: 'the human was unique in the world, from him, to know good and evil'. This is the 'unique' reading, which the English does not adopt.
- apparatus: Onkelos reads הֲוָה יְחִידַי בְּעַלְמָא 'was unique in the world' for כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ; the English follows Ibn Ezra's grammar instead.
- apparatus: MAM has a paseq after וַיֹּאמֶר and after וְעַתָּה.

### Translation under review (3:23)

English: So the LORD God expelled him from the garden of Eden to work the ground from which he was taken.

Literal: and-sent-him the-LORD God from-garden-of Eden to-work [object-marker] the-ground which was-taken from-there

Phrases:
- 3:23.1 3:23.2 3:23.3 | So the LORD God expelled him | 'and-sent-him the-LORD God'
- 3:23.4 3:23.5 | from the garden of Eden | 'from-garden-of Eden'
- 3:23.6 3:23.7 3:23.8 | to work the ground | 'to-work [obj] the-ground'; לַעֲבֹד states the purpose
- 3:23.9 3:23.10 3:23.11 | from which he was taken | 'which was-taken from-there'; the antecedent of the 'he' is the human, who was taken from the ground

Grammar notes:
- וַיְשַׁלְּחֵהוּ is Piel with a suffix; with מִן it means to send away, to expel.
- לַעֲבֹד אֶת־הָאֲדָמָה: the infinitive with ל gives the purpose; the human is sent out to the same work he had in the garden (2:5, 2:15), now under the curse.
- לֻקַּח is Pual perfect: 'was taken'; the 'he' is the human, taken from the ground (2:7).
- Wordplay: הָאָדָם / הָאֲדָמָה.

Translator's note: 'Expelled' follows Ibn Ezra, who reads שלוח followed by מ as expulsion, like גרוש, not as an honorable sending off. The next verse repeats the sense with וַיְגָרֶשׁ.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:3:23:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: every שלוח followed by מ is like גרוש and is pejorative (Jer 15:1 'send them from before me'); it is not honorable, as a Spanish sage said, comparing 'to send them off' (18:16).
- `ibn-ezra:3:23:2` not-applicable: Ibn Ezra on the cherubim: they are the known angels and hold a flaming sword; this belongs to 3:24, not to this verse's words.
- `radak:3:23:1` follows: Radak: this is a sending of expulsion, as in 'send them from before me'.
- `sforno:3:23:1` records: Sforno: God ordered him to go from there, as in 'to send them out of the land' (Ex 12:33).

Grammar notes:
- וַיְגָרֶשׁ is Piel: 'drove out'; the stem is intensive and repeats the sense of וַיְשַׁלְּחֵהוּ.
- The verb וַיַּשְׁכֵּן (Hiphil of שכן, 'cause to dwell') comes before the place; מִקֶּדֶם is 'from the east', here 'on the east side'.
- The second object is marked with וְאֵת: 'and the flame of the sword'. הַמִּתְהַפֶּכֶת is a feminine participle agreeing with חֶרֶב.
- לִשְׁמֹר is an infinitive of purpose, 'to guard'; the accent zaqef gadol sets it off from what precedes.
- The paragraph mark ס closes the section after this verse.

Translator's note: 'Whirling' renders the Hithpael, 'turning itself this way and that'. Rashi takes לַהַט as the flame the sword has, to frighten; the English follows.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:3:24:1` follows: Rashi: 'in the east of the garden of Eden, outside the garden'.
- `rashi:3:24:2` follows: Rashi: the cherubim are 'angels of destruction' (מַלְאֲכֵי חַבָּלָה). The English keeps the name 'cherubim' and does not translate it.
- `rashi:3:24:3` follows: Rashi: the sword has a flame to threaten him from entering the garden again; the Targum renders לַהַט by שְׁנַן ('blade'); Rashi says he comes only to the plain sense, aggadic explanations aside.
- `ibn-ezra:3:24:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: מִקֶּדֶם means 'from the east'. The rest of his comment on the secret of the garden is not about the words of the verse.
- `radak:3:24:3` records: Radak: the flame is a sight, not a body, meant to frighten; the English does not say this.

Grammar notes:
- וְהָאָדָם יָדַע: the noun subject comes first and the verb is a perfect, so the clause is not in the line of narrative; Rashi reads it as a pluperfect: the knowing, the conception and the birth happened before the sin and the expulsion.
- יָדַע means 'know' of a husband and wife.
- קָנִיתִי אִישׁ אֶת־יְהוָה: Eve's words explain the name קַיִן, with wordplay on קָנִיתִי.
- אֶת־ before יְהוָה is not the object marker here; it is the preposition 'with' (the lexicon lists אֵת as a preposition).
- The accents: etnaḥta on אִשְׁתּוֹ ends the first half; zaqef qaton on קַיִן; zaqef gadol on וַתֹּאמֶר.

Translator's note: The pluperfect 'had known' and 'had conceived' follows Rashi. The meaning of Eve and Cain goes in the notes, not in the line.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:1:1` follows: Rashi: this happened before the matters above, before he sinned and was driven out of the garden; so also the conception and the birth. If it had said וַיֵּדַע אָדָם we would understand that children were born after the expulsion.
- `rashi:4:1:2` follows: Rashi: Cain is named after קָנִיתִי; the name goes in the note and grammar.
- `rashi:4:1:3` follows: Rashi: אֶת־יְהוָה is 'with the LORD': when God made me and my husband he alone made us, but in this one we are partners with him (b. Nid. 31).
- `rashi:4:1:4` not-applicable: Rashi: three 'et' marks (4:1, 4:2) are inclusive; a twin sister was born with Cain and two with Abel. This is midrash and does not bear on the plain sense.
- `ibn-ezra:4:1:1` not-applicable: Ibn Ezra: when he saw that his body would not last forever, he had to preserve the species; the name of God is not mentioned (אֱלֹהִים) because 'good' is general.
- `ramban:4:1:1` records: Ramban: against Rashi, he prefers 'this son will be my possession for the LORD', so that when we die he will serve his Maker in our place; he also allows 'with the LORD' as in 'Enoch walked with God'.
- `onkelos:4:1:1` records: Onkelos: 'I have acquired a man from before the LORD' (מִן קֳדָם יְיָ), which is a different reading of the preposition.
- apparatus: Onkelos reads קֳדָם יְיָ, 'before the LORD', Ramban 'for the LORD'; the English keeps Rashi's 'with'.

### Translation under review (4:2)

English: She went on to bear his brother, Abel. Abel was a herder of flocks, and Cain was a worker of the ground.

Literal: and-added to-bear [obj] his-brother [obj] Abel and-was Abel herder-of flock and-Cain was worker-of ground

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4:2.1 | and she added |  | Hiph 2.a | Hiphil wayyiqtol, 3rd feminine singular; with an infinitive, 'she did again' |
| 4:2.2 | to bear |  | Qal 1 | infinitive construct with ל |
| 4:2.3 | - |  |  | direct object marker |
| 4:2.4 | his brother |  | 1 | construct with a 3rd masculine singular suffix |
| 4:2.5 | - |  |  | direct object marker |

| 4:2.7 | and was |  | Qal III | wayyiqtol with a predicate |
| 4:2.8 | Abel |  |  |  |
| 4:2.9 | a herder of |  |  | participle in the construct state: 'one who herds' |
| 4:2.10 | flocks |  |  | collective: sheep and goats |
| 4:2.11 | and Cain |  |  |  |

Phrases:
- 4:2.1 4:2.2 | She went on to bear | 'and-added to-bear'; the idiom means she bore again
- 4:2.3 4:2.4 | his brother | '[obj] his-brother'
- 4:2.5 4:2.6 | Abel | '[obj] Abel'
- 4:2.7 4:2.8 | Abel was | 'and-was Abel'
- 4:2.9 4:2.10 | a herder of flocks | 'herder-of flock'
- 4:2.11 4:2.12 | and Cain was | 'and-Cain was'; the noun first gives a contrast
- 4:2.13 4:2.14 | a worker of the ground | 'worker-of ground'

Grammar notes:
- וַתֹּסֶף לָלֶדֶת: a Hiphil of יסף followed by an infinitive is an idiom, 'she did again to bear'.
- The sentence says that she added to the bearing, not that she conceived again; Radak and the Midrash take this to mean twins.
- רֹעֵה צֹאן and עֹבֵד אֲדָמָה are participles in the construct state, naming occupations; the contrast is marked by the order וְקַיִן הָיָה.
- Wordplay: אֲדָמָה recalls הָאָדָם (see 3:23).

Translator's note: 'Went on to bear' keeps the idiom without saying whether the second birth was a separate pregnancy. Rashi: Abel became a herder because the ground had been cursed.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:2:1` follows: Rashi: because the ground had been cursed, Abel turned away from working it; this is why he took to herding.
- `radak:4:2:1` records: Radak: since it says 'she added to bear' and not 'she conceived again', Cain and Abel were twins, as the rabbis said; the English does not say.
- `radak:4:2:3` records: Radak: when they grew up they divided their work; one herded for milk and wool, the other worked the ground.
- `onkelos:4:2:1` not-applicable: Onkelos: 'Abel was a herder of sheep, and Cain was a tiller of the ground'.

### Translation under review (4:3)

English: After some time, Cain brought an offering of the fruit of the ground to the LORD.

Literal: and-it-was at-end-of days and-brought Cain from-fruit-of the-ground offering to-the-LORD

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4:3.1 | and it was |  | Qal I.2.a.(1) | wayyiqtol; the narrative formula 'and it came to pass' |
| 4:3.2 | at the end of |  | 1 | מִקֵּץ: 'at the end of'; construct |
| 4:3.3 | days |  | 4 | 'a period of time', possibly a year (Radak) |
| 4:3.4 | and brought |  | Hiph 2 | Hiphil wayyiqtol: 'caused to come', 'brought' |
| 4:3.5 | Cain |  |  |  |
| 4:3.6 | from the fruit of |  | 1 | construct; מִן is partitive, 'some of' |
| 4:3.7 | the ground |  | 1 |  |
| 4:3.8 | an offering |  | 3 | מִנְחָה is a gift, here a gift to God; the later grain-offering sense does not apply |
| 4:3.9 | to the LORD |  |  | לַיהוָה |

Phrases:
- 4:3.1 4:3.2 4:3.3 | After some time | 'and-it-was at-end-of days'; ויהי is the narrative formula
- 4:3.4 4:3.5 | Cain brought | 'and-brought Cain'
- 4:3.6 4:3.7 4:3.8 | an offering of the fruit of the ground | 'from-fruit-of the-ground offering'; מִן is partitive
- 4:3.9 | to the LORD | 'to-the-LORD'

Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי מִקֵּץ יָמִים: 'and it was at the end of days': a narrative formula for an unspecified time; 'days' can mean a year (Radak, Ibn Ezra).
- מִפְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה: מִן is partitive, 'some of the fruit of the ground'; the noun מִנְחָה stands after it.
- Accents: etnaḥta on יָמִים, tevir on הָאֲדָמָה; geresh on קַיִן.
- The wayyiqtol וַיָּבֵא is Hiphil of בוא.

Translator's note: 'Offering' covers מִנְחָה, a gift to God; the word does not specify a grain offering. Rashi's 'from the inferior' is recorded, not adopted.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:4:3:1` records: Rashi: 'from the inferior'; one aggadah says it was flax seed. The Hebrew does not say; the English has 'of the fruit of the ground'.
- `ibn-ezra:4:3:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: after Cain worked the ground he brought the offering to the place he had set for his prayer; he rejects the view that it was brought to his father; since Abel's offering is 'of the firstlings of his flock', it implies that Cain did not bring of the firstfruits.
- `ramban:4:3:1` not-applicable: Ramban: these men understood a great secret of the sacrifices and offerings.
- `radak:4:3:1` records: Radak: 'days' may mean a year, as in 'for days (a year) it shall be his redemption' (Lev 25:29).
- `radak:4:3:2` records: Radak: the verse does not say which fruit; it seems to have been from the lesser, or the leftover after he ate; he did not bring from the firstfruits.
- apparatus: WLC reads לַֽיהוָֽה; the base text (MAM) reads לַֽיהֹוָֽה׃ (4:3.9 vowels).
- apparatus: Onkelos has מֵאִבָּא דְאַרְעָא 'of the fruit of the ground'.

- `sforno:4:4:1` follows: Sforno: Abel the offerer was acceptable and his offering also.
- apparatus: Ibn Ezra: וַיִּשַׁע means 'received'; Rashi: 'turned'. The English keeps 'turned to'.
