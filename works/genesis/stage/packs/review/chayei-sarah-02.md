- The two relatives אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ and אֲשֶׁר בִּקְצֵה שָׂדֵהוּ both describe the cave: 'which is his, which is at the end of his field'. The field is named as the cave's location.
- בְּכֶסֶף מָלֵא: the ב is the price, 'for full silver'. The verb יִתְּנֶנָּה (23:9.13) repeats the request; its feminine suffix refers back to מְעָרָה, the cave.
- The etnaḥta on שָׂדֵהוּ (23:9.10) divides the verse. לַאֲחֻזַּת קָבֶר, a noun phrase, ends it with the silluq on קָבֶר (23:9.17).

Translator's note: Abraham's speech opens in 23:8, in the previous unit, and closes at the end of this verse; the closing quotation mark is here. The Hebrew asks for the cave: Radak: Abraham asked only for the cave, not the field. The verb is 'give', not 'sell' (see Ramban). 'Full silver' is silver with nothing short (Ibn Ezra).

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:23:9:1` records: Ibn Ezra explains the name as 'a cave within a cave' (מערה בתוך מערה). The English keeps Machpelah as the name of the place, which the text uses as a name (23:17, 23:19), and the reading is kept here.
- `ibn-ezra:23:9:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: the opposite of מָלֵא is חָסֵר, 'lacking'. Full silver is silver with no shortfall, that is, full weight. The English keeps 'full' and the note gives the sense.
- `rashi:23:9:1` records: Rashi takes the word as a building, 'a house and an upper room on top of it', or as 'doubled in pairs' (Eruvin 53). The English keeps the name, and the reading is recorded.
- `rashi:23:9:2` follows: Rashi: מָלֵא means 'complete, all its worth', as David's 'for full silver' (1 Chronicles 21). This agrees with Ibn Ezra.
- `ramban:23:9:2` follows: Ramban: the verb is 'give', not 'sell' (לֹא הִזְכִּיר לְשׁוֹן מְכִירָה), since Abraham would count it a gift if he took it for full silver; or it is the idiom of sale (Deuteronomy 2:28). The English keeps the Hebrew's 'give'.
- `ramban:23:9:3` follows: Ramban rejects Rashi's building and the midrash of Adam's doubled body (Bereshit Rabbah). The name is the place's name, the field 'in Machpelah' (23:17). The English keeps the name.
- `radak:23:9:1` follows: Radak: Abraham asked from him only the cave, not the field (לֹא שָׁאַל מִמֶּנּוּ אֶלָּא הַמְּעָרָה). The English asks for the cave; the field appears only as its location.
- `radak:23:9:2` follows: Radak: the word is a name in the pattern of מִשְׁטַמָּה; as an adjective it would be spelled מְעָרָה with a final ה. He allows also that the place took its name from the doubled cave. The English keeps the name.
- `radak:23:9:3` follows: Radak: 'full silver' is silver full in its weight, lacking nothing, as merchants require. He speaks of a coin, which the Hebrew does not say; the context note on weighing applies. The sense agrees with Ibn Ezra.
- `onkelos:23:9:1` follows: Onkelos keeps the name in an Aramaic form, כָּפֶלְתָּא, and renders 'at the end of his field' as 'at the side of his field' (בִּסְטַר חַקְלֵיהּ). The English keeps 'end' and the name.
- `sforno:23:9:1` follows: Sforno: Machpelah is a place whose name it is, as in 'the field of Ephron which is in Machpelah' (23:17). The English keeps the name.
- `sforno:23:9:2` not-applicable: Sforno: Ephron would not spoil his inheritance by selling the cave at the edge of his field. This is a motive, not a reading of the words.
- `sforno:23:9:3` follows: Sforno: Abraham does not want the price lowered, out of respect for them. This agrees with 'full' as full, not short.
- `sforno:23:9:4` follows: Sforno: 'among you' means in your presence; Abraham asks no time to pay, and he weighed the silver before them (23:16). The English keeps 'among you'.
- `sforno:23:9:5` follows: Sforno: even if Ephron sells, he may later dispute Abraham's burial near his border, so Abraham asks that the sale be a possession for a grave. The English keeps the words 'as a possession of a grave'.
- apparatus: 23:9.14: the WLC vowels read לִ֛י; the base text reads לִּ֛י. The word is the same and the English is not affected.

Grammar notes:
- לֹא אֲדֹנִי שְׁמָעֵנִי: the negative stands before the vocative. The imperative שְׁמָעֵנִי has the first person suffix: 'hear me'.
- The three qatals נָתַתִּי (23:11.5, 23:11.11, 23:11.15) are completed acts within the speech, rendered 'I have given'.
- The second person suffix is feminine in לָךְ (23:11.6) and לָּךְ (23:11.16), while לְךָ (23:11.10) is masculine. The English 'you' serves for all three.

Translator's note: Ephron's speech. Its opening and closing quotation marks are both in this verse. Whether the 'no' refuses the price (Rashi, Radak) or is a courteous preface (Ibn Ezra) is not decided by the English. Whether the field and cave are a gift (Radak) is also left to the context of 23:15, where a price is named.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:23:11:1` records: Ibn Ezra glosses the 'no' as 'so, my lord; only that you hear me' (כֵּן אֲדֹנִי אַךְ שֶׁתִּשְׁמָעֵנִי): a courteous preface, after which the speech asks to be heard. The English keeps the Hebrew negative, and the reading is kept here.
- `rashi:23:11:1` records: Rashi: 'you shall not buy it for money' (לֹא תִּקְנֶה אוֹתָהּ בְּדָמִים). He reads the 'no' as refusing the price. The English keeps 'No' without saying what it refers to.
- `rashi:23:11:2` follows: Rashi: 'it is as though I had given it to you already'. The qatal is a completed act, and the English 'I have given' follows it.
- `radak:23:11:1` records: Radak: 'no', not for full silver as you say; I give it free, and both the field and the cave, not only the cave as you asked. The English does not say 'for nothing'; Radak's reading is recorded.
- `radak:23:11:2` follows: Radak: 'from now I have given it to you' (מֵעַתָּה נְתַתִּיהָ לְךָ). The English 'I have given' reads the act as made now.
- `radak:23:11:3` follows: Radak: 'in the sight of my people' so that they are all witnesses between Ephron and Abraham.
- `ramban:23:11:2` follows: Ramban: 'here are all the people, the knowers and the witnesses; do not be concerned about denial or retraction; bury your dead from now on, for it is yours.' Ramban adds that Abraham then took possession before the men of the city (23:17–18) before burying. The English keeps 'in the sight of my people'.
- `onkelos:23:11:1` records: Onkelos renders 'hear me' as 'accept from me' (קַבֵּיל מִנִּי), and keeps 'No' (לָא). The reading of 'hear me' is recorded.
- `sforno:23:11:1` records: Sforno: there is no need for the leaders of the assembled people to meet Abraham. The 'no' answers the request of 23:8 that the men of the town intercede, not the price.
- `sforno:23:11:2` follows: Sforno: 'hear me', you, without any intermediary (אַתָּה בִּלְתִּי שׁוּם אֶמְצָעִי). The English addresses Abraham directly.
- `sforno:23:11:3` follows: Sforno: the gift is in Ephron's intention since Abraham spoke (בְּמַחֲשַׁבְתִּי מֵאָז שֶׁדִּבַּרְתָּ). The perfect records a decision already made.
- `sforno:23:11:4` not-applicable: Sforno: it is not fitting that Abraham should cross another field to bury; so the cave and the field are given together. A reason, not a reading of the words.
- `sforno:23:11:5` declines: Sforno: 'in their sight I say I give to you on condition that you can bury your dead there.' The Hebrew states no condition; the English gives the words of the command only.

- וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ is a hitpaʿel wayyiqtol: 'and he bowed down'. The verse is one sequential clause.
- לִפְנֵי: 'before', from פְּנֵי, the construct plural of פָּנֶה; the prepositional phrase gives the place of the bow.
- עַם הָאָרֶץ: a fixed phrase for the men of the land. It is used again in 23:13 and is the audience of Ephron's answer.
- The pashta on וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ (23:12.1) and the silluq on הָאָרֶץ (23:12.5) mark the verse's two parts.

Translator's note: The Hebrew names the people, not the one bowed to. Ibn Ezra takes the bow to be before the people, given to Ephron as praise; Radak takes it to be to Ephron. The English keeps the words.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:23:12:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'this bowing was before the people of the land, to Ephron, to give him praise' (לְעֶפְרוֹן לָתֵת שֶׁבַח לוֹ). The English keeps 'before the people of the land' as the words have it; that the bow honours Ephron is the commentary's reading.
- `radak:23:12:1` records: Radak: Abraham bowed to Ephron before them all (לְעֶפְרוֹן הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה לִפְנֵי כֻלָּם). The English names no object, so the reading is recorded here.
- `onkelos:23:12:1` follows: Onkelos: 'and Abraham bowed before the people of the land' (וּסְגִיד אַבְרָהָם קֳדָם עַמָּא דְאַרְעָא). The same words, with no object named.
- `sforno:23:12:1` records: Sforno: the bow was thanks to them, since Ephron agreed to the request out of respect for them. A reason; the English gives only the act.

Grammar notes:
- וַיְדַבֵּר ... לֵאמֹר: the speech introduction. The quotation opens here, at the start of Abraham's speech.
- אַךְ אִם ... לוּ שְׁמָעֵנִי: 'but if you would hear me'. The particle אַךְ restricts the condition, and לוּ with the imperative makes it a wish.
- נָתַתִּי כֶּסֶף הַשָּׂדֶה: the qatal is performative, 'I give the price of the field'. Rashi reads 'ready with me'.
- קַח מִמֶּנִּי: the imperative with מִן, 'take from me'. The object, the silver, is understood from the clause before.
- וְאֶקְבְּרָה: a cohortative after ו, 'and I will bury'; the burial is the result of taking the price.

Translator's note: The English 'I give' takes the qatal as performative, as Radak does ('from now I will give'). Ibn Ezra's second reading, that the silver is already given, is recorded in the commentary. 'Take from me' carries no object: the Hebrew names none in the verb.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:23:13:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'if you do so, you will hear me'; or, as another reading, 'if you do so, I have given the silver of the field' (נָתַתִּי כֶּסֶף הַשָּׂדֶה). The English follows the first, as a condition on hearing. The second, the silver already given, is recorded here; the English 'I give' does not take the qatal as past.
- `rashi:23:13:1` records: Rashi reads the clause as Abraham's refusal of a free gift: 'you tell me to listen to you and take it for nothing; I am not willing'. The English keeps the offer of a price; Rashi's reading is recorded.
- `rashi:23:13:2` follows: Rashi: 'would that you would hear me' (הַלְוַאי וְתִשְׁמָעֵנִי). This is the wish of לוּ, which the English renders 'if you would hear me'.
- `rashi:23:13:3` records: Rashi glosses נָתַתִּי as 'ready with me', and adds 'would that I had already given it to you'. The English 'I give' keeps 'ready'; the past wish is recorded here.
- `ramban:23:13:1` follows: Ramban: אִם אַתָּה לוּ is a doubled condition for emphasis, 'if you, if you would hear me'. The English keeps one 'if' and stresses 'you'.
- `radak:23:13:1` follows: Radak: 'but, would that you would hear me' (הַלְוַאי שֶׁתִּשְׁמָעֵנִי). The English follows the wish.

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּשְׁקֹל ... אֶת־הַכֶּסֶף: 'weighed out' with the silver as object. The price is weighed, not counted.
- The silver is named twice: 'the silver which he had spoken' and 'four hundred shekels of silver'. The second names the amount.
- אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר בְּאׇזְנֵי בְנֵי־חֵת: the relative clause in the qatal: the amount was named before the Hittites (23:10, 23:13).
- עֹבֵר לַסֹּחֵר: a participle with לְ and a participle used as a noun, 'passing to the merchant': silver the merchant accepts.
- The tipḥa on עֹבֵר (23:16.19) and the silluq on לַסֹּחֵר (23:16.20) end the verse.

Translator's note: The English 'that passes with the merchant' is the merchant's acceptance of the silver. Ibn Ezra reads the phrase as 'chosen silver', and Radak as full weight; the English keeps the merchant's acceptance, which both readings allow. The weighing of silver before coined money is in the context.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:23:16:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: he takes only chosen silver (שֶׁלֹּא יִקַּח כִּי אִם כֶּסֶף נִבְחָר). The English 'silver that passes with the merchant' is silver the merchant accepts, which is the chosen quality the commentary names.
- `rashi:23:16:1` records: Rashi: Abraham paid the full amount, since 'even a little' is not enough to a man of his kind (Bava Metzia 87); the shekels were the large weights called kantars in some places, and accepted by weight everywhere (Bekhorot 50). The English keeps 'shekels'; the reading is recorded.
- `radak:23:16:1` follows: Radak: 'silver full by weight, as the merchants deal in it, for they are exact about the weight of silver'. The English 'passes with the merchant' is the merchants' acceptance.
- `onkelos:23:16:1` follows: Onkelos: 'shekels of silver which are current as merchandise in every province' (סְחוֹרָא). The English follows his sense of current silver.
- `sforno:23:16:1` records: Sforno: Abraham agreed to Ephron's view of the field's value (הִסְכִּים לְדַעְתּוֹ בְּעֵרֶךְ הַשָּׂדֶה). The English keeps 'listened to'; the agreement is recorded.
- `sforno:23:16:2` follows: Sforno: וַיִּשְׁקֹל means 'paid', as in 'a talent of silver you shall weigh' (1 Kings 20:39). The English 'weighed out' keeps the payment by weight.
- apparatus: Rashi remarks on a missing vav in this phrase (חָסֵר וָי״ו) and draws a lesson from it. The defective spelling of the name, לְעֶפְרֹן (23:16.7), is the same name; the English is unaffected.
