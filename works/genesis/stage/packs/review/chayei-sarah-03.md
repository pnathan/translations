Grammar notes:
- The verse is a chain of nouns, each with an אֲשֶׁר clause that attaches it to the nearest place or thing: 'in Machpelah', 'before Mamre', 'in it', 'in the field', 'within its border'. The English uses 'which' for each.
- The verb וַיָּקָם is a wayyiqtol: it continues the narrative in the past, and its subject follows it. The subject is a compound: the field (23:17.2), and the cave and the trees added by וְ and וְכָל.
- הַשָּׂדֶה in 23:17.9 repeats the field of the subject, as an apposition: the field, and then the cave and the trees that are in it and in its border.
- Accents: the etnaḥta on מַמְרֵא (23:17.8) divides the verse, after the place names; the silluq on סָבִיב (23:17.20) ends it.

Translator's note: The English puts the verb after the place names, as the sense of the whole sentence requires. The Hebrew gives no preposition for the buyer in this verse; 'to Abraham' comes in 23:18, and the English adds nothing here.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:23:17:1` follows: Rashi says the field's standing (תְּקוּמָה) was that it passed from a commoner's hand to a king's, which is a midrashic remark; his plain sense is that the field, the cave and the trees stand for Abraham as a purchase. The English follows the plain sense (the sale stood good) and not the king.
- `ibn-ezra:23:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra, normative: the field was confirmed and stood as a purchase (נתקיים ועמד השדה למקנה). He joins this verse to the one before, 'I am a stranger and a settler' (23:4), and says the ten things the Gaon names do not belong to this sentence. His reason after 'וטעם' is cut off in the fetched text. The English adopts his reading of קוּם as 'stood' in the sense of standing good.
- `radak:23:17:1` follows: Radak: it stood for Abraham as a purchase, like 'and it shall stand to him' (Lev 27:19 as he cites it), that is, it was confirmed in his hand. This supports 'stood' as standing good.
- `radak:23:17:2` records: Radak: the field and the cave lay before the city, and the city is called Mamre and is also called Hebron, as in 'on the face of Mamre, that is Hebron' (23:19). The English keeps 'before Mamre' and names Hebron in 23:19.
- `onkelos:23:17:1` follows: Targum Onkelos renders the verb as וְקָם, 'stood', and the field as חֲקַל, 'field', and keeps the cave and the trees. It agrees with the English.
- `sforno:23:17:1` records: Sforno: the field was confirmed by the seals of the deed of purchase (נִתְקַיֵּם בְּחוֹתָמָיו שֶׁל סֵפֶר הַמִּקְנָה). This says how it was confirmed, an account of what happened; the English keeps 'stood' and does not name the seals.
- apparatus: The sense 'stood good' (a legal sense) is the reading taken. The Hebrew of the verse does not say how it was confirmed; Sforno's seals and Radak's 'in his hand' are readings of that, not of the words.

Grammar notes:
- The verse has no finite verb. It is a set of prepositional phrases attached to the deed of 23:17: to Abraham, as a purchase, before the Hittites, among the people at the gate.
- בָּאֵי שַׁעַר עִירוֹ is a participle in the construct: 'those coming in' at the gate of his city. The clause names the people who went through the gate, the public of the city.
- לְעֵינֵי is a dual construct form ('the eyes of'), used as a fixed phrase meaning 'in the sight of'.
- Accents: merkha on לְאַבְרָהָם, tipḥa on לְמִקְנָה, munaḥ on לְעֵינֵי, and etnaḥta on חֵ֑ת (23:18.5) divide the verse; the silluq on עִירֽוֹ ends it.

Translator's note: The verse is a fragment with no verb. The English keeps it as a fragment; the sense of the verb comes from 23:17.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:23:18:1` follows: Ibn Ezra, normative: 'in the sight of the sons of Heth' means 'the men of the place' (אֶת אַנְשֵׁי הַמָּקוֹם). The sons of Heth are the local population. His reason after 'וטעם' is cut off in the fetched text. The English keeps 'the Hittites' as the name of that group.
- `ibn-ezra:23:18:2` follows: Ibn Ezra, normative: 'all who came in at the gate of his city' means 'every one who passes and returns' (כָּל עוֹבֵר וָשָׁב). The phrase is everyone who went through the gate. The English keeps 'came in at the gate' and adopts his reading of who the group is.
- `rashi:23:18:1` follows: Rashi reads בְּכֹל as 'in the midst of all of them, and in the presence of all of them he acquired it for him' (בְּקֶרֶב כֻּלָּם וּבְמַעֲמַד כֻּלָּם הִקְנָהוּ לוֹ). The English takes the preposition as 'among' in his first sense.
- `radak:23:18:1` records: Radak: Abraham weighed the silver before them, Ephron sold to him before them, and the deed of purchase was made. He takes the witnesses to be present at the weighing and the sale. That is an account of what happened, so it stays in the note; the English keeps the words.
- `radak:23:18:2` follows: Radak: all who came in at the gate of his city, who were there at the time of the purchase. He takes the group as the people present as witnesses, which the English keeps.
- `radak:23:18:3` not-applicable: Radak notes a pesek on the word בְּכֹל and cites the same form in Exodus 27:19 (לְכֹל כְּלֵי הַמִּשְׁכָּן). It is a note on the division of the word and does not bear on the sense.
- `onkelos:23:18:1` follows: Onkelos renders למקנה as לִזְבִינוֹהִי, 'to buy it', which supports 'purchase'. He renders בְּנֵי־חֵת as בְּנֵי חִתָּאָה, and reads בָּאֵי שַׁעַר as 'all who go up to the gate' (עָלֵי תְּרַע). The English keeps 'came in at the gate'.

- `ibn-ezra:23:19:1` follows: Ibn Ezra, normative: from then on the field stood as a burial holding for him and his seed (ומאז נתקיים השדה לאחוזת קבר לו ולזרעו). He reads 'after that' as 'from then on', which the English's 'afterwards' allows. The purposes he gives, to show the excellence of the land of Israel for the living and the dead and to keep God's word that Abraham should have a possession, are homily and are not in the English.
- `ramban:23:19:2` follows: Ramban: 'in the land of Canaan' is written to show the place is in the land of Canaan, in the mountain where the Canaanite dwells, and not in the land of the Philistines, where Abraham had lived many days (Gen 21:34 as he cites it). He says the Hittites are of the families of Canaan. The English follows his plain point about the land. His remarks on Abraham's conduct and on burial in the land of Israel are homily.
- `radak:23:19:1` records: Radak: אֶל מְעָרַת means 'in the cave' (כְּמוֹ בִּמְעָרַת), as 'to the ark' means 'in the ark' (Exod 25:21 as he cites it). The English keeps 'into', the sense of motion; his 'in' is recorded here.
- `onkelos:23:19:1` records: Targum Onkelos renders the phrase as 'in the cave of the field of Machpelah' (בִּמְעָרַת חֲקַל כָּפֶלְתָּא), reading אֶל as 'in', as Radak does. The English keeps 'into'; the other reading is recorded here. The rest of his rendering, 'on the face of Mamre' and 'in the land of Canaan', agrees with the English.
- apparatus: The feminine pronoun is read as agreeing with the city Kiriath-arba, and 'that is Hebron' follows the identification in Ramban's quotation. Radak identifies Mamre and Hebron as one city (see 23:17).

Grammar notes:
- The verse returns to the verb of 23:17, 'and stood', with the field and the cave as the subject. The verb is masculine singular for a compound subject, as in 23:17.
- לַאֲחֻזַּת קָבֶר is 'for a holding of a grave': the lamed marks the destination, and the construct 'holding of' comes before 'grave'.
- The source is named as 'from the sons of Heth' (מֵאֵת), not as Ephron, who is the seller named in 23:17. The English keeps the Hebrew's source.
