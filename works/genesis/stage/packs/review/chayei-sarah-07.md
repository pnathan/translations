### Translation under review (24:25)

English: She said to him, "Also straw, also fodder, plenty with us, also room to lodge."

Literal: and-said she to-him also straw also fodder much with-us also place to-lodge

| 24:25.10 | room |  | 5.a |  |

Phrases:
- 24:25.1 24:25.2 | She said to him | 'and-said she to-him': the wayyiqtol continues the answer
- 24:25.3 24:25.4 | Also straw | 'also straw': the first of four gam, each set before its item
- 24:25.5 24:25.6 | also fodder | 'also fodder'
- 24:25.7 24:25.8 | plenty with us | 'much with-us': a verbless clause, kept verbless in the English
- 24:25.9 24:25.10 24:25.11 | also room to lodge | 'also place to-lodge': the last item, a place to spend the night

Grammar notes:
- וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו is a wayyiqtol: the answer comes straight after the question, with no new subject named.
- Four גַּם stand in the answer, each before its own item (straw, fodder, room); the English keeps all four so that the list reads as the Hebrew gives it.
- רַב עִמָּנוּ has no verb; the English keeps it verbless as "plenty with us".
- The etnaḥta on עִמָּנוּ divides the answer: the feed for the animals before it, the room for the night after it.

Translator's note: The answer gives feed and room, more than a yes or no. Radak's reading that she answered beyond the question is recorded in the commentary, not in the English.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:24:25:1` follows: Rashi: every food of the camels is called מִסְפּוֹא, such as straw and barley.
- `radak:24:25:1` not-applicable: Radak: she answered more than he asked, to show that she was generous of heart.
- `radak:24:25:3` follows: Radak: מִסְפּוֹא is the general word for beasts' food other than straw, that is barley and foxtail.
- `radak:24:25:4` follows: Radak: the repeated גַּם is the idiom of the language, each item adding to the one before.
- `sforno:24:25:1` records: Sforno: the גַּם is not only a place for the camels; there is also straw and fodder to feed them.
- `sforno:24:25:2` records: Sforno: the room is for you and your men.

### Translation under review (24:26)

English: The man bowed down and prostrated himself to the LORD.

Literal: and-bowed the-man and-prostrated-himself to-the-LORD

| token | gloss | lex | sense | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24:26.1 | and bowed down |  |  | wayyiqtol; the first of two acts |
| 24:26.2 | the man |  |  |  |
| 24:26.3 | and prostrated himself |  | Hithpa‛lēl 1 | hitpaʿel, reflexive: he bows himself to the ground |
| 24:26.4 | to the LORD |  | I |  |

Phrases:
- 24:26.1 24:26.2 | The man bowed down | 'and-bowed the-man': the first act, a wayyiqtol
- 24:26.3 | and prostrated himself | 'and-prostrated-himself': the hitpaʿel, the second act
- 24:26.4 | to the LORD | 'to the-LORD': the one worshipped, with ל

Grammar notes:
- Two wayyiqtol verbs in sequence: the man first bows down (קָדַד), then prostrates himself (שָׁחָה).
- הִשְׁתַּחֲוָיָה is the hitpaʿel of שָׁחָה; the form is reflexive, so the English has "himself".
- The ל of לַיהוָה marks the one worshipped; the English keeps "to" as the Hebrew has it.
- The munaḥ on וַיִּקֹּד, the tipḥa on וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ and the silluq on the name divide the verse into its two acts and the name.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:24:26:1` follows: Radak: הִשְׁתַּחֲוָיָה is more than קִידָה; the sages say קִידָה is bowing on the face and הִשְׁתַּחֲוָיָה is spreading out the hands and feet. He thanked God that his way succeeded.
- apparatus: 24:26.4 vowels: WLC reads לַֽיהוָֽה; the base text reads לַֽיהֹוָֽה׃ (the same name, differently pointed).

Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי כִּרְאֹת is "when he saw": the wayehi with a temporal infinitive; the English does not render "it was".
- The two object markers אֵת before הַנֶּזֶם and הַצְּמִדִים are not rendered; the English has "the ring and the bracelets".
- The second clause, וּכְשָׁמְעוֹ, repeats the first: "and when he heard" the words of Rebekah.
- לֵאמֹר introduces Rebekah's words, which are quoted inside the narrative; the speech closes before "he came".
- The etnaḥta on הָאִישׁ (24:30.19) divides the two clauses; the silluq on הָעָיִן ends the verse with the scene.

Translator's note: The sentence is long because the Hebrew is: when he saw, when he heard, he came, and look. The English keeps that order. The subject of עֹמֵד is the man, not Laban.

### Translation under review (24:31)

English: He said, "Come in, blessed of the LORD! Why do you stand outside? Now I have cleared the house and a place for the camels."

Literal: and-said come blessed the-LORD why you-will-stand outside and-I cleared the-house and-place for-the-camels

- 24:31.2 | Come in | 'come': the imperative, with 'in' for the lexicon sense
- 24:31.3 24:31.4 | blessed of the LORD | 'blessed the-LORD'

Grammar notes:
- בּוֹא בְּרוּךְ is an imperative with a participle: "come in, blessed of the LORD".
- לָמָּה is "why": the interrogative מָה with the preposition ל.
- תַעֲמֹד is a yiqtol, 2ms; "do you stand" is the English form for a question in the present.
- פִּנִּיתִי is a piʿel perfect; the lexicon has no piʿel sense, so the English "cleared" comes from the sense of the clause.
- The zaqef gadol on וַיֹּאמֶר marks the speech; the etnaḥta on יְהוָה divides the blessing from the question; the silluq on לַגְּמַלִּים ends the verse.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:24:31:1` follows: Radak: "blessed of the LORD" means that the LORD has blessed him with wealth, as Laban sees it.
- `rashi:24:31:1` declines: Rashi: he cleared the house of idolatry, from Bereshit Rabbah; this is midrash, not the plain sense of the verb.
- `radak:24:31:2` not-applicable: Radak: the house was cleared for him and his men, and a place for the camels too.
- `sforno:24:31:1` not-applicable: Sforno: since you asked only for a place to lodge the camels, why do you want to stand outside, you and your men?
- apparatus: פִּנִּיתִי: the lexicon entry for פָּנָה lists no piʿel sense; the English takes its sense from the context.

Grammar notes:
- The wayyiqtol chain וַיָּבֹא, וַיְפַתַּח, וַיִּתֵּן keeps the order of the actions: came, loosened, gave.
- וַיְפַתַּח is a piʿel: "loosened". The lexicon gives "loosen" for this sense; the object is the camels.
- לִרְחֹץ is an infinitive construct after מַיִם: "water to wash".
- אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ is a relative with אֵת: "who with him"; the English keeps "with him" without a relative verb.
- The pashta on וּמִסְפּוֹא divides the gift of feed from the water; the silluq on אִתּוֹ ends the verse.

Translator's note: The subject of וַיְפַתַּח and of the two following verbs is not named. The English leaves it open, as the Hebrew does. Ramban reads the subject as Laban (commentary).

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:24:32:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: מִסְפּוֹא is the name of the beasts' food ("שֵׁם מַאֲכַל בְּהֵמוֹת"), a general name, and barley stands in its place.
- `rashi:24:32:1` records: Rashi: he untied their muzzles, which stopped their mouths so that they would not graze in other fields on the way.
- `ramban:24:32:1` records: Ramban: the man who came is Eliezer, but the subject of "and loosened the camels" is Laban, who did this for his guests; he finds it far-fetched that Eliezer gave the water to wash his own feet.
- `radak:24:32:1` follows: Radak: he freed the camels from their bonds, as one frees beasts from the road; the sages' reading of the muzzles he gives as midrash.
- apparatus: The subject of וַיְפַתַּח is not named; the English leaves it open.
