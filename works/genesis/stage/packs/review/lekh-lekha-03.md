### Translation under review (12:17)

English: Then the LORD struck Pharaoh with great plagues, and his household too, on account of Sarai, Abram's wife.

Literal: and-struck the-LORD [obj] Pharaoh plagues great and-[obj] house-his on-account-of Sarai wife-of Abram

Phrases:
- 12:17.1 12:17.2 | Then the LORD struck | 'and-struck the-LORD'; wayyiqtol of a Piel verb, with the divine name after it
- 12:17.3 12:17.4 | Pharaoh | the object marker has no English counterpart
- 12:17.5 12:17.6 | with great plagues | 'plagues great'; the verb and noun share the root נגע
- 12:17.7 12:17.8 | and his household too | 'and-[obj] his-house'; the household is struck as well, but not with 'great' plagues on Sforno's reading
- 12:17.9 12:17.10 | on account of | 'on the-matter-of'
- 12:17.11 | Sarai | 'Sarai'
- 12:17.12 12:17.13 | Abram's wife | 'wife-of Abram'; construct chain

Grammar notes:
- וַיְנַגַּע is a Piel wayyiqtol with a cognate accusative, נְגָעִים גְּדֹלִים: the verb and its object come from one root.
- The darga and tevir set the divine name and the object off from the verb; the etnaḥta falls after בֵּיתוֹ, so the household forms the first half-verse's last item and the reason clause follows.
- עַל־דְּבַר is a compound preposition, 'on account of', with the construct chain אֵשֶׁת אַבְרָם closing the verse.

Translator's note: I put the household with a clause of its own, as the accents (etnaḥta after בֵּיתוֹ) and Sforno suggest: the household was struck, but the 'great' plagues are said of Pharaoh. Rashi's gloss (the plague of 'raathan', a disease that made intercourse painful) is a midrashic reading and does not change the plain wording, so the English says only 'plagues'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:12:17:1` records: Rashi, from Genesis Rabbah: he was struck with the plague of ראתן, which made marital relations painful for him. The English keeps the general 'plagues'; the specific disease is kept here.
- `rashi:12:17:2` not-applicable: The anchor holds only an opening parenthesis; no comment on the words survives.
- `rashi:12:17:3` records: Rashi: 'and his household' is as the Targum has it, אֱנַשׁ בֵּיתֵהּ, the people of his house; the midrash adds his walls, pillars and vessels (from the old Rashi). The English takes the plain 'household' and keeps the midrash here.
- `rashi:12:17:4` follows: Rashi: על דבר means 'by her word': she told the angel 'strike' and he struck. The English 'on account of Sarai' agrees in the sense 'because of', and the midrash on her speech is only noted.
- `ibn-ezra:12:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: על דבר is like בעבור, 'on account of', and the sense is 'because of this matter'. The English follows this.
- apparatus: Ramban, Radak and Sforno also read the plagues as aimed at Pharaoh, with lesser plagues on the household (Sforno).
- apparatus: Targum Onkelos: 'great afflictions' (מַכְתָּשִׁין רַבְרְבִין).

### Translation under review (13:4)

English: to the place of the altar that he had made there at first; and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.

Literal: to place-of the-altar which he-made there at-the-first and-called there Abram in-name-of the-LORD

Phrases:
- 13:4.1 13:4.2 13:4.3 | to the place of the altar | 'to place-of the-altar'
- 13:4.4 13:4.5 13:4.6 13:4.7 | that he had made there at first | 'which he-made there at-the-first'

Grammar notes:
- The verse continues the sentence of 13:3: Abram came 'to the place of the altar'.
- בָּרִאשֹׁנָה is 'at first', with the article and the ב of time.
- וַיִּקְרָא שָׁם אַבְרָם בְּשֵׁם יְהוָה repeats 12:8: calling on the name of the LORD is an act of worship.
- The verb עָשָׂה is a perfect, 'had made', since it refers to the earlier time.

Translator's note: קָרָא בְּשֵׁם is 'call on the name', as in 12:8; Rashi allows that Abram's calling now repeated the earlier calling, since the earlier calling may also be meant.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:13:4:1` follows: Rashi: 'that he made there at first' may also refer to the calling on the name of the LORD, and one can say 'and he called there now'. The English takes 'at first' with the altar and 'called' as a fresh act.
- apparatus: Radak: 'again' is meant: he called a second time on the name of the LORD; he settled in the same place to teach that a person should fix a place for prayer.
