Phrases:
- 6:21.1 | As for you | 'and-you': the fronted independent pronoun sets Noah against the doomed world
- 6:21.2 6:21.3 | take for yourself | 'take-to-you': ethical dative לְךָ
- 6:21.4 6:21.5 | some of every kind of food | 'from-all-of food': partitive מִן, 'some of every food'
- 6:21.6 6:21.7 | that is eaten | 'which is-eaten', Niphal
- 6:21.8 6:21.9 | and gather it to yourself | 'and-you-shall-gather to-you'; the object is the food, implied
- 6:21.10 6:21.11 6:21.12 6:21.13 | so that it will be food for you and for them | 'and-it-shall-be to-you and-to-them for-food'; the weqatal gives purpose or result

Grammar notes:
- וְאַתָּה begins with an independent pronoun, which contrasts Noah with everyone else.
- קַח is an imperative; וְאָסַפְתָּ is a weqatal continuing the command.
- The etnaḥta on אֵלֶיךָ divides the verse: the gathering, then the purpose.
- לְאָכְלָה is a feminine noun 'food' with prefixed ל, giving 'as food'.

Translator's note: The 'them' of the last clause are the animals of 6:19-20; Radak says even the predators ate the same plant foods, as before the creation of meat-eating.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:6:21:1` follows: Radak: since the animals come to Noah to be kept alive, he must prepare food for himself and for them; the food is fruits, seeds and plants, and even predators eat them.
- `sforno:6:21:1` not-applicable: Sforno: now take various foods for the various kinds; it adds detail, not a different reading.
- `onkelos:6:21:1` follows: Onkelos renders the verse closely: 'take for yourself of all food that is eaten and gather it to you, and it shall be for you and for them to eat'.

Phrases:
- 6:22.1 6:22.2 | Noah did so | 'and-did Noah': the report of obedience
- 6:22.3 6:22.4 6:22.5 6:22.6 6:22.7 | just as God had commanded him | 'like-all that commanded him God'; כֹּל is idiomatic: 'exactly as'

Grammar notes:
- The wayyiqtol וַיַּעַשׂ narrates; the closing כֵּן עָשָׂה is a qatal that summarizes.
- The telisha gedola on כְּכֹל marks a long disjunctive-like prepositive accent.
- אֹתוֹ is the object marker with a 3rd masculine singular suffix: 'him'.
- The Hebrew states obedience twice, before and after the commandment.

Translator's note: The English line moves the clause 'so he did' to the end, as the Hebrew does. Ramban: the double statement shows that he left nothing out of what he was commanded.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:6:22:1` follows: Rashi: 'this is the building of the ark': the making that Noah did is the construction of the ark.
- `ramban:6:22:1` records: Ramban: Noah both built the ark and gathered the food; the repetition 'so he did' shows he omitted nothing.
- `radak:6:22:1` records: Radak: he made the ark exactly as commanded, in length, width, height, nests, and the food he put in it.
- `onkelos:6:22:1` follows: Onkelos: 'and Noah did according to all that the LORD commanded him; so he did'.
- apparatus: Onkelos has יְיָ ('the LORD') for אֱלֹהִים; this is Targum Onkelos's rendering, not a variant of the Hebrew.

Grammar notes:
- אֹתְךָ is the object marker with a suffix, fronted before the verb; it stresses 'you, and no one else'.
- בֹּא־אַתָּה uses an independent pronoun after the imperative for emphasis.
- The etnaḥta on הַתֵּבָה separates the command from its reason.
- צַדִּיק is the predicate of the object: 'I saw you (as) righteous'.

Translator's note: Rashi stresses that the text says only 'righteous', not 'righteous and blameless' as in 6:9: a person is praised in part to his face, in full behind his back. Ramban and Sforno hold the emphatic 'you' implies that the household is saved for his sake only. The English keeps 'you' plain; the emphasis goes in the notes.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:1:1` follows: Rashi: the verse does not say 'righteous and blameless'; one says part of a man's praise in his presence and all of it in his absence (Genesis Rabbah). The English says only 'righteous'.
- `ramban:7:1:2` records: Ramban: Noah is told to enter with his whole household, and the text shows that they are saved in his merit alone, since it does not say 'I have seen you all righteous'.
- `sforno:7:1:1` records: Sforno: 'you, not your house' (the household is saved only because of him).
- `radak:7:1:2` records: Radak: it is fitting that you and all your household escape the flood so that the world is settled again.
- `onkelos:7:1:1` follows: Onkelos: 'enter, you and all the people of your house, into the ark, for I have seen you righteous (zakkai) before me in this generation'.

Grammar notes:
- תִּקַּח is an imperfect used as a command; the accent on לְךָ is tevir.
- שִׁבְעָה שִׁבְעָה is distributive repetition. It can mean seven individuals of each kind or seven pairs; the text itself says 'a male and his mate' after it.
- אִישׁ וְאִשְׁתּוֹ are the words for a human husband and wife, here applied to animals (Radak: a figurative use).
- לֹא טְהֹרָה הִוא ('not clean') is a euphemism for 'unclean' (Radak, and the rabbis: avoid a foul word).

Translator's note: The English leaves the ambiguity in 'seven each' (seven individuals or seven pairs). Radak reads seven pairs; Rashi says only that the extras are for a sacrifice at exit. 'Clean' is not defined in the text; Rashi reads it as 'destined to be clean for Israel', Sforno as 'fit for sacrifice', and Radak that God told Noah the signs of cleanness or that he recognized them by their coming in sevens.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:2:1` records: Rashi: 'the clean' means those destined to be clean for Israel, and from this we learn that Noah studied Torah; the English takes it as simply 'clean'.
- `rashi:7:2:2` follows: Rashi: the seven each are so that he can offer a sacrifice from them on leaving the ark; it explains 'seven' and does not change the English.
- `radak:7:2:1` records: Radak: he says now that Noah should bring seven pairs of each clean animal and one pair (or two) of the unclean; he asks how Noah knew which was clean and gives two answers.
- `radak:7:2:4` records: Radak: אִישׁ וְאִשְׁתּוֹ means male and female, said figuratively; when the pairs are alike the text says 'a woman to her sister', and when they differ as male and female it says 'man and his wife'.
- `radak:7:2:5` follows: Radak: the text says 'not clean' rather than 'unclean' as it often does, and the rabbis learned from it that one should not speak a foul word.
- `sforno:7:2:1` records: Sforno: at that time all clean animals were fit for sacrifice, as the rabbis said, and in their food-nature unlike 'you shall be defiled by them'.
- `onkelos:7:2:1` follows: Onkelos: 'seven seven, male and female' and 'two, male and female' for the unclean.

Grammar notes:
- עוֹף is a collective singular, so the English plural 'birds'.
- שִׁבְעָה שִׁבְעָה is again distributive; here זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה explains it.
- לְחַיּוֹת is a Piel infinitive construct and gives the purpose.
- The tevir on הַשָּׁמַיִם and the etnaḥta on וּנְקֵבָה mark the verse's two breaks.

Translator's note: גַּם ('also') joins the birds to the clean animals of 7:2. Rashi reads the verse as speaking of clean birds only, learning the unstated from the stated; Radak makes the point that the birds came in sevens whether clean or not and discusses it at 7:2.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:3:1` follows: Rashi: the verse speaks of clean birds; the unstated case is learned from the stated one. The English 'also of the birds' does not add 'clean'; the note records this.
- `radak:7:3:2` not-applicable: Radak: 'over the face of all the earth': as at 1:25, the animals' places are divided over the whole earth.
- `onkelos:7:3:1` follows: Onkelos: 'also of the birds of the heavens, seven seven, male and female, to preserve seed over all the earth'.

Grammar notes:
- The participle מַמְטִיר expresses what is about to happen; the weqatal וּמָחִיתִי continues it.
- יָמִים ... עוֹד שִׁבְעָה: 'still seven days'; the preposition ל marks the span after which the event occurs.
- הַיְקוּם is found only here (and 7:23); Ibn Ezra and Radak take it as a general word for every living being.
- The verse uses אֲדָמָה (ground), not אֶרֶץ, for what is wiped out.

Translator's note: The BDB entry gives `substance, existence` for הַיְקוּם; the English 'living thing' follows Ibn Ezra, 'a general term for every living being on the earth' and Radak ('all that is on the face of the ground among living beings'). Radak adds that it does not include fish or those in the ark. Rashi reads the seven days as the mourning for Methuselah and the 'still' as additional to the 120 years; neither changes the plain translation.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:4:1` records: Rashi: these seven days are the mourning for Methuselah the righteous; God spared his honor and delayed the punishment; count his years and you find they end in the six hundredth year of Noah (Sanhedrin 108). The English gives only 'seven more days'.
- `rashi:7:4:2` records: Rashi: what is 'still'? Another period after, added to the 120 years.
- `rashi:7:4:3` not-applicable: Rashi: forty days matches the forming of the embryo, since they corrupted themselves; a homily that does not bear on the translation.
- `ibn-ezra:7:4:1` records: Ibn Ezra: on the seventh day it will rain until the end of forty days; this was on the tenth of the second month, and the middle returns to the front, a secret (the text is cut off). He counts the days differently from the plain reading.
- `ibn-ezra:7:4:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: הַיְקוּם is found only in this section; its root is among verbs whose first letter is not complete (like כרוב), or among hollow verbs, and it is a general term for every living thing on the ground.
- `radak:7:4:1` records: Radak: Noah is to enter the ark seven days before the flood to prepare places and food; the rabbis say the seven days are for Methuselah's mourning.
- `radak:7:4:3` follows: Radak: הַיְקוּם is general for everything alive on the ground; the root is קום with a letter yod added, as in רִיבָה.
- `radak:7:4:4` records: Radak: 'every living thing' means what is on dry land and on the ground, not fish or what is in the ark.
- `onkelos:7:4:1` follows: Onkelos: 'for after a time of seven more days I am bringing rain on the earth forty days and forty nights, and I will wipe out every standing thing that I made from upon the face of the earth'.
- apparatus: Onkelos has 'every standing thing' (יְקוּמָא) and 'from upon the face of the earth' (אַרְעָא), reading the last word as 'earth' where the Hebrew has אֲדָמָה.

### Translation under review (7:6)

English: Now Noah was six hundred years old when the flood came, water upon the earth.

Literal: and-Noah son-of six hundreds year and-the-flood was waters upon the-earth

Phrases:
- 7:6.1 7:6.2 7:6.3 7:6.4 7:6.5 | Now Noah was six hundred years old | 'and-Noah son-of six hundreds year'; the ו is circumstantial
- 7:6.6 7:6.7 | when the flood came | 'and-the-flood was'; the ו means 'when' (Radak)
- 7:6.8 7:6.9 7:6.10 | water upon the earth | 'waters upon the-earth'; מַבּוּל מַיִם is 'flood of water'

Grammar notes:
- בֶּן־שֵׁשׁ מֵאוֹת שָׁנָה is the idiom for age: 'son of six hundred years'.
- The ו of וְהַמַּבּוּל is circumstantial: 'when' (Radak).
- מַיִם is a plural noun 'water'.
- The etnaḥta on שָׁנָה divides Noah's age from the flood.

Translator's note: Radak takes וְהַמַּבּוּל הָיָה מַיִם as 'the flood of water was', the word 'flood' standing for two (flood, water). The English line reads 'the flood came, water upon the earth'.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:7:6:1` follows: Radak: it tells how old Noah was when he entered the ark because of the flood that was coming.
- `radak:7:6:2` follows: Radak: the ו means 'when', as in the verses he quotes; Noah was six hundred when the flood came.
- `radak:7:6:4` follows: Radak: 'flood of water'; the one word 'flood' stands for two.
- `onkelos:7:6:1` follows: Onkelos: 'and Noah was a son of six hundred years, and the flood was waters on the earth'.
