### Translation under review (7:7)

English: Noah went into the ark, and his sons and his wife and his sons' wives with him, because of the waters of the flood.

Literal: and-came Noah and-sons-his and-wife-his and-wives-of sons-his with-him to the-ark from-before waters-of the-flood

Phrases:
- 7:7.1 7:7.2 | Noah went | 'and-came Noah'
- 7:7.3 7:7.4 7:7.5 7:7.6 7:7.7 | and his sons and his wife and his sons' wives with him | the family, men and women listed separately
- 7:7.8 7:7.9 | into the ark | 'to the-ark'
- 7:7.10 7:7.11 7:7.12 | because of the waters of the flood | 'from-before waters-of the-flood'

Grammar notes:
- The verb וַיָּבֹא is singular and agrees with the first subject, Noah; the rest of the party follows in a list.
- The telisha gedola on בָנָיו and the darga/tevir pair keep the men (sons) apart from the women (wife, sons' wives), in the order of the list.
- מִפְּנֵי is literally 'from before' and means 'on account of'; the etnaḥta after הַתֵּבָה divides the verse before this clause.

Translator's note: Rashi reads the separate listing of men and women as a sign of marital abstinence; that bears on the order of the list, not on the English words. 'Because of' follows both Rashi (waters pressed him) and Ibn Ezra (fear of the flood) in taking the clause as the reason for entering.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:7:1` not-applicable: Rashi: the men are named apart from the women because they were forbidden marital relations while the world was in distress; a homiletic point about the word order.
- `rashi:7:7:2` records: Rashi: Noah was of little faith, believing and not believing the flood would come, and entered only when the water forced him. The English says only 'because of the waters', which fits this reading but does not state it.
- `ibn-ezra:7:7:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: מפני means 'out of fear of the flood waters'; in those seven days many clean animals and all others came of their own accord, male and female.

Grammar notes:
- The date is a series of ב phrases: year, month, day of the month, with the time clause leading to the main verbs נִבְקְעוּ and נִפְתָּחוּ.
- Both verbs are Niphal qatal, plural, passive: 'were split', 'were opened'.
- מַעְיְנֹת תְּהוֹם רַבָּה: the springs of the great deep; תְּהוֹם is also found with the feminine adjective here, and Ibn Ezra notes it is both masculine and feminine.
- The symmetry of the verse sets the springs of the earth below against the windows of the sky above.

Translator's note: 'The great deep' keeps תְּהוֹם רַבָּה; Rashi's note sees measure for measure in רַבָּה. Of the two second-month readings in Rashi, the English says only 'second month'; the commentators' disagreement over which month that is does not affect the English words.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:11:1` records: Rashi: R. Eliezer says the second month is Marheshvan; R. Joshua says it is Iyyar. The English says only 'the second month'.
- `rashi:7:11:2` follows: Rashi: נבקעו means that they were split open to let out their waters.
- `rashi:7:11:3` records: Rashi: measure for measure, they sinned with 'great' (רבה) wickedness of man and were punished by a 'great' deep. The English keeps 'great'.
- `ibn-ezra:7:11:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: מעינות is familiar from 'no springs' (Prov 8:24), so 'springs'.
- `ibn-ezra:7:11:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: תהום is both masculine and feminine, as in 'the deep lifted it up' (Ezek 31:4); the English 'deep' stands.
- `ibn-ezra:7:11:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: ארבות is like 'the LORD will make windows' (2 Kings 7:2), a figure of speech in ordinary human terms; hence 'windows'. He also gives two views of the aleph.
- apparatus: Rashi's R. Eliezer and R. Joshua differ over the month; Radak reports the same dispute.

Grammar notes:
- הֵמָּה resumes the subjects of 7:13 and is followed by the list of the animals, with לְמִינָהּ/לְמִינֵהוּ repeated for each class.
- הָרֶמֶשׂ הָרֹמֵשׂ is a noun with its cognate participle: 'the creeping thing that creeps'.
- The suffix on לְמִינָהּ agrees with the feminine noun (חַיָּה, בְּהֵמָה), the suffix on לְמִינֵהוּ with the masculine (רֶמֶשׂ, עוֹף).
- The last clause, כֹּל צִפּוֹר כָּל כָּנָף, is a construct chain: 'every bird of every wing'.

Translator's note: Rashi reads צִפּוֹר כָּל כָּנָף as one chain: 'every bird of every kind of wing'. The English 'every winged bird of every sort' follows it; Rashi also draws in locusts, which the English does not state.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:7:14:1` follows: Rashi: צפור כל כנף is joined: a bird of every kind of כנף, which includes locusts, since כנף means feather; so also here, a bird of every kind of feathered appearance.
