### Translation under review (9:16)

English: The bow will be in the cloud, and I will see it, to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living being, among all flesh that is on the earth.

Grammar notes:
- וְהָיְתָה and וּרְאִיתִיהָ are weqatal forms that carry on the future promise.
- בֵּין ... וּבֵין is the usual 'between ... and' pattern: the preposition is repeated before each member.
- אֱלֹהִים stands where the first person is expected (compare 9:17 בֵּינִי); Rashi and Radak take this as meaningful.
- The etnaḥta on בֶּעָנָן and the zaqef on עוֹלָם and אֱלֹהִים divide the verse into the bow, the seeing, and the covenant partners.

Translator's note: The English keeps 'God' in the third person, as the Hebrew does. Rashi's midrash (the attribute of justice above) is recorded, not adopted. Ibn Ezra's reading (the bow is always in the cloud, unseen by people, seen by God) agrees with the English 'will be in the cloud ... I will see it'. 'Living being' renders נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:16:1` records: Rashi: 'between' God's attribute of justice above and you; the text should say 'between me and', so the verse teaches that when the attribute of justice comes to accuse, God sees the sign and remembers. The English keeps the plain third person 'God'.
- `ibn-ezra:9:16:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'the bow will be in the cloud, which is for ever' means it is there in secret, and God sees it; the English says only that the bow will be in the cloud and God will see it.
- `radak:9:16:3` records: Radak: 'between God' is as if he said 'between me', as elsewhere (Exodus 24:1).
- `onkelos:9:16:1` not-applicable: Onkelos renders 'between the Memra of the LORD', avoiding the direct statement; it does not alter the sense of the Hebrew.
- apparatus: 9:16.1 וְהָיְתָה: feminine verb with the feminine noun קֶשֶׁת; no variant in the pack.

English: Noah, a man of the soil, began and planted a vineyard.

Phrases:
- 9:20.1 9:20.2 9:20.3 9:20.4 | Noah, a man of the soil, began | 'and-began Noah man-of the-soil'; the verb comes first
- 9:20.5 9:20.6 | and planted a vineyard | 'and-planted vineyard'; the two verbs are in a 'began and did' sequence

Grammar notes:
- וַיָּחֶל is a Hiphil wayyiqtol of חלל, 'begin' (BDB Hiph 2); the same root in the Niphal and Piel means 'profane', which gives the wordplay Rashi uses.
- Two wayyiqtol verbs in a row, 'began' and 'planted', mean 'began to plant' (Radak).
- אִישׁ הָאֲדָמָה is a construct chain: 'man of the soil', a farmer.
- The etnaḥta on הָאֲדָמָה separates the description of Noah from the planting.

Translator's note: The English keeps both verbs, 'began and planted', since the Hebrew does; the reading 'began to plant' is Radak's. Rashi's reading of וַיָּחֶל as 'made himself profane' (חֻלִּין), because he should have planted something else first, is recorded, not adopted. 'Man of the soil' is read as an expert farmer (Ibn Ezra), not 'master of the soil' (Rashi), a reading Ramban rejects.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:20:1` records: Rashi: 'he made himself profane' (ḥullin), for he should first have planted something else (Genesis Rabbah).
- `rashi:9:20:2` records: Rashi: 'lords of the soil', like 'Naomi's husband' (Ruth 1). The English reads 'man of the soil' as a farmer.
- `rashi:9:20:3` not-applicable: Rashi: when he entered the ark he took shoots and fig cuttings with him (Genesis Rabbah); a midrashic note on the source of the vine.
- `ibn-ezra:9:20:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: it begins from the doubled-root verbs of the Hiphil, like 'he shut up with doors the sea' (Job 38:8); that is, 'began'.
- `ibn-ezra:9:20:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: he knew the work of the soil, a great skill; the midrash that he drank the wine the day he planted has a hidden meaning and is not the plain sense.
- `radak:9:20:2` follows: Radak: 'began to plant and planted': the infinitive is joined with the word of beginning, as in 1 Samuel 14:35 and Deuteronomy 2:25.

English: Shem and Japheth took the garment and laid it on both their shoulders, and they walked backward and covered their father's nakedness, and their faces were backward, and they did not see their father's nakedness.

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּקַּח is singular though the subject is 'Shem and Japheth', so the verb agrees with the nearer name (Rashi finds a lesson in this); the next verb וַיָּשִׂימוּ is plural.
- עַל־שְׁכֶם שְׁנֵיהֶם has a singular 'shoulder' with a dual 'both': Ibn Ezra says each put part of the garment on his own shoulder, and שְׁכֶם is not in construct with שְׁנֵיהֶם in the strict sense.
- אֲחֹרַנִּית is an adverb, 'backwards'; it occurs twice, in the verb clause and in the verbless clause.
- וְעֶרְוַת אֲבִיהֶם לֹא רָאוּ puts the object first, for contrast with the covering.

Translator's note: 'Both their shoulders' follows Ibn Ezra: each brother took part of the garment on his own shoulder. The repeated אֲחֹרַנִּית is kept; Rashi reads the second as the turning of their faces when they had to approach. Rashi's midrash on the singular וַיִּקַּח (Shem exerted himself more) is recorded, not adopted; Radak takes the singular as a plain idiom, like Exodus 6:10.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:23:1` records: Rashi: it is not written 'they took' but 'he took', which teaches that Shem exerted himself more in the commandment than Japheth; hence his sons earned the fringed garment. The English does not adopt this.
- `rashi:9:23:2` follows: Rashi: why is 'backward' said a second time? When they came near and had to turn to cover him, they turned their faces backward.
- `ibn-ezra:9:23:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: each of them put some of the garment on his shoulder; the word 'shoulder' does not join with 'both'; so the English 'both their shoulders'.
- `ibn-ezra:9:23:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'backward' means the reverse of the way a person goes, since all his motion is forward.
- `radak:9:23:1` records: Radak: 'Shem and Japheth took' is like 'Moses and Aaron came' (Exodus 6:10); they were quick to honour their father and covered him so as not to see him at all. The singular verb is an idiom.
- `sforno:9:23:1` follows: Sforno: even in the act of covering, where they had to turn somewhat toward him, they did not, so as not to see, lest more grief be added to them.
