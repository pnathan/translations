Grammar notes:
- קְחָה is the imperative with the paragogic ה, a mark of urgency or courtesy.
- מְשֻׁלֶּשֶׁת / מְשֻׁלָּשׁ is a Pual participle (feminine, then masculine) of שָׁלַשׁ; it agrees in gender with each animal.
- The accents group the three large animals, each with its participle, and the etnaḥta falls on מְשֻׁלָּשׁ; the two birds follow as a separate pair.
- The list is in a fixed order of three animals and two birds; the birds take no participle.

Translator's note: מְשֻׁלֶּשֶׁת can mean 'three-year-old' (BDB: Pu three years old; Ibn Ezra's choice) or 'threefold', i.e. three of each (Rashi, Onkelos). I read 'three-year-old' and record the other in the commentary. הַגּוֹזָל is any young bird; Rashi and Ibn Ezra take it as the young of the dove, which fits the pairing with the turtledove.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:9:1` records: Rashi: 'three calves', an allusion to three bullocks (the Day of Atonement bullock, the community's bullock for an inadvertent error, and the heifer whose neck is broken). The English reads the Pual participle as an age instead.
- `rashi:15:9:2` records: Rashi: the three goats allude to the goat offered inside, the goats of the festival additional offerings, and an individual's sin-offering goat. This follows his 'threefold' reading and is not adopted.
- `rashi:15:9:3` records: Rashi: the three rams allude to the certain guilt offering, the suspended guilt offering, and an individual's sin-offering lamb; this too follows 'threefold'.
- `rashi:15:9:4` follows: Rashi, citing Bereshit Rabbah: the turtledove and the young of a dove. The English 'young pigeon' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:15:9:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: some say three goats, but 'it is right in my eyes' that the goat was three years old. The English reads three-year-old.
- `ibn-ezra:15:9:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: אַיִל is the large lamb (כבש). The English 'ram' keeps the animal as the grown male sheep.
- `ibn-ezra:15:9:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: גוֹזָל is the young dove, since only these are named among beasts and birds for burnt offerings and every sacrifice. The English follows.
- apparatus: Onkelos reads three of each ('three heifers, three goats, three rams'), against Ibn Ezra's three-year-old.
- apparatus: Radak notes that גּוֹזָל can name the young of any bird, citing Deut 32:11, but takes it here as the young dove.

Grammar notes:
- The object is fronted in וְאֶת־הַצִּפֹּר לֹא בָתָר, which sets the birds against the animals: the animals were cut, the birds were not.
- אִישׁ בִּתְרוֹ לִקְרַאת רֵעֵהוּ is a distributive phrase, 'each one its piece opposite its fellow', as in Exod 26:3 (Ibn Ezra).
- The verb בָּתַר ('to cut in two') recurs here three times and gives the passage its name, בֵּין הַבְּתָרִים.
- The etnaḥta at רֵעֵהוּ closes the first half: the cutting and setting out of the animals.

Translator's note: The text does not say who commanded the cutting; the commentators take it as part of the covenant rite. צִפֹּר is a collective singular; Ibn Ezra reads it as the turtledove and the young pigeon of v. 9, which I follow. The pointing הַצִּפֹּר is that of the base text; WLC reads הַצִפֹּר without the dagesh. בֶּתֶר at 15:10.11 is listed in the lexicon only with the sense 'cleft mountains', which does not fit here; I give no sense number and read 'piece, half', as the lexicon's gloss says.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:10:1` follows: Rashi: he divided each into two parts, for he was making a covenant, and covenant-makers divide animals and pass between the pieces (Jer 34:18). The smoking oven and flaming torch that pass between the pieces are the messenger of the Presence.
- `rashi:15:10:2` not-applicable: Rashi: the nations are likened to bulls, rams and goats and Israel to doves, so the animals were cut as a sign that the nations will be consumed; a homiletic reading, not about the wording.
- `rashi:15:10:3` not-applicable: Rashi: the birds were not cut as a sign that Israel will endure for ever; the second half of the same homily.
- `ibn-ezra:15:10:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: וַיְבַתֵּר means 'he cut them and separated them', as in 'the mountains of Bether' (Song 2:17) and 'between its parts' (Jer 34:18).
- `ibn-ezra:15:10:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: אִישׁ is like בַּעַל, as in 'man of war' and 'worthless man'; it is a distributive form like 'one to her sister' (Exod 26:3). He placed each piece of each animal opposite its fellow, since beast and bird are two kinds.
- `ibn-ezra:15:10:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: הַצִּפֹּר is the turtledove and the young pigeon, because צִפּוֹר is a general term.
- apparatus: 15:10.15 vowels: WLC reads הַצִפֹּ֖ר (no dagesh in the צ); base text הַצִּפֹּ֖ר. Meaning unchanged.
- apparatus: Ramban records that the birds were not cut because of the rule of Lev 1:17 (the bird burnt offering is not divided) and notes Bereshit Rabbah on the difference between burnt-offering and sin-offering birds; not adopted as the plain sense.

English: The birds of prey came down on the carcasses, and Abram drove them off.

Phrases:
- 15:11.1 15:11.2 15:11.3 15:11.4 | The birds of prey came down on the carcasses | and-came-down the-bird-of-prey upon the-carcasses
- 15:11.5 15:11.6 15:11.7 | and Abram drove them off | and-caused-to-blow them Abram; Rashi: 'blowing away', making them fly off

Grammar notes:
- וַיַּשֵּׁב is a Hifil wayyiqtol of נשב ('to blow'), with the doubled shin standing for the assimilated nun; it means 'he blew them off'.
- הָעַיִט is a collective singular with a singular verb: 'the birds of prey'.
- The etnaḥta on הַפְּגָרִים divides the verse; the second half gives Abram's response.
- The subject Abram stands last, after the object, a mark of emphasis on what he did.

Translator's note: הַפְּגָרִים are the bodies of the animals that Abram had just cut up (Rashi: 'the pieces'; Ibn Ezra: 'the bodies'), so I write 'carcasses'. Radak argues that פְּגָרִים are whole bodies, i.e. the two birds; not adopted. For וַיַּשֵּׁב, Rashi and Ibn Ezra both take 'to blow away, make fly off'; Ibn Ezra also offers 'he made the carcasses return to drive off the bird', recorded in the apparatus.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:11:1` follows: Rashi: the עַיִט is a bird that swoops and gasps after carrion, from עָט as in 'and you swooped upon the spoil' (1 Sam 15). The English 'birds of prey' follows.
- `rashi:15:11:2` follows: Rashi: 'on the carcasses' means on the pieces. He argues against the Targums that render it as pieces (פלגיא), since פגרים is related to פגול.
- `rashi:15:11:3` follows: Rashi: וַיַּשֵּׁב is 'blowing and making fly', as in 'he makes his wind blow' (Ps 147). The English 'drove off' follows; the allusion to David and the Messiah is not applicable to the plain sense.
- `ibn-ezra:15:11:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: עַיִט is a bird, as in 'the speckled bird of prey is my inheritance' (Jer 12:9).
- `ibn-ezra:15:11:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: פְּגָרִים are the bodies.
- `ibn-ezra:15:11:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: the shin has a dagesh in place of the absorbed nun, like יְגָרֵשׁ, 'he drove them away from the carcasses', as in 'he makes his wind blow' (Ps 147:18); or alternatively 'he returned the carcasses to drive off the bird'.
- apparatus: Ibn Ezra's alternative: וַיַּשֵּׁב from שׁוּב, 'he put the carcasses back' to scare off the bird; the English keeps the נשב reading, the one the lexicon lists.
- apparatus: Onkelos renders 'and Abram scared them away' (וְאַפְרַח), in line with 'to blow away'.

### Translation under review (15:12)

English: As the sun was about to go down, a deep sleep fell on Abram, and suddenly dread, a great darkness, was falling on him.

Literal: and-was the-sun to-go-in and-deep-sleep fell upon Abram and-behold dread darkness great falling upon-him

Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי with a time clause (הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ לָבוֹא) is a standard way to open a new scene: 'the sun was about to set'.
- The word order moves from qatal (נָפְלָה) to a participle (נֹפֶלֶת): the deep sleep falls, then the dread continues to fall.
- אֵימָה חֲשֵׁכָה גְדֹלָה: three words in a row, 'terror, darkness, great'; the adjective is feminine and may modify חֲשֵׁכָה alone or both nouns.
- הִנֵּה brings the reader into Abram's experience.

Translator's note: The three nouns אֵימָה חֲשֵׁכָה גְדֹלָה can be read as a list ('a dread, a darkness, great') or as 'a dread of great darkness' or 'a great, dark dread'. The English keeps the apposition: 'dread, a great darkness'. The lexicon lists no sense for גָּדוֹל that matches 'great' (it lists 'loud', 'do great things', 'greatness'), so I give none at 15:12.11.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:12:1` not-applicable: Rashi: the dread and darkness are an allusion to the troubles and darkness of the exiles; a reading of the vision, not of the wording.
- `ibn-ezra:15:12:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'the sun was about to set' is a sign that Abram took all these on one day, since he woke from the prophetic vision. The English reads the sunset as the same day's setting.

Grammar notes:
- יָדֹעַ תֵּדַע is the infinitive absolute before the finite verb of the same root: 'you shall surely know'.
- The accents join the clause 'your offspring will be a sojourner in a land not theirs' and then close it at the zaqef; the next verbs are a new clause, whose subject shifts without being named.
- The perfects with wāw (וַעֲבָדוּם וְעִנּוּ) continue the future: 'and they will serve, and will afflict'.
- אַרְבַּע מֵאוֹת שָׁנָה is the number of years in apposition: the period comes at the end.

Translator's note: In וַעֲבָדוּם וְעִנּוּ אֹתָם the subject and object are not named; 'they will serve them … and they will oppress them' keeps that ambiguity, where 'they' is the people of the land. Whether 'four hundred years' counts the whole period or only the oppression is also open: Rashi counts four hundred years from the birth of Isaac, and Ibn Ezra counts from the day of the vision; the English keeps the number without fixing its starting point. Rashi's point that 'a land not theirs' is not only Egypt is followed.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:13:1` records: Rashi: the four hundred years run from the birth of Isaac until Israel left Egypt (Isaac sixty at Jacob's birth, Jacob 130 at the descent, 210 years in Egypt), and he shows that the time in Egypt alone was less. The English does not fix the starting point.
- `rashi:15:13:2` follows: Rashi: it does not say 'in the land of Egypt' but 'a land not theirs': from Isaac's birth Abraham, Isaac and Jacob all sojourned in the land. The English 'a land not theirs' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:15:13:1` records: Ibn Ezra: the גֵּר is like the grain cut from the branch, as the אֶזְרָח is the man with a family, like the branch attached to the root. The English takes the ordinary sense 'sojourner'.
- `ibn-ezra:15:13:2` records: Ibn Ezra: the four hundred years run 'to the end of this term from today'. The English leaves the starting point open.

שֶׁכֵּן שֶׁהַכָּתוּב אָמַר "וְגַם אֶת הַגּוֹי אֲשֶׁר יַעֲבֹדוּ", שֶׁיַּעַבְדוּ הַגּוֹי כֻּלּוֹ, וְהֵם הָלְכוּ מֵעַצְמָם לְמִצְרַיִם. אֲבָל הַטַּעַם כַּאֲשֶׁר כָּתַבְתִּי, וּכְבָר הִזְכִּירוּ רַבּוֹתֵינוּ הָעִנְיָן הַזֶּה, אָמְרוּ בִּוְאֵלֶּה שְׁמוֹת רַבָּה (שמות רבה ל':ט"ו), מָשָׁל לְאָדוֹן שֶׁאָמַר לִבְנוֹ "יַעֲשֶׂה עִם פְּלוֹנִי וְלֹא יְצַעֲרֶנּוּ", הָלַךְ וְעָשָׂה, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁעָשָׂה עִמּוֹ חִנָּם לֹא הִנִּיחַ שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה מְצַעֲרוֹ. כְּשֶׁנִּתְרַצָּה הָאָדוֹן לִבְנוֹ גָּזַר עַל מְצַעֲרָיו הֲרִיגָה. כָּךְ גָּזַר הקב"ה שֶׁיִּהְיוּ יִשְׂרָאֵל מְשֻׁעְבָּדִין בְּמִצְרַיִם, עָמְדוּ עֲלֵיהֶם וְשִׁעְבְּדוּם בְּחֹזֶק. אָמַר הקב"ה הָיָה לָכֶם לִנְהֹג בָּם כַּעֲבָדִים וְיַעֲשׂוּ צָרְכֵיכֶם, "אֲנִי קָצַפְתִּי מְּעָט וְהֵמָּה עָזְרוּ לְרָעָה" (זכריה א טו), עַד כָּאן לְשׁוֹנָם. וְדָבָר בָּרוּר הוּא כִּי הַשְׁלָכַת בְּנֵיהֶם לַיְּאוֹר אֵינָהּ בִּכְלַל "וַעֲבָדוּם וְעִנּוּ אוֹתָם", אֲבָל הִיא עֲקִירָתָם לְגַמְרֵי. וְכֵן מָה שֶׁאָמְרוּ תְּחִלָּה "הָבָה נִתְחַכְּמָה לוֹ פֶּן יִרְבֶּה" (שמות א י), אֵינוֹ בִּכְלַל עַבְדוּת וְעִנּוּי, מִלְּבַד מָה שֶׁהוֹסִיפוּ בָּעִנּוּי עַצְמוֹ "וַיְמָרְרוּ אֶת חַיֵּיהֶם בַּעֲבֹדָה קָשָׁה" וְגוֹ'. וְהוּא מָה שֶׁאָמַר הַכָּתוּב (דברים כו ז), "וַיַּרְא אֶת עָנְיֵנוּ וְאֶת עֲמָלֵנוּ וְאֶת לַחֲצֵנוּ". וְדַע וְהָבֵן כִּי הָאִישׁ שֶׁנִּכְתַּב וְנֶחְתַּם בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה לַהֲרִיגָה, לֹא יִנָּקֶה הַלִּסְטִים הַהוֹרֵג אוֹתוֹ בַּעֲבוּר שֶׁעָשָׂה מָה שֶׁנִּגְזַר עָלָיו, הוּא רָשָׁע בַּעֲוֹנוֹ יָמוּת וְדָמוֹ מִיָּד הָרוֹצֵחַ יְבֻקַּשׁ, אֲבָל כַּאֲשֶׁר תֵּצֵא הַגְּזֵרָה עַל פִּי נָבִיא יֵשׁ בָּעֹשֶׂה אוֹתָהּ דִּינִים, כִּי אִם שָׁמַע אוֹתָהּ וְרָצָה לַעֲשׂוֹת רְצוֹן בּוֹרְאוֹ כַּנִּגְזָר, אֵין עָלָיו חֵטְא, אֲבָל יֵשׁ לוֹ זְכוּת בּוֹ, כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר בְּיֵהוּא (מלכים ב י ל) "יַעַן אֲשֶׁר הֱטִיבֹתָ לַעֲשׂוֹת הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינַי כְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר בִּלְבָבִי עָשִׂיתָ לְבֵית אַחְאָב בָּנִים רְבִעִים יֵשְׁבוּ לְךָ עַל כִּסֵּא יִשְׂרָאֵל". אֲבָל אִם שָׁמַע הַמִּצְוָה וְהָרַג אוֹתוֹ לְשִׂנְאָה אוֹ לִשְׁלֹל אוֹתוֹ, יֵשׁ עָלָיו הָעֹנֶשׁ, כִּי הוּא לַחֲטֹא נִתְכַּוֵּן וַעֲבֵרָה הוּא לוֹ, וְכֵן הַכָּתוּב אוֹמֵר בְּסַנְחֵרִיב (ישעיהו י ה) "הוֹי אַשּׁוּר שֵׁבֶט אַפִּי וְגוֹ' בְּגוֹי חָנֵף אֲשַׁלְּחֶנּוּ וְעַל עַם עֶבְרָתִי אֲצַוֶּנּוּ", וְאָמַר הַכָּתוּב "וְהוּא לֹא כֵן יְדַמֶּה וּלְבָבוֹ לֹא כֵן יַחְשֹׁב כִּי לְהַשְׁמִיד בִּלְבָבוֹ", וְעַל כֵּן הֶעֱנִישׁוֹ בַּסּוֹף, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שם י יב) "וְהָיָה כִּי יְבַצַּע ה' אֶת כָּל מַעֲשֵׂהוּ אֶפְקֹד עַל פְּרִי גֹדֶל לְבַב מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר וְעַל תִּפְאֶרֶת רוּם עֵינָיו" וְגוֹ', וְאָמַר בּוֹ "שֶׂה פְזוּרָה יִשְׂרָאֵל אֲרָיוֹת הִדִּיחוּ הָרִאשׁוֹן אֲכָלוֹ מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר וְזֶה הָאַחֲרוֹן עִצְּמוֹ נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל לָכֵן כֹּה אָמַר ה' וְגוֹ' הִנְנִי פֹקֵד אֶל מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל וְאֶל אַרְצוֹ כַּאֲשֶׁר פָּקַדְתִּי עַל מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר" (ירמיהו נ יז), וְהִנֵּה זוֹ רְאָיָה שֶׁנֶּעֱנַשׁ מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר עַל הָרָעָה שֶׁעָשָׂה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל. וְהִנֵּה נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר שָׁמַע כִּי הַנְּבִיאִים פֶּה אֶחָד קוֹרְאִים אוֹתוֹ לְהַחְרִיב יְרוּשָׁלַיִם, וְהוּא וְכָל עַמּוֹ נִצְטַוּוּ עַל כָּךְ מִפִּי הַנָּבִיא, כְּמוֹ שֶׁכָּתוּב (ירמיהו כה ט) "הִנְנִי שׁוֹלֵחַ לְךָ וְלָקַחְתִּי אֶת כָּל מִשְׁפְּחוֹת צָפוֹן נְאֻם ה' וְאֶל נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל עַבְדִּי וַהֲבִאֹתִים עַל הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת וְעַל יוֹשְׁבֶיהָ וְהַחֲרַמְתִּים", וּכְתִיב (שם לב כח) "הִנְנִי נֹתֵן אֶת הָעִיר הַזֹּאת בְּיַד הַכַּשְׂדִּים וּבְיַד נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל וְהִצִּיתוּ אֶת הָעִיר בָּאֵשׁ", וְאַף עַל בֵּית הַמִּקְדָּשׁ עַצְמוֹ אָמַר (שם כו ו), "וְנָתַתִּי אֶת הַבַּיִת הַזֶּה כְּשִׁלֹה", וְהֵם יוֹדְעִים כִּי מִצְוַת הַשֵּׁם הִיא, כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמַר נְבוּזַרְאֲדָן לְיִרְמְיָהוּ (שם מ ב) "ה' אֱלֹהֶיךָ דִּבֶּר אֶת הָרָעָה הַזֹּאת אֶל הַמָּקוֹם הַזֶּה וַיָּבֵא וַיַּעַשׂ ה' כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֵּר כִּי חֲטָאתֶם לַה'", וְאַף עַל פִּי כֵן נֶעֶנְשׁוּ הַכַּשְׂדִּים כֻּלָּם בַּסּוֹף, וְהָיָה זֶה מִפְּנֵי שְׁנֵי טְעָמִים, הָאֶחָד שֶׁגַּם הוּא נִתְכַּוֵּן לְהַשְׁמִיד כָּל הָאָרֶץ לְהַגְדִּיל מֶמְשַׁלְתּוֹ, כְּמוֹ שֶׁכָּתוּב בּוֹ (ישעיהו יג יא) "וְהִשְׁבַּתִּי גְּאוֹן זֵדִים וְגַאֲוַת עָרִיצִים אַשְׁפִּיל", וּכְתִיב (שם יד יג) "וְאַתָּה אָמַרְתָּ בִלְבָבְךָ הַשָּׁמַיִם אֶעֱלֶה" וְגוֹ' וְכָתוּב "אֶעֱלֶה עַל בָּמֳתֵי עָב אֶדַּמֶּה לְעֶלְיוֹן", וּכְתִיב בְּאֻמָּתוֹ (שם מז ח) "הָאוֹמֶרֶת בִּלְבָבָהּ אֲנִי וְאַפְסִי עוֹד", וְאָמַר בּוֹ חֲבַקּוּק (חבקוק ב ט), "הוֹי בֹּצֵעַ בֶּצַע רָע לְבֵיתוֹ לָשׂוּם בַּמָּרוֹם קִנּוֹ" וְגוֹ', וְהִנֵּה זֶה כְּעָנְשׁוֹ שֶׁל סַנְחֵרִיב. וּלְכָךְ אָמַר הַכָּתוּב (ירמיהו נ יח) "לָכֵן כֹּה אָמַר ה' הִנְנִי פֹקֵד אֶל מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל וְאֶל אַרְצוֹ כַּאֲשֶׁר פָּקַדְתִּי עַל מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר". אֲבָל הָיָה בְּמֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל עֹנֶשׁ אַחֵר, שֶׁהוֹסִיף עַל הַגְּזֵרָה וְהֵרַע לְיִשְׂרָאֵל יוֹתֵר מְאֹד, כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמַר בּוֹ (ישעיהו מז ו) "קָצַפְתִּי עַל עַמִּי חִלַּלְתִּי נַחֲלָתִי וָאֶתְּנֵם בְּיָדֵךְ לֹא שַׂמְתְּ לָהֶם רַחֲמִים עַל זָקֵן הִכְבַּדְתְּ עֻלֵּךְ מְאֹד", וְעַל כֵּן בָּא עֲלֵיהֶם עֹנֶשׁ כָּפוּל וּמְכֻפָּל, שֶׁנִּשְׁמַד זַרְעוֹ לְגַמְרֵי וְלֹא יִהְיֶה לְבָבֶל שֵׁם וּשְׁאָר נִין וָנֶכֶד (ישעיהו יד כב), וְנִשְׁמְדָה עִירוֹ לְעוֹלָמִים, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שם יג יט) "וְהָיְתָה בָבֶל צְבִי מַמְלָכוֹת תִּפְאֶרֶת גְּאוֹן כַּשְׂדִּים כְּמַהְפֵּכַת אֱלֹהִים אֶת סְדֹם וְאֶת עֲמֹרָה לֹא תֵשֵׁב לָנֶצַח וְלֹא תִשְׁכֹּן עַד דּוֹר English: And also the nation that they will serve, I will judge; and afterward they will come out with great wealth.

Grammar notes:
- The object וְגַם אֶת־הַגּוֹי is placed first, before the subject אָנֹכִי; this puts the weight on the nation that will be judged.
- דָּן is a participle ('judging'), which here gives a near-future sense: 'I am about to judge'.
- אֲשֶׁר יַעֲבֹדוּ is a relative clause without a resumptive pronoun: 'the nation that they will serve'.
- בִּרְכֻשׁ גָּדוֹל, 'with great property', returns to the Exodus; the verb יֵצְאוּ matches the 'exodus' formula.

Translator's note: The word order of the Hebrew puts the object first, and the English follows it. Rashi takes גַּם to add the four kingdoms to Egypt; this is recorded rather than adopted. Ibn Ezra's gloss for אֲשֶׁר יַעֲבֹדוּ ('so that they have servants') fits the plain sense of the nation as master.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:14:1` records: Rashi: 'also' adds the four kingdoms, since they too will be destroyed for enslaving Israel. The English keeps 'also' without naming the kingdoms.
- `rashi:15:14:2` records: Rashi: the judgment is 'by ten plagues'. A more specific reading than the Hebrew states; recorded.
- `rashi:15:14:3` follows: Rashi: בִּרְכֻשׁ גָּדוֹל is 'with great wealth', as it says 'and they stripped Egypt' (Exod 12). The English follows.
- `ibn-ezra:15:14:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: אֲשֶׁר יַעֲבֹדוּ means that the nation will have servants, i.e. the nation that they will serve. The English follows.

English: As for you, you will go to your fathers in peace; you will be buried at a good old age.

Phrases:

- 15:15.2 15:15.3 15:15.4 15:15.5 | you will go to your fathers in peace | you-will-come to your-fathers in-peace; a euphemism for death (Ibn Ezra)

Grammar notes:
- וְאַתָּה with the separate pronoun contrasts Abram with his descendants, who suffer in the previous verses.
- תָּבוֹא אֶל־אֲבֹתֶיךָ is a euphemism for death, 'come to your fathers' (Ibn Ezra).
- תִּקָּבֵר is a Nifal, 'you will be buried', passive of קבר.
- בְּשֵׂיבָה טוֹבָה means 'in a good old age'; the adjective follows the noun and agrees in gender.

Translator's note: תָּבוֹא is rendered 'you will go': Hebrew בוא is 'come' or 'go in', and English 'go to your fathers' is the usual idiom for death. The lexicon sense used is Qal 4, 'go'. Rashi's comments on the repentance of Terah and Ishmael use the verse for homily and do not bear on the plain sense.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:15:1` not-applicable: Rashi: 'you will come' means that Abram will not see all this (the exile). It adds a reason and is not a statement about the Hebrew's wording.
- `rashi:15:15:2` not-applicable: Rashi: Terah served idols, so 'to your fathers' teaches that Terah repented; a homily.
- `rashi:15:15:3` not-applicable: Rashi: 'a good old age' is a promise that Ishmael will repent and Esau will not go to bad ways in Abraham's days; a homily on the timing of Abraham's death.
- `ibn-ezra:15:15:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'come to your fathers' is a figure for death, 'the way of all the earth'. The English 'go to your fathers' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:15:15:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: בְּשָׁלוֹם means 'in honour, and not as your offspring'. The English keeps 'in peace', which can bear that sense.

Grammar notes:
- וְדוֹר רְבִיעִי is a singular noun with a plural verb (יָשׁוּבוּ): the 'generation' stands for its members.
- הֵנָּה ('here') appears twice in the verse, once of place (in the land) and once of time (up to now: עַד־הֵנָּה).
- The reason clause כִּי לֹא־שָׁלֵם עֲוֺן הָאֱמֹרִי states why the promise is delayed: the Amorites' guilt is not yet full.
- The etnaḥta on הֵנָּה ends the first half, the return; the second half gives the reason.

Translator's note: I follow Rashi in taking דּוֹר as 'generation' (BDB sense 2), so four generations: Jacob and his sons into Egypt, then the fourth generation returns. Ibn Ezra reads דּוֹר as 'a dwelling' of the lifetime of a person and also counts to the fourth after the sojourn; I record this. The Amorites are a collective singular; the word 'iniquity' is kept, and not softened to 'guilt'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:15:16:1` follows: Rashi: after they go down to Egypt there will be three generations there and in the fourth they return to this land (Jacob, Judah, Perez, Hezron, and Caleb son of Hezron came into the land). The English 'fourth generation' follows.
- `rashi:15:16:2` follows: Rashi: Israel's return is delayed because God does not exact payment from a nation until its measure is full, as in 'in measure, when you send her away, you contend with her' (Isa 27). The English follows.
- `ibn-ezra:15:16:1` records: Ibn Ezra: most commentators err about 'generation'; דּוֹר means a dwelling (as in 'to dwell in the tents of wickedness', Ps 84:11), measured by the span of a person's life, long or short. The fourth after they are strangers in Egypt: Kohath, Amram, Moses and Aaron, and their sons returned to Canaan.
- `ibn-ezra:15:16:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'iniquity' is as in 'my iniquity is greater than I can bear' (Gen 4:13). The Amorite is named because of his strength; Moses killed the great kings, who were Amorite kings, and the five kings of the Amorites gathered against Joshua.
- apparatus: Ibn Ezra rejects deriving הָאֱמֹרִי from אמר ('to say'); the Amorites are named for their strength, as in Amos 2:9.
