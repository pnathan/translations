Grammar notes:
- Abraham's answer to Abimelech's question of 21:29 is one speech, which runs through this verse. The verb in 21:30.1 has no named subject; the context identifies the speaker.
- The object, שֶׁבַע כְּבָשֹׂת (21:30.4–5), stands before its verb תִּקַּח (21:30.6), a second masculine singular yiqtol addressed to Abimelech. The English moves the object to the front to keep the Hebrew order.
- The purpose clause is בַּעֲבוּר with a yiqtol: 'in order that they will be to me a witness'. The verb is third feminine singular (תִּהְיֶה) although its subject, the lambs, is plural; the form is kept as the pack parses it, and the English 'they' takes the lambs as subject.
- The second כִּי (21:30.12) introduces what the witness attests: 'that I dug this well', a qatal of חָפַר. The English keeps the plain claim.
- Accents: the zaqef gadol on וַיֹּאמֶר (21:30.1), the etnaḥta on מִיָּדִי (21:30.7) and the silluq on הַזֹּאת (21:30.16) mark the divisions of the verse.

Translator's note: The speaker is named by context only, so 'He said' is kept without a name. The lambs are the witness; the English gives no reason for the gift, which the commentators supply (see commentary).

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:21:30:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: לְעֵדָה is like לְעֵדוּת, 'as testimony', the feminine noun for a witness, so the lambs are the witness. He adds a remark on the word for oath, שְׁבוּעָה, which he says he will explain at Numbers 30:3. That remark does not bear on the English.
- `rashi:21:30:1` follows: Rashi's gloss on the purpose clause is the single word 'this' (זאת): the lambs themselves are what is to be a witness. The English agrees.
- `rashi:21:30:2` follows: Rashi reads לְעֵדָה as a feminine form of the noun for testimony, citing 'and the pillar is a witness' (Genesis 31:52). This agrees with Ibn Ezra and with the English 'a witness'.
- `rashi:21:30:3` not-applicable: Rashi tells how the herdsmen of Abimelech quarrelled over the well, each claiming to have dug it, and agreed that whoever was seen at the well with the water rising to meet him owned it. This is the story behind the claim, not the sense of 'that I dug'.
- `radak:21:30:1` follows: Radak: 'these lambs will be to me' is the thing that I give as a witness. The same reading as the English.
- `onkelos:21:30:1` follows: Onkelos renders לְעֵדָה as לְסַהֲדוּ, 'as a witness'. The English agrees.
- `sforno:21:30:1` records: Sforno compares the act to the shoe handed over in Ruth 4:7 as the sign of an agreement: the lambs are the owners' acknowledgment of the matter. A reader may take the lambs as the owners' acknowledgment rather than a witness; the English keeps the witness.
- `sforno:21:30:2` records: Sforno glosses בַּעֲבוּר תִּהְיֶה לִּי as 'the covenant' (הַבְּרִית): the purpose is the covenant just made. The English keeps the purpose as the Hebrew states it.
- `sforno:21:30:3` records: Sforno glosses לְעֵדָה as 'on your acknowledgment' (עַל הוֹדָאָתְךָ), taking the witness to be Abimelech's acknowledgment. The English keeps 'a witness'.

- The naming construction is קָרָא with ל, a place, and then the name: 'he called that place Beersheba'. The English follows the Hebrew order of the phrase.
- The name has two words, בְּאֵר and שֶׁבַע. The verse gives the reason for the second, 'because there they swore' (כִּי שָׁם נִשְׁבְּעוּ), which plays on the root שׁבע: 'seven' and 'swear'.
- נִשְׁבְּעוּ (21:31.10) is the Niphal, third masculine plural, of שָׁבַע, with the two parties as subject. The dual שְׁנֵיהֶם (21:31.11) is the subject, and the oath is mutual.
- Accents: the revia on כֵּן (21:31.2) and the tevir on קָרָא (21:31.3) divide the verse; the etnaḥta on שָׁבַע (21:31.7) divides the name; the silluq on שְׁנֵיהֶם (21:31.11) ends it.

Translator's note: The English gives the place name as the text gives it. The meaning of the name is in the grammar notes and the commentary; the English does not explain it.

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:21:31:1` records: Radak takes 'therefore' (על כן) as referring to the seven ewe-lambs. He reads the name as the well's word joined to 'seven' from the lambs, and the naming as a sign of the land's inheritance. A reader may take the name's reason from the lambs; the English keeps the reason the text gives, 'because there they swore'.
- `onkelos:21:31:1` records: Onkelos renders נִשְׁבְּעוּ as 'they confirmed' (קַיָּמוּ) rather than 'swore'. The English 'swore' follows the Niphal sense of שָׁבַע.

Grammar notes:
- Three wayyiqtols carry the narrative: וַיִּכְרְתוּ (making the covenant), וַיָּקׇם (the rising of Abimelech) and וַיָּשֻׁבוּ (the return).
- כָּרַת בְּרִית is the idiom 'cut a covenant'. The object is בְּרִית with no object marker. The English 'made a covenant' gives the sense of the idiom.
- The singular וַיָּקׇם has Abimelech as subject. וּפִיכֹל, 'and Phichol', is added after it with its appositive שַׂר־צְבָאוֹ, and the plural וַיָּשֻׁבוּ takes both men as subject.
- שַׂר־צְבָאוֹ is 'commander of his army': the construct, with a third masculine singular suffix on 'army'.
- Accents: the tipḥa on וַיָּשֻׁבוּ (21:32.10) and the silluq on פְּלִשְׁתִּים (21:32.13) end the parts of the verse.

Translator's note: The English keeps the plural and singular verbs as the Hebrew has them: one subject is named first, then the second is added. 'So' renders the ו of the first wayyiqtol.

Commentary rulings:
- `ramban:21:32:1` records: Ramban takes 'they returned to the land of the Philistines' to mean they returned to their city in that land: Abimelech and Phichol lived in Gerar, which is in Philistine territory, while Abraham lived in Beersheba, also in that land. The English keeps the Hebrew's 'the land'.
- `radak:21:32:1` not-applicable: Radak has Abraham stay at the well with his flocks a day, two days or longer, and then return to his house in Gerar. This supplies a stay the text does not state.
- `onkelos:21:32:1` records: Onkelos renders the covenant-making with 'they decreed' (גְּזָרוּ) and 'a confirmation' (קְיָם), and the return as 'they returned to the land of the Philistines'. The English keeps 'made a covenant'.
- `sforno:21:32:1` records: Sforno: they returned from Beersheba, which was not in Philistine territory. They had come there to speak with Abraham, who had gone to look at his flocks, and there Abraham gave sheep, cattle and ewe-lambs to cut the covenant. The English keeps 'returned to the land' as the Hebrew has it.

Grammar notes:
- וַיִּטַּע is a wayyiqtol, third masculine singular, of נָטַע. Its object אֶשֶׁל follows it and carries no object marker.
- וַיִּקְרָא־שָׁם continues the narrative. 'Call on the name' (קָרָא בְּשֵׁם) is the phrase used of worship at other patriarchal sites (12:8; 26:25).
- The name יְהֹוָה (21:33.8) is rendered 'the LORD' as the decisions set. The epithet אֵל עוֹלָם (21:33.9–10) is two nouns: 'El' and 'eternity' in the construct. It occurs in Genesis only here.
- Accents: the qadma on וַיִּקְרָא (21:33.5) and the tipḥa on יְהֹוָה (21:33.8) divide the verse; the silluq on עוֹלָם (21:33.10) ends it.

Translator's note: El Olam is the name in apposition to 'the LORD' in the English, as the Hebrew has it. Its meaning (God of eternity, everlasting God) and the commentators' readings are in the commentary and the context notes.

Commentary rulings:
- `ibn-ezra:21:33:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: אֶשֶׁל is a tree (אִילָן), and Abraham planted it as a sign (לְאוֹת). The English 'a tamarisk tree' follows the tree reading; the identification with the tamarisk is the lexicon's. The sign is a reason, so it stays out of the English.
- `rashi:21:33:1` records: Rashi, quoting Rav and Shmuel, gives two readings of אֶשֶׁל: an orchard that brought fruit to feast the guests, or an inn with every kind of fruit. He also cites the planting of tents (Daniel 11:45). Neither is the tree of the English; a reader may take the word as an orchard or an inn.
- `rashi:21:33:2` not-applicable: Rashi's midrash on וַיִּקְרָא שָׁם: through that tree the name of the Holy One was called God of the world, because guests were told to bless the one whose food they had eaten. This is homily, not the sense of the words.
- `radak:21:33:1` not-applicable: Radak: Abraham planted near the well so that the tree would witness that the well was his. This is a reason for the planting, not the sense of the words.
- `radak:21:33:2` follows: Radak takes אֶשֶׁל as a planting word, as in 'under the eshel in Ramah' (1 Samuel 22:6), and as large trees, citing the Talmud's 'great eshalim'. His rabbinic reading of the word as an acronym for food, drink and escort is a midrash and is not the sense.
- `radak:21:33:3` records: Radak reads וַיִּקְרָא as 'he gathered the sons there and called to them', in light of the dispute with Abimelech over his wife and the well. The English keeps 'called on the name of the LORD'.
- `radak:21:33:4` records: Radak glosses 'on the name of the LORD' as the content of the calling: that people should hold to his faith and serve no other, and by Abimelech's return of the well they would know that he is El Olam.
- `ramban:21:33:1` records: Ramban takes 'on the name of the LORD' as a proclamation of the LORD who governs time by his strength, and El Olam as the one who rules the whole world. He cites Maimonides (Guide II.13), who took El Olam to point to God's existence before time. Onkelos reads וַיִּקְרָא as prayer. The English keeps 'called'.
- `onkelos:21:33:1` records: Onkelos renders the planting as setting up a tree (נְצֵיב נִצְבָּא), the calling as prayer (וְצַלִּי), and El Olam as 'God of the world' (אֱלָהָא דְעַלְמָא). The English keeps 'called' and the transliteration.
- `sforno:21:33:1` records: Sforno: Abraham called and made known to the many that God the Exalted is the God of time and its maker, against the view of the new and old Epicureans. He reads the name as the eternal God; the English transliterates the epithet.

Grammar notes:
- וַיָּגׇר is a wayyiqtol, third masculine singular: the verb opens the clause, and the subject follows it.
- יָמִים רַבִּים has no preposition: it is an adverbial phrase of duration, 'many days'. The English keeps it without 'for'.
- The adjective רַבִּים agrees with יָמִים in gender and number.
- Accents: the darga on וַיָּגׇר (21:34.1) and the silluq on רַבִּים (21:34.6) end the verse. The פ after the verse marks a paragraph division in the Masoretic layout.

Translator's note: The verse closes the section about the well and the covenant. The English gives 'many days' without a number, as the Hebrew does.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:21:34:1` not-applicable: Rashi counts the years: Abraham's stay in Hebron was twenty-five years, and here it was twenty-six, so 'many days' exceeds the earlier stay. This is chronology, not the sense of the words.
- `radak:21:34:1` not-applicable: Radak explains the long stay by the covenant with Abimelech, since Abraham feared no one. He also compares the land of the Philistines with the Shunammite's stay there for seven years of famine (2 Kings 8). This adds a reason and a comparison the text does not give.
- `onkelos:21:34:1` follows: Onkelos renders 'many days' as יוֹמִין סַגִּיאִין, 'many days'. The English agrees.
