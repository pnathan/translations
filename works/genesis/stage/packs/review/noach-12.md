Grammar notes:
- וַיִּיקֶץ and וַיֵּדַע are wayyiqtols that carry the narrative on from the drunkenness of 9:21.
- אֵת אֲשֶׁר־עָשָׂה is an object marker with a relative clause: 'what he had done'.
- הַקָּטָן is an adjective with the article after a noun with a suffix, so the pair is definite: 'his small son'.
- The etnaḥta after מִיֵּינוֹ divides the verse into the waking and the knowing.

Translator's note: הַקָּטָן is ambiguous. 'Youngest' is the plain sense of the word; Rashi and Ibn Ezra take the son to be Canaan, and Rashi reads the adjective as 'worthless, despised'. The English keeps 'youngest son' and records these readings. The text does not say what was done.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:24:1` records: Rashi: בנו הקטן means the unfit and despised one, as in 'Behold, I have made you small among the nations, despised among men' (Jeremiah 49:15). The English keeps the plain sense 'youngest' and records this.
- `ibn-ezra:9:24:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: Scripture does not reveal what was done; the doer was Canaan, since Ham saw and did not cover him as his brothers did but made the matter known, and Canaan heard. The English leaves the deed unstated.
- `ibn-ezra:9:24:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: בנו הקטן is Canaan, the youngest of Ham's sons (10:6, Cush, Egypt, Put and Canaan), the suffix of בנו returning to 'them'. To say Noah cursed his grandson because God blessed his sons is homiletic. The English 'youngest son' leaves room for this.
- `radak:9:24:3` records: Radak: if it is said of Ham, הקטן means the lesser, since Ham was not young in years; the Sages explain that Canaan saw first or that Canaan castrated him.
- `sforno:9:24:1` records: Sforno: grandsons count as sons; Canaan was the smallest of Ham's sons, or smallest of Noah's grandsons, and also lowly among the nations.
- apparatus: Onkelos: בריה זעירא 'his younger son'.

Commentary rulings:

Grammar notes:

- The Hebrew has no object marker after אֱלֹהִים; the preposition ל marks Japheth.
- The pashta and zaqef qaton set אֱלֹהִים apart as the subject of the first wish.
- The refrain וִיהִי כְנַעַן עֶבֶד לָמוֹ repeats 9:26 word for word.

Translator's note: The subject of וְיִשְׁכֹּן is ambiguous: Japheth, or God. Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Radak take it to be God dwelling in the tents of Shem; the English keeps 'he' for the ambiguity and the note records this reading. Rashi's midrash cites Cyrus as a descendant of Japheth.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:9:27:1` follows: Rashi: the Targum renders יַפְתֵּי, 'may He enlarge'. The English follows this sense.
- `rashi:9:27:2` follows: Rashi: God will rest His Presence on Israel; the Sages add that although God enlarged Japheth, the Presence did not dwell in the Second Temple built by Cyrus, but in the First built by Solomon of Shem. The English keeps 'he' and leaves the subject open.
- `rashi:9:27:3` follows: Rashi: even after the sons of Shem go into exile, slaves from Canaan's sons will be sold to them. The English 'a slave to them' fits.
- `ibn-ezra:9:27:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: יפת comes from יפה but means 'may He enlarge', like Deuteronomy 12:20, as in Aramaic, a hifil like 'and kings He subdues' (Isaiah 41:2); in this verse God blesses Japheth and Shem, since 'dwell in the tents of Shem' means God dwells in them, for the first verse blessed God. The English 'enlarge' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:9:27:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: Canaan will serve Shem and Japheth and also his brothers; he is a slave forever, since the earth was scattered from these, and this was many years after the flood.
- `radak:9:27:1` follows: Radak: the Targum translates 'enlarge'; the root is פתה in the hifil, as in 'and kings He subdues' (Isaiah 41:2); God will enlarge Japheth's border and blessing, but His dwelling will be only in the tents of Shem, an allusion to Israel.
- `radak:9:27:2` follows: Radak: Canaan will also be a slave to the sons of Japheth.
- `sforno:9:27:1` records: Sforno: the tents of Shem are houses of study, apart from the Temple.
- `sforno:9:27:2` records: Sforno: the second servitude is to Shem and Japheth, also outside the time of the Temple.
- apparatus: Onkelos: יַפְתֵּי יְיָ לְיֶפֶת וְיַשְׁרֵי שְׁכִינְתֵּיהּ בְּמַשְׁכְּנֵיהּ דְּשֵׁם.

### Translation under review (9:29)

English: All the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years, and he died.

Literal: and-were all-of days-of Noah nine hundred year and-fifty year and-died

Phrases:
- 9:29.1 9:29.2 9:29.3 9:29.4 | All the days of Noah were | 'and-were all-of days-of Noah'
- 9:29.5 9:29.6 9:29.7 9:29.8 9:29.9 | nine hundred and fifty years | 'nine hundred year and-fifty year'
- 9:29.10 | and he died | 'and-died'

Grammar notes:
- כָּל־יְמֵי־נֹחַ is a construct chain of three words, the subject of וַיִּהְיוּ.
- The plural verb agrees with יְמֵי, 'days'.
- The closing וַיָּמֹת is the formula that ends each life in the genealogy.
- A paragraph break follows, marked פ.

Translator's note: The total, nine hundred and fifty, is the sum of the six hundred years before the flood and the three hundred and fifty after.

Commentary rulings:
- `sforno:9:29:1` records: Sforno: וַיָּמֹת comes before the matter intended, the stirring of Abraham to call on the name of the LORD.
- apparatus: Onkelos: והוו כל יומי נח תשע מאה וחמשין שנין ומית.
