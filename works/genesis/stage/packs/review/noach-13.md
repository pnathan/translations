Grammar notes:
- תּוֹלְדֹת is a construct plural: the account of a man's descendants.

- וַיִּוָּלְדוּ is a Niphal wayyiqtol, passive 'were born'.

Translator's note: The English joins the two clauses with a colon, since the second states what follows from the first.

Commentary rulings:

- `ramban:10:1:2` not-applicable: Ramban says the sons were born only after the flood because God had restrained their fertility so that they would not perish in it.
- `radak:10:1:3` not-applicable: Radak says the verse tells us that Noah's sons did not have children in the ark.
- `onkelos:10:1:1` follows: Onkelos renders the verse closely: 'children were born to them after the flood'.

### Translation under review (10:8)

English: Cush fathered Nimrod. He was the first to be a mighty man on the earth.

Phrases:
- 10:8.1 10:8.2 | Cush fathered | 'and-Cush begot'
- 10:8.3 10:8.4 | Nimrod | object marker has no English counterpart
- 10:8.5 10:8.6 | He was the first | 'he began'; the English draws on the following infinitive
- 10:8.7 10:8.8 10:8.9 | to be a mighty man on the earth | 'to-be mighty in-the-earth'

Grammar notes:
- הוּא הֵחֵל is a fronted subject pronoun, giving emphasis: 'he, he began'.
- גִּבֹּר is an adjective used as a noun: 'a mighty man'.
- The etnaḥta on נִמְרֹד divides the statement of birth from the description.

Translator's note: The English follows Ibn Ezra's plain sense: he began to show the strength of men over the animals, a mighty hunter. Rashi's different reading, that he led the whole world in rebellion against God, is recorded. 'The first to be' renders הֵחֵל לִהְיוֹת, 'began to be'.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:10:8:1` records: Rashi: 'לְהַמְרִיד כָּל הָעוֹלָם עַל הַקָּבָּ"ה בַּעֲצַת דוֹר הַפַּלָּגָה', to make the whole world rebel against God, by the counsel of the generation of the Dispersion. The English does not adopt this reading of גִּבֹּר.
- `ibn-ezra:10:8:1` not-applicable: Ibn Ezra: do not seek a meaning for every name; this does not bear on the English.
- `ibn-ezra:10:8:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'he began to show the mighty deeds of men over the animals, for he was a mighty hunter'. The English follows this.
