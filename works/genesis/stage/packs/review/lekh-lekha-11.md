Grammar notes:
- וַיְהִי with a perfect and a noun-clause (וַעֲלָטָה הָיָה) sets the scene; the sun is the subject of בָּאָה.
- בָּאָה is accented on the first syllable (zaqef qaton on a mil'el form), so it is a perfect, 'had come', not a participle 'is coming' (Rashi, Radak).
- תַּנּוּר and לַפִּיד are in the construct before עָשָׁן and אֵשׁ; Radak reads עָשָׁן as an adjective ('a smoky furnace').
- אֲשֶׁר עָבַר is singular: one passing figure, the torch, goes between the pieces.
- The etnaḥta after הָיָה divides the scene (sunset, darkness) from the vision.

Translator's note: Followed Rashi (and Radak) on בָּאָה: perfect, the sun had set. Ibn Ezra reads that at first the sun had not yet set (cf. לָבוֹא in v. 12). עֲלָטָה is a thick darkness. The verb עָבַר is singular, so the pieces are passed through by the torch, as the next verse's covenant shows.

Grammar notes:
- The final four names of the ten; the paragraph mark (ס) closes the section.
- All are singular gentilics read as collectives.
- The pashta, zaqef qaton and tipḥa divide the four names in pairs; the silluq ends the verse.

Translator's note: The paragraph mark ס follows the verse. 'Canaanites' here is the narrower people, as the other nations are also Canaan's descendants (Ibn Ezra, v. 20).

Commentary rulings:
- `radak:15:21:1` records: Radak: the land of the Philistines is included in 'the Canaanite', as Joshua says 'it is counted to the Canaanite' (Josh 13:3).
- apparatus: Onkelos: אֱמוֹרָאֵי, כְּנַעֲנָאֵי, גִּרְגָּשָׁאֵי, יְבוּסָאֵי.
