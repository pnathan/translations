- 8:15.3 8:15.4 | to Noah | 'to Noah'
- 8:15.5 | saying | 'to-say'; introduces the quotation

Grammar notes:
- וַיְדַבֵּר is a Piel wayyiqtol; the merkha is on the verb, the tipḥa on אֱלֹהִים.
- לֵאמֹר, literally 'to say', is the idiom that introduces direct speech; it is not a purpose clause.

Translator's note: Plain narrative. The Piel of דבר is the ordinary verb for speaking; the English does not distinguish it from Qal אמר.

Commentary rulings:
- `onkelos:8:15:1` not-applicable: Onkelos renders closely: 'And the LORD spoke with Noah, saying.' It does not bear on the English.

Phrases:
- 8:16.1 8:16.2 8:16.3 | Go out of the ark | 'go-out from-the-ark'; the etnaḥta closes the command
- 8:16.4 8:16.5 | you and your wife | 'you and-your-wife'; the zaqef gadol and tevir set the couple off
- 8:16.6 8:16.7 8:16.8 | your sons and your sons' wives | 'and-your-sons and-wives-of your-sons'
- 8:16.9 | with you | 'with-you'

Grammar notes:
- צֵא is the imperative of יצא with the first radical dropped; the tipḥa on it and the etnaḥta on הַתֵּבָה divide the command from the list of those who go.
- The accents pair the household: 'you and your wife', then 'your sons and your sons' wives'. In 6:18 and 7:7 the men and women are listed in separate groups on entering; here the couples are paired.
- The construct plural נְשֵׁי־בָנֶיךָ is 'wives of your sons'.

Translator's note: The wording is plain. The couples are named together here, unlike the entry (6:18, 7:7), which Rashi and Radak take as the point of the verse.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:16:1` not-applicable: Rashi: 'A man and his wife: here He permitted them marital relations.' This is an inference from the pairing of husband and wife and does not change the English wording.
- `radak:8:16:1` not-applicable: Radak joins the women to the men to show that relations were now permitted, as the rabbis said. Not a matter of translation.
- `onkelos:8:16:1` not-applicable: Onkelos translates word for word.

Grammar notes:
- The object (כָּל־הַחַיָּה ...) comes before the verb הַיְצֵא; the zaqef and revia divide the long object from its specification, and the etnaḥta closes the command.
- הַיְצֵא is read (qere) as a Hiphil imperative; the ketiv הוצא is the same form spelled with a vav in place of the yod.
- The three weqatal verbs וְשָׁרְצוּ וּפָרוּ וְרָבוּ follow the imperative and give the purpose of the going out; the echo of 1:22 and 9:1 is clear.

Translator's note: The ketiv/qere is kept in the token note. Following Ibn Ezra's grammar (הַיְצֵא like הַשְׁלֵם, a Hiphil), the English uses 'Bring out'. Ibn Ezra glosses וְשָׁרְצוּ as 'let them give birth' (יולידו); the English keeps 'swarm', the literal sense.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:17:1` records: Rashi: the ketiv is הוֹצֵא, the qere הַיְצֵא. The qere means 'tell them to go out'; the ketiv means 'if they do not want to go out, bring them out yourself'. The English keeps the causative 'bring out' and records both.
- `rashi:8:17:2` records: Rashi: וְשָׁרְצוּ בָאָרֶץ means 'on the earth, and not in the ark', which shows that the animals and birds were forbidden to breed in the ark. This is a deduction and the English does not state it.
- `ibn-ezra:8:17:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: הַיְצֵא is like הַשְׁלֵם, and so 'הַיְשַׁר לפני דרכך' (Ps 5:9): a Hiphil imperative, which the English follows.
- `ibn-ezra:8:17:2` records: Ibn Ezra glosses ושרצו as יולידו, 'let them bear young'. The English keeps 'swarm' and records his sense.
- `radak:8:17:5` follows: Radak: the ketiv has a vav in place of the yod; the first radical vav is silent as is usual, while the qere shows the yod as is normal. He glosses 'bring them out with you as you brought them in with you'.
- `radak:8:17:6` follows: Radak: as at the first creation; the verb שרץ speaks of multiplying offspring and is used there only of fish.
- `onkelos:8:17:1` not-applicable: Onkelos renders ושרצו by יתילדון, 'let them breed', with אפיק for the imperative.
- apparatus: 8:17.14 ketiv-qere: ketiv הוצא (Hiphil imperative with vav), qere הַיְצֵא (with yod).

Grammar notes:
- The verse has two clauses of narration (וַיָּרַח, וַיֹּאמֶר) and then God's speech: two parallel vows, each opening לֹא־אֹסִף ... עוֹד, with a reason (כִּי ...) set between them.
- אֹסִף is a Hiphil imperfect of יסף; with an infinitive it means 'I will do again', so לֹא אֹסִף לְקַלֵּל is 'I will not again curse'.
- The zinor on יְהֹוָה and the segol pair on הַנִּיחֹחַ mark the first clause; the pair of telisha gedolas around אֹסִף and כִּי begin the speech and its reason.
- יֵצֶר לֵב הָאָדָם is a construct chain: 'the formation of the human heart'; the adjective רַע is the predicate, and מִנְּעֻרָיו 'from his youth' modifies it.
- The repeated אֹסִף עוֹד in two clauses is what Rashi takes as an oath.

Translator's note: The notes follow Ibn Ezra on the grammar: וַיָּרַח is a figure of speech (the offering was accepted, as one smells a pleasing scent); אֶל־לִבּוֹ means 'with/to his heart', that is 'to himself'; לְקַלֵּל is a curse on the ground that came through man (cf. 3:17); יֵצֶר is what was formed in the human. For מִנְּעֻרָיו Rashi derives the word from the root נער 'shake out' (from birth) and Ramban records both 'from the beginning of youth' and 'because of youth'; the English keeps the plain 'from youth'. The quoted speech is set in double quotation marks in the English only.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:21:1` records: Rashi: the form is written מנעריו (plural, 'from his youths'), and he reads it as 'from when he was shaken out of his mother's womb the evil inclination was given to him'. The English keeps the plain sense 'from youth' and records his derivation from נער.
- `rashi:8:21:2` follows: Rashi: the doubling of 'I will not add' is for an oath; Isaiah 54:9 says 'as I swore ... the waters of Noah', and the only oath the Torah records is this doubling. The English repeats 'never again' in both vows, and the oath is noted here.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: וַיָּרַח is on the pattern of 'וינח בכל גבול מצרים' (Ex 10:14), with a patah under the resh because of the guttural ח. God does not smell: the meaning is that He accepted the burnt offering and it pleased Him, as a person smells a sweet scent and it is pleasant. The English 'smelled' keeps the verb of the text.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: הַנִּיחֹחַ is from מְנוּחָה 'rest', and the ח is doubled like the pe of ונאפופיה; the sense is that it set down (הניח) His anger, or that it placed a higher strength at rest. The English 'soothing' follows this.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: אֶל לִבּוֹ is like עִם לִבּוֹ, 'with his heart'; afterwards He revealed the secret to Noah, who was a prophet. The English 'to himself' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:4` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'I will not again curse' means as the curse came through man, since it is written 'cursed is the ground' (3:17). The English follows.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:5` follows: Ibn Ezra: יֵצֶר is the nature that was formed in him. The English 'inclination' renders the sense of formation.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:6` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'I will not again' refers to the flood. The English follows.
- `ibn-ezra:8:21:7` follows: Ibn Ezra: the word עוֹד means 'for ever' or 'a second time', as in 'and he wept on his neck still' (Gen 46:29). The English 'never again' takes the second sense.
- `ramban:8:21:1` not-applicable: Ramban: God did not reveal this to a prophet at that time, but later when He commanded Moses to write the Torah; 'because of the human' means the animals were punished on account of man.
- `ramban:8:21:2` records: Ramban gives two readings of the מ in מנעריו: 'from the beginning of youth', as the sages say, or 'because of' youth, which makes the evil of the heart come from youth. He also records 'like בנעריו'.
- `radak:8:21:1` follows: Radak: the Torah speaks as men speak, as a figure; the offering was accepted with favour.
- `radak:8:21:2` follows: Radak: נִיחֹחַ is on the pattern of ניצוץ; the sense is that He set down (הניח) His anger in the world.
- `radak:8:21:3` not-applicable: Radak: God saw by His reason that the evil would not be many again, and that the whole would not be destroyed for the part.
- `radak:8:21:4` not-applicable: Radak: because of the human means that the wicked would end and the ground remain for the good.
- `radak:8:21:5` follows: Radak: it is called יֵצֶר because it is formed with the human; the evil inclination exists in a person before the good.
- `onkelos:8:21:1` follows: Onkelos renders: 'the LORD accepted his offering with favour, and said by His word, I will not again curse the earth on account of the sins of man, for the inclination of the heart of man is evil from his youth.'
- `sforno:8:21:1` not-applicable: Sforno: the offering was then fit to be offered.
- `sforno:8:21:3` not-applicable: Sforno: after the flood the temperaments are weaker, so the power of reason does not shine in them from their youth as it did before.
- apparatus: 8:21 Ramban and Rashi read מנעריו differently: Rashi as 'from the shaking out of the womb', Ramban as 'from the beginning of youth' or 'because of youth'.

Grammar notes:
- עֹד כָּל־יְמֵי הָאָרֶץ is a time clause, 'all the days of the earth still', with the etnaḥta marking its end; the plural verb יִשְׁבֹּתוּ at the end governs all the pairs.
- The three pairs are in an a-b pattern: sowing and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, then day and night; each pair is joined by vav.
- יִשְׁבֹּתוּ is the Qal of שׁבת 'to cease'; the same root gives the Sabbath, 'the ceasing'.
- The unusual accents (pazer, telisha gedola, qadma) give the list a slow, rhythmical delivery.

Translator's note: Rashi divides the year into six seasons of two months each (half Tishri to half Kislev for sowing, and so on); Ibn Ezra divides the year first in two (sowing and harvest) and then in four (cold and heat, summer and winter), which also fits the Hebrew pairs. The English keeps the six nouns as the Hebrew pairs them. 'For as long as the earth lasts' keeps כָּל־יְמֵי הָאָרֶץ, which Ibn Ezra reads as showing that the earth has an appointed end.

Commentary rulings:
- `rashi:8:22:1` records: Rashi: the six seasons are two months each, as the sages taught: half Tishri, Marheshvan and half Kislev is זרע, half Kislev, Tevet and half Shevat is קֹר (חֹרֶף) (Bava Metzia 106). The English keeps the six nouns.
- `rashi:8:22:2` records: Rashi on קור: it is more severe than חֹרֶף.
- `rashi:8:22:3` records: Rashi on חרף: the time of sowing barley and legumes, which are 'sharp' (חֲרִיפִין) and ripen quickly; it is half Shevat, Adar and half Nisan. The English 'winter' is the nearest ordinary season.
- `rashi:8:22:4` records: Rashi on קציר: half Nisan, Iyar and half Sivan.
- `rashi:8:22:5` records: Rashi on קיץ: half Sivan, Tammuz and half Av, the time of gathering figs and drying them in the fields, hence the name קַיִץ as in 2 Sam 16:2.
- `rashi:8:22:6` records: Rashi on חום: the end of the days of heat, half Av, Elul and half Tishri, when the world is hottest.
- `rashi:8:22:7` records: Rashi: 'day and night shall not cease' implies that they had ceased during the days of the flood, since the constellations did not serve and day was not distinguished from night. The English does not state this.
- `rashi:8:22:8` follows: Rashi: they shall not cease from keeping their order. The English 'will never cease' follows.
- `ibn-ezra:8:22:1` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'all the days of the earth' is a sign that the earth has a fixed end; he adds a remark on the midrash 'round about eighteen thousand'. The English 'as long as the earth lasts' keeps the sense.
- `ibn-ezra:8:22:2` follows: Ibn Ezra: He divided the year into two and afterwards into four; 'cold' is set against 'heat'. The English keeps the pairs.
- `ibn-ezra:8:22:3` follows: Ibn Ezra: 'summer' is set against 'winter', and they correspond to the four seasons of the year; all of them divide day and night, so that what one lacks is added to the other. The English keeps the pairs.
- `radak:8:22:1` not-applicable: Radak reports Ibn Ezra that 'all the days of the earth' shows it has an appointed end.
- `radak:8:22:2` not-applicable: Radak: the year is divided into two parts, sowing and harvest, and then into four seasons.
- `radak:8:22:5` not-applicable: Radak: they ceased in the flood year; also seed and harvest, cold, heat, summer, winter and day and night ceased for those in the ark, as no distinction was made; the sages differ on whether the constellations served.
- `onkelos:8:22:1` not-applicable: Onkelos translates word for word.
- `sforno:8:22:1` not-applicable: Sforno: 'will not cease' to continue in the unnatural manner that He set for them after the flood, because the sun now moves on a tilted wheel; before the flood it was always spring.
