Grammar notes:
- The chain of tradition has one verb, מָסַר 'hand over', and drops it after the first link: 'Joshua to the elders, the elders to the prophets'.
- מְסָרוּהָ is repeated in the last link because the verb is needed again for the plural subject 'prophets'.
- הֵם אָמְרוּ שְׁלשָׁה דְבָרִים introduces three imperatives: הֱווּ, הַעֲמִידוּ, עֲשׂוּ. The first is Qal of הָיָה, the others are Hifil and Qal of weak roots.
- קִבֵּל is Piel of קבל, 'received', and in Mishnaic Hebrew is the standard verb for receiving a tradition.
- The third saying is a metaphor: סְיָג is a hedge round a field or a vineyard.

Translator's note: 'Torah' is without the article, as in the Hebrew, and not 'the Torah'. 'From Sinai' is kept literal; Bartenura glosses it as 'from the One who was revealed at Sinai'. 'Members of the Great Assembly' follows decisions.md for אַנְשֵׁי כְנֶסֶת הַגְּדוֹלָה. 'Be deliberate' renders מְתוּנִים, from a root meaning 'slow, patient'; both Bartenura and Rambam read it as waiting before deciding a case.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:1:1` not-applicable: Bartenura: unlike the other tractates of the Mishnah this one is not built on a commandment but on ethics and character traits; the sages of the nations also wrote such books. The mishnah therefore begins with Moses at Sinai, to say that these teachings are not the sages' invention but were received from Sinai.
- `bartenura:1:1:2` follows: Bartenura: 'from Sinai' means from the One who was revealed at Sinai. The English keeps 'from Sinai' and adopts this sense.
- `bartenura:1:1:3` follows: Bartenura: the elders are those who lived long after Joshua, and they passed it to other elders until the first of the prophets, Eli the priest and Samuel of Ramah.
- `bartenura:1:1:4` follows: Bartenura: they were one hundred and twenty elders, among them Zerubbabel, Mordecai and later Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi. They were called 'Great' because they restored the crown to its former state, the divine attributes 'great, mighty and awesome'.
- `bartenura:1:1:5` follows: Bartenura: they said many things, but these three are those on which the keeping of the Torah rests.
- `bartenura:1:1:6` follows: Bartenura: if a case comes before you once, twice, three times, do not say 'I have already studied it'; wait (מַמְתִּינִים) before you decide. The English 'deliberate' takes this.
- `bartenura:1:1:7` follows: Bartenura: against Rabban Gamliel's rule, teach Torah to everyone without examining him (unless his bad conduct is known); or: one who raised disciples in youth should raise them in old age (Eccl 11:6).
- `bartenura:1:1:8` follows: Bartenura: a fence so that one does not come to touch a Torah prohibition, e.g. secondary degrees of forbidden unions and the Sabbath rest-decrees; compare Lev 18:30, 'make a guard for my guard'.
- `rambam:1:1:1` not-applicable: Rambam: he has already explained the nature of the tradition; his aim here is to explain the words of the pious and moralists, to urge the acquiring of useful traits, and to warn about some vices; for the rest he explains only words.
- `rambam:1:1:2` follows: Rambam: let them delay the decision and not rule quickly until they understand it, since new facts may appear that were not seen at first thought.
- `rambam:1:1:3` follows: Rambam: the 'fence' is the decrees and enactments that keep a man far from transgressions, as in 'you shall keep my charge' (Lev 18:30): make a guard for my guard.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:1:1:8` records: Rabbeinu Yonah: 'raise up many disciples' follows Beit Hillel, who teach everyone, against Beit Shammai who teach only the worthy.
- apparatus: Spelling: Vilna reads סייג for סְיָג.

Grammar notes:
- הוּא הָיָה אוֹמֵר: the pronoun, the past of הָיָה and a participle express a habitual saying ('he used to say').
- עַל ... עוֹמֵד: the sentence is a list of three pillars; each is introduced by עַל, and the last two by וְעַל.
- גְּמִילוּת חֲסָדִים is a construct chain: 'the bestowing of kindnesses'.
- מִשְּׁיָרֵי has a partitive מִ: 'one of the remnants', and not 'from (them)'.

Translator's note: הָעֲבוֹדָה is rendered 'the Temple service'; Bartenura (עֲבוֹדַת הַקָּרְבָּנוֹת) and Rambam (the sacrifices) agree. 'Stands' keeps the Hebrew verb; Bartenura glosses it 'the world was created only for these three'.

- `bartenura:1:2:1` follows: Bartenura: מִשִּׁיּוּרֵי: after all the others died the tradition remained in his hand; he was High Priest after Ezra.
- `bartenura:1:2:2` follows: Bartenura: 'he used to say' means this was constantly on his lips; so throughout the tractate.
- `bartenura:1:2:3` follows: Bartenura: the world was created only for the sake of these three things.
- `bartenura:1:2:4` follows: Bartenura: had Israel not accepted the Torah, heaven and earth would not have been created (Jer 33:25; Shabbat 88a).
- `bartenura:1:2:5` follows: Bartenura: the service of the sacrifices; the world stands on it (Taanit 27b); Noah's offerings were the ground of the promise not to bring a flood.
- `bartenura:1:2:6` follows: Bartenura: 'the world is built of kindness' (Ps 89:3); acts of kindness: gladdening brides and grooms, comforting mourners, visiting the sick, burying the dead.
- `rambam:1:2:1` follows: Rambam: the continuing repair of the world rests on wisdom (the Torah), on virtues of character (acts of kindness) and on keeping the commandments (the sacrifices).
- apparatus: Spelling and parse: none.

Grammar notes:
- The saying is built on antithesis: אַל תִּהְיוּ ... אֶלָּא הֱווּ, with the same words repeated, the second time with שֶׁלֹּא.
- עַל מְנָת (לְ) is an idiom for 'on condition (of)'; לְקַבֵּל is a Piel infinitive.
- הַמְשַׁמְּשִׁין is Piel participle plural with the article, and את marks the definite object הָרַב (a Mishnaic use with שמש).
- וִיהִי is the jussive 'and let there be'; the Mishnaic Hebrew uses it with a subject מוֹרָא שָׁמַיִם 'fear of Heaven'.
- The verb forms: תִּהְיוּ is imperfect used as a prohibition with אַל; הֱווּ is the imperative.

Translator's note: פְּרָס is rendered 'gratuity' because both commentators define it against שָׂכָר 'wage': a gift from one who owes nothing. אִישׁ סוֹכוֹ is 'of Sokho' (literally 'man of Sokho').

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:3:1` follows: Bartenura: פְּרָס is from the language of ערך 'valuation' (Targum: purseneh); it is what one gives to one who served him though he owes nothing, as a father gives a small son. Even for such a hope one should not serve one's Maker, but only from love.
- `bartenura:1:3:2` follows: Bartenura: even if you serve from love, serve also from fear: the lover is zealous in positive commandments, the one who fears is careful about prohibitions, and so one's service is complete.
- `rambam:1:3:1` follows: Rambam: פְּרָס is a reward given by one who owes no favour, unlike שָׂכָר which is due by law; one should serve God without hope of a reward, from love; but love does not remove the commandment of fear, hence 'let the fear of Heaven be upon you'.

Grammar notes:
- Pairs (זוּגוֹת): from Yose ben Yoezer on, teachers are named two by two, so קִבְּלוּ is plural; Bartenura says the first of each pair was the nasi and the second av beit din.
- בֵּית וַעַד is 'a house of meeting' for the sages; בֵּית is construct and וַעַד an absolute noun.
- מִתְאַבֵּק is a Hithpael participle ('covering oneself in dust'); with וֶהֱוֵי it makes a continuing action: 'be (continually) covered'.
- וֶהֱוֵי and וֶהֱוֵי: the imperative of הָיָה with vav takes ֶ before a guttural (e instead of u); the saying has three parallel commands.
- The accusative marker אֶת before דִּבְרֵיהֶם is a Mishnaic use; the word order puts בְצָמָא first, before the object.

Translator's note: מִתְאַבֵּק is rendered as the Hebrew image, 'covered in the dust of their feet', and not as the metaphor 'sit at their feet'. Bartenura gives two readings of it: (1) follow them closely, so that you are covered with the dust they raise; (2) sit on the ground at their feet while the teacher sits on the bench. The English keeps the image and takes (1), the first and more literal.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:4:1` not-applicable: Bartenura: in this chapter the sages are named in pairs, each pair receiving from the earlier pair; the first of each pair was the nasi, the second the av beit din.
- `bartenura:1:4:2` follows: Bartenura: when the sages want to gather, let your house be ready for it, so that they say 'let us meet at so-and-so's house'; you will learn something from them, like one who enters a perfumer's shop and carries out a good smell.
- `bartenura:1:4:3` follows: Bartenura: first reading, that you walk behind them and are filled with the dust they raise; second reading, that you sit on the ground at their feet. The English adopts the first and records the second in the apparatus.
- `bartenura:1:4:4` follows: Bartenura: like a thirsty man who drinks eagerly, and not like a sated man who loathes even good food.
- `rambam:1:4:1` follows: Rambam: בֵּית וַעַד is בֵּית מוֹעֵד: make your house ready so that the sages always gather there, so that when someone asks 'where shall we meet?' he is told 'in so-and-so's house'.
- apparatus: Second reading of מִתְאַבֵּק (Bartenura): sit on the ground at their feet.
- apparatus: Variant: in 1:4.16-17 Vilna omits אִישׁ צְרֵדָה in the second mention of the name; the base text has it.

### Translation under review (1:5)

English: Yose son of Yochanan of Jerusalem says: Let your house be open wide; and let the poor be members of your household; and do not talk too much with the woman. They said this of one's own wife; how much more so of the wife of his fellow. From here the sages said: Whenever a man talks too much with the woman, he brings evil upon himself, and neglects words of Torah, and in the end inherits Gehinnom.

Literal: Yose son-of Yochanan man-of Jerusalem says: Let-be your-house open to-breadth, and-let-be poor sons-of your-house, and-not multiply talk with the-woman. Of-his-wife they-said, light and-heavy of-wife-of his-fellow. From-here said sages: All time that-man multiplying talk with the-woman, causing evil to-himself, and-neglecting from-words-of Torah, and-his-end inheriting Gehinnom.

Grammar notes:
- The mishnah has a saying of Yose (three imperatives) and then a comment in the voice of the sages ('they said', 'from here the sages said'). Bartenura notes that the comment is Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's, who edited the mishnayot.
- קַל וָחֹמֶר is the argument a fortiori ('light and heavy'), here without a verb: 'how much more so'.
- עַל / בְּ: בְּאִשְׁתּוֹ אָמְרוּ uses ב to mean 'concerning'; the verb is plural with no stated subject ('they said' = 'people said / the sages said').
- אָדָם is 'a person', but the context is a man speaking with a woman; the English says 'a man'.
- גּוֹרֵם, בּוֹטֵל and יוֹרֵשׁ are participles that state a general result: 'is causing, is neglecting, is inheriting'.
- שִׂיחָה with הִרְבָּה/מַרְבֶּה (Hifil of רבה) is 'to do much talking'; the infinitive-like phrase has no object marker.

Translator's note: הָאִשָּׁה is rendered 'the woman', with the article, because Bartenura reads the article as the reason the warning is first about one's own wife (בְּאִשְׁתּוֹ). Rambam says talk with women is mostly about sexual matters, and gives that as the reason; Bartenura gives the cases of Korach and the wife's contempt. The two do not conflict on the English, and the English does not choose between them. 'Neglects words of Torah' follows both commentators: he is drawn to idle things and wastes his time. 'Open wide' (לִרְוָחָה) is the wide-open house; Bartenura: open to the four directions, as Abraham's was.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:5:1` follows: Bartenura: like the house of Abraham, which was open to the four corners of the world, so that guests would not have to go round to find the door.
- `bartenura:1:5:2` follows: Bartenura: let him not buy slaves to serve him; it is better that Israelites benefit from his property than the accursed seed of Canaan.
- `bartenura:1:5:3` follows: Bartenura: since he says 'the woman' and not 'a woman', we learn that they said it of one's own wife. Some explain it of a wife in her impurity only, but the mishnah indicates even a pure wife (Chagigah 5b: even idle talk between husband and wife is reported at the judgment), unless he must appease her for a religious reason.
- `bartenura:1:5:4` follows: Bartenura: Rabbi (who arranged the mishnayot) wrote this on the basis of Yose's words; when a man tells his wife his troubles, she teaches him to stir up strife (Korach), or she scorns him in her heart; both bring evil on himself.
- `bartenura:1:5:5` follows: Bartenura: he is drawn to idle things and does not engage in Torah.
- `rambam:1:5:1` follows: Rambam: let there be an open gate for travellers, so that anyone on the road who needs something, or is hungry or thirsty, may enter at once.
- `rambam:1:5:2` follows: Rambam: his servants should be the poor and weak, which is better than buying slaves; the sages used to blame those who bought slaves and praise those whose household was the poor.
- `rambam:1:5:3` follows: Rambam: talk with women is mostly about intercourse, so excessive talk with them is forbidden; 'evil to himself' means he acquires base traits and much desire.
- `rambam:1:5:4` follows: Rambam: it is clear that he wastes his time on other affairs.
- `rambam:1:5:5` follows: Rambam: because such talk brings him to rebellion and he becomes liable to punishment.
- apparatus: Spelling: 1:5.23 Vilna reads וחומר; 1:5.44 Vilna reads גיהנום; the base text has the pointed forms.
- apparatus: Reading of לִרְוָחָה: 'for relief', so that the house is open to give relief to the needy (a second sense of רְוָחָה).

Grammar notes:
- קִבְּלוּ is plural because there are two teachers, again named as a pair.
- עֲשֵׂה and קְנֵה are imperatives of final-ה verbs; לְךָ repeated after each verb is an ethical dative ('for yourself').
- וֶהֱוֵי דָן is the imperative of הָיָה with a participle: 'be one who judges', a continuing practice.
- לְכַף זְכוּת is an idiom: the scale of a balance tipped toward merit; the English 'favorably' gives the sense.
- The three commands are short and parallel, each with an object: רַב, חָבֵר, כָּל הָאָדָם.

Translator's note: 'Make for yourself a teacher' is taken in Rambam's sense: treat him as your teacher even if he is not fit. Bartenura quotes Rambam and then gives his own hearing of the words, that one should take one regular teacher; it is recorded. 'Acquire' is kept: Rambam stresses that the verb is קנה 'buy', not 'make'. 'Judge every person favorably' is the idiom; both commentators limit it (for an unknown man; a known wicked man may be judged otherwise), but the saying is kept in its brevity.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:6:1` records: Bartenura: Rambam explains that even if he is not fit to be your teacher, make him your teacher; Bartenura's own view is that he should take one teacher to learn from always, and not learn today from one and tomorrow from another (though Avodah Zarah 19a says one who learns from one teacher sees no blessing, which refers to reasoning only).
- `bartenura:1:6:2` follows: Bartenura: even if you must buy him at a high price and spend money to win his love; this is not said of a teacher, who must teach free of charge.
- `bartenura:1:6:3` follows: Bartenura: when the matter is in the scale with no tilt either way, such as a man of unknown conduct who does something that can be judged for merit or for guilt, the measure of piety is to judge for merit; but one known to be wicked may be judged for guilt.
- `rambam:1:6:1` follows: Rambam: even if he is not fit to be your teacher, set him up as your teacher and imagine him a teacher, and you will gain learning from it; learning from another is firmer than learning alone, even if he is your equal or below you.
- `rambam:1:6:2` follows: Rambam: he says 'acquire', not 'make' or 'join': one must acquire a friend who loves him, through whom his deeds and affairs are put right (as 'a companion or death'), and strive for him with all his heart; he then lists the kinds of friend: of use, of pleasure and of virtue.
- `rambam:1:6:3` follows: Rambam: when you do not know whether a man is righteous or wicked and he does something that may be taken well or badly, take it well; a known righteous man who is seen doing wrong may still be judged for good if there is any possibility; but of a known wicked man who does good, be careful.
- apparatus: The sages in the chain: Joshua son of Perachya and Nittai the Arbelite received from Yose son of Yoezer and Yose son of Yochanan (1:4-5).
- apparatus: The phrase 'to the scale of merit' (לְכַף זְכוּת) is the idiom; the pack's lexicon sense for כַּף is 1.4.2 'pan, vessel'.
