### Translation under review (1:13)

English: He used to say: One who makes a name great loses his name; and one who does not add will cease; and one who does not learn deserves to die; and one who makes use of the crown passes away.

Literal: he was saying: drawing-out name, lost his-name. and-who-not adding, will-cease. and-who-not learning, death liable. and-who-makes-use of-crown, passes-away.

Grammar notes:
- This saying is in Aramaic, except for the opening formula הוּא הָיָה אוֹמֵר; the Aramaic relative is דְּ- (Hebrew שֶׁ-) and the emphatic ending is -ָא (שְׁמָא, קְטָלָא, תָּגָא).
- Four short clauses, each a participle clause followed by its consequence; the first and the last are an inverted pair (name drawn out, name lost; crown used, one passes).
- וּדְלֹא מוֹסִיף יָסֵף: the same root יסף in the participle and the imperfect makes a wordplay, 'who does not add (מוֹסִיף) will cease/vanish (יָסֵף)', as Hebrew 'add' and 'come to an end' are both יסף.
- קְטָלָא חַיָּב: the adjective חַיָּב is predicate with the noun it governs, 'liable (to) death'.

Translator's note: נָגֵד is taken as 'draw out, extend', so one who stretches his name afar through power and the title of rabbi. Bartenura 1:13:3 gives a second reading, יֵאָסֵף ('be gathered to his people', i.e. die before his time); the English takes the first, יָסוּף ('cease'). In the last clause the English takes the first of Bartenura's explanations: the one who uses the crown of Torah as a tool. The jastrow lemma for 4 is a verb with a general sense ('draw a line') and the sense paths given for 2, 3, 15 and 17 are the nearest the pack offers.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:13:1` follows: Bartenura: one whose name is drawn out far through authority and lordship will soon lose it, for lordship buries its owner. נָגֵד is 'drawing', as the Targum has מִשְׁכוּ, נְגִידוּ.
- `bartenura:1:13:2` follows: Bartenura: one who does not add to his learning.
- `bartenura:1:13:3` follows: Bartenura: what he has already learned will cease from his mouth and he will forget his study. The other reading, יֵאָסֵף ('be gathered to his people', die untimely), is recorded in apparatus.
- `bartenura:1:13:4` follows: Bartenura: this is worse than 'does not add', hence 'liable to death', that is, it is fitting that he be killed, as in Pesachim 49: an unlearned person may be torn like a fish.
- `bartenura:1:13:5` follows: Bartenura: one who uses the crown of Torah, like a man using his tools, passes from the world; תָּגָא is a crown. He records two other explanations (תג"א as תלמיד גברא אחרינא, and use of the Ineffable Name), which are not adopted.
- apparatus: Bartenura on 1:13:3 records a variant reading יֵאָסֵף for יָסֵף.
- apparatus: Bartenura on 1:13:5 gives two other explanations of בְּתָגָא: an acronym תג"א (תלמיד גברא אחרינא, 'another man's disciple'), and a reading יִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ with the Ineffable Name.
- apparatus: Vilna reads שמיה (1:13.7), יסיף (1:13.10), חייב (1:13.14), and ודישתמש (1:13.15): spelling and form only.

### Translation under review (1:14)

English: He used to say: If I am not for myself, who is for me? And when I am for myself, what am I? And if not now, when?

Grammar notes:
- Three rhetorical questions, each with a conditional first half; the answer to each is understood.
- אֵין אֲנִי לִי: אֵין negates the existence of the subject 'I' for the benefit 'to me'; the verbless clause has no verb 'to be'.
- The preposition לִי is used twice in different senses: 'for me, to my account' and 'for myself, on my own'.
- וּכְשֶׁאֲנִי לְעַצְמִי: כְּשֶׁ- is the Mishnaic 'when', compounded from כ + שֶׁ.
- אֵימָתַי is the Mishnaic interrogative 'when', and עַכְשָׁיו the Mishnaic 'now'.

Translator's note: The English keeps the three clauses short and unpaired. 'Who is for me?' is left open: Bartenura reads it as 'who will earn merit for me' and Rambam as 'who will rouse my soul', so the English does not choose between them. אֵימָתַי is read by Bartenura as 'after death' and in the other explanation 'in old age'; the English leaves it as plain 'when'.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:14:1` follows: Bartenura: if I do not earn merit for myself, who will earn it on my behalf.
- `bartenura:1:14:2` follows: Bartenura: even if I have earned merit for myself, what is that merit and what is it worth against what I am obliged to do.
- `bartenura:1:14:3` follows: Bartenura: 'now' means in this world.
- `bartenura:1:14:4` follows: Bartenura: after death one can no longer earn merit; another explanation: if not now in youth, when, perhaps in old age it will not come to hand.
- `rambam:1:14:1` follows: Rambam: if I do not rouse my own soul upward, who will, since I have no outside rouser. And in the next clause, 'what am I': what has come of me, I am not complete even after doing this. He takes 'now' to be youth, since habits harden later (Prov 22:6).
- apparatus: Rambam applies the third clause to youth (Prov 22:6, 'train a youth in his way').
- apparatus: Bartenura gives two senses of אֵימָתַי: after death, and in old age.

### Translation under review (1:15)

English: Shammai says: Make your Torah fixed. Say little and do much, and receive every person with a pleasant countenance.

Literal: Shammai saying: make your-Torah regular. say little and-do much, and-be receiving [object-marker] all-of the-person with-countenance-of faces pleasant.

Phrases:
- 1:15.1 1:15.2 | Shammai says | 'Shammai saying'
- 1:15.3 1:15.4 1:15.5 | Make your Torah fixed | 'make your Torah regular'; Bartenura: your main occupation by day and night is Torah; work comes when you are weary of study
- 1:15.6 1:15.7 1:15.8 1:15.9 | Say little and do much | 'say little and do much'; Bartenura and Rambam both cite Abraham (Gen 18)
- 1:15.10 1:15.11 1:15.12 1:15.13 1:15.14 | and receive every person | 'and be receiving all the person'; Bartenura: as a host does with guests
- 1:15.15 1:15.16 1:15.17 | with a pleasant countenance | 'with countenance-of faces pleasant'; Rambam: dealing with people gently and with pleasing, welcome words

Grammar notes:
- Three imperatives, each with a different construction: עֲשֵׂה and אֱמֹר are plain imperatives, וֶהֱוֵי with a participle gives a continuous 'be receiving'.
- עֲשֵׂה תוֹרָתְךָ קֶבַע: a double accusative of result, 'make your Torah (into) something fixed'.
- אֱמֹר מְעַט וַעֲשֵׂה הַרְבֵּה: an antithesis, with מְעַט and הַרְבֵּה as adverbs.
- בְּסֵבֶר פָּנִים יָפוֹת: סֵבֶר is in the construct before פָּנִים; the adjective is feminine plural, as פָּנִים takes feminine adjectives.

Translator's note: קֶבַע is rendered 'fixed' in the sense of a permanent, not a passing, occupation. Bartenura also records a second reading: that you hold yourself to the same standard in your Torah as for others. סֵבֶר פָּנִים is a phrase for a look or expression of the face; 'pleasant countenance' is Rambam's 'gently and with welcome words'.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:15:1` follows: Bartenura: your main occupation is Torah, day and night; when weary of study, do work. The other way round (work as main, Torah when work allows) is excluded. He adds a second explanation: do not be strict with yourself and lenient with others, or the reverse.
- `bartenura:1:15:2` follows: Bartenura: as Abraham said 'I will fetch a morsel of bread' and then took a tender and good calf (Gen 18).
- `bartenura:1:15:3` follows: Bartenura: when you bring guests in, do not give with your face to the ground, for such giving is counted as nothing; Shammai's three warnings correspond to Jeremiah's wise, strong and rich.
- `rambam:1:15:1` follows: Rambam: Torah study is the root and chief thing and all other occupations follow it; if there is time, good, and if not, no harm. The righteous say little and do much, as Abraham; the wicked say much and do nothing, as Ephron.
- `rambam:1:15:2` follows: Rambam: to deal with people gently and with pleasant, welcome words.
- apparatus: Gen 18:5 (וְאֶקְחָה פַת לֶחֶם) and 18:7 (וַיִּקַּח בֶּן בָּקָר רַךְ וָטוֹב) are cited by Bartenura and Rambam for 'say little, do much'.
- apparatus: Vilna reads אמור (1:15.6) for אֱמֹר: spelling only.

### Translation under review (1:16)

English: Rabban Gamliel used to say: Make for yourself a teacher, and withdraw from doubt, and do not make a habit of tithing by estimates.

Literal: Rabban Gamliel was saying: make for-you teacher, and-withdraw from the-doubt, and-do-not increase to-tithe estimates.

Grammar notes:
- עֲשֵׂה לְךָ: 'make for yourself' is the idiom for choosing or acquiring something.
- הִסְתַּלֵּק is Hitpael: 'remove yourself, withdraw'.
- אַל תַּרְבֶּה לְעַשֵּׂר: the prohibitive אַל with the imperfect, followed by a Piel infinitive: 'do not do much tithing'.
- Here רַב is 'teacher'; elsewhere in Avot it can also mean 'great'.
- Vilna omits הָיָה in 'used to say' (1:16.3).

Translator's note: עֲשֵׂה לְךָ רַב: both Bartenura and Rambam say this concerns legal rulings, not study. The final clause has a plain sense, tithing (the Mishnaic technical term) from estimated produce. Rabbeinu Yonah's extension of this to any judgement made by estimate is not adopted in the English.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:16:1` follows: Bartenura: this concerns rulings: if a case comes before you and you are in doubt, make yourself a teacher and do not rule alone, as Rava gathered the butchers of the town.
- `bartenura:1:16:2` follows: Bartenura: tithing by estimate does not escape fault: too little, and the tithe is right but the fruit spoiled; too much, the fruit right and the tithe spoiled.
- `rambam:1:16:1` follows: Rambam: the teacher here is for rulings on forbidden and permitted, not for study; as the Yerushalmi has 'bring me an elder from the market that I may rely on him'. Likewise flee from tithing by estimate, for it is a doubtful case.
- apparatus: Vilna omits הָיָה at 1:16.3 and reads אומדות (1:16.14): spelling only.

Grammar notes:
- Three sayings in one mishnah, with the same three-part structure: personal testimony, then a negative form (וְלֹא ... אֶלָּא), then a general rule.
- וְלֹא מָצָאתִי לַגּוּף טוֹב אֶלָּא שְׁתִיקָה: a negated clause with אֶלָּא, 'nothing ... except'; לַגּוּף 'for the body' is the person.
- וְלֹא הַמִּדְרָשׁ הוּא הָעִקָּר: the pronoun הוּא stands as copula between subject and predicate.
- וְכָל הַמַּרְבֶּה: כָּל with the articular participle makes a general statement, 'every one who multiplies'.
- The last clause echoes Proverbs 10:19, בְּרֹב דְּבָרִים לֹא יֶחְדַּל פֶּשַׁע, as Rambam notes.

Translator's note: לַגּוּף: 'body' is read as the person; Bartenura's gloss is the person who hears his own shame and is silent. The mishnah's מִדְרָשׁ is rendered 'study', and Rambam and Bartenura both read it as speech about Torah that must lead to action. The last clause is read by Rambam as about speech in general and by Rabbeinu Yonah as about Torah rulings; the English leaves the words general.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:17:1` follows: Bartenura: one who hears his own shame and is silent.
- `bartenura:1:17:2` follows: Bartenura: even exposition and speaking in Torah, than which there is no better trait, is rewarded chiefly for the action; one who expounds and does not carry it out is better off silent.
- `bartenura:1:17:3` follows: Bartenura: Eve multiplied words ('neither shall you touch it'), added a touch not forbidden, and the serpent pushed her to touch it and then to eat; Solomon: 'do not add to his words' (Prov 30).
- `rambam:1:17:1` follows: Rambam: 'in many words transgression does not cease' (Prov 10:19); a man who speaks much is bound to say something improper; few words mark the wise, many the fool.
- `rambam:1:17:2` not-applicable: Rambam: first of a wise man's four classes of speech, all harm and no benefit, such as cursing and foul speech.
- `rambam:1:17:3` not-applicable: Rambam: second class, harm on one side and benefit on the other, such as praise that angers an enemy.
- `rambam:1:17:4` not-applicable: Rambam: third class, neither benefit nor harm, such as idle talk about buildings and foods.
- `rambam:1:17:5` follows: Rambam: fourth class, wholly beneficial; the wise man speaks only in that class and is silent in the others, which gives the sense of 'silence' as the best course for the body.
- `rambam:1:17:6` not-applicable: Rambam: the first of five classes under the Law, speech that is commanded, namely reciting and studying Torah.
- `rambam:1:17:7` not-applicable: Rambam: second class, forbidden speech: false witness, slander, cursing, foul speech and evil speech.
- `rambam:1:17:8` not-applicable: Rambam: third class, repulsive speech, idle talk (שיחה בטלה), which the pious avoided.
- `rambam:1:17:9` not-applicable: Rambam: fourth class, beloved speech, praising virtues and condemning vices; he says this is called דרך ארץ.
- `rambam:1:17:10` follows: Rambam: fifth class, permitted speech about livelihood, food and clothing, to be kept short; and for מדרש he cites 'fair are words from those who do them', that deeds must agree with words and few words carry much.
- apparatus: Proverbs 10:19 (בְּרֹב דְּבָרִים לֹא יֶחְדַּל פֶּשַׁע) is cited by Rambam.
- apparatus: Vilna reads העיקר (1:17.18) for הָעִקָּר: spelling only.

English: Rabban Shimon son of Gamliel says: On three things the world stands: on justice, and on truth, and on peace, as it is said (Zechariah 8): Truth and a judgment of peace: judge in your gates.

Grammar notes:
- עַל שְׁלשָׁה דְבָרִים הָעוֹלָם עוֹמֵד: the preposition עַל means 'upon' (the world rests on); the participle עוֹמֵד is 'stands, endures'.
- The three items are each introduced with עַל and the article: הַדִּין, הָאֱמֶת, הַשָּׁלוֹם.
- שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר introduces the verse (Zech 8:16) as a proof text; the mishnah reads the verse as naming the three things, truth, judgment, and peace.
- אֱמֶת וּמִשְׁפַּט שָׁלוֹם: the verse has truth and a judgment of peace, with שִׁפְטוּ, a plural imperative, 'judge'.
- בְּשַׁעֲרֵיכֶם: 'in your gates', the place of the court.

Translator's note: הָעוֹלָם עוֹמֵד: Bartenura says that here 'the world' means the settlement of human beings, not the world of the previous mishnah (1:2). The English keeps 'the world'. Rambam reads the three things as the political order (justice), the intellectual virtues (truth), and the moral virtues (peace); this is recorded but not adopted. The verse is translated from the Hebrew of the mishnah, the three items answering to 'truth', 'judgment', 'peace'.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:18:1` follows: Bartenura: the settlement of human beings endures; this is not the 'world stands' of the earlier mishnah.
- `bartenura:1:18:2` follows: Bartenura: justice is to acquit the innocent and convict the guilty.
- `bartenura:1:18:3` follows: Bartenura: truth is that no man lie to his fellow.
- `bartenura:1:18:4` follows: Bartenura: peace between kingdoms and between a man and his fellow.
- `rambam:1:18:1` records: Rambam: justice is conduct of the state with uprightness; truth is the intellectual virtues and peace the moral virtues, and when the three are found, existence is as perfect as it can be.
- apparatus: Zechariah 8:16: אֱמֶת וּמִשְׁפַּט שָׁלוֹם שִׁפְטוּ בְּשַׁעֲרֵיכֶם.
- apparatus: Vilna reads טז (the verse number 16) where the base has the chapter number ח at 1:18.19; the citation is Zech 8:16.

- `2` crown. Targ. Cant. III, 11 (some ed. תַּגָּא, תָּגָּא). Targ. II Esth. II, 17 (ed. Vien. תַּגָּא); a. e.—Snh. 105ᵃ, v. חוֹצַף. Ab. I, 13, v. שְׁמַשׁ. Gitt. 57ᵃ שקליה קיסר לתָאגֵיה וכ׳ the Cæsar took off his crown and put it on the ground. Meg. 6ᵇ תלת מאה מלכי …
- `3` crownlet on letters. Men. 29ᵇ מ"ט אית ליה תאגא why has the letter ה a crownlet?; Yalk. Gen. 19. Sabb. 104ᵃ מ"ט … תָּגֵיה דקו"ף וכ׳ why is the crownlet of the Kof turned towards the Resh?—Pl. תָּגִין, תָּגי. Targ. Esth. I, 3.—Treat. Sof’rim IX, 1 ב׳ דבראשית … ת…

### `jastrow:תּוֹרָה` תּוֹרָה (f.): teaching, law, esp. ת׳(ה) the Law of Moses, Pentateuch; in gen. the Jewish law, Religion. Ber. 5ᵃ (ref. to Ex. XXIV, 12) ת׳ זה מקרא וכ׳ torah means the Pentateuch, ‘and the mitsvah’ means the Mishnah, ‘which I have written’ means the Phrophets and Hagiographa …
