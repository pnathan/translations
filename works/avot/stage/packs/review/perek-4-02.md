### Translation under review (4:7)

English: Rabbi Ishmael his son says: One who holds himself back from judging removes from himself enmity, robbery, and a false oath. But one who is overbold of heart in giving rulings is a fool, wicked, and haughty of spirit.

Grammar notes:
- הַחוֹשֵׂךְ and הַגַּס are articular participle and adjective used as subjects: 'the one who holds back', 'the one who is coarse'.
- The first sentence gives a cause and its three results, enmity, robbery and a false oath; the second sets the opposite case, with three damning terms.
- גַּס לִבּוֹ is a subject-predicate phrase: 'coarse of heart'; גַּס רוּחַ is a construct chain: 'coarse of spirit'.
- Rabbi Ishmael is called 'his son': the son of Rabbi Yose in 4:6.

Translator's note: Rabbi Ishmael 'his son' is, by Rabbeinu Yonah's heading, the son of Rabbi Yose in 4:6. Bartenura and Rambam give the sense of גַּס לִבּוֹ בַהוֹרָאָה as giving rulings without study or fear, and the English 'overbold' follows that; the literal sense is 'coarse of heart'. The pack's lexicon entries for גַּס I (falcon) and עֶצֶם (Ezem) do not match the words, and I have kept the data and noted the mismatch.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:7:1` follows: Bartenura: one holds back where there is a greater person present, or tells the litigants to compromise.
- `bartenura:4:7:2` follows: Bartenura: whoever leaves the court as the losing party hates the judge, saying in his heart that the judge did not turn the case in his favor.
- `bartenura:4:7:3` follows: Bartenura: the judge might hold the innocent party liable, so that a robbery comes about through him.
- `bartenura:4:7:4` follows: Bartenura: the judge might impose an oath on one not liable to it and so bring about a false oath; or the tanna calls a lying oath 'an oath of falsehood'.
- `bartenura:4:7:5` follows: Bartenura: he rules 'without examination and without waiting', which gives 'overbold' for גַּס לִבּוֹ.
- `rambam:4:7:1` follows: Rambam: 'his heart is coarse in rulings, emboldened to give rulings without awe and fear'.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah's lemma reads 'Rabbi Ishmael son of Rabbi Yose'; the pack's base text has only 'his son'.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah cites Deut 16:18-20 and the duty to judge, answered by 'where there are no other judges'.

### Translation under review (4:8)

English: He used to say: Do not judge alone, for none judges alone but One. And do not say, "Accept my opinion," for they are permitted, and you are not.

Grammar notes:
- הוּא הָיָה אוֹמֵר is a past habitual: 'he used to say'.
- אַל תְּהִי דָן is a prohibition made of אַל, the jussive of הָיָה and a participle.
- שֶׁאֵין דָּן יְחִידִי אֶלָּא אֶחָד: 'there is none judging alone except One'; the play is on יְחִידִי and אֶחָד.
- שֶׁהֵן רַשָּׁאִין: the word רַשַּׁאי is the Mishnaic adjective 'authorized'.

Translator's note: 'None judges alone but One': Bartenura reads אֶחָד as the Holy One; the English keeps 'One' with a capital so the ambiguity of 'one man' is not lost. The pack's lexicon entry for דָּן is the name Dan, which does not fit the participle.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:8:1` follows: Bartenura: even an expert who may judge alone should not, as a way of piety, unless the parties have accepted him; then he may.
- `bartenura:4:8:2` follows: Bartenura: 'One' is the Holy One, blessed be He, who is alone in His world.
- `bartenura:4:8:3` follows: Bartenura: 'do not say' to your colleagues who disagree with you.
- `bartenura:4:8:4` follows: Bartenura: 'accept my opinion' means 'I am an expert and could have judged without your joining'.
- `bartenura:4:8:5` follows: Bartenura: since you joined them with you, the ruling should be decided by the majority.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah brings a second explanation of 'except One': the expert (mumhe) alone may judge, but others should not count themselves experts.

Grammar notes:
- The two halves are in chiastic parallel: fulfilling from poverty leads to fulfilling from wealth; neglecting from wealth leads to neglecting from poverty.
- מֵעֹנִי and מֵעשֶׁר: the מִן is of the circumstance, 'out of'.
- The suffix הָ on לְקַיְּמָהּ refers to the Torah, which is feminine.
- סוֹפוֹ לְ + infinitive means 'his end is to ...', 'he will end by ...'.

Translator's note: 'Fulfills' and 'neglects' are chosen from Bartenura and Rambam: studying Torah under the pressure of need, as against leaving it for the preoccupation of wealth. The pack's lexicon entries for קְיָם ('decree') and עָשַׂר (tithing verb) do not fit קִיֵּם and עֹשֶׁר.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:9:1` follows: Bartenura: from poverty means he is pressed for food and leaves his work to engage in Torah.
- `bartenura:4:9:2` follows: Bartenura: from wealth means that for the abundance of money he must turn his heart one way and another and has no leisure for Torah.
- `rambam:4:8:1` not-applicable: Rambam (filed at 4:8): the Torah allowed an expert to judge alone; the mishnah warns only as a moral counsel; colleagues who disagree cannot be forced. It bears on 4:8, not on the words of 4:9.
- `rambam:4:9:1` follows: Rambam: one who studies Torah in poverty will in the end study it in wealth, free of distraction; one who neglects it for the sake of abundance will become poor, and his trouble to find bread will cause his neglect.
- apparatus: Variants: Vilna reads המקיים, מעוני, לקיימה, מעושר (twice) and מעוני; the base text has the short spellings. No difference of meaning.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah cites Prov 30:8, 'give me neither poverty nor riches'.

Grammar notes:
- הֱוֵי and וֶהֱוֵי are the Mishnaic imperatives of הָיָה; וַעֲסֹק is an imperative of עסק.
- The two conditions are parallel: if you neglect, if you toil; the first result is 'you have' (לְךָ), the second 'he has' (לוֹ), a shift to the third person.
- בְטֵלִים הַרְבֵּה is 'many idle ones'; הַרְבֵּה is a Hifil infinitive absolute used as an adverb or adjective.
- יֶשׁ לוֹ ... לִתֶּן is 'he has ... to give': a possessive idiom with an infinitive.

Translator's note: בְטֵלִים is kept as 'idle ones': Bartenura offers two readings, those who help make you idle, and the wicked and beasts who are idle, by whom the Holy One brings punishment. Rabbeinu Yonah has the second. The English keeps the plain word. 'He has' keeps the Hebrew shift to the third person; Bartenura reads God as the subject.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:10:1` follows: Bartenura: reduce your trade and work, and let your main occupation be Torah.
- `bartenura:4:10:2` follows: Bartenura: be humble in order to learn even from one lesser than you in wisdom. Rambam reads it more widely, humble even with the great and the small.
- `bartenura:4:10:3` records: Bartenura gives two readings: those who help to make you idle, or the idle wicked and beasts through whom the Holy One brings punishment. The English keeps the plain 'idle ones'.
- `bartenura:4:10:4` follows: Bartenura: He Himself pays your reward, not through a messenger; thus the measure of good exceeds the measure of punishment.
- `rambam:4:10:1` follows: Rambam: reduce trade and persist in Torah, and be humble before every person as if he were greater than you; this is to flee from pride.
- apparatus: Variants: Vilna reads ועסוק and ליתן; base וַעֲסֹק, לִתֶּן.
- apparatus: Bartenura's second reading of בְטֵלִים, and Rabbeinu Yonah's 'bears and lions idle from work', both rest on the sense 'those who are idle'.

Grammar notes:
- הָעוֹשֶׂה and הָעוֹבֵר are participles with the article used as subjects; קוֹנֶה לוֹ is the predicate.
- פְרַקְלִיט and קַטֵּגוֹר are Greek loanwords (paraklētos, katēgoros); one speaks for, the other against.
- תְּשׁוּבָה וּמַעֲשִׂים טוֹבִים is a short sentence without a verb: a nominal predicate with כ-.
- With Rabbi Johanan the Sandal-maker the saying changes: the two halves differ by לְשֵׁם שָׁמַיִם and its negation.

Translator's note: פְרַקְלִיט and קַטֵּגוֹר are rendered 'advocate' and 'accuser', following Bartenura's 'good intercessor' and 'evil intercessor' and Rambam's court setting before the ruler. פֻּרְעָנוּת is 'punishment'; Rambam says blows and diseases. The pack's lexicon entries for קָנָה, טוֹב and שֵׁם are proper names and do not fit; I have kept the data and noted the mismatch.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:11:1` follows: Bartenura: פְרַקְלִיט is an angel who intercedes for good.
- `bartenura:4:11:2` follows: Bartenura: קַטֵּגוֹר is an intercessor for evil; the word comes from 'calling a quarrel', and its opposite סַנֵּגוֹר means 'hating a quarrel'.
- `rambam:4:11:1` follows: Rambam: the פרקליט speaks well for a man before the ruler; the קטיגור informs against him to the king and seeks to put him to death.
- apparatus: Variants: Vilna reads עבירה, קטיגור, הפורענות and להתקיים (twice); base spellings are the short forms.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah's lemma 4:13:4 is anchored here in error; it belongs to 4:13.

Grammar notes:
- יְהִי is the jussive of הָיָה: 'let ... be'.
- The three clauses form a chain: the last term of each clause is the first term of the next (honor of your student, honor of your colleague, reverence of your teacher, reverence of Heaven).
- כְּשֶׁלְּךָ uses the Mishnaic particle שֶׁל: 'like that of yours'.
- שָׁמָיִם is spelled with a qamets in the pack (שָׁמָיִם), the usual form is שָׁמַיִם; the dual noun stands for God.

Translator's note: The three comparisons increase in rank: student, colleague, teacher, Heaven. The decision on שָׁמַיִם, of God, is 'Heaven'. The commentators (Bartenura) bring a verse for each clause; see the apparatus.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:12:1` follows: Bartenura: Moses made Joshua his student equal to himself, saying 'choose us men'.
- `bartenura:4:12:2` follows: Bartenura: Aaron said to Moses 'Oh my lord', though Moses was his younger brother, and called him lord.
- `bartenura:4:12:3` follows: Bartenura: 'my lord Moses, destroy them': whoever rebels against your teacher rebels as if against the Holy One and deserves destruction.
- apparatus: Variant: Vilna reads חבירך for חֲבֵרְךָ.
- apparatus: Bartenura's verses: Exod 17:9 (Moses to Joshua, 'choose us men'); Num 12:11 (Aaron to Moses, 'Oh my lord'); Num 11:28 (Joshua, 'my lord Moses, destroy them').
