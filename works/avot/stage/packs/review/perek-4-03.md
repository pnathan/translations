Grammar notes:
- Two sayings joined in one mishnah: Rabbi Yehuda on care in study, Rabbi Shimon on the three crowns.
- שִׁגְגַת תַּלְמוּד עוֹלָה זָדוֹן: a wordplay of opposites, שְׁגָגָה (inadvertent error) and זָדוֹן (deliberate sin). עוֹלָה is 'rises to the level of, counts as'.
- עוֹלָה / עוֹלֶה: the same verb used twice, first for an error that 'rises' to deliberate sin, then for the good name that 'rises' above the crowns.
- כֶּתֶר is in the construct state throughout: 'crown of Torah', 'crown of priesthood', 'crown of kingship', 'crown of a good name'.
- גַּבֵּיהֶן has a feminine plural suffix, referring to the three crowns (כְּתָרִים is treated as feminine).

Translator's note: תַּלְמוּד is rendered 'study' (Bartenura: one's learning done with precision and according to halakha), not the later Talmud. The pack lexicon gives the Jastrow entry for תַּלְמוּד and for זָהִיר. For וְכֶתֶר שֵׁם טוֹב the vav is rendered 'but' because the good name is set against the three crowns above it.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:13:1` follows: Bartenura: 'be careful in study' means that your study should be precise and in accord with halakha.
- `bartenura:4:13:2` follows: Bartenura: if you err in a ruling because you were not precise in your study and you permit what is forbidden, the Holy One, blessed be He, counts it against you as if you had acted deliberately.
- `bartenura:4:13:3` follows: Bartenura: the three crowns are those written in the Torah, for which the Torah obliges us to show honour.
- `bartenura:4:13:4` follows: Bartenura: of the crown of Torah it is written 'you shall honour the face of an elder' (Lev 19:32); this is one who has acquired wisdom.
- `bartenura:4:13:5` follows: Bartenura: of the crown of priesthood it is written 'you shall sanctify him, he shall be holy to you' (Lev 21:8).
- `bartenura:4:13:6` follows: Bartenura: of the crown of kingship it is written 'you shall surely set a king over yourself' (Deut 17:15), so that his awe is upon you.
- `bartenura:4:13:7` follows: Bartenura: the one with good deeds and a good reputation has no crown in the Torah that obliges honour; but the good name rises above all three, since all three crowns need it. Without it a sage may be despised and a high priest not obeyed.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:13:1` records: Rabbeinu Yonah: one must review one's learning and not rely on a first impression; since error is common in Torah matters, one who does not take care is a negligent sinner rather than an inadvertent one.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:13:2` not-applicable: Rabbeinu Yonah: priesthood was given to the house of Aaron, kingship to the house of David, while the crown of Torah lies before all who come into the world.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:13:3` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: all the crowns need the crown of a good name, which itself depends on the crown of Torah; he cites 'a good name is better than good oil' (Eccl 7:1).

Grammar notes:

- גּוֹלֶה is a participle after הֱוֵי: 'be one who goes into exile'.
- יְקַיְּמוּהָ is a Piel (here Aramaic-influenced קָם) with a 3rd masculine plural subject and a 3rd feminine singular suffix referring to the Torah.
- אַל תֹּאמַר and אַל תִּשָּׁעֵן: negative commands use אַל with the jussive-form imperfect.
- The Hebrew sets the two clauses by position: the move to a place of Torah is paired with the refusal to rely on colleagues, then on one's own insight.

Translator's note: Bartenura and Rabbeinu Yonah agree that the clause שֶׁחֲבֵרֶיךָ יְקַיְּמוּהָ בְיָדֶךָ is what one must not rely on: colleagues who return from the teacher will not secure the Torah for you. In 4:14.16 the pack parses וְאֶל under the lemma אַל (adverb 'not') while the sense is the preposition 'on' (אֶל for עַל, as in Prov 3:5); the data is kept and the sense is noted here. Grammar notes:
- Two sayings: Rabbi Yannai on the unanswerable question of the wicked and the righteous; Rabbi Matya on greeting and on where to stand.
- אֵין בְּיָדֵינוּ לֹא מִ... וְאַף לֹא מִ...: 'it is not in our hands, neither from ... nor even from ...'. The מִ governs each object: neither the tranquility nor the suffering is within our grasp.
- מַקְדִּים בִּשְׁלוֹם: a Hiphil participle after הֱוֵי: 'be one who is first in greeting'.
- וֶהֱוֵי with the vav pointed with segol before the guttural-initial imperative; it is the same imperative as הֱוֵי.
- זָנָב לָאֲרָיוֹת and רֹאשׁ לַשּׁוּעָלִים form a chiastic pair of opposites: tail/head, lions/foxes; לְ marks the group one is tail or head of.

Translator's note: אֵין בְּיָדֵינוּ is kept in its literal, ambiguous form. Bartenura and Rabbeinu Yonah take it as 'we cannot grasp (why the wicked prosper and the righteous suffer)'; Bartenura's second reading, 'in exile we have neither the ease of the wicked nor the sufferings of the righteous', is recorded in the commentary entry and not adopted. In 4:15.23 וֶהֱוֵי follows the same lemma as הֱוֵי. The lexicon (BDB) lists רֹאשׁ with the proper name sense as first entry; 'head' is meant.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:15:1` follows: Bartenura: 'like לֹא הֲוָה בְיָדֵיהּ': the matter is not known to us, why the way of the wicked prospers and why the righteous are afflicted. A second explanation: in exile we have neither the ease God usually gives the wicked to drive them from the world to come, nor the sufferings reserved for the righteous.
- `bartenura:4:15:2` follows: Bartenura: greet first 'even a non-Jew in the market'.
- `bartenura:4:15:3` follows: Bartenura: a tail to lions means to those greater than you.
- `bartenura:4:15:4` follows: Bartenura: the head of foxes means of those smaller than you.
- `rambam:4:15:1` follows: Rambam: it is better and more fitting to be a student of a greater sage than a teacher of a lesser one, for the first adds and the second diminishes; he compares the rule that one raises but does not lower in holy matters.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:15:1` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: the righteous do not have the quiet, security and wealth that the wicked have.

Grammar notes:
- The saying is a simile (דּוֹמֶה לְ) followed by an imperative drawing the practical consequence.
- בִּפְנֵי הָעוֹלָם הַבָּא: 'before the world to come', in front of it as an entrance hall is in front of the hall.
- הַתְקֵן is a Hiphil imperative of תקן: 'make ready, put in order'; the object is the reflexive עַצְמְךָ.
- פְּרוֹזְדוֹר and טְרַקְלִין are loanwords from Greek (prozdor, triklinion); the first is the entrance room, the second the inner hall.
- הָעוֹלָם הַבָּא is the standard term for the world to come; הַבָּא is a participle used as an adjective.

Translator's note: פְּרוֹזְדוֹר has no lexicon key in the pack; it is 'vestibule', Bartenura's 'gatehouse, porch (Targum: אוּלָם)'. טְרַקְלִין is 'the great hall', Bartenura 'the king's seat', Rambam 'the temple or palace hall'; the pack's Jastrow entry is a cross-reference only. The grammatical term for the particle כְּדֵי is a preposition with דַּי 'enough'.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:16:1` follows: Bartenura: פְּרוֹזְדוֹר means a gatehouse, rendered in Targum as אוּלָם (porch).
- `bartenura:4:16:2` follows: Bartenura: טְרַקְלִין is the place where the king sits; so prepare yourself in this world to merit the world to come.
- `rambam:4:16:1` follows: Rambam: טְרַקְלִין is the palace hall and פְּרוֹזְדוֹר the gatehouse; in this world one acquires the virtues that bring merit in the next, for this world is a road and a passage to the next.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:16:1` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: טְרַקְלִין is the palace; this world exists only to earn the world to come.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah's text of the saying lacks the name; the pack matches it on the sentence.

Grammar notes:
- The saying has two balanced halves, each pairing one hour in one world against all the life of the other.
- הוּא הָיָה אוֹמֵר: 'He used to say', a habitual past formed with הָיָה plus a participle.
- יָפָה שָׁעָה is a predicate adjective before its subject: 'better is an hour'.
- קוֹרַת רוּחַ: the idiom means contentment; literally 'coolness of spirit' (Jastrow's קוֹרָה II) rather than 'beam of spirit'.
- מִכָּל after יָפָה is the comparative מִ: 'than all'.

Translator's note: The pack's lexicon links קוֹרָה to BDB 'rafter, beam', which does not fit; Jastrow's קוֹרָה II 'cold, cooling' (קוֹרַת רוּחַ) is the sense used, and the data is kept. 'Spiritual contentment' renders the idiom; the literal sense is in the phrase note. The subject of הוּא is Rabbi Yaakov of 4:16 per the plain sequence.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:17:1` follows: Bartenura: 'for the sake of repentance and good deeds'.
- `rambam:4:18:1` not-applicable: Rambam (anchored on הַזֶּה of 4:17.11 but filed at 4:18): this is clear; these are moral teachings on the proper ordering of human society, putting each thing in its useful place.
- `bartenura:4:17:2` follows: Bartenura: repentance and good deeds give no benefit in the world to come, which exists only to receive reward for what one did in this world.
- `rambam:4:17:1` follows: Rambam: as explained in Sanhedrin chapter 10, after death there is neither perfection nor increase; a person perfects himself and adds merit in this world, as Solomon hints (Eccl 9:10), so one must strive in this short time.
- apparatus: Eccl 9:10 is cited by Rambam, not by the mishnah.

Grammar notes:
- Four prohibitions, each with the same shape: אַל + verb + the person + בִשְׁעַת (at the time of) + his state.
- אַל תְּרַצֶּה אֶת חֲבֵרְךָ: the Piel of רצה governs the direct object marked by אֶת.
- תְּנַחֲמֶנּוּ has the energic nun (-ֶנּוּ) before the 3rd masculine suffix, a feature of the Mishnah's style after אַל.
- מֵתוֹ מֻטָּל לְפָנָיו: the dead is 'laid out before him' - the period between death and burial.
- תִּשְׁאַל לוֹ בִשְׁעַת נִדְרוֹ: 'ask for him' - the commentators read it as inquiring about grounds for annulment (a petach) of a vow.
- The repeated בִשְׁעַת / בְּשָׁעָה gives the saying its rhythm: the right time matters as much as the act.

Translator's note: שָׁעָה is 'hour, moment' (time), rendered 'at the time of'. For תִּשְׁאַל לוֹ the plain 'ask him' would be לוֹ with a different sense; Bartenura and Rabbeinu Yonah both take it as asking on his behalf, seeking openings to release his vow, and the English follows them. The pack parses שָׁדַל as a Hithpael; 'strive' is the Mishnaic sense of 'seek to persuade, make an effort'.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:18:1` follows: Bartenura: as it is written 'My face shall go and I will give you rest' (Ex 33:14): the Holy One told Moses to wait until the face of wrath passed (Berakhot 7a).
- `bartenura:4:18:2` follows: Bartenura: at the destruction God, so to speak, was mourning, and the ministering angels sought to comfort Him, but the holy spirit answered 'do not press to comfort me' (Isa 22:4).
- `bartenura:4:18:3` follows: Bartenura: do not seek openings to release his vow, because he will reply to each opening that he vowed with that in mind, and you will not find one; he cites God's oath to Moses.
- `bartenura:4:18:4` follows: Bartenura: 'at the time of his disgrace' means when he has fallen into sin; he is ashamed before everyone, as with Adam, to whom God did not appear until they made loincloths (Gen 3).
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:18:1` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: you will lead him to say unseemly words, for you add anger to his anger.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:18:2` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: in the hour of grief consolation adds anger and he says bad words.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:18:3` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: do not ask him for openings to release, as he is angry and vowing and will state every question to be bound into the vow so that no release can be found; you cause him harm.
- `rabbeinu-yonah:4:18:4` follows: Rabbeinu Yonah: do not see and shame him in the hour of his misfortune or sin, when he is already ashamed of himself.
