Grammar notes:
- Samuel the Small quotes Proverbs 24:17-18 word for word; the mishnah's own words are only the introduction.
- בִּנְפֹל and בִּכָּשְׁלוֹ are infinitives construct with ב: temporal clauses ('when he falls', 'when he stumbles').
- אַל with the imperfect (תִּשְׂמָח, יָגֵל) is a prohibition; the second is in the third person, 'let your heart not exult'.
- פֶּן ('lest') introduces the feared result, three verbs in a chain: He sees, it is evil in His eyes, He turns away.
- הֵשִׁיב is Hiphil of שׁוב ('cause to return, turn back'), not the Qal שָׁב: Bartenura draws a lesson from this.

Translator's note: The quoted verse is kept in the order of the Hebrew. 'Turn His anger away from him' is the plain sense; Bartenura reads the Hiphil as: He removes it from your enemy and brings it back onto you. Both readings take the anger as turned away from the enemy. Rambam's remark on אַפּוֹ (not 'his burning anger') is recorded.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:19:1` not-applicable: Bartenura: this is a verse in Proverbs, but Samuel the Small was accustomed to admonish people with this saying.
- `bartenura:4:19:2` follows: Bartenura: 'lest the Lord see' (and it be evil in His eyes) because you made Him, in your heart, as though He were your agent to fulfil your wish. The English keeps the text's sense that it is evil in His eyes.
- `bartenura:4:19:3` follows: Bartenura: since the verse says 'turn' (וְהֵשִׁיב) and not 'return' (וְשָׁב), it means He will remove the anger from your enemy and bring it onto you. The English 'turn His anger away from him' agrees that the anger leaves the enemy; the transfer to the reader is Bartenura's reading of the Hiphil.
- `rambam:4:19:1` records: Rambam: the verse does not say 'his burning anger' but 'his anger', which teaches that all his sins are forgiven; Solomon said this as a precept but this sage used to admonish with it against this transgression. The forgiveness reading is recorded, not added to the English.
- apparatus: Source verse: Proverbs 24:17-18. In the verse the MT has יֵרַע (it is evil), ending וְרַע בְּעֵינָיו וְהֵשִׁיב מֵעָלָיו אַפּוֹ.
- apparatus: Variant: Vilna reads יז for the chapter number in the citation (Prov. 24 here; the reference is a gloss).
- apparatus: Variant: Vilna spells בנפול.

Grammar notes:
- The three sayings share the formula לְמַה הוּא דוֹמֶה ('to what is he like'), which opens a mashal (comparison).
- The pairs run in parallel: child and old man (ink on new or erased paper); the young and the old (unripe and ripe grapes, wine fresh from the press and aged wine).
- רַבִּי יוֹסֵי בַר יְהוּדָה: בַר is Aramaic for 'son of'; the Vilna text reads 'son of Rabbi'.
- Rabbi answers with a different image: אַל תִּסְתַּכֵּל with בְּ takes the jar as the object looked at, and אֶלָּא בְמַה שֶּׁיֶּשׁ בּוֹ gives what to look at instead.
- The last sentence is elliptical: 'a new jar full of old' means old wine, and 'an old one that has not even new in it' means an old jar with not even new wine.

Translator's note: The Hebrew of the first saying leaves the child as the learner ('learns a child'); the English reads 'learns as a child', as the commentators do. אִישׁ כְּפַר הַבַּבְלִי: 'a man of Kefar HaBavli', a place name; the literal 'village of the Babylonian' is given in the token glosses. דְּיוֹ, ink, is spelled לִדְיוֹ in the base text.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:20:1` follows: Bartenura: paper that is new 'endures'; so the learning of a child is lasting. He adds that the reading 'childhood' (יַלְדוּת) is not found. The English follows the text as it stands.
- `bartenura:4:20:2` follows: Bartenura: unripe grapes have not ripened fully and set the teeth on edge (קֵהוֹת); so the wisdom of a child has not settled and his words are not accepted or settled in the heart.
- `bartenura:4:20:3` follows: Bartenura: wine from its winepress still has its lees mixed in it; so the wisdom of a child is a mixture of doubts.
- `bartenura:4:20:4` follows: Bartenura: Rabbi disagrees with Rabbi Yose son of Yehudah: there is a new jar full of old wine, and so there is a child whose taste is the taste of the old, and there are old men who fall short of the young in wisdom.
- `rambam:4:20:1` follows: Rambam: learning in childhood endures and is not easily forgotten; the reverse for old age. Rabbi Yose says the wisdom of the young has questions and doubts not yet refined; Rabbi says 'do not judge the wine by its jar', for there is a new jar with old wine and an old jar that is empty.

- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah (not normative): the old man's learning is lost quickly; he adds a comparison from Mivchar HaPeninim: engraving on stone against engraving on sand.

Grammar notes:
- The three nouns, each with the article, form the subject; the plural participle מוֹצִיאִין agrees with all three. The ending ־ין is the Mishnaic plural.
- אֶת הָאָדָם is a definite object ('the person'): a person in general.
- מִן הָעוֹלָם: 'out of the world' (this world and the world to come, Rambam and Rabbeinu Yonah); the Hebrew does not say which.
- The saying is a short triad with no explanation, and the English leaves it so.

Translator's note: 'Honor' stands for the pursuit of honor (Rambam: אהבת הכבוד; Bartenura: that people honor him). 'Desire' is Bartenura's appetite for food, drink and sex; 'envy' is קִנְאָה, as in the commentary. The ambiguity of 'out of the world' is kept.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:21:1` follows: Bartenura: תַּאֲוָה is one who seeks to fulfil his desire in eating, drinking, sexual relations and the like. The English 'desire' is kept general.
- `bartenura:4:21:2` follows: Bartenura: כָּבוֹד is that people should honor him, that is, the wish to be honored. The English 'honor' keeps this.
- `rambam:4:21:1` follows: Rambam: envy, desire and the love of honor: by these traits or any one of them a man loses faith in the Torah, and neither intellectual nor moral excellence reaches him. The English keeps the plain 'take out of the world'.
- apparatus: Variant: Vilna reads אליעזר for אֶלְעָזָר.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah (not normative) cites Prov. 14:30 (envy), 21:25 (desire) and the death of Joseph (honor, Berakhot 55a) for how each takes a person out of the world.

### Translation under review (4:22)

English: He used to say: Those who are born are to die, and the dead are to be revived, and the living are to be judged; to know, to make known, and to come to know that He is God, He is the Former, He is the Creator, He is the Discerner, He is the Judge, He is the Witness, He is the Plaintiff, and He is about to judge. Grammar notes:
- The first line is a threefold sequence with לְ + infinitive (or participle): born to die, dead to be revived, living to be judged.
- לֵידַע לְהוֹדִיעַ וּלְהִוָּדַע is a series of three infinitives (Qal, Hiphil, Niphal) of ידע, and the three cases, Bartenura says, are from others, to others, and of oneself.
- A string of הוּא clauses names God in seven roles; הוּא before each noun is a pronoun used as a copula ('He is ...').
- Five times the same phrase עַל כָּרְחֲךָ ('against your will') opens a clause; each time אַתָּה with a passive or stative form follows.
- מֶלֶךְ מַלְכֵי הַמְּלָכִים ('king of the kings of kings'): the construct chain heaps up the superlative; the Vilna text has the same.
- וְדַע שֶׁהַכֹּל לְפִי הַחֶשְׁבּוֹן is a bare imperative and a short sentence: the Hebrew does not say who reckons.

Translator's note: The Hebrew leaves open who 'knows', 'makes known' and 'comes to know'; the English keeps the three infinitives bare, and Bartenura's glosses (from others, to others, from oneself) are given in the commentary entries. אֵל is read as 'God', following Bartenura's silence and Rabbeinu Yonah's gloss 'strong, mighty' recorded in the apparatus; the pack lexicon links token 14 to אַל ('not'), which is not the sense here (data kept). עָתִיד לָדוּן is 'is about to judge' (Bartenura: in the world to come). שֶׁהַכֹּל לְפִי הַחֶשְׁבּוֹן: 'according to the reckoning'; Bartenura gives the sum of small coins, which the English does not add. In the line on the impulse, שְׁאוֹל is Sheol; the pack lemma (Saul) is wrong (data kept).

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:4:22:1` follows: Bartenura: one who has been born is already destined to die.
- `bartenura:4:22:2` follows: Bartenura: the dead are destined to live and to stand on the Day of Judgment, some to eternal life, some to shame and eternal contempt (Dan. 12:2). The English keeps the plain 'to be revived'.
- `bartenura:4:22:3` follows: Bartenura: the living are standing to be judged on the Day of Judgment.
- `bartenura:4:22:4` follows: Bartenura on לֵידַע: from others. The English 'to know' keeps the bare infinitive.
- `bartenura:4:22:5` follows: Bartenura (on 'וּלְהוֹדִיעַ'): to others. The English 'to make known' renders the Hiphil.
- `bartenura:4:22:6` follows: Bartenura on וּלְהִוָּדַע: to understand of oneself. The English 'to come to know' renders the Niphal.
- `bartenura:4:22:7` follows: Bartenura: the whole world is in His hand like clay in the potter's hand, and the reason is that He is the Creator, who made the whole world from nothing; therefore He can do with it as He wishes. The English follows the potter image of 'the Former'.
- `bartenura:4:22:8` follows: Bartenura: since He has understood all their deeds, He brings them to judgment before Him. This is why the Discerner is followed by the Judge.
- `bartenura:4:22:9` follows: Bartenura: 'plaintiff' means that He claims against sinners. The English 'Plaintiff' follows.
- `bartenura:4:22:10` follows: Bartenura on 'He is about to judge': in the world to come.
- `bartenura:4:22:11` follows: Bartenura: He does not show partiality even to a perfect righteous man, to spare him punishment for a light transgression that came his way, and He does not take a bribe, a commandment's reward set against a transgression, but gives reward for the commandment and punishment for the transgression.
- `bartenura:4:22:12` follows: Bartenura: coin by coin is added to a great sum; so light transgressions, when many, add up to a great reckoning. The English keeps 'the reckoning' without the coin image.
- `bartenura:4:22:13` follows: Bartenura: the soul does not wish to leave the curtain (the pure place where souls are kept) and enter a woman's womb, an impure place; an angel comes and brings it out against its will. The English keeps the bare 'against your will'.
- `bartenura:4:22:14` follows: Bartenura: at the time of birth he does not want to come out, and an angel brings him out against his will.
- `bartenura:4:22:15` follows: Bartenura: there is a man crushed by suffering who wants to die and cannot.
- `rambam:4:22:1` follows: Rambam: the Holy One knows those who will be born, those born now and those who will live after death, and from these three classes one learns that He is the Former and the Creator. 'The Judge' and 'about to judge': He judges all now, in life, death and the affairs of the world, and will also judge those who live in the world to come, with reward and punishment. 'No taking of bribes' is as the Torah says. The English follows this.

- apparatus: Rambam (normative, on 'says'): the Holy One knows those who are to be born, those being born now, and those who will live after death; from these three classes it is learned that He is the Former and the Creator; 'the Judge and about to judge' means He judges now in life, death and world affairs, and will judge those who live in the world to come with reward and punishment. 'No taking of bribes' is as the Torah says (Deut. 10:17).
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah (not normative): אֵל from the sense 'mighty' (as in 2 Kings 24:15, 'the mighty of the land').
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah: 'Former' is the start of the work and 'Creator' its completion.
- apparatus: Rabbeinu Yonah on 'let your impulse assure you': do not say that Sheol is a place of refuge, as the heretics say, 'in the world to come there is neither judgment nor judge'.
