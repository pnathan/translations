### Translation under review (1:7)

English: Nittai the Arbelite says: Keep far from a bad neighbor, and do not associate with a wicked person, and do not despair of retribution.

Literal: Nittai the-Arbelite says keep-far from-neighbor bad and-not associate-yourself to-the-wicked and-not despair from-the-retribution

Phrases:

- 1:7.3 | says | participle
- 1:7.4 1:7.5 1:7.6 | Keep far from a bad neighbor | 'keep-far from-neighbor bad'; a bad neighbor, as Bartenura reads, so as not to learn from his deeds or share his downfall
- 1:7.7 1:7.8 1:7.9 | and do not associate with a wicked person | 'and-not associate-yourself to-the-wicked'
- 1:7.10 1:7.11 1:7.12 1:7.13 | and do not despair of retribution | 'and-not despair from-the-retribution'; do not lose hope that punishment will come

Grammar notes:
- הַרְחֵק is a Hifil imperative: 'make yourself distant'.
- Two prohibitions use אַל with the imperfect (Hitpael): אַל תִּתְחַבֵּר and אַל תִּתְיָאֵשׁ.
- The three clauses are parallel and short; the saying moves from neighbor to companion to the wicked's fate.
- הָאַרְבֵּלִי is a gentilic: 'the man of Arbel'.

Translator's note: וְאַל תִּתְיָאֵשׁ מִן הַפֻּרְעָנוּת: Bartenura and Rambam both read 'do not give up hope that the wicked will be punished'; Rambam adds that punishment may come swiftly in this world.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:7:1` follows: Bartenura: keep away so that you do not learn from his deeds, and so that you are not caught in his downfall: 'woe to the wicked, woe to his neighbor'.
- `bartenura:1:7:2` follows: Bartenura: whoever clings to the wicked receives a reward like theirs even if he does not act as they do, like one who enters a tanner's house and carries the bad smell out with him.
- `bartenura:1:7:3` follows: Bartenura: do not say 'this wicked man prospers, I will cling to him'; know that retribution will come quickly, 'for his calamity comes suddenly'.
- `rambam:1:7:1` follows: Rambam: this is a kind of love and fellowship, forbidden so that you not learn from his deeds; when a sinner is seen, do not trust that God will punish him only in the world to come, and do not despair of swift vengeance on him.
- apparatus: Vilna reads הפורענות (full spelling) for base הַפֻּרְעָנוּת.

Grammar notes:
- The saying has two parallel temporal clauses with וּכְשֶׁ: 'when they stand ... when they depart'.
- יִהְיוּ ... כִרְשָׁעִים and כְזַכָּאִין compare in similes: before the verdict, treat each as a likely wrongdoer; afterwards, as innocent.
- בַעֲלֵי דִינִין is an idiom: 'masters of judgment', that is, the parties to a case; a Mishnaic plural ending in -ין.
- The first clause begins with אַל plus the imperfect, a prohibition; עַצְמְךָ is 'yourself' (lit. 'your bone, your self').
- קִבְּלוּ מֵהֶם: Piel perfect, 'received from them', the standard formula of transmission in this chapter.

Translator's note: כְעוֹרְכֵי הַדַּיָּנִין: Bartenura gives two readings, 'those who arrange the litigants' pleas before the judges' (so also Rambam: those who coach litigants) and 'the great ones of the judges'; the English follows the first. A third reading Bartenura heard: do not act as a senior judge to compel litigants to appear.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:8:1` follows: Bartenura: like those who arrange and set out the pleas of the litigants before the judges, for it is forbidden to reveal the law to one litigant. His second reading, 'like the great judges', is recorded here as an alternative and is not adopted.
- `bartenura:1:8:2` follows: Bartenura: let your heart not incline to either party, saying 'so-and-so is a person of standing', and let him not plead a false plea; if you think so, you will not see his guilt.
- `bartenura:1:8:3` follows: Bartenura: do not suspect the one found liable of being a thief; say he perhaps erred. If one has sworn, do not say he swore falsely.
- `rambam:1:8:1` follows: Rambam: the 'arrangers of the judges' are people who learn pleas until they are expert, and compose questions and answers for litigants; the mishnah warns not to resemble them, i.e. not to teach a litigant a plea that will help him.

Grammar notes:
- אֱהֹב and שְׂנָא are two imperatives (Qal) with the object marker אֶת: two commands in parallel, then a prohibition.
- רַבָּנוּת is a Mishnaic abstract noun from רַב: 'mastery, rabbinate'.
- The third clause is a prohibition: אַל with the Hitpael imperfect of יָדַע, 'do not make yourself known'.
- לָרָשׁוּת: the definite 'the authority' is expressed by ה assimilated into ל.

Translator's note: וּשְׂנָא אֶת הָרַבָּנוּת: Bartenura gives two readings: do not say 'I am a great man and it is beneath me to work', and 'keep away from lording it over the community, for authority buries its owner'. The English 'lordship' keeps both. לָרָשׁוּת: the commentators agree it means the ruling power (שררה, שלטנות).

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:10:1` not-applicable: Bartenura: they were righteous converts, descended from Sennacherib; Avtalyon was named 'father of the little ones' because he was head of a court. This concerns the names, not the sense of the saying.
- `bartenura:1:10:2` follows: Bartenura: even one who has a livelihood must engage in work, for idleness leads to boredom.
- `bartenura:1:10:3` follows: Bartenura: his second reading, to distance yourself from exercising authority over a community, is the sense kept in 'lordship'; his first reading, do not say 'I am too great to work', is recorded in the note.
- `bartenura:1:10:4` follows: Bartenura: do not make yourself known to the authority in order to gain rabbinic office through it, or lest they remove you from the will of your Maker, as happened to Doeg the Edomite; authority is called רשות because the power is in its hand to do as it wishes.
- `rambam:1:10:1` follows: Rambam: רשות is rulership; these three qualities protect faith and the world: without work one is driven to theft and sin; seeking office brings trials; nearness to power corrupts, as Doeg shows.
- apparatus: Vilna reads אהוב for base אֱהֹב.

Grammar notes:
- Hebrew chains six verbs with וְ: תָחוּבוּ, תִגְלוּ, יִשְׁתּוּ, יָמוּתוּ, נִמְצָא, מִתְחַלֵּל: a sequence of consequences after שֶׁמָּא.
- חֲכָמִים is a vocative: 'O sages'.
- The Hebrew has a play of sound and meaning in חוֹבַת גָּלוּת, 'the liability of exile'.
- נִמְצָא plus participle: 'it turns out that (the name of Heaven) is being profaned'.
- מַיִם הָרָעִים: figurative: the evil waters are heretical teachings that the disciples would 'drink'.

Translator's note: מַיִם הָרָעִים: the English keeps the metaphor and gives the sense in the note; Bartenura and Rambam both take the 'evil waters' as heresy (מִינוּת, אפיקורסות). Bartenura sees the exile as the way the sages' words fall among heretics, who mis-read them.

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:11:1` follows: Bartenura: do not leave room in your words for the heretics (מִינִים) to err.
- `bartenura:1:11:2` follows: Bartenura: even if no heretics are where you are, sin may bring exile to a place where people give the Torah a false appearance; those are the 'evil waters', and the disciples who come after drink these heretical words and die in their guilt.
- `bartenura:1:11:3` follows: Bartenura: those false opinions remain in the world, as happened with Antigonos of Socho and Zadok and Boethus, from whom the Sadducees and Boethusians arose.
- `rambam:1:11:1` follows: Rambam: 'evil waters' is a figure for heresy; be careful in your words among the crowd so that they cannot be read another way, for heretics would read them according to their own belief, the disciples would turn to heresy and the name of Heaven would be profaned, as happened with Antigonos, Zadok and Boethus.
- apparatus: The mishnah as printed here has no variant readings recorded in the pack.

Grammar notes:
- הֱוֵי מִתַּלְמִידָיו שֶׁל אַהֲרֹן: מִ means 'of, among', and the possessive uses שֶׁל, a Mishnaic form.
- Four participles, אוֹהֵב, רוֹדֵף, אוֹהֵב, מְקָרְבָן, follow the imperative as a list of Aaron's qualities.
- שָׁלוֹם is repeated: 'love peace and pursue peace': a pair of verbs of increasing effort.
- הַבְּרִיּוֹת is 'creatures': here human beings generally, not only Israel; מְקָרְבָן has the feminine suffix -ן on 'them'.

Translator's note: הַבְּרִיּוֹת: literally 'the creatures'; rendered 'people' because the context (bringing them to the Torah) is of human beings. מְקָרְבָן: the suffix ן is a feminine plural form on a collective noun (בְּרִיּוֹת is feminine plural).

Commentary rulings:
- `bartenura:1:12:1` follows: Bartenura (citing Avot de-Rabbi Natan): Aaron would go to each of two quarrelling people and say that the other regrets it, until they kissed; and he would befriend one who had sinned, who would feel shame and return to the good, as in Malachi 2:6.
- `rambam:1:12:1` follows: Rambam: Aaron would greet one who he knew had sinned, befriend him and speak much with him, until the man was ashamed and returned to good, and became one of his disciples; so the prophet says 'he walked with Me in peace and uprightness, and turned many from iniquity'.
- apparatus: Quotation of Malachi 2:6 (בְּשָׁלוֹם וּבְמִישׁוֹר הָלַךְ אִתִּי) appears in Bartenura, not in the mishnah.
- apparatus: No variant readings are recorded in the pack for this mishnah.
